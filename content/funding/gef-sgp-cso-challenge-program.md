The GEF Small Grants Programme CSO Challenge Program is a USD 10 million global grants facility funding civil society organizations with proven environmental solutions ready for scaling and replication. Grants run up to USD 300,000 per project. The program is implemented by IUCN and executed jointly by IUCN and RTI International, funded through the GEF Trust Fund under GEF-8 (GEF Project ID 11757).

## Mandate and scope

The program identifies, rewards, and supports high-impact innovative environmental approaches spearheaded by civil society organizations. It targets five thematic areas: sustainable agriculture, community-based management of threatened ecosystems and species, sustainable cities, low-carbon energy access, and chemicals and waste management.

Geographic eligibility covers all GEF-eligible countries. The program prioritizes youth, women, and Indigenous Peoples as lead actors.

## Grant mechanics

Individual grants up to USD 300,000. Total GEF project grant: USD 9,075,229, with co-financing of USD 4,258,100. The concept was approved in December 2024, the project approved for implementation in December 2025, and the first call for proposals opened in early 2026. Guidance documents (concept notes and applicant guidelines) are available in English, Spanish, and French through the program website.

## Anticipated outcomes

The program targets 12,000 hectares under restoration, 159,000 hectares under improved management practices, and 25,000 direct beneficiaries (50 percent women).

## Alignment with the BioConomy

The CSO Challenge Program's mandate overlaps the wiki's subject matter at several points. Its emphasis on community-based ecosystem management and sustainable agriculture maps directly onto [[content/glossary/a-e/bioregional-economics|bioregional economics]] as practiced through [[concepts/biohub|BioHub]] coordination. The prioritization of Indigenous Peoples and traditional knowledge aligns with the wiki's treatment of [[commons|commons]] governance as a living institutional form rather than a policy abstraction. The grant scale (up to USD 300,000) sits in the range relevant to bioregional pilot projects: large enough to fund a multi-year coordination effort, small enough to require the kind of local institutional depth the [[frameworks/time-framework|TIME framework]] describes.

The program's theory of change, scaling proven solutions through civil society actors rather than state or market channels, is itself a +N coordination pattern: it routes resources through network-form organizations rather than through bilateral aid or market intermediaries.
