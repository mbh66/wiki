Daniel C. Dennett (1942-2024) was an American philosopher who taught at Tufts University, where he co-directed the Center for Cognitive Studies [IC]. He died in April 2024 [IC].

## Contribution

Dennett argued against the picture of a Cartesian theatre, an inner stage where experience is shown to an observer [IC]. In *Consciousness Explained* (1991) he proposed a multiple drafts model, in which parallel processes in the brain revise content with no single moment when it becomes conscious, and treated the human self as a virtual machine installed by language and culture [IC]. He held that the [[glossary/f-l/hard-problem|hard problem]] rests on intuitions that a full account of the brain dissolves [IC]. His later books, including *Darwin's Dangerous Idea* (1995) and *From Bacteria to Bach and Back* (2017), carried the same naturalism into evolution and culture [IC]. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Dennett sits in the critics' row and dissents on the hinge.
