American organizational theorist (1923-2013), long at Yale and then Harvard, whose work on organizational learning produced the [[ladder-of-inference|ladder of inference]], the distinction between espoused theory and theory-in-use, and the concept of double-loop learning.

## Contribution

Argyris studied why intelligent people in organizations so often fail to learn from their own mistakes. With Donald Schön he distinguished the theories people say guide their action (espoused theories) from the theories their behavior actually reveals (theories-in-use). Single-loop learning corrects actions within existing assumptions; double-loop learning questions the assumptions themselves. Most organizations, he argued, run on defensive routines that protect people from embarrassment and threat and, in doing so, block double-loop learning.

The ladder of inference comes out of this work. It maps the fast climb from observable data through selection, meaning, assumptions and conclusions to belief and action, and the reflexive loop by which beliefs pick the next round of evidence. Argyris's remedies work on reasoning: make each rung visible, invite others to walk you down your ladder, and test inferences against directly observable data. *The Fifth Discipline Fieldbook* (Senge et al. 1994) carried the ladder and his left-hand column exercise to a wide practitioner audience.

## Contribution to the corpus

The ladder is the base model for [[concepts/ladder-lens-self-talk|The Ladder, the Lens and Self-Talk]], which adds three layers to it: the substrate, the [[lens|lens]] and [[self-talk|self-talk]]. The corpus credits Argyris with the ladder only. Where the corpus departs from him is on the remedy. Argyris located the problem in participant reasoning; the [[concepts/substrate-hypothesis|Substrate Hypothesis]] predicts that better reasoning habits fade when the coordination substrate keeps re-selecting the data. The two positions are complementary: ladder work helps people see that their beliefs have a substrate history, and substrate change keeps that insight from evaporating.

## Selected works

- Argyris, C., and Schön, D. A. (1974). *Theory in Practice: Increasing Professional Effectiveness*. Jossey-Bass. Espoused theory and theory-in-use.
- Argyris, C., and Schön, D. A. (1978). *Organizational Learning: A Theory of Action Perspective*. Addison-Wesley. Single-loop and double-loop learning.
- Argyris, C., Putnam, R., and Smith, D. M. (1985). *Action Science*. Jossey-Bass.
- Argyris, C. (1990). *Overcoming Organizational Defenses: Facilitating Organizational Learning*. Allyn and Bacon. Defensive routines and the reasoning that sustains them.
