Julian Jaynes (1920-1997) was an American psychologist who taught at Princeton University for most of his career [IC]. He is known for one book, *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind* (1976), which argues that introspective consciousness is learned through language and arose in historical time [IC].

## Contribution

Jaynes defined consciousness narrowly, as an inner space in which a person narrates and reflects on their own life, and held that it is built from metaphor and language [IC]. Before it, he argued, people lived with a [[glossary/a-e/bicameral-mind|bicameral mind]]: commands from one hemisphere were heard as the voices of gods and obeyed. He dated the breakdown of that mentality to about 3,000 years ago, under the pressure of writing and large, mixed societies [IC]. Ned Block's 1981 review argued that Jaynes confused consciousness with the concept of consciousness, and most specialists reject the dating; the Julian Jaynes Society continues his work and publishes replies to critics [IC]. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Jaynes stands for the boldest version of step A3, the claim that the structure of awareness itself has changed.
