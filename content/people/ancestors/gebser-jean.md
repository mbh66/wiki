Jean Gebser (1905-1973) was a German-born philosopher, linguist and poet who lived in Spain in the 1930s, left during the Spanish Civil War, and settled in Switzerland in 1939 [IC]. His major work, *Ursprung und Gegenwart* (1949 and 1953), was published in English as *The Ever-Present Origin* in 1985 [IC].

## Contribution

Gebser described five [[glossary/m-s/structure-of-consciousness|structures of consciousness]], each a distinct way of experiencing space and time: archaic, magic, mythical, mental and integral [IC]. He called the changes between them mutations and declined to read them as progress, and held that every earlier structure stays active in every person [IC]. He saw the mental structure in a deficient phase in his own century and an integral, [[glossary/a-e/aperspectival|aperspectival]] structure emerging in art, physics and literature [IC]. The wiki's [[concepts/structures-of-consciousness|Structures of Consciousness]] and [[concepts/major-evolutionary-transitions|Major Evolutionary Transitions]] build on his work through Roger Briggs and Ken Wilber; the five network names are Michael Haupt's [MS]. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Gebser affirms steps A3, C1, C2 and C4.
