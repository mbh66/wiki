Pierre Teilhard de Chardin (1881-1955) was a French Jesuit priest, geologist and palaeontologist [IC]. He spent much of his working life in China, where he took part in the excavations at Zhoukoudian that recovered the fossils known as Peking Man [IC].

## Contribution

Teilhard argued that evolution is a rise in complexity matched by a rise in consciousness, a within of things present in some degree in all matter [IC]. With humans, he held, evolution becomes reflective, and humanity forms a thinking layer around the Earth, the [[glossary/m-s/noosphere|noosphere]], converging toward an Omega Point [IC]. His order did not allow *The Phenomenon of Man* to be published in his lifetime; it appeared in French in 1955, the year he died, and in English in 1959 [IC]. Peter Medawar's 1961 review rejected it as science [IC]. Vladimir Vernadsky developed the noosphere in a different, naturalist direction. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Teilhard affirms steps A1, B3, C2, C3 and the hinge, and he is a source of the network's image of planetary consciousness.
