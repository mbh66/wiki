American anthropologist whose work traced credit and obligation through economies that ran without markets or coinage.

## Contribution

In *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011), Graeber argued that credit systems came before coinage and that the familiar story of money arising from barter has no support in the anthropological record. Relations of obligation, recorded and enforced in many forms, organized economic life long before markets did.

He taught at Yale University and at Goldsmiths, University of London, and from 2013 was a professor of anthropology at the London School of Economics. He took part in the Occupy Wall Street movement in 2011. He died in Venice in September 2020.
