Dutch expert in sustainable innovation, sustainability strategy, and integrated reporting. Co-initiator of [[baue-bill|r3.0]] (Redesign for Resilience and Regeneration) with [[baue-bill|Bill Baue]], where he serves as content curator and facilitator. Co-founder of the ThriveAbility Foundation. Earlier career positions include Head of the Sustainability Strategy Council at Siemens, COO of the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI), and Director of Sustainability and Innovation at Deloitte. Thurm was involved in the development of all four generations of the GRI Guidelines.

## Contribution

Thurm's central intellectual contribution is the diagnosis that mainstream sustainability practice, including ESG reporting, the SDGs, and the EU taxonomy, operates within a paradigm that cannot deliver regeneration. His "Big Sustainability Illusion" essay series argues that the ESG industry rates, ranks, and certifies organizations in ways that improve share prices while leaving the ecological and social substrate unrepaired. The argument is structural: the reporting frameworks measure incremental improvement against a baseline of continued extraction, when what is needed is threshold-based assessment against planetary boundaries and social floors.

This diagnosis connects to the BioConomy corpus through the [[coercion-continuum|coercion continuum]] and the [[cheapest-available-behavior|cheapest available behavior]] thesis. The ESG infrastructure Thurm critiques is itself a product of the M-form coordination substrate: it measures what Markets can price, rewards what Markets can trade, and leaves unmeasured the ecological outcomes that fall outside the pricing mechanism. Thurm's prescription, threshold-based sustainability context applied through the r3.0 blueprints, is an attempt to impose +N accountability on M-form actors without changing the monetary substrate they operate on. The BioConomy's own framing treats this as a necessary and valuable intervention that nonetheless faces the (+T+I+M)^+N constraint: any accountability framework that leaves the monetary substrate intact defaults to triform coordination with a +N overlay.

The r3.0 platform produces transformation blueprints across accounting, reporting, data, new business models, education, and governance. Thurm also co-established the Global Thresholds and Allocations Council (GTAC), which develops the methodological infrastructure for threshold-based sustainability assessment.

## Selected writings and media

Thurm's blog A|HEAD|ahead carries a running commentary on sustainability governance, ESG reporting failures, and systemic transition.

The "Big Sustainability Illusion" series (four parts, published on LinkedIn):

- "How the Whole Crowd of ESG Progress..."
- "How Limited ESG Progress..."
- "How to Escape the ESG Bubble..."
- "Goodbye ESG Lalaland, Hello..."

His "Sunday Thought" series on LinkedIn documents the accumulating evidence for structural failure in the sustainability reporting ecosystem, covering collapse reporting, degrowth, career risk for sustainability officers, and the gap between SDG commitments and ecological outcomes.

A three-part interview with Dr Robin Lincoln Wood (May 2021) captures Thurm's position in its sharpest form: "Ask any organization about inter-generational equity and they don't have a clue what you mean, but they all publish sustainability reports, they all have chief sustainability officers, they're all rated and ranked and actually improve their share price being 'sustainable'."
