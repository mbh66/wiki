Australian economist and critic of mainstream economics, known for arguing that private debt and bank lending drive the boom and bust of modern economies.

## Contribution

Keen builds on Hyman Minsky's financial instability hypothesis. He argues that mainstream macroeconomic models leave out banks and private debt, and that these omissions hide the main cause of financial crises. Before 2008 he warned that rising private debt would end in a crisis. He developed Minsky, an open-source program for modeling economies as systems of monetary flows between balance sheets.

He was an associate professor at the University of Western Sydney until 2013 and head of the School of Economics, History and Politics at Kingston University London from 2014. He is now an honorary professor at University College London and a distinguished research fellow at its Institute for Strategy, Resilience and Security.
