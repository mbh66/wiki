Terrence W. Deacon is an American biological anthropologist and neuroscientist, professor emeritus of anthropology at the University of California, Berkeley, where he formerly chaired the Department of Anthropology and is a member of the Helen Wills Neuroscience Institute [IC]. He is named on Michael Haupt's list of mycelial thought leaders for his two books on the emergence of mind [MS].

## Contribution

*The Symbolic Species* (1997) argues that symbolic reference, the ability to use signs that stand for other signs, is the threshold that sets human minds apart, and that language and the brain coevolved over a long period once early *Homo* crossed it [IC]. *Incomplete Nature* (2011) proposes an account of how purpose and mind emerge from matter, through what he calls constraints and absences in physical processes [IC]. Both books work within naturalism. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Deacon affirms step A2 and qualifies A1 and A3: he describes a change in cognitive capacity and stops short of claims about the structure of awareness.
