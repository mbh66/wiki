Merlin Donald is a Canadian psychologist and cognitive neuroscientist, professor emeritus in the Department of Psychology and the Faculty of Education at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, where he has taught since 1972 [IC]. In 2005 he became founding chair of the cognitive science department at Case Western Reserve University [IC]. His *Origins of the Modern Mind* (1991) is one of the most cited accounts of how human cognition evolved [TBV].

## Contribution

Donald describes four stages of human culture: episodic (the primate baseline), [[glossary/m-s/mimetic-culture|mimetic]] (with *Homo erectus*), mythic (with spoken language) and theoretic (with writing and other external memory) [IC]. Each stage adds a way of representing and storing knowledge, and each is kept inside the next, so the modern mind is a hybrid [IC]. *A Mind So Rare* (2001) develops the argument that human consciousness is shaped by culture. Robert Bellah built his history of religion on Donald's stages, and Roger Briggs drew on them for the early part of his sequence [IC]. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Donald gives the clearest dated account of step A2 and strong support for co-presence (C1). The network's first structure takes his word.
