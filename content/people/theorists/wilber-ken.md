Ken Wilber is an American writer and the founder of the Integral Institute (1998), whose books since *The Spectrum of Consciousness* (1977) have set out what he calls integral theory [IC].

## Contribution

Wilber's model maps development in four quadrants (interior and exterior, individual and collective) and holds that each stage transcends and includes the one before it [IC]. He draws on Gebser for the stages of culture and on developmental psychology for the stages of a person, and argues that the two broadly correspond [IC]. He reads evolution as Spirit coming to know itself, and holds consciousness to be ultimately nondual [IC]. Jeff Meyerhoff's *Bald Ambition* (2010) criticises his use of sources [TBV]. Roger Briggs drew on Wilber for his sequence, and the network's synthesis builds on Briggs [MS]. In the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|evolution of consciousness grid]] Wilber affirms seven of the eleven steps, more than any other source.
