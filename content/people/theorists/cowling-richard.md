South African vegetation ecologist and conservation scientist, Distinguished Professor in Botany at Nelson Mandela University and an honorary professor at the University of Cape Town. Richard Cowling's research on the plant diversity of the Cape and its conservation has shaped protected-area planning and restoration practice in the region.

## Contribution

Cowling studied botany at the University of Cape Town through to his PhD, held a postdoctoral fellowship at Curtin University in Australia, and later served as UCT's Leslie Hill Professor of Plant Conservation and director of its Leslie Hill Institute for Plant Conservation. His research covers community ecology, plant diversity and evolution, palaeoecology, conservation planning, restoration ecology, invasion biology and ecosystem services. His research on restoring degraded subtropical thicket with spekboom guides public and private restoration programmes.

He held an NRF A-rating across five evaluation periods from 1998 to 2021, is a foreign member of the US National Academy of Sciences, and received the Academy of Science of South Africa's Gold Medal in 2023.

The research brief on [[research/bioprecipitation|Bioprecipitation]] cites his 2013 work with Anthony Mills and others on transformed renosterveld as part of the restoration evidence base for the fynbos biome.
