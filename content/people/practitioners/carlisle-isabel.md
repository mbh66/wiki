British founder and director of the [[bioregional-learning-centre|Bioregional Learning Centre]] (BLC) in South Devon, UK. Since 2017 the BLC has been mapping South Devon for the green shoots of climate resilience. In September 2019 the centre launched its first leadership program for systems change, a Climate Resilience Learning Journey around the bioregion, which kick-started the Climate Resilience Strategy in South Devon. The strategy invites all sectors into co-design of a sustainable future for the bioregion.

## Contribution

Carlisle's work sits at the intersection of bioregional organizing and systems-change leadership. The BLC is a working instance of what the BioConomy corpus calls a [[bioregional-learning-centre|Bioregional Learning Centre]]: a community-facing learning lab for real-world resilience, social and ecological regeneration, grounded in a specific place. Her approach to the South Devon bioregion demonstrates the pattern at landscape scale: mapping existing regenerative activity, convening multi-sector conversations, and building shared capacity for thinking in systems and across systems.

During the Covid-19 period Carlisle pivoted the BLC's convening work online, hosting multi-sector conversations on regional resilience so that participants would be skilled in joined-up systems thinking when they regrouped after the crisis. This adaptation illustrates one of the practical realities the [[biohub|BioHub]] model faces: coordination nodes must sustain their convening function through disruptions, and the ones that survive are the ones that can shift modality without losing the relationships.

Carlisle is an Open Fellow at One Planet Business and a member of the Doughnut Economics Action Lab (DEAL) network. Her essay "Bioregioning Is a Way of Acting and Mobilising People in Their Locality" (published at The Alternative UK, June 2020) describes the South Devon approach in terms that parallel the BioConomy's treatment of [[bioregion|BioRegion]] formation: identifying the geography through ecological and watershed boundaries, mapping existing actors, and building coordination capacity from the ground up.
