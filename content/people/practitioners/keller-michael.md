Organizational development consultant, coach and educator based in Victoria, British Columbia. Co-founder, with Jean Singer, of [Ecology of Design in Human Systems](https://www.ecologyofdesigninhumansystems.com/), and Associate Faculty in the Master of Arts in Leadership program at Royal Roads University. A friend and dialogue partner of [[haupt-michael|Michael Haupt]], whose conversation on 25 September 2026 brought lens and self-talk into the corpus's reading of the [[ladder-of-inference|ladder of inference]].

## Contribution

Keller's work centers on building collaborative, multicultural relationships in organizations. His expertise grew out of a decade living in Nepal and Tibet, and he holds a master's degree in Intercultural Communications from Antioch University. His consulting emphasizes multicultural dynamics and cross-cultural adjustment, alongside executive and team coaching, for clients across government, non-profits, financial and educational institutions and professional associations.

Ecology of Design in Human Systems describes its purpose as co-creating learning environments in which people develop their innovation and adaptive capacities to meet emerging challenges, while maximizing the fit between their people, resources and sustainable living. Its change model works with four questions: the motivation for change and its impact, how the problem is defined and resolved, whether implementation is feasible, and which methods fit the situation.

## Contribution to the corpus

In a Zoom dialogue with Michael Haupt on 25 September 2026, lens and self-talk were named as additions to Argyris's ladder of inference: the [[lens|lens]] as the frame through which participants interpret the world, and [[self-talk|self-talk]] as the narrative through which each person explains the world to themselves, carrying their cognitive biases. Haupt developed both into the three-layer model set out in [[concepts/ladder-lens-self-talk|The Ladder, the Lens and Self-Talk]] and applied it to governance in the [[e-form-emergent|Emergent form]], where people from very different perspectives meet without a built-in tie-breaker. The dialogue also prompted the research brief [[research/time-profile|A TIME Profile for Multi-Perspective Governance]].

Keller's intercultural practice bears directly on the lens concept, which treats interpretation as something a person inherits by belonging to a community, a language and a history.
