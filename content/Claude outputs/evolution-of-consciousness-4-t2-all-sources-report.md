# Research report: coding every source on T2, information that has not been explained

This run followed the brief in order: Part A (31 works) first, then Part B (82 works). It used ordinary web search and fetches, about seventy calls. Most Part A codings rest on reviews, reference entries, publisher descriptions or open texts; few in-copyright books were read directly. Where a reading is thin, the grade says so.

## 1. Summary

- Works reached: 113 of 113 (Part A 31, Part B 82).
- Positions found:
  - Part A: affirms 7, qualifies 5, dissents 3, silent 16.
  - Part B: silent 82.
  - All works: affirms 7, qualifies 5, dissents 3, silent 98.
- Graded `[IC]`: 6 of the 15 non-silent codings (Augustine and Martin, Kelly et al., James 1898, James 1902, Laszlo, Aurobindo). The other 9 are `[TBV]`.
- Part B works moved to Part A: none. Three Part B works were checked more closely because they came near the claim (Jaynes, Taylor, Tao Te Ching); all three stay silent, with notes.
- Not reached: none.
- Possible miscoding among the 28 works already coded: one (`parnia-2023-aware-ii`), see Notes.

## 4. Notes

### Judgement calls

- **Huxley (#1), qualifies.** The coding rests on Huxley's endorsement of Broad's reading of Bergson and on his statement that by-passes of the reducing valve admit something more than useful perception without reaching perception of everything. That last limit is from memory of the text; the passage was not read in a permitted copy. If the filing session reads the passage and finds no stated limit, the square becomes affirms.
- **Van Lommel (#2), qualifies.** The paper reports the dentures case and argues that medical factors cannot explain the experiences, then frames the result as a challenge to mind-brain ideas. Craffert reads it as a stronger claim. Reading the Lancet discussion directly would settle both the position and the grade.
- **James 1898 (#8), affirms.** James writes that "to my own mind some of them are facts" (p. 25) without saying which. The brief's `qualifies` includes accepting some cases with stated limits; James accepts some and states no limit, so affirms fits better. A filer who reads the hedge as a limit would code qualifies.
- **Edwards (#5), dissents.** This is an anthology with both sides represented. The square codes the editor's framing (introduction and his own two essays). The Badham and Beloff chapters would code differently on their own.
- **Lamont (#4), dissents.** The only account of Lamont's handling of psychical evidence found is Froom's, which also glosses Lamont as granting "undeniable phenomena". That gloss is Froom's and was not used for the square.
- **Webb (#18), qualifies.** The paper questions the textual warrant Huxley built for Mind at Large. A reader could treat "curated trope" as a conventional explanation of the experience and code dissents. The abstract alone does not show Webb ruling on whether information arrives, so qualifies is the safer reading.
- **Jung (#14), qualifies.** Jung offers the vision as evidence that motifs appear without transmission and explains it by inheritance. That explanation changes the claim. Critics (Noll, Shamdasani) argue for cryptomnesia and note an earlier 1903 edition of the liturgy; those critiques are about the case, and belong to a separate square if the grid ever adds them.
- **Churchland (#22) and Lewis-Williams (#20), silent.** Both explain experiences in neural terms. Under the brief's rule they dissent only where they apply the explanation to claims of information gained. Neither account consulted shows them doing so. Churchland's ch. 3 discusses van Lommel's data (pp. 70-71 per Lane and Diem-Lane); if the text there disputes veridical reports, the square becomes dissents.
- **Laszlo (#11), H2.** On T2 the coding is affirms. On H2 a filer could argue that Laszlo presents the A-field as physics (the zero-point field), which would make the route explained by his science and move the square to qualifies. Laszlo himself presents the field as an addition that current science still needs, so this run keeps affirms on both wordings.
- **Aurobindo (#15), `[IC]`.** The independent account is a sympathetic study outline by a named author, published outside the Ashram that publishes the text. It meets the brief's test of independence from author and publisher. A filer who wants a scholarly account would keep `[TBV]`.
- **Testimony.** Bache (#12) and the Ra Material (#13) are coded as their authors present them. The Ra source is uncheckable by its nature; Bache's sessions are first-person reports with no outside check described.

### Indigenous works

Deloria (#29), Wilson (#30), Odora Hoppers (#63), Yunkaporta (#73), Two-Eyed Seeing (#108, #110-113), Kimmerer (#109) and the DST policy (#106) are coded silent. None of the accounts consulted shows these works stating that knowledge arrives by routes science does not explain. Knowledge held in relationship, ceremony, story and Country is described in their own terms in the Says column and has not been translated into the language of psi. Deloria argues that oral traditions record real events; the knowledge passes through tradition, which is a known route.

### Part B works checked more closely

- **Jaynes (#41).** Jaynes explains oracles and prophetic voices as bicameral hallucinations. He applies the explanation to the experience and makes no claim, in the accounts known to this run, that the voices carried outside knowledge. Under the brief's rule that is silent.
- **Taylor (#64).** Taylor writes about the spiritual lives of peoples outside "civilized" Eurasia. No account located shows The Fall claiming telepathy or other anomalous information. His essays place peoples on a developmental scale; per the brief, that placement is not repeated here.
- **Tao Te Ching (#69).** Chapter 47 speaks of knowing the world without going out of the door. In the work's own terms this concerns the Way and cultivation, and the run did not translate it into a claim about psi.

### Placements

Gebser (#16) attributes telepathic powers to the magic structure. His sources sometimes tie structures to named peoples. The Says sentence names the structure only and repeats no placement.

### Possible miscoding among the 28 coded works

- **`parnia-2023-aware-ii`, coded dissents.** The AWARE II abstract reports that nobody identified the hidden visual image and one of 28 identified the auditory stimulus. The authors class recalled experiences of death as distinct from hallucinations and dreams, and the press release speaks of the dying brain opening access to new dimensions of reality. The paper does not argue that conventional explanations account for claimed information, and it does not argue that the effect does not occur. On the brief's definitions it reads as qualifies. Sources: SUNY record, https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/awareness-during-resuscitation-ii-a-multi-center-study-of-conscio/; Elsevier press release, 14 September 2023, https://www.elsevier.com/about/press-releases/new-evidence-indicates-patients-recall-death-experiences-after-cardiac. A second account: Agudo, Fulco and Rivas, "Comments on the Results of the AWARE II Study", Journal of Near-Death Studies 41(1), 2024, 9-22. Not changed.

### Sources avoided

Several in-copyright works surfaced as copies on Anna's Archive mirrors, Basecamp attachments, Scribd, course-page PDFs (LSE, USP) and private ebook collections (Dennett, Chalmers, Jung CW 9i, Wilber, Sahtouris, the Ra Material). None was opened for coding or cited. Wikipedia and its mirrors appeared in searches and were used only to find primary or independent sources.

## 5. Gaps

Five works the T2 column most lacks, on both sides. Not coded.

1. **Ian Stevenson, Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation (1966; 2nd ed. 1974).** The grid holds Edwards's critique of reincarnation research and lacks the case studies it targets.
2. **Daryl J. Bem and Charles Honorton, "Does Psi Exist? Replicable Evidence for an Anomalous Process of Information Transfer", Psychological Bulletin 115 (1994).** The ganzfeld meta-analysis that started the modern debate over free-response psi.
3. **Julie Milton and Richard Wiseman, "Does Psi Exist? Lack of Replication of an Anomalous Process of Information Transfer", Psychological Bulletin 125 (1999).** The direct reply to Bem and Honorton; pairs with item 2.
4. **Janice Miner Holden, "Veridical Perception in Near-Death Experiences", in Holden, Greyson and James (eds.), The Handbook of Near-Death Experiences (2009).** The standard survey of veridical NDE reports, the evidence most at issue in squares 2 and 6.
5. **Dean Mobbs and Caroline Watt, "There is nothing paranormal about near-death experiences", Trends in Cognitive Sciences 15 (2011).** A short neuroscientific dissent on NDEs; van Lommel replied to it, so it would sit opposite square 2.
