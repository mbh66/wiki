### 1.1 The question

Since about 2020, companies and funds that buy land for carbon, biodiversity and "natural capital" returns have bought large Scottish estates, sometimes in competition with community bodies that wanted the same land. The register holds community-held catchments (Törbel, the Regole d'Ampezzo, Eigg) and counterpoints in which a claim on mountain water became a tradeable asset (Chile, California, the Murray-Darling Basin). It holds no case in which a natural capital investor and a community contested the same living landscape.

This run asks:

1. **Cases.** Which Scottish estates bought by natural capital investors since 2020 have a documented community claim, contest or agreement, and which of them have enough published evidence to enter the register?
2. **Transactions.** Who sold, who bought, at what price, with whose money, and on what terms?
3. **Community position.** What did the community bodies seek, what did they get, and by which route (community right to buy, Scottish Land Fund award, investor sale at cost, gift, agreement)?
4. **Value flow.** Where does money come from (equity, bank debt, public grants, carbon and biodiversity units, renewables, ecotourism) and where does it go?
5. **The living system.** What restoration was done, what was measured, and whether anything about water, peat hydrology or the catchment was measured at all.
6. **Outcome and durability.** What has happened to ownership, jobs, housing and the land since purchase, up to the date of the run.

A finding that a case lacks the evidence to enter the register is a full answer. Report it as plainly as a positive one.

### 1.2 Why these cases

Michael asked for Oxygen Conservation's Tayvallich estate as an edge case. A preliminary check found that Oxygen Conservation never owned Tayvallich: the buyer was Highlands Rewilding Ltd. The run keeps Tayvallich, attributed correctly, and adds Oxygen Conservation's own estates, where the preliminary check found documented community disputes.

### 1.3 The distinctions to keep

- **Investor versus charity versus community body.** Record the legal form of each owner (company limited by shares, charitable trust, company limited by guarantee with charitable status, community body under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003). Record who controls each and who funds it.
- **Owning versus managing.** Highlands Rewilding manages land it sold, under a lease. Oxygen Conservation manages land it does not own (Leighon, Devon). Code ownership and management separately.
- **Community ownership versus community benefit.** A memorandum of understanding, a benefit fund or a seat on an advisory board is a benefit arrangement. Title held by a community body is ownership. Code them apart.
- **Validated versus issued versus sold.** A Woodland Carbon Code or Peatland Code project can be validated, hold Pending Issuance Units, hold issued units, or have sold units. Record which, from the registry.
- **Public versus private money.** Forestry Grant Scheme, Peatland ACTION, Scottish Land Fund, National Wealth Fund (formerly UK Infrastructure Bank) and Triodos Bank are different kinds of money. Record each amount and its source.
- **The owner versus its sister companies.** Low Carbon and Oxygen Conservation are reported as sister companies under Oxygen House. Record which company holds which project.
- **Measured versus claimed.** Record whether a restoration outcome was measured by an instrument or a survey, modeled, or stated.

## 2. The inclusion test

The register's rule is that a case enters only if it mobilized A7 (living systems), and that a community holds, governs or is paid for a living catchment. An edge case enters as a counterpoint: a case where a community's claim on a living catchment was contested, displaced or partly met by an outside investor. For each candidate, report:

1. Is there a living catchment, with the watercourses and the river system named?
2. Is there a documented community claim, contest or agreement, from a community body or a community council, in a published record?
3. Is the transaction confirmed by an official record or two independent sources?
4. Does the case add something the register's existing counterpoints and the Eigg case do not?

A case that fails test 2 is reported in the hand-back and not coded.

## 3. Coding frame

Use the register's codes exactly, as defined on https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/cases/catchments/.

**Assets.** A1 individual gifts and skills, A2 associations, A3 local institutions, A4 physical and built assets, A5 economic exchange, A6 culture and story, A7 living systems, A8 the retention function itself, named as an asset.

**Coordination,** using the wiki's TIME Framework: T (Tribal), I (Institutional), M (Market), E (Emergent). Dominant form first.

**Beyond-market markers.** B1 collective or customary ownership of the catchment or its water; B2 rules on use, sharing and rest set by the users; B3 reciprocal or in-kind exchange with downstream users; B4 payments received into a collective fund under community rules; B5 legal personhood or rights of nature; B6 value retained through local currency, cooperative ownership or a community trust; B7 the community contracted as a provider of water outcomes; B8 decisions held by the community over an outside party's money.

**Lineage type.** The register's controlled values are `abcd-by-name`, `abcd-framed`, `commons`, `customary`, `community-forestry`, `community-land-trust`, `territory-of-life`, `biocultural-heritage`, `reciprocity`, `legal-personhood`, `negotiated-payments`, `water-fund`, `state-program`, `ngo-program`, `practitioner-innovation`. None fits a natural capital investor cleanly. Code with the nearest value and propose a new value (for example `natural-capital-investment`) in the hand-back, with a one-line definition. Michael decides whether the vocabulary changes.

**Tenure type:** `communal`, `customary`, `private`, `state`, `trust`, `legal-personhood`, `contested`, `mixed`.

## 4. The policy frame

Read each case against the Scottish rules in force at the time of sale and now:

- The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, Part 2 (community right to buy with a registered interest), Part 3A (abandoned, neglected or detrimental land) and Part 5 (sustainable development).
- The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2016 and the Land Rights and Responsibilities Statement.
- The Scottish Government's Interim Principles for Responsible Investment in Natural Capital and the Scottish Land Commission's guidance on community engagement and on Delivering Community Benefits from Land (November 2023).
- The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 (confirm its title, date and commencement): prior notification of large sales, lotting, the public interest test, the Land and Communities Commissioner, and land management plans.

For each case, state which provisions applied at the time and whether any provision of the 2025 Act would apply to a similar sale now. Report legal treatment as recorded; give no legal advice.

## 9. The predictions to grade

Grade each with evidence for, evidence against, a grade and one line on what the wiki can safely say.

**P1.** Where a community body competes with a natural capital investor on the open market, the community body loses because its public funding is capped at valuation.

**P2.** Investor–community agreements move land into community or charitable ownership mainly when the investor's finance is under strain.

**P3.** Debt-financed natural capital purchases depend on credit and nature markets that, in these cases, did not deliver on the expected timetable.

**P4.** Public grants carry most of the restoration cost on the investor estates studied.

**P5.** Recorded community complaints concern communication, jobs, farming and renewables more than ownership itself.

**P6.** None of the estates studied measures a water or catchment outcome.

**P7.** Renewables projects by companies in the same group change the value case of estates bought for conservation.

**P8.** Provisions of the 2025 Act would have changed the course of at least one of these sales.
