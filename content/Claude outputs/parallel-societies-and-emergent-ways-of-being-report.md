# Parallel Societies and Emergent Ways of Being: A Research Map for the Ward 2 Development Forum

The research that best supports the Forum's ethos comes from transitions studies on protected niches, from commons and community-economies research, and from food-sovereignty work, with evidence from moderate to strong; the frontier fields the essays draw on remain contested, and the ethos stands firmest when it treats them as Eisenstein's positions `[MS]`.

**TL;DR**

- **Shielding has a close mainstream counterpart, with thin outcome evidence.** "Protective space" and strategic niche management describe how young initiatives are shielded, nurtured and later exposed. This is the best-supported bridge between the essays and the valley's co-operative, Learning Campus and river work. The companion brief [Protective Space for Young Initiatives](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/research/protective-space-for-young-initiatives) (17 November 2026) finds the concepts well defined, and the evidence that protection secures lasting survival resting mostly on single cases.
- **Local tokens can be explored, with known risks.** Sarafu in Kenya is the best-documented African case of commitment pooling. In South Africa, crypto-asset intermediaries need an FSCA licence, and the Reserve Bank holds that crypto assets are neither money nor legal tender.
- **The frontier fields stay open questions.** Psi, low-energy nuclear reactions, UAP studies and speller communication each face a strong mainstream assessment and an active reply from within the field. The valley can use their method questions about how exposure changes what is studied, without staking anything on their claims.

## 1. Findings in brief

**The map.** Twelve categories hold the research the essays describe. Transitions research, commons governance, food sovereignty and community-currency evaluation have the deepest bases. Inner transformation, relational ontologies and narrative work have thinner outcome evidence. Frontier research is contested at its core. Main exception: African evidence on parallel structures sits mostly in history and anthropology, written for other purposes (inference).

**The pairings.** Agriculture and Business Development have the strongest African evidence: agroecology reviews, participatory guarantee systems and the Sarafu data. Education has strong Nordic and Moravian history and little African outcome data. Conservation has good South African evidence on alien clearing `[TBV]` and weak evidence on biodynamics and Korean Natural Farming. Tourism's most relevant local study, on Greyton's tourism-led gentrification `[IC]`, qualifies the pairing. Main exception: no study found tests commitment pooling in a South African rural ward.

**The counterparts.** Most organisational ideas have close counterparts: the parallel society in Benda's "parallel polis" and Wright's interstitial transformation `[TBV]`; shielding for young initiatives in Smith and Raven's "protective space" `[IC]`; the organ of discernment in evidence grading and participatory guarantee systems. The decline effect read as an effect of exposure has a counterpart only inside parapsychology, in Kennedy's work on "actively evasive" psi `[IC]`. Main exception: the courtship gift and prophecy as an invitation into roles have no research counterpart found.

**The registers.** The two registers hold 109 entries. The W2DF register is strongest on the mission, the colonial labour regime and land, and on water and restoration in the Cape mountains; it already holds the critical scholarship on mission labour. The BioConomy register holds the network's own briefs on TIME, pooling, water and money. Neither holds any work on transitions and protective space, food sovereignty or frontier research, and neither holds an evaluation of community currencies. The transitions gap has since been filled (Section 7, gap 1). Main exception: the W2DF register's history entries hold partial counterparts for an idea Section 5 lists as having none, prophecy as an invitation into roles (inference).

## 3. The categories in detail

### 3.1 Parallel structures and prefigurative change

**Field.** People build new institutions alongside existing ones that later change the wider system. Benda's 1978 essay proposed a "parallel polis" of independent education, culture and economy in communist Czechoslovakia `[TBV]`. Wright called change in the cracks of the existing order "interstitial transformation" `[TBV]`.

**Works.** Benda, "The Parallel Polis" (1978): an essay for parallel structures that serve needs without seeking to seize the state, close to the Forum's symbiosis decision `[TBV]`. Havel, *The Power of the Powerless* (1978): change starts in everyday conduct `[TBV]`. Wright, *Envisioning Real Utopias* (2010): names ruptural, interstitial and symbiotic routes `[TBV]`; the last two match the Forum's choices (inference). Gibson-Graham, *A Postcapitalist Politics* (2006): action research mapping the "diverse economy" of unpaid and co-operative work `[TBV]`, a ready method for the Political Economy Analysis (inference). Boggs (1977) defines prefiguration as living now the relations one seeks `[TBV]`. Ronfeldt's TIMN framework names tribes, institutions, markets and networks `[TBV]`; Michael's TIME framework adapts it as layers that all operate at once `[MS]`.

**Contested.** Supporters say interstitial building creates alternatives that can scale `[TBV]`. Marxist critics say such projects are absorbed or stay marginal without shifts in state power `[TBV]`.

**The Fuller saying.** Quote Investigator (18 August 2024) traced "build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete" to an interview with Fuller reported by Mike Vance in a Career Press book published after Fuller's death in 1983 `[IC]`. No instance in Fuller's own writings was found in this run `[TBV]`.

**Africa and gaps.** See 3.12. No study found compares African parallel institutions against these frameworks.

### 3.2 Commons and polycentric governance

**Field.** A commons is a shared resource governed by its users under their own rules. Ostrom's fieldwork on irrigation, fisheries, forests and grazing found design principles shared by long-lived commons `[MS]`.

**Works.** Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990), already in the BioConomy wiki `[MS]`. Bollier and Helfrich, *Free, Fair, and Alive* (2019), a pattern language of commoning `[TBV]`. Cox, Arnold and Villamayor Tomás (2010), a meta-analysis finding broad support for the design principles `[TBV]`.

**Contested.** Critics argue the principles understate power and outside pressures `[TBV]`.

**Africa and gaps.** The BioConomy wiki holds *Mountain Water Commons* `[MS]`. No study found applies the principles to TRANCRAA land or Farm 39.

### 3.3 Transitions research and protective space

**Field.** New practices grow inside "niches" and later change "regimes", the settled rules and technologies of a sector. The central claim for the valley is that young initiatives need protection from full selection pressure until they mature. The concepts are well defined `[IC]`; the evidence that protection secures lasting survival rests mostly on single cases of initiatives that lasted, and the largest comparisons find that material resources, context and the terms of exit matter as much as the shelter `[IC]`. This is the closest mainstream counterpart to shielding for young initiatives. The full treatment is in the companion brief [Protective Space for Young Initiatives](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/research/protective-space-for-young-initiatives).

**Works.**
- Kemp, Schot and Hoogma (1998), *Technology Analysis and Strategic Management* 10(2): founded strategic niche management, the deliberate creation of protected spaces `[IC]`.
- Geels (2002), *Research Policy* 31(8–9): a historical case of sail to steam that set out the multi-level perspective of niche, regime and landscape `[IC]`.
- Smith and Raven (2012), *Research Policy* 41(6), 1025–1036: identifies effective protection as having three properties, shielding, nurturing and empowerment, and calls empowerment "the least developed in current niche literature" `[IC]`; it also separates passive shielding (existing spaces) from active shielding (built on purpose), and names two routes to empowerment, fit-and-conform and stretch-and-transform `[IC]`. Eisenstein's closed room resembles strong shielding; full public scrutiny resembles empowerment (inference).
- Verhees, Raven, Veraart, Smith and Kern (2013), *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 19: Dutch solar PV survived four decades of hostile policy by building and moving between protected spaces `[IC]`.
- Loorbach (2010), *Governance* 23(1): transition management through arenas, experiments and reflexive evaluation, drawn from the Dutch energy transition programme `[IC]`.
- Sharpe, *Three Horizons* (2013): "pockets of the future" in the present `[IC]`, echoing the Tara brief's "islands of the future" `[MS]`.

**Contested.** Lawhon and Murphy (2012) argue that the multi-level perspective treats the regime as too singular and too detached from place and power; Geels (2014) replied by adding incumbent power and resistance to the framework `[IC]`. Wieczorek (2018) finds regimes in developing countries less uniform than in Europe, and the frameworks in need of adapting there `[IC]`. Smith and Raven warn that protection can be captured by those who gain from it, that governments find it hard to withdraw, and that protective space can harden into an institution that shelters poor innovation `[IC]`; this is the main risk in keeping shielding (inference). They don't use the word "dependency".

**Africa and gaps.** Stellenbosch's Centre for Complex Systems in Transition works on transitions in African settings, and hosts Wlokas's 2015 review of community trusts in the renewable energy procurement programme `[IC]`. Swilling and Annecke's *Just Transitions* (2012) covers Lynedoch EcoVillage and the Sustainability Institute in Chapter 10, "Pioneering Liveable Urbanism", written by their co-founders `[IC]`. No study found applies protective space to a rural co-operative in the Western Cape, or to communal or mission-station land.

### 3.4 Inner transformation and worldviews

**Field.** Values and inner capacities can drive or block change. Meadows put a society's paradigm, its shared mindset, near the top of her places to intervene `[TBV]`.

**Works.** Meadows (1999) `[TBV]`. Woiwode and others (2021), *Sustainability Science* 16 `[TBV]`. Ives, Freeth and Fischer (2020), *Ambio* `[TBV]`. Wamsler's work on mindfulness and sustainability, with mixed evidence of cause `[TBV]`. Andersen and Björkman (2017) on Bildung and the Nordic folk high schools, a historical argument not tested causally (inference). Kegan (1994) on adult development `[TBV]`. Gebser (1949 to 1953) on structures of consciousness `[TBV]`; Michael's five structures draw on this lineage and on Roger Briggs, who the BioConomy wiki reports agreed these shifts qualify as cognitive major evolutionary transitions `[MS]`.

**Contested.** Critics warn inner work can draw attention from power, and that stage models can be read as ranking people `[TBV]`. Michael's frame states the sequence ranks no one `[MS]`.

**Africa and gaps.** Little found; no outcome study of Bildung programmes in South Africa.

### 3.5 Relational ontologies and plural ways of knowing

**Field.** A relational ontology holds that beings exist through their relations. This category also holds methods that keep several ways of knowing side by side, as *The frame* asks.

**Works.** West and others (2020), *Ecosystems and People* 16(1) `[TBV]`. Escobar (2018) `[TBV]`. Ramose (1999) and Metz on Ubuntu as relational personhood `[TBV]`, the closest African counterpart to the Story of Interbeing (inference). Bartlett, Marshall and Marshall (2012) on Two-Eyed Seeing, Elder Albert Marshall's principle of using Indigenous and Western knowledge together `[TBV]`, the clearest model of holding both without choosing (inference). Thich Nhat Hanh's "interbeing" `[TBV]`.

**Contested.** Critics warn of romanticising or extracting Indigenous knowledge `[TBV]`.

**Africa and gaps.** South Africa's Protection, Promotion, Development and Management of Indigenous Knowledge Act 6 of 2019 bears on work with Khoekhoe heritage `[TBV]`. No study applies Two-Eyed Seeing to Moravian and Khoekhoe knowledge.

### 3.6 Myth, narrative and place

**Field.** Stories shape what places become. Regenesis Group's Story of Place traces a place's living patterns to guide design `[TBV]`.

**Works.** Mang and Haggard (2016), practitioner-based `[TBV]`. Ganz on public narrative `[TBV]`. Berry (1988) on the "New Story" `[TBV]`. Macy and Johnstone (2012) on the Great Turning `[TBV]`. Byrne (2000) read Le Guin's fiction beside South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission `[IC]`.

**The Le Guin passage.** This run did not locate "Truth goes in and out of stories" in Le Guin's published work `[TBV]`. The verified nearest line, from *The Left Hand of Darkness* (1969), is "Truth is a matter of the imagination" `[IC]`.

**Contested.** Place stories can serve property markets `[TBV]`; Greyton is the local case (4.5).

**Africa and gaps.** Genadendal's music archive has been digitised since 2020 with funding from the German Federal Foreign Office and Stellenbosch University `[MS]`. No outcome study of Story of Place in Africa was found.

### 3.7 Community economies, currencies and commitment pooling

**Field.** A complementary currency circulates beside national money. Commitment pooling, Ruddick's term, pools vouchers that represent promises of goods or services so members can swap commitments `[IC]`.

**Works.** Ruddick (2023), *International Journal of Community Currency Research* 27, 54–57 `[IC]`. "Holding Periods: Measuring the Inverse of Money Velocity from Transaction Records" (arXiv 2209.01512), on Sarafu `[IC]`. "Affordances of Digital and Blockchain-Based Community Currencies: The Case of Sarafu Network in Kenya" (arXiv 2512.04030) `[IC]`. The Eco-Pesa evaluation (IJCCR, 2012) `[MS]`. Stodder on WIR `[TBV]`.

**Contested.** Practitioners report local circulation gains `[MS]`. The holding-periods paper notes digital community currencies are seen as promising for humanitarian aid `[IC]`. A study listed by Grassroots Economics finds community currencies "only sometimes economically advantageous" `[MS]`.

**Africa and gaps.** Kenya has the deepest evidence (4.2). No independent evaluation of a South African commitment pool was found.

### 3.8 Food sovereignty and parallel food systems

**Field.** Food sovereignty is the right of peoples to define their food systems, a term La Via Campesina brought to the 1996 World Food Summit `[TBV]`. Agroecology applies ecological principles to farming and food `[TBV]`.

**Works.** HLPE (2019), thirteen agroecological principles `[TBV]`. Nyéléni Declaration (2007) `[TBV]`. IFOAM on participatory guarantee systems (PGS), where farmers and buyers certify each other by peer review `[TBV]`. Studies of Brazil's PNAE, where Law 11.947 of 16 June 2009, Article 14, requires at least 30 per cent of federal school-food funds to buy directly from family farming, and Law 14.660 of 2023 added priority for women's groups `[IC]`.

**Contested.** Critics cite lower yields in some systems and labour demands `[TBV]`.

**Africa and gaps.** The Meat Safety Act 40 of 2000 governs abattoirs `[TBV]`. The African Centre for Biodiversity criticises the 2018 Plant Improvement and Plant Breeders' Rights Acts for limiting farmer seed saving `[TBV]`. No study found on village-scale processing in the Overberg.

### 3.9 Technologies of reunion in land and water

**Works.** Kravčík and others (2007), *Water for the Recovery of the Climate*, on small water cycles `[TBV]`; local cooling claims have support and wider climate claims are contested `[TBV]`. Arvari johad studies in Rajasthan `[TBV]`. Palmiet wetland studies in the Cape `[TBV]`. Working for Water evaluations showing alien clearing raises water yield `[TBV]`. CapeNature's *Riviersonderend Complex Protected Area Management Plan 2021 to 2031*, in the W2DF register `[MS]`.

**Contested.** Hydrologists warn local retention can reduce downstream flows `[TBV]`.

**Africa and gaps.** The BioConomy wiki holds *Community-Led Landscape Restoration in Sub-Saharan Africa* `[MS]`. No monitoring found for leaky dams on Riviersonderend tributaries.

### 3.10 Evidence, measurement and what study does to its subject

**Field.** Goodhart's law states that a measure made into a target stops being a good measure `[TBV]`; Campbell's law says the same of social indicators `[TBV]`.

**Works.** Schooler (2011), *Nature* 470, calls for open archives of all results `[TBV]`. Open Science Collaboration (2015), *Science* 349, replicated 100 psychology studies and found a mean replication effect size of r = 0.197 against 0.403 in the originals, with 36 per cent of replications significant `[IC]`. Eyben, Guijt, Roche and Shutt (2015) on results frameworks in development `[IC]`. Participatory action research `[TBV]`.

**Contested.** Mainstream science explains declines by regression to the mean, publication bias and flexible analysis `[TBV]`. Eisenstein reads them as a sign that some phenomena are unsettled `[MS]`. The mainstream remedy is more exposure through pre-registration; Eisenstein's is staged exposure (inference).

### 3.12 South African precedents

**Mission stations.** Genadendal, founded as Baviaanskloof by Georg Schmidt in 1738 and renamed in 1806, is described as South Africa's oldest mission station `[IC]`; the country's first teachers' training college opened there in 1838 `[IC]`. The mission's account, relayed by heritage sources, is that it gave Khoekhoe people land, schooling and protection `[MS]`. South African History Online records that under colonial laws of the early 1800s Khoisan people without a fixed abode could be forced into farm labour, and mission stations, which gave land in return for conversion, became an alternative `[IC]`; farmers complained of losing labourers to Genadendal `[IC]`. A 1976 SALDRU paper by Wilson records farmers' complaints from 1793 and men supplied under pressure for commando service `[TBV]`.

**Stokvels.** A stokvel is a rotating savings club, known in research as a ROSCA `[IC]`. National figures should come from the National Stokvel Association of South Africa, not reached in this run `[TBV]`.

**Other cases.** The Black Community Programmes (1972) ran clinics and home industries `[TBV]`. The 1980s civics and street committees ran township affairs as apartheid local government lost legitimacy `[TBV]`. Orania's founders state an aim of Afrikaner self-determination `[TBV]`. Sakeliga and AfriForum have published statements on building institutions that work where the state fails `[TBV]`. Apartheid's separate development assigned black South Africans to ethnic homelands `[TBV]`.

**How "parallel society" will be heard.** Inference: in the valley, "parallel society" and "parallelle samelewing" are likely to call up apartheid's separate development and current debates about groups building their own structures. Families moved from Greyton to Heuwelkroon between 1968 and 1974 `[TBV]` may hear it as separation. The research offers terms with less charge: "community economy", "complementary" or "interstitial" institutions, "niche", "commons", and in Afrikaans "gemeenskapsekonomie" (inference). The Forum's "symbiosis" is closest to Wright's "symbiotic transformation" `[TBV]`.

## 4. The five pairings

### 4.1 Agriculture and Food Sovereignty

**Strongest works.** HLPE (2019); IFOAM PGS guidance; PNAE studies `[TBV]`; African Centre for Biodiversity and Biowatch South Africa `[TBV]`.

**Results.** PNAE studies report higher family-farm incomes `[TBV]`. PGS studies report lower costs than third-party certification and limited acceptance by large buyers `[TBV]`. Practitioner accounts report better soil and food security `[MS]`.

**Valley.** The Voorstekraal growers met on 5 November 2026 to plan the Agri-Heritage Co-operative and show their garden on 18 November `[MS]`. Early tasks suggested by the research: a PGS for the shared brand, a buyer who accepts it, and a shared-equipment plan (inference).

**Open.** Will the provincial school nutrition programme buy from a co-operative? Will Overberg retailers accept a PGS label?

### 4.2 Business Development and Local Procurement

**Strongest works.** Ruddick (2023) and the Sarafu studies `[IC]`; Preston's community wealth building through anchor buying `[TBV]`; REIPPPP economic development obligations `[TBV]`.

#### Commitment pooling and local currency tokens

**How it works.** A member issues a voucher for something they can supply, such as food, labour, transport or seedlings `[MS]`. The pool admits vouchers under rules, values them against each other, limits flows, and lets members swap or redeem them `[MS]`. In Sarafu, a voucher can carry a demurrage rate (a holding fee, for example 2 per cent) and an expiry date, with fees going to a community fund `[IC]`. A 2026 arXiv paper on "regenerative bonds" uses Ruddick's protocol to link formal debt to local commitments `[IC]`.

**Evidence from Sarafu.** The 2020–21 dataset, published by Mattsson, Criscione and Ruddick in *Scientific Data* 9:426 (2022), covers around 55,000 users from 25 January 2020 to 15 June 2021, and the holding-periods paper analyses about 400,000 transactions among about 40,000 accounts `[IC]`. Independent analysis found Sarafu small, heavily re-transacted and almost closed to national currency, with mostly local circulation and strong involvement of savings groups `[IC]`. Fieldwork found chamas of 10 to 30 members at the centre and use for reciprocal labour its designers had not planned `[IC]`. Grassroots Economics reports 33 commitment pools as of September 2024 `[MS]`. Ruddick reports that in 73 village pools, over 80 per cent of exchanges in a recent 90-day window were commitment-to-commitment swaps `[MS]`. A 2024 interview reports currencies in over 80 Kenyan communities and in Cameroon and South Africa, with about 60,000 households and about US$4 million in exchange `[MS]`. In 2013 Ruddick was arrested with five others on suspicion of forgery over a voucher currency `[TBV]`, a sign that regulators can read vouchers as counterfeit money (inference).

**Other currencies.** WIR in Switzerland has run since 1934 and is found counter-cyclical `[TBV]`. Chiemgauer has circulated with demurrage since 2003 `[TBV]`. ITV News reported on 30 September 2021 that the Bristol Pound's digital scheme closed in August 2020 and its paper notes left circulation on 30 September 2021, after the community interest company said in February 2021 it could not continue without new funding `[TBV]`. Sardex grew as a business credit circuit `[TBV]`. Banco Palmas tied a currency to a community bank `[TBV]`. Schemes tied to real trade and credit tend to last longer than schemes run mainly for local pride `[TBV]`.

**South African regulation.**
- December 2014: the SARB's position paper said virtual currencies had no legal status or regulatory framework `[IC]`.
- 11 June 2021: the Intergovernmental Fintech Working Group's position paper said crypto assets must enter the regulatory framework `[TBV]`.
- 19 October 2022: the FSCA's General Notice 1350 of 2022 (Government Gazette 47334) declared crypto assets a financial product under the FAIS Act `[IC]`. A crypto asset is defined as a digital representation of value not issued by a central bank that uses cryptography and distributed ledger technology `[IC]`. Anyone giving advice or intermediary services on crypto assets as a regular business must be licensed `[IC]`.
- Joint Communication, *Crypto assets for domestic payment purposes* (SARB with other regulators; file name suggests 28 May 2026 `[TBV]`): crypto assets are not money or funds under the National Payment System Act and are not legal tender `[IC]`. It names the risk of "parallel, unregulated and fragmented payment systems" `[IC]`. The SARB is unlikely to regulate unbacked crypto assets as payment instruments and wants to test rand-pegged stablecoins in the IFWG sandbox `[IC]`. Peer-to-peer exchange on decentralised protocols carries no current licensing duty `[IC]`.
- Exchange control applies to any cross-border value flow `[TBV]`.

Inference: a voucher redeemable only for goods and services from named local issuers, without rand convertibility, sits furthest from FAIS and the NPS Act. Any rand reserve or cash-out raises licensing questions, and legal advice should precede issue.

**Valley.** The MSME supplier open day in Caledon on 12 November 2026, with the municipality and Klipheuwel Wind Farm (Globeleq), is the anchor-buying route Preston describes `[MS]`. SEDFA funded the October 2026 course, which about twenty people completed `[MS]`. The Palmiet Principle's stretch pools are the Forum's version of commitment pooling `[MS]`.

**Open.** Will REIPPPP spending accept a co-operative supplier? What legal form keeps a stretch pool outside FAIS?

## 6. Frontier research

### 6.1 Psi research and the elusiveness of its effects

**Field.** Parapsychology studies claimed perception beyond the senses and mind influencing matter, with J.B. Rhine's Zener card trials at Duke from the 1930s `[TBV]`. Most evidence appears in the field's own journals.

**Works.**
- Cardeña (2018), *American Psychologist* 73(5), 663–677: an umbrella review of meta-analyses concluding the evidence is comparable to that for established phenomena, with no consensual theory `[IC]`.
- Storm, Tressoldi and Di Risio (2010), *Psychological Bulletin*: ganzfeld meta-analysis reporting above-chance hits `[TBV]`.
- Bem (2011), *JPSP* 100(3), 407–425: nine experiments with more than 1,000 participants, a mean effect size of d = 0.22, and significant results in all but one experiment `[IC]`; failed replications followed `[TBV]`.
- Wiseman and Schlitz (1997): the same protocol produced an effect for Schlitz and none for Wiseman `[TBV]`.
- Kennedy (2001), *Journal of Parapsychology* 65(3): reviews eleven hypotheses for elusiveness `[IC]`. Kennedy (2003), 67(1), 53–74: describes reversals, lost intended effects and declines across participants, experimenters and research lines, and proposes that psi occurs against supporting and opposing motivations in a polarised population `[IC]`.
- von Lucadou's Model of Pragmatic Information: psi as correlations that shift when used to signal `[TBV]`; a preliminary test on spontaneous cases is published `[IC]`.
- Walach, von Lucadou and Römer (2014), Generalized Quantum Theory `[TBV]`.

**Bodies.** Parapsychological Association `[TBV]`; Society for Psychical Research, publisher of the Psi Encyclopedia `[IC]`; Rhine Research Center, publisher of the *Journal of Parapsychology* `[IC]`; Koestler Parapsychology Unit `[TBV]`; Division of Perceptual Studies, University of Virginia `[TBV]`; Institute of Noetic Sciences `[TBV]`; Society for Scientific Exploration `[IC]`; Windbridge Research Center `[TBV]`.

**Mainstream assessment.** Reber and Alcock (*American Psychologist*, 2019 online; 75(3), 391–399) argued psi claims conflict with established physics and so cannot be true, and declined to engage the experiments `[IC]`.

**Field's reply.** Cardeña, "The Data Are Irrelevant" (*Journal of Scientific Exploration* 33(4), 2019) `[IC]`.

**Bearing on the valley.** The method question of how exposure shapes results bears on the PEA (inference).

### 6.6 Speller communication
