## What the sources show

In the 1991 drought, California's Department of Water Resources ran a drought water bank. The Legislative Analyst's Office reported that the bank had bought about 835,000 acre-feet, roughly 2.5% of the water delivered in a normal year, mainly from farmers in the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta, and that about 435,000 acre-feet had been allocated by 24 October 1991. The Metropolitan Water District of Southern California took about half of the allocated water. The department estimated that about 150,000 acres of farmland would be taken out of production. Eligible buyers were urban areas receiving less than 75% of normal supplies and farm areas facing permanent production losses [IC].

A University of California, Davis report on the [[glossary/t-z/water-bank|water bank]] gives the final figures: purchases of over 820,000 acre-feet from 348 individuals, firms and agencies, and sales of almost 390,000 acre-feet to 12 agencies, with the remainder going to Delta instream flows and to carryover storage for 1992 (Lund, Israel and Kanazawa 1992) [IC]. The report's section on the bank's concerns, its price figures and its tables of fallowing payments by county were not read. Neither source measured the effect of the transfers on the selling communities.
