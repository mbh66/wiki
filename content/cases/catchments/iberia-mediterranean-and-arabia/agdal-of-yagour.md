## What the sources show

Genin and Alifriqui describe agdal as collective areas whose use is regulated by a local institution (a village, several villages or tribes), often under religious protection, with closures timed to the growth cycle and rules on what may be cut, when and where. They report that over the long term agdal forests have kept or even increased their area despite heavy pressure on resources [IC]. Domínguez and colleagues (2012) document the ecological, economic and cultural values of the Aït Ikiss agdal of Yagour. The run's coding of this case as weakening is not supported by these sources and has been withdrawn.

**French-language check, 9 November 2026.** No French source found describes the Yagour agdal as eroding. Domínguez (2026), a CNRS eco-anthropologist whose earlier work covers the Yagour, describes the agdal as a living institution whose governance "continue d'évoluer avec son temps": representatives of each tribal fraction negotiate the opening and closing dates from close observation of the pasture, and tens of villages of the Mesioua hold use rights [IC]. The regional record shows strain. In the Aït Bou Guemez valley of the central High Atlas, Auclair and Aubert (2024) found forest cover rising on 21.4% of the forest area, in sectors that match "de manière quasi parfaite" the village agdals, while about 21.3% of forest outside them, mostly on village boundaries, was destroyed between 1960 and 1985; they also report a progressive weakening of community institutions since the forest service arrived in 1985 and, by 2023, reforestation expanding at the agdals' expense [IC]. Romagny and colleagues (2018) describe Morocco's commons, agdals among them, as in crisis, with communities losing control of water, pasture and forest to the state and private actors under productivist policy, and Mahdi (2010) notes that high pastures including the Yagour have drawn the interest of capital seeking building land. These are general or neighboring findings. The coding keeps the agdal as persistent and records the pressures as risks.
