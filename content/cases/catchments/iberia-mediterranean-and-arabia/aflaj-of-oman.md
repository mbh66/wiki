## What the sources show

A 1990s government inventory counted just over 4,000 aflaj, 3,000 in use, irrigating up to half of Oman's fields. In 2014 ministers reported that a quarter had stopped flowing and a third had declined sharply, mainly because farmers sink private boreholes that lower water tables below the tunnels. In one village, permanent rights to 30 minutes of flow a week sold for a little over US\$3,000, and the seventh day's water is auctioned at the mosque to pay for maintenance (Pearce 2015) [IC]. Five aflaj were inscribed as a World Heritage site in 2006.

Each falaj divides its flow by time. A *baddah* is about 12 hours of flow and holds 24 *athar* of about half an hour each, so a share is a turn at whatever the channel carries. Rights fall into four kinds: private rights; *bayt al-mal* (state) rights; *waqf* (endowment) rights; and a common right of one full day of flow, set aside so that renting it can pay for minor and major maintenance. A falaj committee headed by a *wakil* runs day-to-day affairs, sells the common right and settles disputes among shareholders, and an *areef* keeps the customary scheduling. Falaj Daris in Nizwa and Falaj al-Malki in Izki rent the common right at weekly ascending auctions; in 2018 auction prices at Daris ran from US\$46.56 at the summer peak to US\$8.45 in winter, and daytime turns sold at a premium over night turns. Falaj al-Khatmeen in Birkat al-Mouz allows no auction and rents its common right on annual contracts at agreed prices. Its about 862 *athar* are divided into 335 private, 398 *bayt al-mal*, 33 *waqf* and 96 common (Al-Marshoudi and Sulong 2024) [IC].

Al-Marshoudi (2018) links the difference to the flow itself. On the ministry's flow records, Falaj al-Khatmeen's flow varies less than that of Daris and al-Malki (a coefficient of variation of 0.96, against 1.02 and 1.55), and the author reads its fixed annual contracts as fitted to a steadier supply; when a government-appointed official proposed an auction market there, no one from the community came to the meeting [TBV: one paper by the same author, in a journal whose review standards are unclear]. A research run on 10 November 2026 also reported, from Nash, that one *athar* at Falaj Al-Hamra rented for 60 to 120 rials and sold outright for about 6,000 rials in the mid-1990s; that paper could not be opened [TBV]. The run's split of 865 *athar* at Falaj al-Khatmeen does not match either paper read and has been dropped.
