## What the sources show

Lorenzi and Borrini-Feyerabend (2009) report that the Regole hold more than 16,000 hectares on behalf of about 1,000 families. The land cannot be sold, divided or acquired by long occupation, and must stay in forest and pasture use. In 1990 the Veneto Region entrusted the Natural Park of the Ampezzo Dolomites to the Regole, which run it with their own staff on a budget of about €2 million a year. Membership passes from father to sons among descendants of the valley's old families; newcomers have none, and an effort to reduce the gap between male and female members had not succeeded [MS]. Lorenzi writes from within the Regole.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run found regional press reports, relaying the Corriere del Veneto, that the Regole assembly voted in 2022 to return to the State Demanio the management of peaks, crags, trails and streams leased from the state when the park was created, citing liability for climate-driven rockfall after the Marmolada collapse of 3 July 2022. The Regole keep their 16,000 hectares of forest and pasture. The Laudo of 2018 records 15,305 hectares held by the Comunanza and 545 hectares held by individual Regole [MS]. The handback rests on one press origin, so the grade stays [MS].
