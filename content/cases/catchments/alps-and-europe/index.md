Alpine commons such as Törbel and the Regole d'Ampezzo have held forests, pastures and water under their own statutes for centuries, with membership rules that also exclude newcomers.

## The cases

**[[torbel-commons|Törbel]] (Switzerland).** Village statutes from 1483 limit cows on the common alp to the number each household can winter, and the commune governs forest and irrigation (Netting 1981; [[sources/ostrom-governing-commons|Ostrom 1990]]) [IC]. No water measurement.

**[[bisses-of-the-valais|The Valais bisses]]**. Reynard (2002) reports a network that shrank from more than 1,400 km to about 600 km, with about 52.5% of working channels run by farmer corporations and 42.5% by municipalities, and weaker corporations handing their channels to public bodies [IC].

**[[regole-dampezzo|The Regole d'Ampezzo]]**. The Regole hold more than 16,000 hectares as inalienable collective property for about 1,000 families and have run the regional natural park since 1990; membership passes through male descendants of old families, and newcomers have none, by the Regole's own account [MS]. After the Marmolada collapse of 2022, the Regole returned to the state the management of the peaks, crags, trails and streams they had leased, and kept their forest and pasture (regional press) [MS].

**[[agrargemeinschaften-of-tyrol|Tyrol's Agrargemeinschaften]]**. Austria's Constitutional Court ruled in 2008 that the value of former municipal land beyond the members' use rights belongs to the municipality, which set agrarian commons against municipalities [IC]. A 2010 law amendment and rulings of 2010 and 2011 confirmed the municipalities' claim, and a 2014 amendment placed a municipal administrator inside these communities, which they challenged in court [IC].

**[[isle-of-eigg-buyout-and-hydro|Eigg]]** and **[[slovakias-landscape-revitalization-program|Slovakia's 2010 to 2012 program]]**. Eigg's community trust, which bought the island in 1997, runs a grid that draws 95% of energy use from hydro, wind and solar, and the population has risen from 65 to 109; a peer-reviewed study and the European Commission confirm the system's design and funding, including the residents' own cap of 5 kW per household [IC]. Slovakia's program ended with its funding, its auditor found duplicate invoices, and one of its dams breached fatally in 2021 [IC].
