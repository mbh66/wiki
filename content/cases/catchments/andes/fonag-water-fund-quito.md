## What the sources show

Porras and Neves (2006, with later updates) describe FONAG as launched in January 2000 by The Nature Conservancy and the municipality, with Quito's utility paying 1% of monthly water sales and hydropower producers and a brewery also contributing. The endowment passed US\$7 million by the end of 2009. Upstream farmers receive support for watershed programs and no direct cash, and the profile lists representatives of local communities on the board. The wiki's [[concepts/exclusion-from-water-finance|water finance review]] found communities kept off water fund boards. Until the board's composition is checked against FONAG's own records, the governance entries stay [TBV].

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run read FONAG's own trust records. Its 2013 annual report describes the Junta del Fideicomiso as made up of representatives of the constituent entities, which are EPMAPS, Empresa Eléctrica Quito, The Nature Conservancy, Tesalia CBC, Cervecería Nacional and Consorcio CAMAREN. Chafla and Cerón (2016) state that each contributor sits on the steering committee "y recibe un porcentaje de voto proporcional a su contribución" [IC]. No community, comuna or junta de agua is a constituent. Páramo communities appear in conservation agreements, such as the one with the Asociación Virgen del Carmen in Pintag (about 4,674 ha held by 50 families, 604 ha in strict conservation, with decisions resting with 20 male members), and as paid guardapáramos. The IIED profile's mention of community representatives on the board is outweighed by FONAG's records; its mention may reflect CAMAREN, a consortium of training institutions, though this is untested. This supports the finding in [[concepts/exclusion-from-water-finance|Exclusion from Water Finance]]. No measured water result was found.
