## What the sources show

IIED, a long-standing partner of the Asociación ANDES, describes the Potato Park as about 120 square kilometers governed collectively by Quechua communities under customary law as an Indigenous biocultural heritage territory, home to about 6,000 people. It reports around 1,350 potato varieties, 450 repatriated from the International Potato Center in 2004, and community enterprises mostly led by women [MS]. The page gives the number of governing communities as both six and five. The park's water work is mentioned only in passing and has not been measured.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run found an official record. Peru's Ministry of Agriculture recognized the Zona de Agrobiodiversidad Parque de la Papa by Resolución Ministerial 0081-2020-MINAGRI, published 5 March 2020. It covers 7,238.33 hectares across four comunidades campesinas: Chahuaytire (3,301.22 ha), Amaru (1,200.78 ha), Paru Paru (1,419.83 ha) and Pampallacta (1,316.50 ha), and directs INIA to prepare a master plan with them [IC]. El Peruano reported more than 1,330 varieties kept in fields and a communal gene bank. Hernández Asensio (2013) discusses the park's governance and the difficulty of scaling it up. The official area is smaller than the 12,000 hectares in IIED's account. Livelihood outcomes remain the partners' account [MS].
