## What the sources show

Chile's Water Code of 1981 privatized water rights, separated them from landownership and restricted government regulation of water use, on the expectation that free markets in tradeable rights would raise national economic efficiency. Rights are property rights protected by the Constitution. Bauer (2015) reports the benefits as legal security for private investment and reallocation of water to higher-value uses in some areas, and the costs as poor coordination among uses and weak conflict resolution, with concerns about speculation, hoarding and monopoly power [IC].

In the Copiapó valley the state water directorate (DGA) granted paper rights to several times more groundwater than was recharged each year, and the market price of water more than tripled between 2000 and 2011 (Bauer 2015, citing Bitran et al. 2014). Mining firms bought irrigation rights and paid more than farmers could, and where dams are present electricity law takes precedence over water law [IC]. The 2005 reform (Law No. 20,017) introduced fees for non-use, aimed mainly at non-consumptive hydropower rights, and strengthened the DGA's authority over new grants, groundwater and ecological flows; it did not apply to existing rights, which are the vast majority [IC]. Ley 21.435, published on 6 April 2022, rewrote the Code's core articles. Water in any state is declared a national good for public use, and access to drinking water and sanitation an essential human right that the state must guarantee. Use for human consumption, domestic subsistence and sanitation always prevails, both when rights are granted and when their exercise is limited. New rights granted by concession run for 30 years and renew automatically unless the DGA shows by reasoned resolution that the water is not used or that the source's sustainability is harmed; rights lapse for non-use after five years for consumptive rights and ten for non-consumptive ones. No rights may be granted on glaciers, the state may reserve water for subsistence and ecosystem functions, and in Indigenous territories the state is to protect the integrity of land and water for Indigenous communities. Rights recognized or granted before the law remain in force and can be extinguished only on the Code's non-use grounds, though rights not entered in the water property registry by 6 April 2027 lapse (Ley 21.435, transitory articles 1 and 2) [IC]. The text read is the consolidated version current to 18 February 2025. No study of the reform's effects was read for this page.
