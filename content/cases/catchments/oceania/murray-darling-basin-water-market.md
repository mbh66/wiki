## What the sources show

On 8 August 2019 the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission was directed to inquire into markets for tradeable water rights in the Murray-Darling Basin, and it released its final report on 26 March 2021. Basin water trading has an average annual value of more than A\$1.8 billion (2012-13 to 2019-20, in 2019-20 dollars) [IC].

The Commission found a lack of quality, timely and accessible information for market participants; trade processes and registries that differ between Basin states; manipulation and insider trading not adequately regulated; few rules on participant conduct and no specific agency overseeing trade; and trade rules that do not reflect storage and delivery capacity, causing channel congestion with third-party and environmental impacts. Only professional traders and large agribusinesses had the time and knowledge to use the markets well, and many participants doubted that the markets and key institutions are fair. It found no evidence that investors used market power to raise prices in the Southern Connected Basin. It recommended an independent Basin-wide Water Markets Agency and Basin-wide conduct and integrity laws, including bans on manipulation and insider trading [IC]. The full report's figures on who holds water, and later government responses, were not read for this page.
