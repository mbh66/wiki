## What the sources show

The Development Trusts Association Scotland case study reports that the Isle of Eigg Heritage Trust bought the island in 1997 for £1.5 million, with residents holding half the votes and the Scottish Wildlife Trust among the other partners. Hydro, wind and solar (184 kW in all) supply 95% of the island's energy use, and the population has risen from 65 to 109. It also notes that doubled tourist numbers have brought water shortages [MS]. The hydro uses the island's streams; no water measurement was found.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run found independent sources. Chmiel and Bhattacharyya (2015) modeled the Eigg off-grid system and report a renewable fraction of 0.89 in one configuration. The European Commission records a £1.6 million network funded by the trust (£140,000), residents' connection fees (£51,000), the Big Lottery Fund, Highlands and Islands Enterprise, ERDF (£764,000), the Highland Council and the Energy Saving Trust, and notes that residents agreed to cap use at 5 kW per household and 10 kW per business [IC]. The cap is a rule the users set for themselves.
