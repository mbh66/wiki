## What the sources show

Reynard (2002) reports that the Valais channel network was more than 1,400 km long at the end of the nineteenth century and is about 600 km today. Of the channels still in use, about 52.5% are run by farmer corporations (consortages), 42.5% by municipalities and 5% privately. More than half were abandoned in the twentieth century, open channels were replaced by pipes and gravity irrigation by sprinklers, and many weaker consortages are handing their channels to public administrations. Of 190 channels inventoried, 37% now serve farming and tourism together [IC].
