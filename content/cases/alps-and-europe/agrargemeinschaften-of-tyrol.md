## What the sources show

In its ruling of 11 June 2008 in the Mieders case (B 464/07), Austria's Constitutional Court held that where municipal common land (Gemeindegut) had been transferred to an agrarian community, the value of the land beyond the members' use rights for farming and forestry still belongs to the municipality, and that refusing to account for it was an arbitrary application of the law [IC]. The ruling set agrarian commons against municipalities over who holds the value of mountain land.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run traced the dispute after 2008. A 2010 amendment to Tyrol's agrarian law gave municipalities the substance value of Gemeindegut agrarian communities, a debate the Land government said had "einen tiefen Riss durch unser Land gezogen". The Constitutional Court found no constitutional objection to the 2010 rules and ruled in December 2010 (B 639/10) that atypical Gemeindegut requires former municipal ownership; the Administrative Court ruled in June 2011 (2010/07/0091) that the substance value belongs to the municipality as its share. A 2014 amendment created a municipal administrator inside these communities, which they challenged before the Constitutional Court (E 1006/2017) [IC]. The outcome of that challenge was not read.
