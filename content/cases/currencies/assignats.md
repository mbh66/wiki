## What the sources show

In December 1789 the National Assembly issued the assignat as a bond bearing 5% interest, secured on church lands that had recently been nationalized. By September 1790 it had been made paper currency, and circulation rose from 400 million livres to 1.2 billion (Britannica) [IC]. A December 1795 decree capped issue at 40 billion livres, with 36.4 billion in circulation by one count (Wikipedia, Mandats territoriaux) [IC]. The Directory returned to metal money on 4 February 1797 (Britannica) [IC]. The mechanism of failure was issue far beyond the value of the land that secured the notes. The run's figure of about 45 billion livres by early 1796 and its quoted line on value beyond the confiscated properties were not found in the sources read.
