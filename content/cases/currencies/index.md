The register holds one page per currency, voucher, receipt or token whose value was tied to a natural resource, with community currencies that have no resource backing kept beside them for comparison.

## Overview

Each case page records what stood behind the unit, what the issuer said stood behind it, who issued it and set its rules, whether the resource was counted, measured or modeled, what it cost to hold, whether the claim could leave the place, what happened to circulation and to the resource, and what ended it. The first 29 cases come from the resource-backed currencies research run of 10 November 2026, which has no brief on the wiki. Their entries were checked against their sources on 10 November 2026: 19 cases are graded [IC], 4 [MS] and 6 [TBV], and their analytical codes were reviewed the same day under the rules below.

Across the 39 cases the run coded, no currency was redeemable for water or tied by rule to a measured flow. The units that tied value to a living resource by measurement were water rights and pumping fees, and none of them circulated as money. ## How a case is coded

**Resource group.** `grain-and-commodities`, `land`, `carbon-and-nature-tokens`, `ecological-work` (units issued for work on waste, trees, farms or public works, whether or not a natural resource stands behind them), and `no-resource-backing` (comparison currencies).

**Backing,** dominant code first: K1 redeemable for a stored commodity; K2 redeemable for a resource right or allocation; K3 issued for verified work on the resource; K4 anchored to a measured change in the resource; K5 anchored to a modeled or estimated change; K6 a reserve of national money or another currency; K7 mutual credit or member commitments to accept; K8 themed only, with the resource in the name, story or spending rules. No case in the register is coded K4.

**Backed, anchored or themed.** A unit is *backed* if a holder can redeem it for the resource or a defined claim on it, *anchored* if a rule ties its quantity or value to a measured state of the resource without redemption, and *themed* if the resource appears only in its name, story or spending rules. Comparison currencies are coded `none`.

**Measurement** of the resource behind the unit: counted, instrument, model, declared or none.

**Coordination** uses the [[frameworks/time-framework|TIME Framework]]: [[glossary/t-z/t-form-tribal|T-form (Tribal)]], [[glossary/f-l/i-form-institutional|I-form (Institutional)]], [[glossary/m-s/m-form-market|M-form (Market)]] and [[glossary/a-e/e-form-emergent|E-form (Emergent)]], dominant form first.

**Holding cost:** none, [[glossary/a-e/demurrage|demurrage]] with its rate, expiry, or inflation by over-issue.

**Design principles** follow the refined principles in Table 4 of [[sources/cox-2010-design-principles-review|Cox, Arnold and Villamayor-Tomás (2010)]]:

| Code | Principle, in the paper's words |
|---|---|
| 1A | User boundaries: clear boundaries between legitimate users and nonusers |
| 1B | Resource boundaries: clear boundaries that define a resource system and separate it from the larger biophysical environment |
| 2A | Congruence with local conditions: appropriation and provision rules are congruent with local social and environmental conditions |
| 2B | Appropriation and provision: benefits obtained by users are proportional to the inputs required of them in labor, material or money |
| 3 | Collective-choice arrangements: most individuals affected by the operational rules can participate in modifying them |
| 4A | Monitoring users: monitors who are accountable to the users monitor users' appropriation and provision |
| 4B | Monitoring the resource: monitors who are accountable to the users monitor the condition of the resource |
| 5 | Graduated sanctions, applied by other users, by officials accountable to them, or by both |
| 6 | Conflict-resolution mechanisms: rapid access to low-cost local arenas |
| 7 | Minimal recognition of rights to organize: users' rights to devise their own institutions are not challenged by external governmental authorities |
| 8 | Nested enterprises: governance activities organized in multiple layers |

Each principle is scored P (present), A (absent), U (unknown) or n/a, and the `design_principles` key lists them in that order. The currency and the resource are scored as one system. The coding rules and the basis for every score are in the coding notes below.

**Evidence levels,** graded separately for the economy (circulation, trade, income) and for the resource. E1 randomized or controlled design; E2 long-term monitored record with a baseline and credible counterfactual; E3 before-after comparison with no control; E4 model or structured case study by independent researchers; E5 issuer or practitioner account, testimony or media report.

**Claim grade.** [IC] two or more independent sources or an official record; [MS] the account of the party the claim is about; [TBV] plausible and unconfirmed. The page's `epistemic_status` follows the grade: [IC] gives `academic-inference`, [MS] gives `working-hypothesis`, and [TBV] gives `stub`.

## Coding rules

These rules were applied to every case on 10 November 2026. They replace the research run's own codes.

**Basis.** A code other than U rests on a sentence in the case page's account of what the sources show. If the page does not say it, the code is U. Coding never changes a case's claim grade. A case that changed over time is scored on its later or peak state, and the note says so.

**Backing.** The dominant code is what a holder can get for the unit, or what makes others accept it: redemption for a commodity (K1), a resource right (K2) or national money (K6), and otherwise members' commitments to accept (K7). Secondary codes give the issuance trigger (K3 work, K4 measured change, K5 modeled change), and K8 marks a resource that appears only in the name or story.

**Backed, anchored or themed** asks only about a natural resource: *backed* if the unit is redeemable for the resource or a claim on it, *anchored* if a rule ties issuance to a measured or modeled quantity of the resource, *themed* if the resource appears only in the name, story or issuance rules, and *none* if no natural resource is involved. **Measurement** records how the resource behind the unit was known; hours of work measure the work, not the resource.

**Design principles.** P needs the page to show the principle; A needs it to show the opposite; U otherwise. 1B, 2B and 4B are n/a where there is no natural resource, and 7 is n/a where the state itself designed and issued the scheme, since there are no users' institutions for it to challenge. Elected representatives count as collective choice for 3. 4A and 4B are read strictly: monitoring by a state inspector, an exchange or an issuing NGO is scored A, because those monitors are not accountable to the users, and the note records that monitoring of another kind existed. Demurrage and interest are holding or borrowing costs, not sanctions under 5.

**Institutional fit** follows [[sources/cox-2012-diagnosing-institutional-fit|Cox (2012)]], who argues that there is no single overall fit: each theory of fit links features of a system to a particular outcome. The outcome judged here is keeping a unit's value tied to the resource behind it. Each score (good, partial, mismatch, U or n/a) names the theory applied: *extent fit*, whether the unit covers the same area or stock as the resource; *spatiotemporal fit*, whether a resource that varies across space and time is matched by flexible shares or access; *nesting fit*, whether layers of governance mirror nested resource units; and *quantity fit*, the wiki's own extension of extent fit, whether the number of units moves with the quantity of the resource.

**Coordination** uses the TIME code of whoever sets and enforces the rules: T kinship or custom, I a state, municipality, church or NGO hierarchy, M a company, E a network of members bound by peer guarantees or multi-party commitments.

**Evidence levels** grade the research design of the strongest source for each outcome; who wrote it is recorded under `independent` (`yes` if the economy outcomes rest on at least one source not written by the issuer, `partly` if the headline figures are the issuer's, `no` if all are).

## Nearest arrangements

Some arrangements tie value to ecological work without a circulating unit. They are recorded here and kept out of the register's base.

**Câmbio Verde, Curitiba (Brazil, 1991 to running).** Curitiba's "Compra do lixo" program, begun in January 1989, paid communities in transport tokens equal to the cost of conventional waste collection, and the Câmbio Verde (Green Exchange) built on it from December 1991 by exchanging recyclables for fruit and vegetables (Oliveira 2001; Takeda et al. 2013) [IC]. An analysis of city cleaning-department data records 31,258.82 tonnes of recyclables collected from December 1991 to the end of 2001, roughly 18,000 beneficiaries a month at about 63 points, at 5 kg of recyclables per 1 kg of produce, diverting about 0.97% of household waste in 2001 (Takeda et al. 2013) [IC]. The city reports 2,850 tonnes of recyclables collected and 890 tonnes of produce handed out to 61,800 people in 2023, at 103 points and 4 kg per kg [MS]. Oliveira (2001) traces the program's origins to a cholera epidemic that cut vegetable consumption and a cauliflower glut that led small producers to destroy crops, and argues that the city's environmental account followed the practice: "não seria o discurso que orientava a prática, mas o contrário" (it was not the discourse that guided the practice, but the reverse) [IC].

Residents hand in recyclables and receive food in the same transaction, so no unit circulates; the arrangement is an in-kind exchange run by the municipality. Sources: Takeda, A. K., Takeda, I. J. M., Botari, A., Botari, J. C. and Macinelli, N. B. (2013). Análise da gestão dos resíduos sólidos urbanos da cidade de Curitiba. XIII Safety, Health and Environment World Congress (SHEWC 2013): 413-417. Conference paper. https://copec.eu/congresses/shewc2013/proc/works/94.pdf; Oliveira, M. de (2001). A trajetória do discurso ambiental em Curitiba (1960-2000). *Revista de Sociologia e Política* 16: 97-106. Peer-reviewed. https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-44782001000100007; Prefeitura de Curitiba (25 February 2024). Câmbio Verde troca recicláveis por 890 toneladas de hortifrúti em Curitiba em 2023. Issuer publication. https://www.curitiba.pr.gov.br/noticias/cambio-verde-troca-reciclaveis-por-890-toneladas-de-hortifruti-em-curitiba-em-2023-saiba-como-funciona/72350

## Adding and enriching cases

A new case gets its own page at `cases/currencies/<case-title>.md` with the `currency-case` tag first and its resource group second, so that it stays out of the catchment register's views. An enrichment run that re-reads a case updates the entries, sets `last_checked`, and raises the grade and `epistemic_status` only where a source supports it. Keep `case_id` and the file name unchanged, so links and filters keep working. A case that governs a catchment belongs in the catchment register and is linked from this page.

## Provenance

Created on 10 November 2026 from the 39-row case table of the resource-backed currencies research run. Thirty rows became pages here; the nine water rows were matched to existing or new pages in the catchment register. Entries were copied from the table and then checked the same day against the sources each page lists; corrections are recorded on the case pages. The `continent`, `resource_group`, `backing_dominant` and `failure_or_capture` keys were added for filtering, and `capture_or_harm` holds the table's failure column. The analytical codes were reviewed the same day under the coding rules above, against the wording of Cox, Arnold and Villamayor-Tomás (2010), Table 4; the basis for each score is in the coding notes. Câmbio Verde, first a case page, was moved to the nearest arrangements section because no unit circulates. Wörgl was moved from ecological work to the comparison group, because its certificates paid for public works with no natural resource behind them. The fit rule was revised the same day after reading Cox (2012), so that each fit score names the theory of fit it applies.
