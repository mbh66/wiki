## What the sources show

A popular claim holds that grain deposited in Ptolemaic Egypt's state granaries was transferred between accounts as a giro, carried storage charges like [[glossary/a-e/demurrage|demurrage]], and circulated as money. Neither source read for this page supports it. Monson's review of von Reden's *Money in Ptolemaic Egypt* (2007), the standard monetary history of the period, describes royal banks that held accounts in coin and payments in kind, and does not mention grain transfers between accounts or grain receipts circulating as money. A general history describes government grain stockpiles that might be sold or distributed, and treats coinage as the monetary system [TBV]. The claim stays in the register so that it can be tested against von Reden's book and the papyrological literature.
