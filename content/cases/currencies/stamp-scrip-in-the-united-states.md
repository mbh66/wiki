## What the sources show

Stamp scrip first appeared in the United States in Hawarden, Iowa, in 1932, and most was issued in early 1933, when bank suspensions were frequent. The historian Joel Harper found 95 issues from 37 cities, 8 counties and many business groups. On dated scrip, holders bought a two-cent stamp each week and fixed it to the note; on undated scrip, a stamp was added each time the note was spent; issuers redeemed the scrip once the boxes were full. Most issues were US\$300 to US\$500, none above US\$2,000 succeeded, and the largest municipal issue, US\$120,688 in Polk County, Iowa, failed. Workers saw undated scrip as a tax on spending, businesses were reluctant to accept it, and Harper concluded that "the effort required to maintain the use of local scrip seems to have been greater than the results"; it faded once other money was available again (Champ 2008, Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland) [TBV: one secondary source]. The run's statement that the 1933 federal banking measures ended it is not supported by that source.
