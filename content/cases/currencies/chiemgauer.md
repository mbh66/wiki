## What the sources show

The Chiemgauer began as a school project in 2002, and its issuer, the nonprofit association Chiemgauer e.V., was founded in 2003. One Chiemgauer equals one euro, held in reserve. A business converting back to euros pays 5% plus VAT, about 6% in all, so 100 Chiemgauer return €94.05; of the 5%, 3% goes to the nonprofit the customer chose and 2% covers operating costs. [[glossary/a-e/demurrage|Demurrage]] was 8%, paid with quarterly stickers, from 2003 to 2015; it is now 6% a year, charged daily on electronic Chiemgauer from the 90th day and as 3% per half-year on paper. The founder and his co-author report that the Chiemgauer "changed hands four times per year in 2019" and "circulates more than three times faster than the euro," and that nonprofits had received about €740,000 by 2020 (Gelleri and Stodder 2021) [MS]. Godschalk (2011) estimated a velocity of 10.6 in 2009 from surveys at acceptance points, found that the level of the demurrage rate "seems to be not crucial for the economic results," and cautioned that "empirical data does not yet allow statements on the effects of demurrage on the velocity" [IC].
