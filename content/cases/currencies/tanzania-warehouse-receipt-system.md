## What the sources show

Tanzania's first warehouse receipt pilot was a coffee system under the Tanzania Coffee Board from 1998. In 2002 the government and IFAD piloted an inventory credit scheme for cereals in 11 districts, in which farmers used receipts as collateral for loans of up to 70% of the stock's value through Savings and Credit Cooperative Societies; IFAD reports that it raised farm-gate prices by up to 300% [MS]. The Warehouse Receipts Act of 2005 formalized the system to replicate it nationally. It applies to all agricultural commodities, and it set up the Tanzania Warehouse Licensing Board, a public-private body with representatives of the ministry, cooperatives, private farmers, banks, insurers and warehouse operators. Crop laws followed, including the Cashewnut Industry Act and the Cereals and Other Produce Act of 2009 (Wehling and Garthwaite 2015) [IC]. A comparative table gives no volume figure (Thunde and Baulch 2020). The run's finding that receipts are thinly traded was not found in either source.
