## What the sources show

*Fureai kippu* (tickets for a caring relationship) are time credits volunteers earn for helping older people, which they can bank for themselves or pass to a relative elsewhere. The lineage begins with the Volunteer Labour Bank founded in Osaka in 1973 by Teruko Mizushima; Tsutomu Hotta, founder of the Sawayaka Welfare Foundation, coined the generic name and the foundation coordinated expansion across Japan. Most schemes also use money: members without credits pay small fees, and some volunteers choose cash. Hayashi (2012) cites 391 branches in 2012, finds redemption low (one national network redeemed about 0.6% of banked credits in 2010), reports that growth stalled after the 2000 Long-Term Care Insurance Act offered cheaper statutory services, and calls the system "not a universal panacea" [IC]. The University of Sheffield's MoPAct project counts about 400 branches and 70,000 active participants [IC].
