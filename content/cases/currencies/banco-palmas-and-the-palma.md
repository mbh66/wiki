## What the sources show

Joaquim Melo and the Conjunto Palmeiras Residents' Association in Fortaleza founded Banco Palmas in 1998, and its legal status was recognized in early 2000 (FAPESP) [IC]. Its currency, the *palma*, was in circulation by 2003. *Rest of World* reports that Brazil's Central Bank sued Melo and the association in 2003 for running a currency parallel to the real, that Melo won, and that in 2009 the Central Bank's legal counsel published a paper arguing that social currencies can be public policy tools compatible with monetary policy; FAPESP places the suit before 2000. The organization rebranded as Instituto E-dinheiro in 2014 [IC, media]. Social currencies must keep parity with the real and banks must hold an equivalent reserve; FAPESP reports 167 community banks inspired by Palmas, and *Rest of World* more than 40 running their own currencies on its platform [IC].
