## What the sources show

Eco-Pesa began circulating in August 2010 in three Kongowea villages in Mombasa, about 20,000 people, after design work from May. Residents who returned a filled trash bag received a single 5 Eco-Pesa note, Mombasa Municipal Council supplied trucks, and the implementing NGO Eco-Ethics kept Kenyan shillings equal to all Eco-Pesa in circulation in reserve. In the first month businesses got a 20% discount buying Eco-Pesa and paid a 20% fee returning it. Over three months, 26,400 Eco-Pesa (US\$352) entered circulation, 20,325 of them (77%) through service events; 9,280 remained in circulation on 10 November 2010; a note had been used 12 times on average; 75 registered and over 25 other businesses accepted it; two collection events removed about 4 and 16 tonnes of waste; and 32 of 41 businesses interviewed (the paper says 80%) reported changes, averaging a 22% rise in monthly income [MS]. The evaluation was written by the program's designer. The 2015 Bangla-Pesa paper reports that the program was donor-dependent and that "once all the issued Eco-Pesa was redeemed for the donor funds, the program ended" [MS].
