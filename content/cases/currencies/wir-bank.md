## What the sources show

The WIR cooperative (Wirtschaftsring, "Economic Circle"), now the WIR Bank, was founded in Switzerland in 1934, inspired by Silvio Gesell, and has issued its own currency, not backed by Swiss francs, ever since. It stopped charging [[glossary/a-e/demurrage|demurrage]] in 1948 and charges interest on large overdrafts and commercial loans. Prices are quoted in WIR credits denominated in, but not redeemable for, Swiss francs. A 1972 by-law restricts membership to small and medium enterprises, and registered members must accept WIR for at least 30% of the first CHF 2,000 of a bill (Stodder 2009; Stodder and Lietaer 2016) [IC].

Participants grew from 814 in 1948 to a peak of 82,793 in 1997 and stood at 77,668 in 2003; turnover peaked at CHF 2,521 million in 1993 and was CHF 1,650 million in 2003, when WIR credit balances of CHF 784.4 million were about 0.165% of Swiss M2 (Stodder 2009) [IC]. In 2005 the bank had 74,732 members, of whom 60,703 were enterprises, about 16% of all Swiss enterprises; they turned over CHF 1,614 million on balances of CHF 624 million, a velocity of about 2.6, with registered members spending faster (3.6) than non-registered ones (2.0) (Stodder and Lietaer 2016) [IC].

Stodder (2009) finds that WIR turnover is "negatively correlated with GDP in the short run," unlike ordinary money: firms short of cash in a recession make more use of WIR credit. With data to 2013, Stodder and Lietaer (2016) confirm the countercyclical pattern, find that it works through larger balances for larger firms and faster velocity for smaller ones, and argue that the countercyclical multiplier on WIR spending is highly leveraged, so that its effect on the Swiss economy is greater than its turnover suggests [IC]. The research run's figures of about 68,000 members, CHF 1.6 billion turnover in 2009, and balances of CHF 612 million in 2007 equal to a quarter of one percent of the money supply do not appear in either paper. Godschalk (2011) calls WIR "the oldest and most successful CC in the world" [IC].
