## What the sources show

The Wära was launched by Hans Timm and Helmut Rödiger, followers of Silvio Gesell, after a trial in the Physiocratic League from about 1926; the Wära circulation agency was founded in Erfurt in October 1929. One Wära equaled one Reichsmark, Wära could be exchanged for Reichsmarks at exchange stations, and holders paid 1% of face value a month by gluing [[glossary/a-e/demurrage|demurrage]] stamps to the back of the note. In 1931 the engineer Max Hebecker reopened the bankrupt Schwanenkirchen mine with a 50,000 Reichsmark credit paid mostly in Wära, employing 45 and later 60 workers and paying two-thirds of wages in Wära. The Reich finance ministry forbade the experiment on 30 October 1931 (Wikipedia, citing Onken, Broer and Fisher) [IC]. The Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland also records that the Finance Ministry declared the Wära illegal [IC].
