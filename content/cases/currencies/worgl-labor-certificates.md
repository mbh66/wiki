## What the sources show

Michael Unterguggenberger, a follower of Silvio Gesell, became burgomaster of Wörgl in December 1931. By spring 1932 the parish of over 4,000 people had 350 unemployed, its share of federal and provincial taxes had fallen sharply, and it owed the Innsbruck savings bank 1,290,000 schillings. In early July 1932 the council printed 32,000 schillings of *Arbeitswertscheine* (labor certificates) in 5 and 10 schilling notes. Each note lost 1% of its value at the end of every month unless the holder bought and fixed a stamp, a form of [[glossary/a-e/demurrage|demurrage]] amounting to 12% a year, and the parish converted notes to schillings at a 2% deduction. Trustees, among them the parish priest, deposited an equal sum in schillings at the Raiffeisen Bank as cover, which the bank lent to wholesalers at 6%. The parish paid its clerical and manual workers first 50% and later 75% of wages in certificates, beginning with 1,800 schillings in July 1932 and rising to about 3,000 a month. Because the notes returned quickly as taxes, only 12,000 schillings were ever issued and deposited, and about a third of those were kept by collectors. Over nine months 34,500 schillings of certificates were converted to banknotes (von Muralt 1934) [IC].

Von Muralt, who visited in 1933, found no rise in prices. A senior Tyrolese official's records showed parish tax receipts rising from 1931 to 1932, for example supplements to the ground tax from 16,500 to 28,570 schillings, which he attributed to payment of arrears; the burgomaster's larger claim that 79,000 of 118,000 schillings of arrears had been paid could not be confirmed at the provincial offices. Relief works worth about 100,000 schillings, paid in certificates and also funded by unemployment-fund subsidies and a 12,000 schilling provincial credit, improved drains, repaired and asphalted streets, lit Railway Street, built a ski jump, a wash-house and a public kitchen, and replaced 32 chestnut trees along the station road, uprooting the old ones and planting new ones at wider spacing [IC]. The neighboring parish of Kirchbichl issued 3,000 schillings of its own certificates from 1 January 1933, and four other communes waited on the outcome.

The Austrian state bank opposed the issue from the beginning as an infringement of its note-issuing privilege. From the beginning of May 1933 the district authorities suspended further issue, and the parish appealed to the Administrative Court (von Muralt 1934), which ended the scheme later in 1933: WU Vienna gives mid-September, the Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland November [IC]. September (2026) concludes that "questions on the long-term viability of the Wörgl Experiment will remain largely unanswered" and identifies "a potential inconsistency in the data related to redemption" of the certificates for schillings [IC]. The figures most often repeated in advocacy, tax revenue up 34.4%, arrears down 53.8% and investment up 219.7%, are attributed to Broer (2007), which could not be obtained; they stay [TBV] until someone reads the book.
