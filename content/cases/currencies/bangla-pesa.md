## What the sources show

Bangla-Pesa launched on 11 May 2013 in Bangladesh settlement, Mombasa, as mutual credit among a business network. Each member needed four guarantors and received 400 Bangla-Pesa, of which 200 went to a community fund for social service work, including cleaning up the area. Membership grew from 56 businesses at launch to 109 the following week, and the designers estimated that the vouchers accounted for about 22% of trade among community members after one week (Ruddick, Richards and Bendell 2015) [MS]. Authorities banned the scheme on 29 May 2013 after a newspaper claimed it was a secessionist plot. Six people, including Will Ruddick and five members of the charity Koru Kenya, were jailed and charged, first with treason and then with forgery (*The Africa Report*, 11 October 2013) [IC]. The 2015 paper states that the charges were brought by the Central Bank of Kenya and that in August 2013 the Director of Public Prosecutions found no law had been broken and dropped them; the program relaunched in November 2013 with local government support, and over 200 businesses used it by August 2014 [MS]. *Nation* later reported about 2,000 residents using it [IC, media].
