## What the sources show

The Agricultural Commodity Exchange for Africa in Malawi has run a warehouse receipt system since 2011, holding about 28,000 tonnes in its latest reported year. Transaction data for 2011 to 2018 show the system was used 710 times, mostly for maize, pigeon peas and soybeans. Large traders took out the most receipts and deposited the largest volumes, followed by medium and small traders, farmer associations and then farmers, most of them medium to large scale. Of all receipts, 339 (48%) yielded a profit; 73% of pigeon pea receipts made losses, against 48% for maize and 53% for soybeans. Maize depositors who borrowed against receipts lost about 7.4% on average, while those who stored without borrowing gained about 14.2%; borrowers paid between 25% and 42% a year in interest (Thunde and Baulch 2020) [IC]. Aboagye (2023) reports that no smallholder farmer had used the system in the surveys he cites [IC].
