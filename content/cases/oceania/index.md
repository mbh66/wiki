Aotearoa New Zealand's settlements made a forest, a river and a mountain legal persons with iwi-Crown governance; no change in water has yet been measured.

## The cases

**[[cases/oceania/te-urewera|Te Urewera]] (Aotearoa New Zealand).** The [[sources/te-urewera-act-2014|Te Urewera Act 2014]] ended the national park and made Te Urewera a legal entity governed by the Te Urewera Board, with Tūhoe holding the majority. Reynecke (2022) finds a real shift of governance from the Department of Conservation to the Board and calls it a tentative step toward Māori management [IC]. No water measurement.

**[[cases/oceania/te-awa-tupua-whanganui-river|Te Awa Tupua]] (Whanganui River).** The [[sources/te-awa-tupua-act-2017|2017 Act]] declares the river, "from the mountains to the sea", a legal person. Te Pou Tupua, one iwi nominee and one Crown nominee, is its human face. The Crown provided NZD 80 million in redress and NZD 1 million for implementing the framework, and the riverbed held by the Crown was vested in Te Awa Tupua [IC]. Te Awa Tupua holds no rights to use water. Resource consents to take water do not require Te Pou Tupua's consent, and decision-makers must only have regard to its status [IC]. A University of Canterbury thesis found no prior research on effects on the ground. A 2025 socio-legal review says Te Pou Tupua's effectiveness has rarely been evaluated.

**[[cases/oceania/te-kahui-tupua-taranaki-maunga|Taranaki Maunga]] (Mount Taranaki).** The [[sources/taranaki-maunga-act-2025|Taranaki Maunga Collective Redress Act 2025]] makes Te Kāhui Tupua, comprising Taranaki and its companion peaks, a legal person (s 18). It vests the national park in Te Kāhui Tupua and creates Te Tōpuni Kōkōrangi, with four iwi and four Crown-appointed members, to act for it. Public access remains [IC]. Outcomes are too recent to assess.
