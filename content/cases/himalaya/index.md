[[glossary/m-s/springshed|Springshed]] programs in the Indian Himalaya name the mountain's recharge function most precisely of any tradition found, and most are led by the state or NGOs.

> This page describes communities, organizations and programs only through their published records and statements. None has yet been invited to comment. Responses and corrections will be recorded on the case pages with their date. [MS] marks a claim that rests on the account of the party the claim is about.

## The cases

**[[cases/himalaya/dhara-vikas-springsheds-sikkim|Dhara Vikas, Sikkim]] (India).** The Rural Management and Development Department launched springshed development under India's rural employment guarantee scheme (MGNREGA). [[sources/tambe-2012-reviving-dying-springs|Tambe et al. (2012)]], citing their own 2009 survey, recorded that 80% of rural households in Sikkim depend on spring water and that lean-season discharge was perceived to have fallen by about half in drought-prone areas. In five treated springs, lean-season discharge rose from 4.4 to 14.4 liters per minute [IC]. A 2026 study recorded Chipchey Dhara rising from 12 to 35 liters per minute and Gaddi Khola from 6 to 40 between 2020 and 2024 [IC]. A Manipur policy brief on applying the Dhara Vikas model reported discharge declines in the villages it studied. All of these are before-after designs without controls (E3).

**[[cases/himalaya/artificial-glaciers-and-ice-stupas-of-ladakh|Ladakh's ice reservoirs]]**. Nüsser and colleagues (2019) inventoried 14 structures and measured about 1,010 to 3,220 m³ of ice at Nang in a poor winter; only five catchments could irrigate all fields even in good years, maintenance suffered from labor shortages, and the ice stupa caused disputes over water sharing [IC].

**[[cases/himalaya/community-forest-user-groups-of-nepal|Nepal's community forest user groups]]**. Oldekop and colleagues (2019) linked community forest management by more than 18,000 groups to a 37% relative fall in deforestation and a 4.3% relative fall in poverty; water was not measured [IC].

**[[cases/himalaya/acwadam-and-peoples-science-institute-springsheds|ACWADAM and People's Science Institute springsheds]]**. The implementers' own assessments report lean-season discharge gains at named springs in Nagaland and Uttarakhand, from before-after comparisons without controls, and one atlas attributes a gain at one spring to higher rainfall [MS]. No independent evaluation was found. A 2025 survey of 10,063 Uttarakhand springs found 76% with falling discharge.

**[[cases/himalaya/paro-watershed-payment|The Paro payment (Bhutan)]]**. In the Namey Nichu agreement, signed on 29 October 2015 for 2016 to 2020, hotels (five by SNV's count, four by IUCN's) and the Satsham Water Association pay a watershed group of 29 village households for verified care of two streams and their springs, with payment due only after a verification team confirms the work (World Bank 2019; IUCN 2019) [IC]. Consultations on a second phase raised concerns about how water is allocated to the hotels. No water measurement was found.

**[[cases/himalaya/yakpugang-watershed-payment|The Yakpugang payment (Bhutan)]]**. Since a contract of December 2010, Mongar municipality has paid the Yakpugang community forest group to protect the springs that supply the town, at Nu 52,000 a year in the first three-year contract; the contract was renewed, though renewal and payment ran late, and a 2021 study reports payments of Nu 228,200 a year from town users and the regional hospital [IC].
