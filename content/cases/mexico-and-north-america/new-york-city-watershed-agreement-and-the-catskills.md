## What the sources show

An Institute for Agriculture and Trade Policy account from the 1990s describes the Whole Farm Program, begun in 1992, in which New York City pays cost-share for best management practices on watershed farms through the farmer-led Watershed Agricultural Council. Farmers chose whether to join, and the target of 85% participation within five years was reported met ahead of schedule. The program was expected to spare the city a filtration plant estimated at about US\$6 billion to build and US\$300 million a year to run. These figures date from the 1990s; current records from the city's filtration avoidance determinations were not read in this pass.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run read the current regulatory record. The New York State Department of Health issued the Revised 2017 Filtration Avoidance Determination in December 2022, after a comment period the Coalition of Watershed Towns asked to extend. The determination requires the city to register a contract or amendment giving the Watershed Agricultural Council US\$11 million. The city's 2022 annual report states that "the WAP did not approve any new WFPs on WOH farms" that year and that no new conservation easements were conveyed to the state [IC]. The council's board composition and the substance of the towns' objections were not read.
