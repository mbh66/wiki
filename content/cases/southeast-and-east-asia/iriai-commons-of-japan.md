## What the sources show

McKean's 1993 working paper describes the legal evolution of Japan's common lands and a century of conflicts over iriai use rights. Only its abstract was read in this pass, so the run's coding of the commons as largely dissolved after the 1950s remains unconfirmed.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run read the Forestry Agency's record. The 1966 Act to promote the modernization of rights in iriai forests (Act No. 126) aimed to replace iriai rights with ownership and other stable rights. About 1.85 million hectares of iriai forest existed when it was enacted; by FY2023, 0.58 million hectares had been reorganized under the law and 0.89 million including voluntary reorganization. A survey of owners confirmed only 0.45 million hectares as remaining iriai. The agency attributes the slowdown to aging iriai groups and a depressed forestry sector (Forestry Agency 2025) [IC]. Noguchi (2014) describes modernization policy as a history of denying iriai rights and dissolving iriai forests. Nakamura (2009) gives over 2 million hectares at enactment, and Takamura and Yamashita (2021) put the share reorganized at about a quarter, against the agency's 31% to 48%. No source documented a water role for surviving iriai.
