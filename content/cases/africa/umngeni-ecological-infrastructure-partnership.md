## What the sources show

Hughes and colleagues (2018) mapped water-related ecosystem services across the uMngeni catchment with a daily hydrological model, to prioritize investment in ecological infrastructure. They estimate gains in dry-season baseflow and lower sediment yield from rehabilitating degraded land and clearing invasive alien plants, and say the estimates likely understate the benefits [IC]. The paper does not describe the partnership's governance or any community role, so those entries stay unverified.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run found that the partnership began with a 2013 memorandum of understanding signed by 21 parties and adopted a strategy in July 2016; SANBI now lists 17 member organizations from government, business, academia and civil society. A coordination committee is co-chaired by eThekwini Municipality and SANBI, with a full-time coordinator and a research committee co-chaired by the Water Research Commission and SANBI. Pringle and colleagues (2023) confirm this structure and found the partnership was a leading voice in the catchment management strategy, though ecological infrastructure itself did not appear in the final document [IC]. No rural community, traditional council or land-rights holder appears as a signatory or committee member in any source read, so the coding records no community seat or money.
