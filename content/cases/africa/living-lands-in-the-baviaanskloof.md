## What the sources show

Commonland formed a partnership in 2014 with Living Lands and Grounded for the catchments that supply Port Elizabeth, committing to at least 20 years. It describes the Baviaanskloof Development Company as a farmer-owned business that has secured over €2.5 million, and sets targets of 32,000 hectares under restoration or regenerative agriculture and about 500 jobs. It reports no measured water result [MS]. The farmer-owned company is a market form with a local owner; no collective tenure of the catchment was found.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run found that most academic work on this case is co-produced with Living Lands. A 2026 article in *People and Nature* (de Villiers et al.), written as a collaborative reflection with Living Lands from interviews with 12 Baviaanskloof and 6 Langkloof residents, reports that farmers remained willing to keep working with the organization after a disappointment. Commonland's 2020 case study states that only four farmers were in the development company's loan scheme within a 41,000-hectare area [MS]. Independently, Mills and Robson (2017) found that about 21.5 million spekboom cuttings planted under the regional thicket restoration program from 2004 to 2016 had an average survival of 28% [IC]; their plots were outside Living Lands' sites, so the finding bears on the method.
