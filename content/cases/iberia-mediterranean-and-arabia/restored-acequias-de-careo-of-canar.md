## What the sources show

The Barjas channel at Cáñar, a medieval irrigation ditch abandoned for 30 years, was recovered in 2014 by MEMOLab, the University of Granada's biocultural archaeology laboratory, with the irrigators' community of Cáñar and Barjas. It is cleaned each year with traditional techniques; about 50 volunteers took part in May 2023 [MS]. The report describes the channel as securing summer irrigation and does not give recharge figures.

**Second enrichment run.** The second enrichment run found that the 2014 restoration under the EU MEMOLA project involved 180 volunteers and the Comunidad de Regantes de Cáñar, and that Sierra Nevada's Global Change Observatory announced it as a way to evaluate the channel's efficacy; no published evaluation was found. Cleaning days continue, funded in 2025 by a national demographic-challenge grant [MS]. The channel's length is given as 5 km by MEMOLA and about 3.5 km by a 2026 press report. Independently, Molina Rojas and colleagues (2022) sampled 45 springs in the Bérchules basin in 2019 and found careo influence on the chemistry of most of its groundwater [IC]; their study does not cover Barjas.
