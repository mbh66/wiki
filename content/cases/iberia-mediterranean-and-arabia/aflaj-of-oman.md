## What the sources show

A 1990s government inventory counted just over 4,000 aflaj, 3,000 in use, irrigating up to half of Oman's fields. In 2014 ministers reported that a quarter had stopped flowing and a third had declined sharply, mainly because farmers sink private boreholes that lower water tables below the tunnels. In one village, permanent rights to 30 minutes of flow a week sold for a little over US\$3,000, and the seventh day's water is auctioned at the mosque to pay for maintenance (Pearce 2015) [IC]. Five aflaj were inscribed as a World Heritage site in 2006.
