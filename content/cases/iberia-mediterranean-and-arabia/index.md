Irrigators' communities in Sierra Nevada maintain snowmelt recharge channels with measured aquifer effects, while customary systems in the Atlas and Oman are weakening under state forestry and private wells.

## The cases

**[[cases/iberia-mediterranean-and-arabia/acequias-de-careo-of-berchules|Acequias de careo, Sierra Nevada]] (Spain).** Andalusí-era channels divert snowmelt onto schist slopes to recharge aquifers that feed springs and rivers in summer. Comunidades de regantes maintain them. In the Bérchules watershed in 2014 to 2015, careo channels supplied 48% of total aquifer recharge and natural infiltration 52% (Jódar, Zakaluk et al. 2022; [[sources/jodar-2022-recharge-channels|Jódar, Martos-Rosillo et al. 2022]]) [IC]. Isotope work found a 40% increase in total water resources attributable to careo [IC]. The threats are abandonment, depopulation and park rules. Restorations of abandoned channels came from university researchers working with the remaining irrigators [TBV].

**[[cases/iberia-mediterranean-and-arabia/tribunal-de-las-aguas-of-valencia|Valencia's Tribunal de las Aguas]]**. Nine irrigation communities on the Turia meet every Thursday at the cathedral door, and UNESCO listed the Tribunal as intangible heritage in 2009 [IC]. It governs river water and leaves the mountain ungoverned.

**[[cases/iberia-mediterranean-and-arabia/agdal-of-yagour|The High Atlas agdal]]**. Genin and Alifriqui report that agdal, collective areas closed and opened by a local institution, have kept or increased their forest area over the long term [IC]. The run's coding of the agdal as eroding was not supported and has been withdrawn. A French-language check found the Yagour's governance still working, with tribal representatives setting the opening dates (Domínguez 2026), and regional evidence of strain: in a neighboring valley, agdal forests gained cover while community institutions weakened after the forest service arrived in 1985 (Auclair and Aubert 2024) [IC].

**[[cases/iberia-mediterranean-and-arabia/aflaj-of-oman|Oman's aflaj]]**. Of just over 4,000 aflaj counted in the 1990s, a quarter had stopped flowing and a third had declined sharply by 2014, mainly because private boreholes lower the water table; shares are bought, sold and auctioned within communities, a market inside customary rules (Pearce 2015) [IC].

**[[cases/iberia-mediterranean-and-arabia/restored-acequias-de-careo-of-canar|The Barjas channel at Cáñar]]**. Recovered in 2014 after 30 years of abandonment by the University of Granada's MEMOLab with the irrigators' community, and cleaned each year by volunteers [MS].
