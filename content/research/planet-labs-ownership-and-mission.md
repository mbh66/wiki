Planet Labs PBC is a founder-controlled [[public-benefit-corporation|public benefit corporation]] that earned about 70 percent of its revenue in the quarter to 31 July 2026 from defense and intelligence customers, and the terms of its Planet AI app, published on 5 November 2026, keep users' queries out of model training while assigning ownership of every generated report to Planet and forbidding users to distribute a Deep Research Report outside their own organization. [IC]

## Overview

Planet Labs PBC (NYSE: PL) builds and operates Earth-imaging satellites. Former NASA staff founded it in December 2010 as Cosmogia, Inc.; it took the name Planet Labs in 2013 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange on 8 December 2021 through a merger with the special purpose acquisition company dMY Technology Group, Inc. IV, reincorporating at listing as a Delaware public benefit corporation [IC]. Its SuperDove satellites image most of the Earth's land surface daily at medium resolution; SkySat and Pelican satellites take high-resolution images on request, and Tanager carries a hyperspectral sensor for methane detection [MS].

On 5 November 2026 Planet made its agentic AI app generally available at ai.planet.com, free of charge for now. A user asks a question in plain language about a place; the app searches ten years of Planet imagery, runs change detection, adds public web material and can write a "Deep Research Report" [IC]. Planet calls the product the Planet AI app. This page uses "Ask Planet" as the name practitioners have given it.

A bioregional practitioner who puts a valley, a catchment or a community through the app is handing information about that place to a company. This brief sets out what the public record says about who controls that company, where its money comes from, how it has acted when its stated mission and its revenue pulled in different directions, and what its terms say happens to the questions and reports. It gives no verdict on anyone's character or sincerity, and it gives no investment advice.

## Ownership and control

Planet has two classes of voting stock. Class A shares carry one vote each and Class B shares carry twenty. The fiscal 2025 annual report states that co-founders William (Will) Marshall and Robert (Robbie) Schingler Jr. "hold all of the issued and outstanding shares of our Class B common stock" [IC]. The 2026 proxy statement (record date 15 May 2026) reports 332,899,400 Class A and 23,493,796 Class B shares outstanding [IC]. On those figures the Class B votes alone come to 58.5 percent of all votes, before the founders' own Class A shares are counted [IC, calculated]. In January and September 2025 the founders approved corporate actions by written consent, without a shareholder meeting, holding about 61 and 60 percent of the votes [IC].

The 2023 proxy statement gives the company's reason for the structure: it "strengthens our founders' ability to focus on our public benefit purpose without the risk of challenges by hostile takeovers or other actions that could be focused on short-term gains" [MS]. The same votes decide every board election.

Google received its stake in 2017, when Planet took over Google's Terra Bella satellite business and its SkySat fleet, and it is also Planet's hosting supplier [IC]. It is the only outside holder above 5 percent in the 2026 proxy [IC].

Before listing, the company raised money from venture investors. Draper Fisher Jurvetson led the first round (about US\$13.1 million). Data Collective (DCVC) led a Series C announced in January 2015 with US\$70 million of equity, and the International Finance Corporation of the World Bank Group added US\$23 million in April 2015, bringing that round to US\$118 million [IC]. Named participants in 2015 included Yuri Milner, Founders Fund, Lux Capital, Capricorn Investment Group and First Round Capital [IC]. The leads of the 2013 Series B and the 2019 Series D, the date of Space Capital's entry and the terms of the 2021 listing investment were not confirmed [TBV].

Marshall sold 600,000 Class A shares between December 2025 and July 2026 for about US\$16.1 million, under a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan adopted on 12 July 2025; the sales did not touch his Class B shares [IC]. Schingler sold about US\$7.2 million under a plan adopted on 14 July 2025 [TBV]. An aggregation of Form 4 filings puts all insider sales in the twelve months to 6 November 2026 at US\$43.2 million, 70 percent of it under pre-arranged plans [TBV].

## The mission on paper

Planet's restated certificate of incorporation, effective 7 December 2021, states:

> [!quote] Public benefit purpose
> "The specific public benefit purpose of the Corporation is to accelerate humanity toward a more sustainable, secure, and prosperous world, by illuminating the most important forms of environmental and social change." [IC]

The charter requires the company to report to stockholders on its public benefit at least every two years [IC]. Delaware law (sections 362 to 367 of the General Corporation Law) obliges directors of a public benefit corporation to balance stockholders' financial interests, the interests of those materially affected by the company's conduct and the stated public benefit, and it limits suits to enforce that duty to holders of at least 2 percent of the shares or, for a listed company, shares worth US\$2 million [IC]. Planet published a "PBC Report 2023" for this purpose [IC]. A report for 2025 was not located [TBV].

The word "secure" sits in the charter purpose. Sales to defense and intelligence agencies therefore fall within the public benefit as written. Marshall's argument that sustainability and security are "totally wrapped up together" [MS] is a position the charter adopted in 2021.

Planet's own filings set limits on what the form promises. Its 2022 prospectus states: "We do not believe that an investment in the stock of a public benefit corporation differs materially from an investment in a corporation that is not designated as a public benefit corporation" [IC]. The annual report adds: "There is no assurance that we will achieve our public benefit purpose" [IC].

The same figures support two readings. As a share of revenue, defense and intelligence has risen in every period. In dollars, civil and commercial revenue together has stayed close to flat: US\$126.1 million in fiscal 2024, US\$128.1 million in fiscal 2025, US\$127.5 million in fiscal 2026 and US\$134.3 million in the twelve months to July 2026 [IC]. Defense growth has been added on top of the civil and commercial base without shrinking it. Planet does not report costs by sector, so whether defense revenue pays for the satellites that environmental users rely on cannot be measured from the filings.

The largest contracts named in recent releases are for governments' security services: a €240 million multi-year contract with the German government (July 2025) for dedicated Pelican capacity "in support of European peace and security", a US\$8 million award from the US National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (August 2026), and the handover of a satellite to the Swedish Armed Forces (2026) [IC]. Civil contracts in the same releases include a national data program with the Rwanda Space Agency, described as Planet's first of its kind in Africa, and monitoring of more than 9 million acres of New Mexico state trust land [IC]. Growth has also been funded by new shares and debt: in the quarter to 31 July 2026 Planet raised about US\$120 million by selling stock into the market at an average net price of US\$31.95 [IC].

## Conduct where mission and revenue pull apart

Free tropical forest data. From September 2020 Norway's International Climate and Forest Initiative (NICFI) paid for free monthly high-resolution basemaps of the tropics, supplied by Planet with KSAT and Airbus; NICFI reports more than 37,000 users in 158 countries [MS]. The program ended on 23 January 2025 when Norway's contract term ran out, and Norway cancelled a follow-on procurement on 9 September 2025 [IC]. Planet's chief financial officer told analysts that flat civil revenue in fiscal 2026 was "driven in large part by the end of our contract with Norway for their NICFI program" [IC]. The Education and Research, Media and Crisis Response programs remain listed in Planet's terms [IC].

Gaza, 2023. The Business and Human Rights Resource Centre recorded reports that imagery of Gaza was restricted or delayed after October 2023 [IC]. Planet stated that the restrictions followed "standard operating protocols for our product that are not in any way specific to this region or event" and that it "does not censor, modify, obscure, or manipulate images" [MS].

The Middle East, 2026. In March 2026 Planet delayed release of Middle East imagery, first by 96 hours and then by 14 days. On 5 April 2026 it began withholding that imagery indefinitely, backdated to 9 March, after the US government asked imagery providers to withhold images of designated areas voluntarily (Reuters; Bloomberg) [IC]. Planet told customers it would move to a "managed distribution of images" that it judged posed no safety risk [IC]. Bloomberg reported that the US government and some other governments and commercial customers kept unrestricted access [TBV].

The values review. Marshall has said that a group of employees reviews deals against the company's values and that sales follow US, EU and UN embargo lists [MS]. No public record was found of how the group works or of any deal it stopped.

## The founders

Will Marshall is co-founder, chief executive officer and chair of the board. Robbie Schingler is co-founder, chief strategy officer and a director [IC]. The 2026 proxy states that they are the only directors who are not independent and that the company entered no related-party transactions with directors or officers in fiscal 2026 [IC]. Their earlier careers at NASA, Marshall's Oxford doctorate and their outside boards and holdings rest on company biographies and interviews and were not checked [MS]. A third co-founder, Chris Boshuizen, left in 2015 [TBV].

Marshall's recent public statements sit on both sides of the mission. On the German contract: "the demand for sovereign access to geospatial intelligence is more urgent than ever before" (1 July 2025) [IC]. On the quarter to July 2026: "our strategy pairs AI-enabled analytics with sovereign satellite services" [IC]. In interviews he describes the company's mission as "using space to help life on earth" and says that "ultimately all companies should be public benefit corporations" [MS].

## The terms of Ask Planet

Two documents govern the app, both effective 5 November 2026: the Planet AI Terms of Use and the Planet privacy policy, which the terms incorporate [IC]. Reports produced during the earlier beta fell under separate beta trial terms that were not retrieved [TBV]. All quotations below are from those two documents as published on 7 November 2026.

### What the app collects and where it goes

The privacy policy lists among the inputs "the prompts, questions, areas of interest, geometries and files you submit" [IC]. It states: "we send your Inputs and relevant context to the providers of the large language models that power Planet AI, currently Anthropic, PBC and Google LLC," under contracts that bar them from training on the data, and "They may keep Inputs and Outputs for a limited period to detect and prevent abuse" [IC]. The app "is not intended for processing Personal Information in Inputs," and users are asked not to include personal information about other people [IC]. Planet's published subprocessor list, dated 1 February 2025, does not name Anthropic [IC].

### Training

Both documents state that Planet does not use inputs or outputs to train or fine-tune AI models [IC]. The exception is feedback. When a user rates or comments on an interaction, the terms allow Planet to "review and use the associated Inputs and Outputs to evaluate and improve Planet AI, including its models" [IC]. The privacy policy describes the same review without the words "including its models," and offers an objection route through mydata@planet.com [IC]. Separately, the terms let Planet use anonymized, aggregated usage data "for any business purpose, including without limitation to generate industry benchmarks" [IC].

### Retention and deletion

Inputs, uploaded files and outputs are kept "until you delete them or the relevant conversation, or until your account is closed." Deleted items leave active systems within 30 days and backups within 90 days, unless Planet needs them to comply with law, resolve a dispute or investigate a breach of the terms [IC].

### Who owns a report

The terms state that Planet "retains all right, title, and interest, including all intellectual property rights, in and to Planet AI, the Content, the Outputs." They continue: "To the extent you acquire any right, title, or interest in any Output, you hereby assign it to Planet," or, where assignment is not allowed, grant Planet "an exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free license" [IC]. Deep Research Reports are outputs. Users keep their rights in their inputs, including any part of an input reproduced in an output [IC].

### Who may share a report

The license depends on the user's category, and a category applies "only if you meet every element of its definition" [IC]. A self-employed person using the app in the course of their trade or profession falls in the Enterprise category, which also catches anyone who fits no other [IC].

| User category | What may be distributed | Deep Research Reports |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Outputs, for noncommercial promotion or independent research | "shall not be distributed externally" |
| Education and Research | Articles, theses and papers based on outputs | Internal education or research use only |
| NGO or Non-Profit (registered tax-exempt bodies) | Derivative products, for reporting and promotion of the organization's mission | "shall not be distributed externally" |
| Media | Imagery and derivative products as part of a larger news story | "shall not be distributed externally" |
| Government | Nothing | May not be published or distributed |
| Enterprise | Nothing outside the organization | May not be published or distributed externally |

[IC] for every row. Permitted sharing must carry attribution, for a report in the form "AI-generated report produced using Planet AI. ai.planet.com. [DATE]." [IC]. A shareable link inside the app lets "any logged-in Planet AI user with the link" view the report [IC].

### Government access and accuracy

The terms state: "From time to time, Planet may be compelled by governmental licensing authorities to provide access to Planet AI to regulators or others as may be required for compliance purposes" [IC]. The privacy policy allows disclosure "to comply with legal process served on us or our affiliates (such as a court order or subpoena)" [IC]. Neither document describes notice to a user whose inputs are requested. The acceptable use rules forbid using the app to "identify, locate, track, or surveil specific individuals" [IC]. On accuracy, the terms say outputs "may be incorrect, incomplete, outdated" and that users "should not rely on Outputs without independently checking their accuracy" [IC]. No published accuracy evaluation of the app was found.

Governing law is California for users in the United States and most of the world, and Germany for users in Council of Europe countries [IC].

## Questions the record leaves open

1. Will Planet grant a written exception, or a license back, that lets a community or a [[biohub|BioHub]] publish a Deep Research Report about its own place?
2. Which user category applies to a community forum, a residents' association or a practitioner who is not a registered non-profit?
3. How long do Anthropic and Google keep inputs and outputs "for a limited period," and in which jurisdictions?
4. Will Planet tell a user when a government or court asks for that user's inputs or reports?
5. Do the aggregated usage data include which places are being asked about, and are they sold or shared with government customers?
6. Do the new terms apply to reports made during the beta, and can those reports be shared?
7. When will the subprocessor list name the model providers?
8. Who sits on the employee values-review group, what criteria does it apply, and has it published a decision?
9. Will the next public benefit report address imagery withholding and the end of NICFI?

## What the record means for place-based work

The terms of the app bear more directly on a practitioner than the company's direction. The questions a practitioner asks about a place stay the practitioner's own and stay out of model training unless feedback is given, and they can be deleted [IC]. The report the app writes about that place belongs to Planet, and in no user category may it be passed to the community it describes outside the user's own organization [IC]. For a practitioner who treats knowledge about a place as part of its [[commons|commons]], the second clause is the one to read before the first query. A practitioner can still use the imagery and the analysis to inform their own writing, within their category's license, and can delete conversations once they have what they need.

The company record supplies context for those terms. Two people control the votes; the charter names security alongside sustainability; the growth since 2024 has come from defense and intelligence; and in 2026 the company followed a US government request to withhold imagery from most users [IC].
