# and What This Implies for the Design of Regenerative Participation Income

## TL;DR

- The Market form (+M) of the [[time-framework|TIME Framework]] retains participants through wage compulsion, which confiscates their available hours before any other coordination form can claim them; this is the primary structural bottleneck for Emergent (+E) coordination, and it binds on the coordination architect's own hours rather than on any project's funding. `working-hypothesis`
- Every major [[universal-basic-income-ubi|Universal Basic Income]] experiment (Finland, Mincome, Madhya Pradesh, Stockton, Kenya, OpenResearch, Ontario, the Dutch municipalities) improved wellbeing and reliably failed to reduce work in any damaging way, but none changed the coordination substrate, because each denominated its payment in national fiat currency and paid individuals with no [[coordination-surface|coordination surface]] to flow into; released hours defaulted to leisure, private investment, or improved market position, not +E coordination. `documented-fact`
- [[regenerative-participation-income-rpi|Regenerative Participation Income]] (RPI), designed as a BioHub-distributed stipend that funds a coordination role, produces legible coordination surfaces, and is denominated in a bioregional [[demurrage|demurrage]] currency that cannot re-accumulate as +M capital, is the substrate intervention UBI gestures toward but doesn't achieve; the minimum viable version is a single [[biohub|BioHub]], a single stipended coordinator, a defined coordination surface, and a 90-day cycle with auditable outputs. `working-hypothesis`

## Key Findings

1. **[[retention-logic|Retention Logic]] is an undertheorized but essential property of coordination architectures.** `structural-inference` The T, I, and M forms have well-documented retention mechanisms (kinship obligation; legal duty and path-dependent lock-in; wage compulsion and debt). The +N form's retention logic is undeveloped in the literature. This brief proposes that +N retains through the compound returns of coordination infrastructure itself.

2. **The Market form's retention mechanism operates on time directly.** `documented-framework` Where tribal and institutional mechanisms raise the cost of exit, wage compulsion confiscates the hours before the question of exit arises. This is grounded in Polanyi's [[fictitious-commodities|fictitious-commodity]] analysis, Standing's precariat, the time-use literature, Graeber, and Gorz.

3. **No UBI experiment changed the substrate.** `documented-fact` Coordination effects appeared only weakly and at the margins (Madhya Pradesh collectives, Ontario volunteering, Mincome schooling and caregiving), always inside the existing monetary substrate.

4. **Participation income (Atkinson), commons-based peer production (Benkler), [[commons|commons]] governance costs (Ostrom), and cooperative governance funding (Mondragon) already contain every element RPI needs except their combination.** `academic-inference` What none supplies, and RPI adds, is [[demurrage|demurrage]] denomination that prevents re-accumulation as +M capital.

5. **A three-month Overberg case exhibits the predicted signature of a substrate change rather than a motivation change.** `structural-inference` Ten coordination outputs in ninety days, with no change in capability or resources, only the suspension of the wage claim on the coordinator's hours.

6. **Illegibility is itself a retention mechanism that locks the wage-compulsion bottleneck in place.** `structural-inference` Because +N coordination cannot be measured in +M terms, it cannot be funded in +M terms; legibility infrastructure (wikis, published tools, llms.txt endpoints) breaks this loop by making coordination inspectable on its own terms.

## Details

### 1. Thesis statement

`working-hypothesis` The [[m-form-market|Market form]] (+M) of the TIME Framework retains participants primarily through wage compulsion: the structural requirement to sell labor for income, which commits the participant's available hours to market-serving activity before any other coordination form can lay claim to them. This retention logic is the primary structural bottleneck for the emergence of the [[e-form-emergent|Emergent form]] (+E) coordination form, because the people most capable of building +E infrastructure are precisely the people whose hours the +M substrate holds hostage. The bottleneck is a substrate problem, not a resource problem. It follows that releasing a coordinator from wage compulsion, even temporarily, should produce +E coordination outputs at a rate that increased motivation alone cannot explain, because the substrate changed and the behavior followed. Regenerative Participation Income (RPI), understood as a stipend that funds a coordination role and is denominated in a bioregional [[demurrage|demurrage]] currency, is the substrate intervention that Universal Basic Income (UBI) gestures toward but does not achieve, because UBI supplements purchasing power inside the existing monetary substrate while RPI changes the substrate itself.

### 2. The retention logic of coordination forms

`documented-framework` The TIME Framework (formerly [[timn-framework|TIMN]]) is [[people/theorists/ronfeldt-david|David Ronfeldt]]'s four-form model of societal evolution, developed at RAND and set out in *Tribes, Institutions, Markets, Networks: A Framework About Societal Evolution* (RAND P-7967, 1996). Ronfeldt's claim is that human societies organize through four cardinal forms that arise in sequence: the kinship-based tribe (T), the hierarchical institution (I), the competitive-exchange market (M), and the collaborative network (N), which the [[bioconomy|BioConomy]] wiki renames Emergent (E/+N) following Ronfeldt's own October 2025 proposal. Each form matures in a different historical epoch, each is associated with a distinct information and communications revolution, and each, once established, does not disappear but layers under the next. [[people/theorists/ronfeldt-david|Ronfeldt]] frames the progression as [[monoform]] to [[biform]] to [[triform]] and potentially to [[quadriform]], and argues for pursuing "the quadriformist option."

`documented-framework` The wiki's [[concepts/substrate-hypothesis|Substrate Hypothesis]] holds that coordination behavior is a function of engineered or evolved substrate, not participant disposition, so that changing behavior requires changing the substrate. This brief adds a companion proposition that the TIME literature has left underdeveloped: each form has a [[concepts/retention-logic|retention logic]], a mechanism by which it captures and holds participants' time, attention, and relational capacity within its own coordination architecture. Retention logic is distinct from the form's coordination logic. A form can coordinate through one principle while retaining through another. The retention logic is the answer to the question: once a participant is inside this form, what holds them there, and what does it cost them to act inside a different form?

`structural-inference` The [[t-form-tribal|Tribal form]] retains through kinship obligation, identity, and belonging. [[people/theorists/ronfeldt-david|Ronfeldt]] describes the tribe as defining "the cultural realm in particular," structured by extended families, clans, and lineage systems. The retention mechanism is the felt cost of exit from a web of kin obligations and shared identity, which is why, as the [[p2p-foundation-wiki|P2P Foundation]] gloss on Ronfeldt notes, "tribalism is alive and well in online social networks." The participant is retained because leaving means becoming no one to the people who made them someone.

`structural-inference` The [[i-form-institutional|Institutional form]] retains through legal duty, institutional role, and bureaucratic process. [[people/theorists/ronfeldt-david|Ronfeldt]] locates the institutional form in "the army, the (Catholic) church, and ultimately the bureaucratic state," organized by hierarchy and administrative office. The retention mechanism here is well theorized in the historical-institutionalist literature on path dependence. Paul Pierson's "Increasing Returns, Path Dependence, and the Study of Politics" (*American Political Science Review* 94, 2000), building on W. Brian Arthur (1989, 1994) and Paul David (1985), shows that institutions reproduce themselves through increasing returns, coordination effects, learning effects, and rising exit costs, so that as arrangements become more widely adopted, they become more valuable to remain within and more expensive to exit. Sydow, Schreyögg, and Koch, "Organizational Path Dependence: Opening the Black Box" (*Academy of Management Review* 34, 2009), describe how an organization's scope for action narrows through self-reinforcing dynamics until it is locked in. The institutional participant is retained by the accumulated switching costs of a role, a pension, a professional license, and a bureaucratic identity that only this institution recognizes.

`structural-inference` The [[m-form-market|Market form]] retains through wage compulsion and debt obligation. The participant must sell labor to survive, which means available hours are structurally committed to market-serving activity before they can be committed to anything else. Its distinctive feature is that it operates on time directly. The tribal and institutional retention mechanisms raise the cost of exit; the market retention mechanism confiscates the hours before the question of exit even arises.

`working-hypothesis` The [[e-form-emergent|Emergent form]]'s retention logic is underdeveloped in the literature, and this brief proposes a specific mechanism: the +N form retains through the compound returns of coordination itself. Once coordination infrastructure exists, it generates its own demand, creates relational obligations, and becomes self-reinforcing. A wiki that documents a bioregion's coordination vocabulary makes the next coordination act cheaper, which invites more coordination, which deepens the wiki. This is the increasing-returns dynamic that Arthur and David identified for technologies and Pierson identified for institutions, applied to coordination infrastructure. The difference is that the +N form's compound returns accrue to a [[commons|commons]] rather than to a firm or a bureau, so the retention is relational and reputational rather than contractual. The participant is retained because the [[coordination-surface|coordination surface]] they built now needs them, and because the relationships the coordination created now expect them. This is the weakest-sourced claim in the retention-logic schema, precisely because the +N form is, as the wiki's essay on [[concepts/obligation-and-invitation|obligation and invitation]] puts it, "still nascent."

`academic-inference` The value of naming retention logic as a general property is that it reframes a set of findings usually treated as separate. Institutional lock-in, the "busy trap," time poverty, and the decline of civic participation are, on this reading, the same phenomenon observed from different forms. Each is a retention mechanism doing its work. The busy trap is not a personal failing of the overcommitted professional; it is the +M retention logic operating as designed.

### 3. The Market form's capture mechanism

`documented-framework` Wage compulsion is structural, rather than moral. The clearest theoretical grounding is Karl Polanyi's *The Great Transformation* (1944), which argues that the self-regulating market required labor, land, and money to be treated as commodities even though none of them was produced for sale. Polanyi calls these the "[[fictitious-commodities|fictitious commodities]]." Labor, he writes, is "only another name for a human activity which goes with life itself," and the attempt to subject that activity to the price mechanism means "to subordinate the substance of society itself to the laws of the market" (Polanyi 1944, 2001 Beacon Press edition, p. 72). The commodification of labor is the mechanism by which human hours become market-serving hours by default. Once a person's capacity to act is a commodity they must sell to eat, the market has first claim on their time, and every non-market coordination act competes against the wage the same hours could have earned.

`documented-fact` Guy Standing's work on the precariat sharpens Polanyi's point into a claim about time and control. In *The Precariat: The New Dangerous Class* (2011), Standing defines the precariat by a bundle of insecurities and argues that "the precariat is at the risk of being in a permanent spin, forced to juggle demands on limited time," which he summarizes as "a loss of control over knowledge, ethics and time" (Standing 2011, pp. 203-205). Standing also documents "work-for-labour," the growing body of unpaid effort (job searching, retraining, benefit compliance, commuting) that the wage relation demands without compensating, further eroding the hours available for anything else. The precariat's defining condition is not low income alone; it is the confiscation of temporal sovereignty.

`documented-fact` The empirical relationship between wage-labor hours and civic participation is documented in the time-use literature. The International Labour Organization's *Working Time and Work-Life Balance Around the World* (2022) reports, citing Fagan et al., that "work-family incompatibility, less engagement in community and civic life and lower fertility rates are all common outcomes of long hours of work." Michael Bittman's analysis of Multinational Time Use Study data (2005) found that Australians required to work Sundays "enjoyed substantially less time for recreational activities, family conviviality and civic association" and that this lost time "was not made up during the week." The US Bureau of Labor Statistics American Time Use Survey (2025 release) shows the share of adults who socialized on an average day fell from 38 percent in 2015 to 30 percent in 2025. These findings establish that market hours displace coordination hours in a measurable, direction-specific way.

`documented-framework` David Graeber's *Bullshit Jobs: A Theory* (2018) adds that the confiscation is often of hours spent on work that even the worker believes is pointless. Graeber defines a bullshit job as "a form of paid employment that is so completely pointless, unnecessary, or pernicious that even the employee cannot justify its existence," and argues that the proliferation of such roles represents "a colossal misallocation of human potential." Graeber's own remedy is UBI, on the logic that survival security would free people to "pursue more meaningful activities, whether that's community work, art, further education, or genuine innovation." The retention-logic analysis predicts why Graeber's remedy underdelivers: income security alone leaves the wage relation intact around the recipient, so the hours remain formally free but substantively captured.

`documented-framework` André Gorz supplies the theoretical bridge from wage critique to coordination release. In *Critique of Economic Reason* (1989) and *Reclaiming Work: Beyond the Wage-Based Society* (1999), Gorz argues that economic rationality demands the continuous reduction of necessary labor, and that the task is to expand "the sphere of autonomy" against "the sphere of heteronomy" (wage labor), supported by a guaranteed income that decouples subsistence from the sale of labor. Gorz's phrase for the goal is a "civilization of time," in which autonomous individuals "choose their own mix of activities, with capital and technology banished to subordinate roles." Gorz insists on "the divorce of quantity of work from the amount of wage a worker receives." The BioConomy's coordinator is a concrete instance of what Gorz described: a person whose hours, once released from wage compulsion, flow into autonomous coordinating activity.

`academic-inference` Feminist economics establishes that unpaid care work is the invisible coordination substrate on which the market rests, and that the +M form retains disproportionately by offloading care onto uncompensated hours. Standing notes that women face "a triple burden" becoming "a quadruple one, of having to care for children, care for elderly relatives and do perhaps not one but two jobs." The care economy is coordination work: it holds families and communities together, and the market neither prices it nor frees hours for it. The RPI design in section 9 treats care as qualifying coordination activity precisely to invert this.

`structural-inference` The critical claim of this brief is that wage compulsion is the primary structural bottleneck for +N emergence, and that the bottleneck operates on the coordination architect, not on the project. The standard framing of why bioregional and commons initiatives fail is that they lack funding for projects. The retention-logic framing is different: the coordinator who could build the coordination infrastructure cannot do so because the +M substrate holds their own hours hostage. This is what the wiki's (+T+I+M)^+N Test formalizes. Any +N effort that leaves the monetary substrate intact defaults to triform coordination with a +N overlay, because the coordinators are still selling their hours into +M and coordinating in whatever time remains. The bottleneck is that +N maturation is rate-limited by how many coordinator-hours can escape wage capture.

### 4. The UBI landscape assessed against the substrate-change criterion

`documented-framework` The modern case for Universal Basic Income is set out in Philippe Van Parijs and Yannick Vanderborght, *Basic Income: A Radical Proposal for a Free Society and a Sane Economy* (Harvard University Press, 2017), and in Guy Standing, *Basic Income: And How We Can Make It Happen* (Penguin, 2017). Both defend an unconditional cash payment to individuals, financed and denominated in the national fiat currency. The assessment criterion this brief applies is single: did the intervention change the coordination substrate, or did it supplement income within the existing +M substrate? A substrate change would show up as recipients moving hours into non-market coordination. An income supplement would show up as recipients improving their position within market coordination. The distinction matters because income without substrate change is purchasing power, and purchasing power operates inside +M by definition.

`documented-fact` The Finnish basic income experiment (2017-2018) is the decisive case. Kela, the Social Insurance Institution of Finland, paid 2,000 randomly selected unemployed people aged 25 to 58 a monthly tax-exempt sum of 560 euros, unconditionally and without means testing, against a control group of roughly 5,000. The employment finding was small: over the measured period from November 2017 to October 2018, basic income recipients were employed on average 78 days, six days more than the control group, an effect the researchers described as slight and complicated by the parallel introduction of an activation model in 2018. The wellbeing finding was strong: recipients "were more satisfied with their lives and experienced less mental strain, depression, sadness and loneliness" and reported better perceived economic security (Kela and Ministry of Social Affairs and Health, press release, 6 May 2020). The retention-logic reading is that the Finnish result is exactly what the thesis predicts. The +M substrate remained intact around the recipients; the payment raised their perceived security and did not move their hours, because unconditional fiat cash is purchasing power and purchasing power changes position inside +M, not the substrate itself.

`documented-fact` The Mincome experiment (Dauphin, Manitoba, 1974-1979) supports the substrate reading from the other direction. Evelyn Forget's reanalysis, "The Town with No Poverty: The Health Effects of a Canadian Guaranteed Annual Income Field Experiment" (*Canadian Public Policy*, 2011), documented "an 8.5 percent reduction in the hospitalization rate for participants relative to controls, particularly for accidents and injuries and mental health," along with more adolescents completing grade 12 and overall work effort barely falling. The only groups that reduced labor were new mothers and teenaged boys, and their withdrawn hours went into caregiving and schooling. That is a small movement of hours into non-market activity, and it appeared only where the guarantee coincided with a life stage in which the wage claim was already weak. David Green's 2021 reanalysis cautions that the pre-existing downward hospitalization trend means the health effect may not be cleanly causal. Mincome shows substrate movement at the margins, in exactly the demographic pockets where +M's grip was loosest.

`documented-fact` India's Madhya Pradesh pilot (SEWA and UNICEF), the Madhya Pradesh Unconditional Cash Transfers Project running roughly June 2011 to November 2012, is reported in Sarath Davala, Renana Jhabvala, Soumya Kapoor Mehta, and Guy Standing, *Basic Income: A Transformative Policy for India* (Bloomsbury, 2015). It covered over 6,000 individuals across nine villages, who received a basic income for 12 to 17 months, paying adults a monthly sum equal to about 30 percent of subsistence expenditure. The Stanford Basic Income Lab summary records "anecdotal evidence regarding an increase in collective work, describing nascent workers' collectives," alongside an emancipatory effect in which recipients could negotiate better wages and a shift from bonded and wage labor toward own-account work. The Madhya Pradesh result is important because it shows collective coordination emerging, but the coordination emerged inside a still-monetized fiat economy, which is why it appears as workers' collectives bargaining within +M rather than as an exit from +M.

`documented-fact` The Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration (SEED, 2019-2021) is the case most often cited to refute the idea that cash reduces work, and it is the clearest instance of an income supplement improving +M position. SEED gave 125 residents 500 dollars a month for 24 months, funded philanthropically, with a 1 million dollar grant from the Economic Security Project. Per the official SEED evaluation (West and Castro Baker, for the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation's Evidence for Action program, 2021): "In February 2019, 28% of recipients had full-time employment. One year later, 40% of recipients were employed full-time," a 12-percentage-point rise, against the control group's move from 32 percent to 37 percent (5 points). The evaluators explain the mechanism in substrate terms without using the word: "when every dollar of wage work is allocated for bills before it is earned, most cannot afford to skip work or take necessary steps toward better employment." SEED moved people deeper into full-time market employment. That is a +M optimization, and it is a good outcome on SEED's own poverty-reduction terms. It is the opposite of a substrate change.

`documented-fact` The GiveDirectly Kenya experiment (ongoing since 2016, first results December 2023) is the largest UBI study in the world, a 295-village, 12-year experiment covering roughly 23,000 recipients, testing a long-term arm (12 years), a short-term arm (2 years), and a lump sum against a control. The headline finding from Banerjee, Faye, Krueger, Niehaus, and Suri, "Universal Basic Income in Kenya" (NBER, 2023), is that "labor supply did not change overall but shifted out of wage employment and towards self-employment," with "substantial economic expansion, more enterprises, higher revenues," concentrated in non-agricultural retail. GiveDirectly reports "no evidence of UBI promoting laziness." The Kenya result shows recipients reorganizing their market activity from wage work to self-employment and enterprise, which is a movement within +M toward its entrepreneurial pole, not a movement into +N coordination. The lump sum and the long-term guarantee outperformed the short-term arm, which tells us that the horizon of the guarantee matters, a point the RPI design takes up.

`documented-fact` The OpenResearch Unconditional Cash Study (Y Combinator's originally proposed Oakland pilot, executed as a US study with results in July 2024) gave 1,000 recipients 1,000 dollars a month for three years against 2,000 controls receiving 50 dollars. The NBER work by Vivalt, Rhodes, Bartik, Broockman, and Miller (WP 32784, 2024) found a 2.0-percentage-point decrease in labor-market participation and a 1.3 to 1.4 hour per week reduction in hours worked, with total individual income excluding transfers falling roughly 1,500 dollars a year relative to controls; lowest-income recipients were markedly more likely to participate in education or job training, and there was no significant rise in entrepreneurial activity. The authors conclude that recipients "placed a high value, at the margin, on additional leisure." The reallocated hours went to leisure, childcare, and education. This is consistent with the substrate reading: absent a coordination surface to flow into, released hours default to rest and private investment, not to +N coordination. Cash without a coordination role produces leisure; a stipend attached to a coordination role produces coordination.

`documented-fact` The Dutch municipal experiments ("Weten wat werkt," roughly 2017-2019, across Utrecht, Groningen, Tilburg, Wageningen, Nijmegen, and Deventer) tested relaxed conditionality on existing welfare recipients rather than universal payment. Timo Verlaat et al.'s final report (Utrecht University, 2020) and Femke Roosma's analysis (2022) found "generally insignificant results for the interventions on the outflow to work," with a significant positive effect only in Utrecht's intensified-support arm. The politically important conclusion is that removing coercion did not reduce work: an approach "based on attention, trust, customisation and self-reliance" did as well as one enforced with punishment. The Dutch case matters for RPI because it dissolves the objection that releasing the wage obligation collapses productive activity. It does not.

`documented-fact` The Ontario Basic Income Pilot (2017-2019, cancelled early) produced the strongest civic-participation signal of any recent experiment. Ferdosi, McDowell, Lewchuk, and Ross, *Southern Ontario's Basic Income Experience* (McMaster University, 2020), surveying 217 recipients, found that recipients reported "participating more frequently in extracurricular activities (76%)" and "volunteering more often (48%)," while "nearly three-quarters of respondents who were working when the pilot project began kept at it," many moving to better jobs, and 40.6 percent of those who left work did so "to pursue further education or training." Ontario shows the clearest movement of hours into non-market coordination, and it is suggestive that this appeared in a design with a relatively generous payment (up to CAD 1,415 a month) and a longer implicit horizon than the Finnish or SEED designs.

`academic-inference` The pattern across the UBI landscape is that **unconditional cash in fiat currency reliably improves wellbeing and reduces poverty, reliably fails to reduce work in any damaging way, and only weakly and inconsistently moves hours into non-market coordination**. Where coordination effects appear (Madhya Pradesh collectives, Ontario volunteering, Mincome schooling and caregiving), they appear at the margins and inside the existing monetary substrate. **No UBI experiment changed the substrate, because none was designed to; every one denominated its payment in the national fiat currency and paid individuals qua individuals with no coordination surface to flow into**. This is the precise gap that RPI is designed to fill.

### 5. Participation income and coordination funding

`documented-fact` Anthony Atkinson's "The Case for a Participation Income" (*The Political Quarterly* 67, 1996) is the closest prior formulation to RPI. Atkinson (1944-2017), of Oxford and the London School of Economics, proposed conditioning income on social contribution: the benefit is paid to those who contribute to society or community in some way, where contribution "goes beyond paid work," including care work, education, training, and volunteering. Heikki Hiilamo's *Participation Income* (Edward Elgar, 2022) provides the first book-length treatment and records that the condition "retains the reciprocity norm which has strong philosophical and pragmatic grounds," marking "a departure from both workfare and activation policies." Atkinson's proposal is closer to RPI than UBI is, because it **ties income to participation rather than paying it unconditionally, and because it explicitly counts non-market coordination work as qualifying**.

`documented-fact` The standard criticism of participation income is administrative: Atkinson "did not give a precise rule to define the forms of contributions that entitle people to receive participation," which leaves the scheme vulnerable to the objection that defining and verifying "participation" is costly and intrusive (Hiilamo 2022, ch. 7). Cristian Pérez-Muñoz, "A Defence of Participation Income" (*Journal of Public Policy*, 2015), reframes participation income as *"a particular type of civic service programme designed to address a large number of unmet social needs that are not met by entrepreneurs in the marketplace."* Hiilamo notes that the Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, and Finland have "borrowed elements of PI in their social assistance reforms," and that the administrative hurdles proved surmountable "only when PI is not rolled out as a universal programme." This last point is decisive for RPI design: **the verification problem shrinks dramatically when the scheme is bounded to a defined coordination surface rather than a whole population**, which is exactly the minimum viable version proposed below.

`documented-framework` Yochai Benkler's *The Wealth of Networks* (Yale University Press, 2006), building on his "Coase's Penguin, or, Linux and the Nature of the Firm" (*Yale Law Journal*, 2002), establishes that non-market coordination is a real and productive mode, which he calls commons-based peer production, characterized by "weak property rights, an emphasis on intrinsic rather than extrinsic (monetary) rewards, and the exploitation of dispersed, tacit knowledge." Benkler's key claim for this brief is motivational: participants in peer production are driven primarily by "hedonic and social-psychological rewards" rather than the profit motive, and the networked environment increases "the relative salience of social sharing and exchange as a modality of economic production." Benkler shows that +N coordination does not require wages to motivate it. **It requires that the coordinator's survival not depend on wages earned elsewhere**. This is the distinction between motivation and substrate that the whole brief turns on: Benkler proves the motivation exists; the retention-logic analysis explains why the motivation cannot express itself while the +M substrate captures the hours.

`documented-fact` Elinor Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* (Cambridge University Press, 1990) establishes that commons governance is possible without either state or market, through self-organized institutions, and, critically for this brief, that such governance is not free. Ostrom insists on accounting for "the total of production costs, transaction costs, and enforcement costs," and warns that "a focus on production costs alone leads to a narrow interpretation of efficiency." Her design principles require monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict-resolution mechanisms, and collective-choice arrangements, all of which consume participant time and attention. Ostrom's "Reformulating the Commons" (2002) notes that group size "may well exacerbate the problems of self-governing systems" because of the transaction costs of arriving at acceptable allocation formulas. The Ostrom point for RPI is that the time and attention costs of commons governance are real and recurring, and someone has to bear them. **In an unfunded commons, they are borne by volunteers in their residual hours, which is why they are chronically undersupplied**.

`documented-fact` The undersupply shows up empirically as coordinator burnout in exactly the movements that attempt +N coordination without funding the coordination role. The Transition Towns movement, founded by Rob Hopkins in Totnes in 2006, has generated a substantial internal literature on volunteer and coordinator burnout, and the movement's own reflections identify unpaid coordination labor as a recurring failure point where initiatives stall when key volunteers exhaust their residual hours. Platform cooperativism, theorized by Trebor Scholz and Nathan Schneider (*Ours to Hack and to Own*, 2016), confronts the same problem from the digital-labor side and argues that the coordination work of running a cooperative platform must be compensated or it will not be sustained. These movements are natural experiments in what happens when +N coordination is attempted on residual hours: it burns out the coordinators.

`documented-fact` The Mondragon cooperative complex in the Basque Country, founded in 1956, is the largest and longest-running demonstration that governance labor can be compensated inside a cooperative structure rather than left to residual hours. Mondragon governs through the General Assembly, the Governing Council, and the Social Council, and its member cooperatives pay for the coordination and governance functions as part of the cooperative's operating structure, with wage-solidarity ratios constraining the spread between highest and lowest paid. Mondragon shows that a federated structure can internalize the cost of governance labor. The BioConomy's wiki concept of [[concepts/federated-cooperative-supply-chains|Federated Cooperative Supply Chains]] draws directly on this lineage. Mondragon is the existence proof that coordination roles can be funded from within a value-retaining structure rather than depending on external grants for projects.

`academic-inference` The synthesis is that the literature already contains every element RPI needs except their combination. Atkinson supplies the participation condition, Benkler supplies the proof that non-market motivation is real and productive, Ostrom supplies the accounting for governance time, and Mondragon supplies the demonstration that governance labor can be internally funded. What none of them supplies, and what RPI adds, is the denomination of the coordinator's income in a demurrage currency that cannot be accumulated as +M capital, which is the move that changes the substrate rather than merely funding a role within it.

### 6. The empirical case: the three-month Overberg record

`documented-fact` This case is offered as illustration, not as the argument. The argument stands on the structural analysis in sections 2 through 5. The case shows what the analysis predicts.

`documented-fact` A single coordinator in the Overberg region of the Western Cape, South Africa, received a stipend for three months beginning July 2026. During that period, with no other change in capability or resources, the following coordination outputs were produced. 

- First, a Quartz-based public wiki at wiki.bioconomy.earth, with a structured glossary (the machine-readable index describes 118 controlled-vocabulary entries as of retrieval, grown from an earlier 57-page state), structured frontmatter, epistemic-status tagging, and an extraction pipeline for ongoing population, machine-readable via llms.txt. 
- Second, a published high school decision tool at hs.valleyofgrace.co.za mapping ten variables for family decision-making, with its own llms.txt endpoint. 
- Third, a position paper and accreditation pathway of 15,000 to 25,000 words for a Waldorf project-based-learning high school, covering Western Cape Education Department registration, Umalusi accreditation, school-based assessment design, teacher qualification, and financial modeling. 
- Fourth, a historical research thread connecting Comenian pedagogy to the valley's Moravian educational lineage, corresponding to the wiki's Comenius-to-Schmidt Chain research brief. 
- Fifth, a meeting with Rotary Hermanus that shifted external perception of the valley from a recipient of development support to a contributor, reframing the valley as "needed" rather than "needy," which is the transition the wiki essay "[[from-bioregion-to-bioregion|From a Bioregion to a BioRegion]]" describes.
- `documented-fact` Sixth, a series of strategic engagements with the Transformasie Komitee, deepening operational understanding of the unresolved TRANCRAA process over Farm 39 (4,800 hectares, with attached water rights, 26 years unresolved). 
- Seventh, the convening of the Ward 2 Development Forum (W2DF), a new institutional body, on September 16, 2026, with municipal counterpart engagement. 
- Eighth, a planning document for an Ushahidi-based community engagement platform with eight organizational categories, governance protocols, fact-status tagging, and a phased rollout through January 2027. 
- Ninth, a domain-portfolio scan of .earth top-level-domain registrations across the bioregional movement. 
- Tenth, a three-corpus llms.txt architecture making the coordinator's published output machine-readable and attachable to any future AI research session. 

None of these ten outputs generates market income. All of them are coordination infrastructure. None would have been produced if the coordinator had spent those months earning a wage. None were pre-planned, and they were not deliverables envisaged at the beginning of the experiment; they all emerged through ongoing engagement in the valley.

`structural-inference` The case exhibits the predicted signature of a substrate change rather than a motivation change. The coordinator's capability and resources did not change; only the wage claim on their hours was lifted. Ten distinct coordination outputs across pedagogy, land governance, municipal institution-building, and machine-readable knowledge infrastructure appeared in ninety days. A motivation account (the stipend made the coordinator want to coordinate more) cannot explain the volume or the diversity, because motivation does not manufacture the hours; it only directs them. The substrate account explains both: the hours existed all along and were captured by wage compulsion, and when the capture was suspended, the hours flowed into the coordination the person was already positioned and motivated to do. This is the corollary in the thesis, observed once. Section 10 states plainly what a single case can and cannot establish.

`academic-inference` The case also illustrates the compound-returns retention logic proposed for the +N form. The wiki makes the decision tool more legible; the llms.txt architecture makes both attachable to future research sessions and makes the content publicly available; the W2DF gives the Transformasie Komitee engagements an institutional home; the "needed not needy" reframe from the Rotary meeting changes the terms on which every subsequent engagement proceeds. Each output lowers the cost of the next. The coordinator is now retained by the coordination surface they built, which is the +N retention logic beginning to operate.

### 7. Transvestment and the stipend as substrate transfer

`documented-framework` [[transvestment|Transvestment]], in the wiki's usage, is "the structured, voluntary conversion of capital from extractive financial forms into regenerative commons wealth, crossing between value regimes rather than seeking return within the same regime." The term originated with [[people/theorists/bauwens-michel|Michel Bauwens]] in the P2P Foundation literature and was refined by [[people/theorists/bollier-david|David Bollier]] and Natasha Hulst in their 2025 essay on relationalized finance, where [[people/theorists/bollier-david|Bollier]] writes that transvestment "underscores that the transfer of money is executing a serious shift in the value-paradigm" and is "simultaneously an investment, gamble, donation, and solidarity statement." The defining feature is that value crosses from the +M regime of price into the +N regime of generativity, and does not expect a return in the original terms.

`documented-framework` The wiki documents five transvestment pathways (labeled A through E), set out in [[people/theorists/haupt-michael|Michael Haupt]]'s "Transvestment vs. Divestment" (Framer OS, Framework 8, June 2026). 
- Pathway one transfers land and productive assets into a Bioregional Commons Trust held in perpetuity on ninety-nine-year renewable ground leases modeled on the Community Land Trust. 
- Pathway two uses financial capital to capitalize the currency reserves of a Bioregional Financing Facility that backs a regenerative currency at full reserve. 
- Pathway three is patient capital endowing regenerative enterprises through interest-free or below-market loans repaid in bioregional currency. 
- Pathway four converts inheritance into commons endowment through intergenerational vehicles that pass an income stream to heirs in bioregional currency while the underlying capital is held perpetually by the commons trust. 
- Pathway five is the demurrage dividend, the slow-acting recirculation of a holding charge back into the commons. 

The prime real-world example the wiki cites is Yvon Chouinard's 2022 transfer of Patagonia into the Patagonia Purpose Trust and the Hold Fast Collective, announced with the line "Earth is now our only shareholder."

`working-hypothesis` The stipend that funded the three-month Overberg case is an instance of transvestment, and it fits none of the five documented pathways cleanly, which suggests a sixth pathway not yet described. The five existing pathways all move capital into assets, reserves, enterprises, estates, or currency holdings. They transvest stock. **The stipend transvests flow: it converts a stream of +M capital directly into a stream of +N coordinator-hours, without passing through an asset, an enterprise, or a currency reserve**. Call this the coordination-role pathway: value crosses from +M into +N by purchasing the release of a coordinator's time from wage compulsion, and the return is coordination infrastructure held in the commons. This pathway is distinct because its object is the substrate of the coordinator's own hours rather than any external asset, and because its yield is legibility and coordination capacity rather than land, currency, or enterprise equity.

`documented-fact` The philanthropic model closest to the coordination-role pathway is MacKenzie Scott's unrestricted giving, which is significant because it funds organizational capacity rather than restricted projects. Scott's giving has reached 26.3 billion dollars since 2019, with her 2025 round alone totaling 7.17 billion dollars to roughly 225 organizations (Yield Giving; CNBC, 13 December 2025), delivered as no-strings-attached grants "with few reporting requirements and no time limits" (Center for Effective Philanthropy). The Center for Effective Philanthropy's research finds these gifts "helped nonprofits build reserves, invest in talent, and dramatically increase impact." Scott's approach counters what Dan Pallotta calls the nonprofit "starvation cycle," in which restricted grants systematically underfund organizational infrastructure and talent. Scott's model demonstrates at scale that unrestricted funding of capacity, rather than restricted funding of projects, produces durable institutional strength. The coordination-role pathway is the bioregional, demurrage-denominated specialization of that insight: **fund the coordinator, not the project, and denominate the funding so it cannot re-accumulate as +M capital**.

`academic-inference` Social impact bonds and community development finance institutions are the +M-native attempts to fund coordination and social outcomes, and they illustrate by contrast what the coordination-role pathway avoids. A social impact bond keeps the value inside the +M regime: it prices a social outcome, contracts for it, and pays a financial return to investors when the outcome is achieved. The value definition remains price and return. **The coordination-role pathway, being a transvestment, abandons the return-in-original-terms and so escapes the +M value definition.** This is the same structural difference the wiki draws between [[glossary/t-z/transvestment|transvestment]] and [[impact-investing|impact investing]], where impact investing "keeps the underlying value definition (return on investment) intact" and therefore constitutes "a move inside the first regime" rather than a crossing.

### 8. The legibility problem

`documented-framework` **Coordination work is invisible to the +M form's accounting systems**. A coordinator who builds a wiki, convenes a forum, and shifts regional relationships has produced nothing that appears in GDP, employment statistics, or tax returns. James C. Scott's *Seeing Like a State* (Yale University Press, 1998) supplies the governing concept: states and other central authorities can act only on what they can see, and they make society "legible" by collapsing complexity into statistics, maps, and administrative categories. Scott's insight is that legibility is "a central problem in statecraft" and a precondition for any intervention, and that the functionary "sees the human activity that is of interest to him largely through the simplified approximations of documents and statistics." **What is illegible to the accounting system is, for practical purposes, invisible to it, and what is invisible cannot be funded.**

`structural-inference` This illegibility is itself a retention mechanism, and it is the mechanism that locks the wage-compulsion bottleneck in place. The argument runs in a loop. Because +N coordination work cannot be measured in +M terms, it cannot be funded in +M terms; because it cannot be funded in +M terms, coordinators must earn their subsistence in +M and coordinate in whatever hours remain; because they coordinate only in residual hours, the coordination surface stays thin and never becomes legible enough to attract +M funding. Illegibility and wage compulsion reinforce each other. The coordinator is retained in +M not only because +M holds their hours but because the coordination they would rather do is invisible to the system that could pay for it.

`working-hypothesis` The BioConomy wiki, the high school decision tool, and the three-corpus llms.txt architecture are legibility infrastructure, and this is their primary structural function. They make coordination work visible, attributable, and auditable. A wiki with structured frontmatter, epistemic-status tagging, and controlled vocabulary renders a body of coordination work inspectable by any human or machine. An llms.txt endpoint renders it attachable to any AI research session, so that the coordination surface becomes queryable and its claims checkable against their sources. This is legibility built from the bottom up by the coordinated, rather than imposed from the top down by the state, which is the inversion of Scott's high-modernist legibility. Scott's warning was that state-imposed legibility silences local expertise; bottom-up legibility infrastructure aims to make local expertise legible without surrendering it to a central authority. The wiki's [[concepts/carbon-silicon-partnership|Carbon-Silicon Partnership]] concept theorizes this as making place-based knowledge machine-readable so that it can travel between BioHubs while remaining anchored in place.

`documented-fact` The existing method for making coordination and social value legible in +M terms is Social Return on Investment (SROI), and its documented limitations show why it is insufficient. SROI, developed by the Roberts Enterprise Development Fund and stewarded by Social Value International, expresses social value as a monetized ratio of social value created per unit invested. The critical literature is consistent about its central weakness. Maier, Schober, Simsa, and Millner, "SROI as a Method for Evaluation Research: Understanding Merits and Limitations" (*Voluntas* 26, 2015), and Daniel Fujiwara, "The Seven Principle Problems of SROI" (Simetrica, 2015), document that SROI struggles to attach credible financial values to "soft outcomes," to establish the counterfactual, and to produce ratios comparable across interventions. The deeper critique, articulated by Farr and Cressey (2019), is that "SROI reduces social complexity to an economic ratio." SROI is legibility that translates +N value into +M terms, and the translation destroys the thing it measures, because it forces relational and generative value back into the price frame. The wiki's legibility infrastructure takes the opposite approach: it makes coordination legible on its own terms (as documented, auditable, attributable coordination outputs) rather than translating it into a monetary ratio.

`academic-inference` David Graeber's *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011) and his broader work on the relationship between measurement and value support the claim that quantification is never neutral. Measurement systems encode a theory of what counts, and the +M measurement system counts priced transactions. **Coordination work that produces no priced transaction is, to that system, valueless by construction, regardless of how much coordination it accomplishes.** The legibility problem is therefore not a technical gap that better metrics will close; it is a structural feature of measuring value as price. RPI's legibility infrastructure resolves this by making coordination outputs directly inspectable rather than by pricing them.

### 9. Design principles for Regenerative Participation Income

`documented-framework` RPI is a glossary entry in the wiki, currently undertheorized, and the wiki's own definition anchors the design. The wiki's author defines it in "The Six Inversions of the [[coercion-continuum|Coercion Continuum]]" (Framer OS, Framework 6, June 2026): "every resident of the bioregion receives a base income, in the bioregional currency, for doing the work the place actually needs: restoring soil, stewarding water, caring for children and elders, teaching, and monitoring the health of the ecosystem." The definition explicitly inverts wage compulsion, the third phase of the Coercion Continuum: "Right now the only work that pays is the work distant markets reward, and the work a place needs to stay alive comes last, if it gets paid at all. An RPI flips that order. It makes the regenerative work of the bioregion the work with the first claim on new money, the work the currency is issued to call into being." The following design principles follow from the retention-logic analysis and the wiki's definition together.

`working-hypothesis` First, a [[biohub|BioHub]] distributes RPI, not a state. The wiki defines a BioHub as "a coordination body that organizes distributed regenerative contributions within a living landscape into verifiable commitments the outside world can contract with." State distribution reintroduces the +I retention logic (legal duty, bureaucratic process) and Atkinson's administrative-verification problem at population scale. BioHub distribution keeps the scheme bounded to a defined coordination surface where verification is tractable, which is the condition Hiilamo (2022) identified as the one under which participation income's administrative hurdles prove surmountable.

`working-hypothesis` Second, RPI funds coordination roles, not individuals qua individuals. This is the sharpest distinction from UBI. UBI pays everyone unconditionally and, as the OpenResearch study showed, released hours default to leisure and private investment absent a coordination surface to flow into. RPI funds a person to hold a coordination role against a defined coordination surface, so the released hours have somewhere to go. The Overberg case shows the difference: the stipend did not merely relieve subsistence pressure; it funded a coordination role, and ten coordination outputs followed.

`working-hypothesis` Third, RPI makes outputs legible through coordination surfaces: wikis, published tools, convened forums, and llms.txt endpoints. Legibility is not an afterthought or a reporting burden; it is the mechanism that breaks the illegibility retention loop. The coordination surface is simultaneously the output, the audit trail, and the compound-returns engine of the +N retention logic. This inverts SROI: rather than translating coordination into a monetary ratio, RPI publishes the coordination itself in inspectable, attributable, machine-readable form.

`working-hypothesis` Fourth, RPI differs from UBI by changing the substrate rather than supplementing income. Every UBI experiment surveyed denominated its payment in national fiat currency and thereby operated inside the +M substrate, which is why they improved position within +M (SEED's full-time employment, Kenya's self-employment) without moving hours into +N. RPI is designed as a substrate intervention, and the substrate move is the denomination in a bioregional demurrage currency.

`working-hypothesis` Fifth, RPI differs from a wage because the labor is not sold and the coordinator chooses what to coordinate. A wage is a sale of labor under the direction of the buyer; the wage relation is the +M retention mechanism itself. RPI is closer to Gorz's guaranteed income supporting the sphere of autonomy: the coordinator is funded to coordinate what the place needs as they judge it, which is why the Overberg outputs span pedagogy, land governance, and municipal institution-building rather than executing a single funder's project specification. This autonomy is also what distinguishes RPI from a social impact bond, which contracts for a specified outcome and pays a return.

`working-hypothesis` Sixth, RPI relates to demurrage currency in a way that further weakens the +M retention logic. If RPI is denominated in a bioregional demurrage currency, the income cannot be accumulated as +M capital, because demurrage imposes a holding cost that pushes the money toward use and out of the store-of-value function. The wiki, drawing on Silvio Gesell's Freigeld and the 1932 Wörgl experiment (in which stamp-scrip circulated far faster than the national schilling), treats demurrage as the inversion of interest: "Holding costs you, so money moves toward use." The consequence for retention is structural. UBI paid in fiat can be saved, which means it can be converted into +M capital and re-enter the accumulation logic; RPI paid in demurrage currency cannot, which means it can only be spent into the bioregional economy, keeping both the coordinator and the value inside the +N circuit. Demurrage is what makes RPI a substrate change rather than an income supplement.

`working-hypothesis` Seventh, the minimum viable version is a single BioHub, a single stipended coordinator, a defined coordination surface, and a 90-day cycle with auditable outputs. The Overberg case is a proof of concept for exactly this configuration, minus the demurrage denomination, which the case did not yet include (the stipend was paid in conventional currency). The minimum viable RPI adds the demurrage denomination to the Overberg configuration and holds everything else constant: one coordinator, one coordination surface, ninety days, published and auditable outputs. This bounded design solves Atkinson's verification problem (the coordination surface is the verification), avoids the population-scale administrative burden, and produces a legible, replicable unit that other BioHubs can run, which is the wiki's stated objective of equipping any BioHub with the tools and skills they need.

`academic-inference` The design principles cohere around a single move: RPI attacks the wage-compulsion bottleneck at the point where it binds, which is the coordinator's own hours, and it does so with an instrument (demurrage-denominated, role-funding, legibility-producing) that cannot be re-absorbed into +M. This is why RPI is neither a variant of UBI nor a variant of wages. It is the substrate intervention that the retention-logic analysis identifies as necessary, whereas UBI is an income intervention that leaves the substrate intact and wages are the substrate's retention mechanism itself.

### 10. Open questions and research gaps

`structural-inference` This brief does not resolve the following, and states them plainly.

The +N retention logic is a working hypothesis with the thinnest evidentiary base in the entire schema. The claim that coordination infrastructure generates compound returns and becomes self-reinforcing is consistent with the increasing-returns literature (Arthur, David, Pierson) and with the Overberg case, but it has not been tested against a case where the coordination surface failed to compound. The falsifying observation would be a funded coordinator who produces coordination outputs that do not lower the cost of subsequent coordination and do not create relational obligations that retain them. Such cases likely exist and have not been sought.

The empirical case is a single, unblinded, self-documented instance. It cannot establish a rate, a counterfactual, or a causal claim that survives scrutiny. It is consistent with the substrate hypothesis and it is equally consistent with a highly capable and motivated individual who would have produced similar outputs under other conditions. The brief's structural argument does not depend on the case, which is why the case is offered as illustration. A genuine test requires the minimum viable RPI run across multiple coordinators and multiple bioregions, with pre-registered output definitions and a comparison condition.

The demurrage denomination is untested in the RPI context. The Overberg stipend was paid in conventional currency, so the specific claim that demurrage denomination prevents +M re-accumulation and thereby changes the substrate has not been observed even once. The Wörgl and Chiemgauer precedents show demurrage currencies circulate faster, but neither was used to fund a coordination role, so the interaction between demurrage denomination and coordinator retention is entirely inferential.

The verification problem that sank participation income at scale is only partially solved by bounding RPI to a coordination surface. The claim that the coordination surface is its own verification holds for outputs that are inherently publishable (wikis, tools, forums). It holds less well for coordination work that is relational and unpublishable, such as the perception shift achieved in the Rotary meeting or the trust built with the Transformasie Komitee. How to make relational coordination legible without either destroying it (the SROI failure) or leaving it unverifiable is unresolved.

The coordination-role transvestment pathway proposed in section 7 is a working hypothesis about a sixth pathway, and it has not been validated against the wiki's own five-pathway architecture by the framework's authors. Whether the release of coordinator-hours from wage compulsion is genuinely a distinct value-regime crossing, or a special case of pathway three (patient capital) applied to human time rather than enterprise, is a question the framework's maintainers should adjudicate.

Finally, the scaling question is open. Everything in this brief concerns a single coordinator and a single BioHub. Ostrom's warning that group size exacerbates the transaction costs of self-governance applies directly to any attempt to scale RPI from one coordinator to a coordinating population, and the wiki's own constellation and federation concepts ([[concepts/federated-cooperative-supply-chains|Federated Cooperative Supply Chains]], the constellation of bioregional bodies) are the proposed answer, but they are proposals, not demonstrations. The path from one stipended coordinator to a functioning [[quadriform]] coordination economy is the largest gap this brief leaves open.

## Recommendations

Staged and concrete, with the benchmarks that would change them.

**Stage 1 (0-90 days): Run the minimum viable RPI as a controlled replication of the Overberg configuration, with the one modification that makes it a substrate test.** Fund one coordinator through a [[biohub|BioHub]] for a single 90-day cycle against a defined [[coordination-surface|coordination surface]], and denominate the stipend in a bioregional [[demurrage|demurrage]] currency rather than conventional cash. Pre-register the coordination outputs expected (count, categories, publication venues) before the cycle begins, so that the result is falsifiable rather than narrated after the fact. Benchmark that would confirm the thesis: coordination-output volume and diversity comparable to the Overberg ten, produced with no capability or resource change. Benchmark that would falsify or weaken it: released hours defaulting to leisure or private income-seeking (the OpenResearch pattern), or outputs that do not compound (each requiring as much setup cost as the last).

**Stage 2 (90-270 days): Test the demurrage-retention claim directly.** Track whether the demurrage-denominated stipend is spent into the bioregional economy rather than converted to fiat and saved. Benchmark: if a material share leaks back into fiat savings, the demurrage denomination is not doing the substrate work claimed for it, and the design reverts to a conventional stipend plus a separate legibility mandate. If the stipend circulates locally and cannot re-accumulate as +M capital, proceed.

**Stage 3 (9-24 months): Replicate across three to five coordinators in at least two bioregions, with a comparison condition.** Include at least one coordinator funded in fiat and one in demurrage currency, to isolate the denomination effect from the role-funding effect. Benchmark that would justify scaling: consistent coordination-output production across coordinators, with the demurrage arm showing measurably weaker +M re-accumulation and stronger local circulation. Benchmark that would halt scaling: coordinator burnout reappearing (the Transition Towns pattern) despite funding, which would indicate the role definition, not the wage claim, is the binding constraint.

**Stage 4 (24+ months): Only then attempt federation.** Do not scale from one coordinator to a coordinating population until Ostrom's transaction-cost warning has been addressed with an explicit governance design for the constellation. Benchmark: governance-labor costs per coordinator should not rise faster than coordination output as the number of coordinators grows. If they do, the federation design is reproducing the +I [[concepts/retention-logic|retention logic]] it was meant to escape.

**Cross-cutting recommendation:** keep the empirical case in its proper place. Use it to illustrate the structural argument, never to carry it. Fund the pre-registered multi-coordinator test before making any public claim that RPI produces coordination at a rate motivation cannot explain, because that claim currently rests on a single self-documented instance.

## Caveats

- This brief mixes documented findings, structural inferences from the TIME Framework, and working hypotheses, and it tags each accordingly; readers should not read the `working-hypothesis` claims (the +N [[concepts/retention-logic|retention logic]], the sixth [[transvestment|transvestment]] pathway, the entire RPI design) as established. The empirical case is a single, unblinded, self-reported instance and cannot establish causation. 
- The [[bioconomy|BioConomy]] framework is explicitly early-stage and self-described as such by its author; its core RPI and [[demurrage|demurrage]] definitions were retrieved through the framework author's primary essays. Several forward-looking elements in the framework's surrounding literature (the "Great Taking" thesis, the timing of a predicted late-2026 financial dislocation, the WEF "own nothing" forecast) are speculative claims by the framework's author and its cited sources, not documented facts, and this brief does not endorse them. 
- The Mincome health finding is contested (Green 2021). The UBI experiments cited measured employment, income, and wellbeing well, and measured non-market coordination activity poorly or not at all, so the claim that they failed to move hours into +N is partly an argument from what was not measured. 
- Finally, the demurrage-denomination claim at the heart of the RPI design has never been observed in a coordination-funding context, so the single most important design proposition in this brief is entirely inferential pending the Stage 1 and Stage 2 tests above.
