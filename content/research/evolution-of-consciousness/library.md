This library holds 158 books, papers, talks and declarations on whether human consciousness has a history; its firmest ground is the dated record and the science of animal minds, and every part of the argument now has critics on file.

## Overview

For two centuries, writers in fields that seldom meet have argued that the way people perceive, remember, reason, and belong has changed in steps, and may still be changing. Palaeoanthropologists, psychologists, philosophers, historians, contemplatives, and Indigenous scholars have each said something about it. They rarely read each other.

This page gathers their work in one place, so a reader can check the sequence the network writes from: the [[concepts/major-evolutionary-transitions|Major Evolutionary Transitions]], five [[concepts/structures-of-consciousness|structures of consciousness]] (Mimetic, Magical, Mythic, Material, and Mycelial) joined by four transitions. A [[glossary/m-s/structure-of-consciousness|structure of consciousness]] is a shared way of experiencing self, time and world; the term is Jean Gebser's. The five names and the synthesis across them are Michael Haupt's, built on Roger Briggs, whose own stages are survival, magical, mythical, mental/material and integral/emerging [MS]. Every author in the library keeps their own terms.

The library grades the works. The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|companion grid]] grades the claims: it sets 93 of these sources against each step of the argument, and includes the critics.

## How the library was built

It began with two of Michael's Roam Research lists: 32 favourite reads on the evolution of consciousness, and 24 people bringing [[glossary/m-s/mycelial-consciousness|Mycelial Consciousness]] into business and life. Every author on the reading list has a row here. Works named on the second list (Terrence Deacon's two books and Suzanne Simard's) are included too, which makes 49 works from Michael's reading [MS]. A research run on 19 November 2026 checked each record against publisher, library and journal pages, then added the works the field treats as central, and looked as hard for critics as for supporters.

Each work has its own page in the [[sources/index|sources]] register, and the tables below are drawn from those pages. Each work carries four codes.

**Tier.** What the work contributes to this field.

| Tier     | Meaning                                                                                  | Works |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| canon    | Sets out a sequence or a structure of consciousness that subsequent research builds upon | 43    |
| evidence | Supplies data a claim rests on                                                           | 30    |
| critic   | Disputes a sequence, a step or a source                                                  | 41    |
| practice | Applies the ideas                                                                        | 6     |
| context  | Frames the field                                                                         | 38    |

**Evidence type.** The design behind the work.

| Code | Type | Works |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Measured study, with replication | 14 |
| E2 | Dated record (fossil, archaeological or documentary) | 3 |
| E3 | Theory built on reviewed evidence | 52 |
| E4 | Synthesis, framework or speculation | 80 |
| E5 | Tradition or testimony | 9 |

E5 is a separate kind of knowledge and should not be rated as lower worth than any of the others merely because it isn't deemed to be 'scientific' by academics. The table records which evidence type each work emerges from, so readers can see it.

**Claims.** These are the main parts of the overall argument. The table classifies each of the works according to one or more of these claims.

| Code | Claim                                                                            |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A1   | Minds evolved: awareness exists in graded forms across many animal lineages      |
| A2   | Human cognition changed in identifiable, datable steps                           |
| A3   | The steps changed the structure of awareness itself, beyond what people believed |
| B1   | Individuals develop through measurable stages                                    |
| B2   | Culture and person follow parallel sequences                                     |
| B3   | Complexity has a direction, and consciousness rides it                           |
| C1   | Earlier structures stay present in everyone                                      |
| C2   | A new structure is emerging now                                                  |
| C3   | A sign of the new structure is networked, collective mind                        |
| C4   | The current structure resists the one that's emerging                            |
| H    | Consciousness is fundamental and isn't produced by human brains                  |
| H2   | Some information reaches minds by routes current science has not explained       |

**Stance.** For each claim, a provisional lean: `a` affirms, `q` qualifies, `d` disputes. On the 93 works in the companion grid, the stance codes follow the grid's reading of each step; on the other works they remain the first run's provisional leans.

### Contested works

Four authors make central claims that most specialists in their fields reject: Julian Jaynes, Rupert Sheldrake, David Hawkins and Graham Hancock. Each page states the claim, then the main rejection and any reply, without a verdict. Ned Block's 1981 review of Jaynes, for example, is answered by the Julian Jaynes Society [IC].

Hawkins's Map of Consciousness rests on [[glossary/a-e/applied-kinesiology|applied kinesiology]], a muscle-testing method; double-blind tests in 1988 and 2014 found its results no better than chance [IC]. Hawkins's Map places nations and groups on its scale, and Spiral Dynamics assigns colours to cultures. The library records that these works do so and repeats none of the placements. No sequence in this library may be used to place a people, a community or a person behind or ahead of anyone else.

### The mycelial metaphor

The network uses fungal networks as a metaphor for a form of coordination. Simard's *Finding the Mother Tree* (2021) is evidence about fungi. Karst, Jones and Hoeksema (2023) report that unsupported claims about [[glossary/a-e/common-mycorrhizal-network|common mycorrhizal networks]] in forests have doubled in 25 years [IC]. Their finding bears on the biology. It leaves the metaphor where it was.

## Coverage tests

The library was checked against five tests.

1. **Supporters and critics for every claim.** Each claim needs at least three works in support and two critics or qualifiers. Met for every claim. C4 was the last to pass, with five critics or qualifiers after the critics added on 19 November 2026; C1 now has six, two of them dissents. H2, added on 21 November 2026, has thirteen works in support and twenty-seven critics or qualifiers. B3 has twenty-six critics or qualifiers since 22 November 2026, when two critics of the tribe as a stage of social evolution (Fried, 1975; Yoffee, 2005) joined the library.
2. **Every discipline covered.** At least three works per discipline. Met, except African philosophy of personhood, which has two (Ramose and Menkiti).
3. **Africa.** The dated record includes Blombos Cave, where Henshilwood and colleagues (2018) report a 73,000-year-old drawing on silcrete [IC], and the Rising Star cave system, where the claim that *Homo naledi* buried its dead (Berger and colleagues, 2025) remains disputed: one eLife reviewer found it convincingly shown, and another judged the evidence incomplete [IC]. Pinnacle Point and Wonderwerk Cave are still to be added.
4. **Indigenous knowledge in its own voice.** Tyson Yunkaporta, Gregory Cajete, Robin Wall Kimmerer and the South African San Council's *San Code of Research Ethics* (2017). Vine Deloria Jr. and San scholars' own publications are still to be added.
5. **Who wrote the works.** Counted from author lists, as an inference: 13 of the 116 works have at least one woman author (11%), and 10 have at least one author based outside Europe and North America (9%). The eight critics added for C1 and C4 include no woman author and one author outside Europe and North America (Corballis, in New Zealand). For the 49 works from Michael's reading, the figures are 4 (8%) and 1 (2%). These counts cover the 116 works in the library before 21 November 2026; the works added since were not recounted. All eleven works added on 22 November 2026 after the TIMN research run are by men based in North America (inference from author lists).

Also thin: critics of measured adult stages; a direct test of the strong form of C1; any peer-reviewed critique of the metacrisis framing; the validation studies for adult-development instruments; Thaddeus Metz on Ubuntu; and Piaget, Francis Heylighen and Patricia Churchland. The library holds 158 works against a target of 120 to 200.

## Provenance

Built on 19 November 2026 from a Claude deep research run (*The Evolution of Consciousness Library*, Run 1 of 2) on Michael Haupt's two seed lists [MS].

Editorial decisions at filing. Titles were restored to their published punctuation. Deacon's two books and Simard's book were marked as works from Michael's reading, because his thought-leaders list names them; this raised the seed count from the run's 46 to 49, and the seed-list shares in the coverage tests were recounted on that basis. Three works already had source pages (Gebser, *The Ever-Present Origin*; Beck and Cowan, *Spiral Dynamics*; Popper, *The Poverty of Historicism*); the library fields were added to those pages, and 92 new source pages were created. The run's types were mapped to the wiki's: edited volumes are filed as books, Menkiti's chapter as an essay, and the two talks as video. Grades are the run's; most reception notes remain [TBV], which is why this page is a stub.

The run reached its search limit before checking several publishers, page ranges and dates; each is marked [TBV] on its page. The run also built a register of the people on Michael's lists; it isn't published here, because most practitioners on it are not yet confirmed from public sources.
