The universe has a direction, and the overall trend is towards wholeness.

This is step T6 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "The universe has a direction". The connecting word from the step before is *so*.

## Evidence

Evidence type: Argument and tradition

Partly coded: a grid step covers part of the claim.

Drawn from grid step C2, a new structure is emerging, with B3 for the direction itself. All 27 sources on this step are now coded directly: 8 affirm, 11 qualify, 5 dissent and 3 are silent. On part (a), Teilhard, Aurobindo, Wilber, McIntosh, Beck and Cowan, Bache, the Ra Material and Smuts hold that the universe, life or history has a direction. Monod (1970) and Medawar (1961) deny it, as do Gould's Wonderful Life (1989) and Full House (1996), and Butterfield (1931) for the writing of history. Popper (1957) denies a law of historical development and accepts that trends exist, and Jaspers (1949) holds one origin and goal of mankind as an article of faith; both qualify. On part (b), wholeness as a trend now has a direct statement in print: Smuts's Holism and Evolution (1926) holds that the universe moves towards ever deeper wholeness. Teilhard's Omega and Wilber's differentiation and integration affirm it in their own terms. Bohm (1980) holds wholeness to be a feature of reality and claims no trend; Gebser, Barfield, Kauffman, Briggs and Schmachtenberger qualify. Daoist scholarship reads the movement of the way as return and transformation with no end named, so Lau (1963) and Ziporyn (2009) qualify, and Ruse (1996) traces belief in evolutionary progress to cultural ideas of Progress. Donald, Habermas and Ong are silent. The strongest affirming works are Smuts on part (b) and Teilhard on both parts; the strongest dissent is Monod (1970). Smuts's book has few admirers today, and historians dispute its ties to his segregationist politics.
