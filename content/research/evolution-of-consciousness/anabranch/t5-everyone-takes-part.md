Every individual takes part in this process, consciously or unconsciously. Curricula that teach it, such as Montessori's cosmic education and the Big History Project, have reached a minority of schools.

This is step T5 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "The universe has a direction". The connecting word from the step before is *and*.

## Evidence

Evidence type: Argument

Partly coded: a grid step covers part of the claim.

Grid step B1, individuals develop through stages, is the nearest coded claim. All 19 sources on this step are now coded directly: 2 affirm, 11 qualify, 3 dissent and 3 are silent. Taking part in a cosmic process is a stronger claim than developing through stages. On part (a), Berry (1988) and Swimme and Tucker (2011) affirm, both read from summaries or descriptions. Wilber, McIntosh and Smuts (1926) hold that the individual expresses a larger evolutionary process and say nothing on part (b), so they qualify, as do Briggs and Sahtouris. Loevinger, Kegan, Beck and Cowan, and Jung's account of individuation describe development with no cosmic claim, and qualify; Tomasello, Siegler, and Fischer and Bidell are silent. Monod (1970) dissents on (a), holding that humans arose by chance in a nature without purpose; Dundes (2005) and Segal (1991) dissent against Campbell's claim of a hero story shared by every culture. On part (b), Debs and colleagues (2022) count 15,763 Montessori schools worldwide, and reports put the Big History Project in 1,200 to over 3,000 schools. Montessori's Cosmic Education (1948) and the Big History Project (2011) qualify; Conrad (2019), which finds the Big History curriculum Eurocentric, does not take up the step. Part (b) was reworded after run 7a, at Michael Haupt's decision, and these two works were coded against the earlier wording. The fullest statement of part (a) in print is Smuts (1926); the strongest dissent is Monod (1970).
