Now the two streams are learning to work side by side, rooted in place and connected across the planet, and together they can create the conditions for thriving. [[glossary/a-e/bioregionalism|Bioregional]] and [[glossary/a-e/cosmo-local-production|cosmo-local]] commons are two of the forms this takes on the ground.

This is step T9 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "The Anabranch". The connecting word from the step before is *pointing towards*. The [[glossary/a-e/confluence|Confluence]] is the second of the two moments of the [[glossary/a-e/anabranch|Anabranch]].

## Evidence

Evidence type: Theory on evidence (E3), with national policy (E2)

Partly coded: no grid step tests this claim, so sources are coded on it directly, each against the wording above.

No grid step tests this, so every source is coded on it directly. Forty-three sources are coded: 10 affirm, 27 qualify, 4 dissent and 2 are silent. Thirty-one were coded against the wording of 22 November 2026. Twelve keep codes made against earlier wordings and await rereading: Odora Hoppers, Kimmerer, Yunkaporta, Alexander, Purcell and Brown, the South African IKS policy, Ronfeldt's 2026 paper and post, Massey, Escobar, Tsing and Harvey; the affirmations of Odora Hoppers (2002) and Kimmerer (2013) are among them. On part (a), every affirming work coded against the new wording comes from authors close to the BioConomy network: Kostakis and colleagues (2015, 2018), the Cosmo-Local Reader, Bauwens (2026), Bauwens and Pazaitis (2019), Life (2026), Brewer (2021) and Sahtouris. Works independent of that circle join scales in other terms and qualify. Ostrom (2010) and Quilligan (2026) join levels of governance; Robertson (1995) and Swyngedouw (1997) describe the global and the local made together through power; Benkler (2006) and Ronfeldt and Arquilla (2020) join knowledge at planetary scale with no grounding in place. The Two-Eyed Seeing works join Indigenous and Western knowledge in their own terms and qualify; none frames the joining as place-held and globally shared knowledge. On part (b), support comes as design, hope or expectation. Born and Purcell (2006) dissent, holding that no scale is better in itself, and Nadasdy (1999) finds that integrating traditional knowledge moves power to administrative centres. On part (c), both named forms are documented in print, and sources name others: polycentric governance, planetary subsidiarity, networked commons days and peer-for-peer federation. The strongest affirming work is Life (2026); the strongest dissent is Born and Purcell (2006); the main independent critique of cosmo-localism is Liodaki et al. (2025). Two readings of the streams were also tested. On abstraction from place, five works affirmed (Scott, Porter, Escobar, Life, the Cosmo-Local Reader), four qualified and none dissented. On place-based and globally shared knowledge being joined, seven affirmed and nine qualified, most of the affirming works from authors close to the network.
