The universe and everything within it strives towards higher expressions of itself. Some call this building complexity. Others call the guiding force God, and others the Evolutionary Impulse.

This is step T4 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "The universe has a direction".

## Evidence

Evidence type: Measured for the rise in complexity; argued for the striving

Partly coded: a grid step covers part of the claim.

Drawn from grid step B3. All 28 sources on this step are now coded directly: 8 affirm, 14 qualify, 4 dissent and 2 are silent. The rise in complexity is measured (Chaisson) and modelled (Prigogine, Kauffman, Maynard Smith and Szathmáry); these qualify the step, since none claims that the universe strives. Each of the step's names for the guiding force has a printed source: Teilhard's complexity-consciousness, Aurobindo's Supermind, Wilber's Eros, Laszlo's A-field, and Holism, the whole-making tendency Smuts named in 1926. Nagel (2012) proposes a teleology in nature, and Sober (2012) rejects his argument. Monod (1970), Medawar (1961) and Meyerhoff (2010) dissent, against any directing force in evolution. Gould's Full House (1996) and Popper's Poverty of Historicism (1957) allow trends in some systems while denying a built-in direction, and qualify. Yunkaporta's Sand Talk critiques progress narratives in human history and takes up no striving in the universe. The scholarly works on building complexity do not equate it with striving, so the sentence that names building complexity groups them under a claim they do not make. The strongest affirming work is Teilhard (1955); the strongest dissent is Monod (1970). The affirming squares for Aurobindo, McIntosh and Wilber rest on summaries.
