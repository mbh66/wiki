Everything in the universe strives towards higher expressions of itself. Some call the guiding force God; others call it the Evolutionary Impulse.

This is step T4 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "Part 2: The Universe is not random".

## Evidence

Evidence type: Measured for the rise in complexity; argued for the striving

Partly coded: a grid step covers part of the claim.

Drawn from grid step B3. On 25 November 2026 a further 150 sources were coded silent from their source pages alone, graded [TBV], for works that never take up the step; 186 sources now carry a code here, and 17 works await a research run. All 35 sources on this step are now coded directly: 8 affirm, 12 qualify, 6 dissent and 9 are silent. Since 24 November 2026 the step no longer names building complexity. On 25 November 2026 Anabranch run 9 reread the ten squares that rested on it, and confirmed the other 25 at their positions. The rise in complexity is measured (Chaisson) and modelled (Prigogine, Kauffman, Maynard Smith and Szathmáry); these qualify the step: they accept that higher forms arise and claim no striving. Fantappiè's syntropy (1942), a law of finality in living systems only, qualifies on scope, and Feinberg and Mallatt (2016) and Ginsburg and Jablonka (2019) qualify on the natural rise of consciousness. Each of the step's names for the guiding force has a printed source: Teilhard's complexity-consciousness, Aurobindo's Supermind, Wilber's Eros, Laszlo's A-field, and Holism, the whole-making tendency Smuts named in 1926. Nagel (2012) proposes a teleology in nature, and Sober (2012) rejects his argument. Monod (1970), Medawar (1961) and Meyerhoff (2010) dissent, against any directing force in evolution. Gould's Full House (1996) now dissents too: it argues that the rise of complexity in life is a passive spread from a wall of simplicity, with no drive. Popper's Poverty of Historicism (1957) denies any law of evolution and treats belief in unconditional trends as a mistake, which counts as a dissent under the rule for this step. Yunkaporta's Sand Talk critiques progress narratives in human history and takes up no striving in the universe. Ronfeldt's TIMN paper (1996) treats rising complexity as a social process, warns against teleology and is silent on the step. Six of his later works are silent too, from his 2007 paper on the tribal form to his 2026 noosphere writing: each treats the rise of complexity as social or noospheric and claims no striving universe. The strongest affirming work is Teilhard (1955); the strongest dissent is Monod (1970). The affirming squares for Aurobindo, McIntosh and Wilber rest on summaries.
