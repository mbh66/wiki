The brain filters consciousness from the field and does not produce it. The thesis adds that environment, education and enthusiasm shape what comes through.

This is step T3 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "Part 1: Consciousness is non-material". The connecting word from the step before is *so*.

## Evidence

Evidence type: Theory. The three Es are Michael Haupt's synthesis [MS].

Partly coded: a grid step covers part of the claim.

Grid step H covers the claim that brains do not produce consciousness. On 25 November 2026 a further 155 sources were coded silent from their source pages alone, graded [TBV], for works that never take up the step; 189 sources now carry a code here, and 14 works await a research run. James's Human Immortality (1898), Bergson's Matter and Memory (1896) and Myers's Human Personality (1903) are the primary filter texts, Huxley's reducing valve (1954) is its best-known modern statement, and Kelly and colleagues (2007) defend the filter model. Kastrup (2019), REBUS, McGilchrist (2021), James's Varieties, Aurobindo, Chalmers and Webb (2023) qualify it; Webb questions how Huxley built the valve from Bergson and Broad. Augustine and Martin (2015), Churchland (2013), Lamont (1935), Paul Edwards's Immortality (1992), Lewis-Williams, Seth, Dennett, Deacon, Feinberg and Mallatt, and Ginsburg and Jablonka hold that brains give rise to consciousness. No critic found so far presses the point that the founders of filter theory gave analogies and no mechanism. Run 7a checked the last six positions copied from H. Bache (2019) affirms, on testimony read through reviews. Wilber (2000) and Barfield (1957) give idealist accounts with no filtering brain, and The Embodied Mind (1991) sits outside both models; all three qualify. Jaynes (1976) treats consciousness as a learned operation of the brain and dissents, and Gebser is silent. Run 7a found no evidence on the three Es in any work it read.
