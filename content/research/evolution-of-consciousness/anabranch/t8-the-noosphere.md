As growth pursued at any cost meets its [[sources/meadows-limits-to-growth|limits]], where the [[glossary/m-s/s-curve|S-curve]] levels off, the thesis holds, in its own reading of Teilhard's [[glossary/m-s/noosphere|Noosphere]], that the individual can learn to draw on the wisdom of the universe to grow towards wholeness.

This is step T8 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "The Anabranch". The connecting word from the step before is *so*.

## Evidence

Evidence type: Argument and tradition

Partly coded: a grid step covers part of the claim.

All 24 sources on this step are now coded directly against the wording of 22 November 2026: none affirms, 18 qualify, 1 dissents and 5 are silent. On part (a), three works make the charge of growth pursued regardless of its costs in their own terms: Sahtouris (EarthDance, ch. 20), The Limits to Growth and Schmachtenberger (2022). Bauwens and Pazaitis (2019) fault accounting that assumes infinite material growth on a finite planet. Smil (2019), Tainter (1988), Modis (2002) and Korotayev (2020) hold that growth levels off, or meets declining returns, without that charge. So does Ronfeldt (1996), who holds each form of organisation self-limiting and warns that a realm expanding unchecked brings out its worst. His 2007 paper holds each form self-limiting in the same way, and his 2026 noosphere paper holds that S-shaped curves govern growth in every sphere, citing Michael Haupt's briefs for the point. The Limits to Growth allows overshoot and collapse as well as a smooth levelling. On part (b), no source places the Noosphere at a point on the curve. Teilhard (1955) and Vernadsky (1945) describe a collective sphere of thought, and neither has an individual drawing on it. Arquilla and Ronfeldt (1999), Ronfeldt and Arquilla (2020) and Ronfeldt's 2026 noosphere writing describe a collective realm of mind, led in the 2026 works by AI entities, with no individual drawing on it, and qualify. Bache (2019), read in the publisher's excerpt, describes the individual dissolving into wider fields of consciousness as his sessions moved from personal to collective transformation. That speaks to part (b) in his terms and moves its aim from the individual to the species, so he qualifies; his sessions are testimony whose source cannot be checked. Medawar's review of Teilhard (1961), read in the journal copy, dissents. The wording now states the reading of the Noosphere as the thesis's own. Levin (2022), Simard, Karst, Yunkaporta and Ronfeldt's 2025 series are silent on the step. The strongest qualifying work is The Limits to Growth; the strongest dissent is Medawar (1961).
