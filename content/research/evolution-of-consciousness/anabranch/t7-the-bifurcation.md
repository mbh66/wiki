Over the last 12,000 years, from the first farms to the scientific revolution, abstract thinking branched away from grounded, place-based thinking. Place-based thinking carried on as [[glossary/m-s/municipal-consciousness|Municipal Consciousness]]. The thesis reads [[glossary/m-s/material-consciousness|Material Consciousness]] as the force behind the steep climb of the [[glossary/m-s/s-curve|S-curve]].

This is step T7 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]], Michael Haupt's position [MS], in the part headed "Part 3: Two streams of thinking, rejoining". The connecting word from the step before is *together*. The [[glossary/a-e/bifurcation|Bifurcation]] is the first of the two moments of the [[glossary/a-e/anabranch|Anabranch]].

### The Garden of Eden reading, outside the claim

The thesis reads the garden as its own image of the Bifurcation [MS], and the works coded here read the story in their own terms. Barr (1992) reads Genesis 2 and 3 as the loss of a brief chance at immortality, with death natural from the start, and finds the idea of a Fall unneeded. Fretheim (1994) reads the first sin as mistrust and keeps fall language for a broken relationship with God, while denying that death entered with it. Trible (1978) reads an earth creature not yet marked as male or female, and treats male rule as an outcome of sin in a love story gone awry. Meyers (2013) reads the story against the hard labour and many pregnancies of Iron Age highland farm households, with Eve as a strong partner. Kimmerer (2013) sets Skywoman's fall and her gifts beside Eve's exile from a garden. Quinn (1996), in one reviewer's account, ties the coming of farming to the Fall, as a change of mind rising out of a way of life. Earlier runs recorded readings of the story in Eisler (1987), in Sahtouris's rewritten Genesis and in Graeber and Wengrow (2021); run 9 did not reread them. None of these works reads the garden as a split in thought or as a growth curve (inference).
