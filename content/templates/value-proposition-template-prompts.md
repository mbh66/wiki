The runnable text of the [[templates/value-proposition-template|BioConomy Value Proposition Template]], version 0.3. A BioHub cohort uses it to produce the Value Proposition Statement, the Value Proposition Evidence Pack and the [[tender-compact|Tender Compact]], populated through the [[value-proposition-canvas-bioconomy|BioConomy Value Proposition Canvas]]. Read the template page first for what the template is for and where it sits in the five-template [[templates/index|founding suite]], and [[templates/running-a-template|Running a Template]] for the conventions every template shares.

Copy each prompt's context and task into a deep-research AI platform with the listed documents attached. Replace the words in square brackets with your own. Do not run a prompt until the cohort work before it is done.

## When to Run This Template

Run this template after the BioHub Identity Template and the BioRegion Establishment Template have both been completed. The BioHub should have:

- The three Identity Template outputs (Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, Founding Compact) produced and accepted by the cohort.
- The three BioRegion Template outputs (Definition, Atlas, Charter) produced and accepted by the cohort.
- Cohort agreement that the BioHub is ready to move from establishment work to tendering work.

Do not run this template as a discovery exercise for a BioHub still forming or still establishing its BioRegion. Run it when the establishment work is complete and the cohort needs a coherent value proposition to bring to counterparties and community.

## Prerequisite Documents to Attach

Attach to every submission:

1. **Three outputs from the BioHub Identity Template:**
   - Identity Statement.
   - Field and Lineage Positioning.
   - Founding Compact.
2. **Three outputs from the BioRegion Establishment Template:**
   - BioRegion Definition.
   - BioRegion Atlas.
   - BioRegion Charter.
3. Your BioStack and BioConomy foundational document (definitions, retention versus throughput logic, dependency direction).
4. Your framework of coordination forms (TIME, or the framework you work within).
5. Your commitment pooling document (the Ruddick and Burgess-Bergstra basis, and your local adaptation).

The Metabolic BioHubs Best Practices Research Brief and other field-mapping documents are inherited through the Field and Lineage Positioning document. Prior essays and research establishing the BioHub's positioning are inherited through the Identity documents.

## User-Supplied Inputs to Gather

Most inputs for this template are inherited from the six preceding documents. The coordinator should compile only the additions and refinements below:

**Inherited from the Identity Statement:**
- BioHub name, cohort, anchor location, purpose statement.
- Entity form and patronage architecture (used to structure how tenders are held and how revenues are received).

**Inherited from the Field and Lineage Positioning:**
- Global BioHub field baseline (used to compare tender structures with peer BioHubs).
- Intellectual lineage informing retention economics.
- Adjacent BioHubs (relevant to multi-BioHub tender coordination).

**Inherited from the Founding Compact:**
- Cohort structure and decision-making forms (used for cohort review steps).
- Patronage architecture and how capital is held.
- Multi-BioHub coordination protocols (used to structure tender coordination in a multi-BioHub context).

**Inherited from the BioRegion Definition:**
- Bioregion boundary.
- Coordination bodies recognized within the BioRegion.
- Jurisdictional overlays and regulatory context.
- Contested tenure and other structural constraints.

**Inherited from the BioRegion Atlas:**
- Ecological, hydrological, jurisdictional, cultural, institutional, infrastructural, and climate profiles (used to populate the Living Substrate panel and identify tenderable services).
- Water Retention Landscape baseline (used for water yield tenders).
- Soil carbon baseline (used for carbon sequestration tenders).
- Biodiversity inventory (used for biodiversity data tenders).
- Heritage anchor and cultural profile (used for heritage and tourism tenders).

**Inherited from the BioRegion Charter:**
- Governance principles (used to structure the Retention Guarantee).
- Consent and custodial principles (applied to cultural and heritage tenders).
- Multi-BioHub coordination protocols (refined for tender-level coordination).

**New user-supplied inputs to compile:**

- **Metropolitan counterparty focus.** The nearest city or metropolitan region whose water, energy, food, and biodiversity demands intersect with the BioRegion's productive capacity. Even where this is documented in the BioRegion Atlas, name the specific counterparty focus for this tender cycle.
- **Perceived counterparty challenges.** What the metropolitan area is currently stating as its infrastructure, resource, and resilience challenges. Cite the most current official planning documents, utility reports, and municipal strategy statements available.
- **Key institutional relationships in tender view.** Named partnerships with government agencies, conservation authorities, universities, philanthropic bodies, and corporate ESG buyers that are currently active or in negotiation.
- **Existing commitment pools and tender-relevant coordination.** Any commitment pools already established, any prior tenders submitted, any active contracts, and any pending negotiations.

## The Three Output Documents

The template's final deliverables are three separate documents produced in Prompt 3, each with a distinct purpose and audience. The tripartite structure parallels the Identity template's (Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, Founding Compact) and the BioRegion template's (Definition, Atlas, Charter).

1. **Value Proposition Statement.** A short, formal, foundational reference document. States what the BioHub tenders and why, populated from the six-panel BioConomy Value Proposition Canvas. Summarizes both the external face (counterparties and capital patrons) and the internal face (participants and community). This is what the BioHub uses to introduce its offer. Length: five to ten pages.

2. **Value Proposition Evidence Pack.** A long, structured referential document. Compiles the counterparty profiles, participant profiles, market signals, mechanisms, precedent cases, retention economics scaffolding, readiness diagnostic, and gap register. Structured for section-by-section reference use. This is the BioHub's operational knowledge base for engaging counterparties, structuring tenders, and briefing participants. Length: thirty to fifty pages.

3. **Tender Compact.** A governance and commitment document. Establishes what the BioHub commits to tender, on what retention terms, to which counterparties and participants. Includes multi-BioHub tender coordination protocols, sequencing of tenders, and provisions for tender revision, participant dissent, and tender dissolution. Co-signed by the cohort and, where applicable, by counterparty and participant representatives. Length: five to ten pages.

## The BioConomy Value Proposition Canvas

The template populates a six-panel canvas as the working structure for the Value Proposition Statement. The canvas is distinct from the Osterwalder Value Proposition Canvas. Osterwalder frames value as gain creators and pain relievers for a customer segment. The BioConomy canvas frames value as retention services tendered by a coordinated BioRegion into markets already structured to purchase them, with substrate health as the mandatory measurement.

The canvas has six panels:

1. **The Living Substrate.** What the BioRegion holds: watershed, soil, biodiversity, heritage, human capacity, coordination history. Populated from the BioRegion Atlas.

2. **The Coordination Layer.** What the BioHub holds that no participant holds alone. Stakeholders coordinated, commitments verified, pools operated, contracts held. Populated from the Founding Compact and BioRegion Charter.

3. **The Tenderable Services Portfolio.** The services the BioRegion can tender into markets, each with its retention logic and contractable form named. Six service categories are covered as a portfolio: water yield, carbon sequestration, biodiversity data, heritage and tourism, food systems, and coordination-as-employment. Coordination-as-employment has a dual character: it is both a service the BioHub offers to external counterparties (its convening and verification capacity) and an employment pathway for community members.

4. **The Counterparty Portfolio (external face).** The identified buyers of each service. Metropolitan utilities, corporate ESG buyers, philanthropic patrons via transvestment, tourism markets, food supply chains, research institutions.

5. **The Participant Portfolio (internal face).** The people whose commitments the BioHub coordinates. Landowners, farmers, custodial knowledge-holders, service providers, cooperative members, employees. What each contributes, what each receives, and how gainful employment is generated for those who want it.

6. **The Retention Guarantee.** How each service is designed so value circulates within the BioRegion, substrate function increases, and coordination-form capture is prevented (the Market colonizing the Emergent).

Where Osterwalder elements prove useful in specific BioHub contexts, they can be integrated inside Panel 4 (for example, in framing counterparty jobs, pains, and gains). The overall structure of the BioConomy canvas is not derived from Osterwalder and does not depend on his framework.

The Canvas populates the Value Proposition Statement's structure. It is not itself one of the three final outputs.

## Sequencing

- **Prompt 1** produces a first-pass draft value proposition, populated to the six-panel Canvas. The Canvas is the hypothesis grounded in the six preceding documents and the newly compiled counterparty inputs.
- **Between Prompts 1 and 2:** the coordinator convenes the cohort to review the draft and confirm which services and counterparties warrant deeper evidence work. This is cohort work under the decision-making forms established in the Founding Compact. It cannot be delegated to the AI.
- **Prompt 2** takes the Prompt 1 draft and builds the evidence pack. Deep research into counterparty profiles, participant profiles, market signals, mechanisms, precedent cases, and retention economics scaffolding. Also produces the readiness diagnostic and gap register.
- **Between Prompts 2 and 3:** the coordinator convenes the cohort to review the evidence pack, resolve any tensions, and confirm priority tenders and tender terms. This is also cohort work.
- **Prompt 3** produces the three final documents (Value Proposition Statement, Value Proposition Evidence Pack, Tender Compact), incorporating the cohort's resolutions.

Run the prompts in sequence, on the same platform where possible.

## Cross-Platform Notes

The prompts are written for portability across ChatGPT Deep Research, Claude Research, Perplexity Deep Research, and Gemini Deep Research.

- Claude and ChatGPT accept long submissions with attached documents. Submit the full prompt with prerequisites attached.
- Perplexity works best with tighter, more focused queries. Condense the prerequisite documents into a single summary and paste it inline before the prompt where full attachment is not possible.
- Gemini handles structured briefs well and supports document uploads through its research mode.
- Where a platform cannot carry context across sessions, manually attach the outputs of Prompts 1 and 2 to the subsequent submissions.

Prompt 3 produces three separate documents with a combined length that may exceed some platforms' output limits. Where a platform cannot produce all three in a single response, split Prompt 3 into three separate submissions (3a Value Proposition Statement, 3b Value Proposition Evidence Pack, 3c Tender Compact), each carrying the Prompt 2 evidence pack as an attached input.

The Value Proposition Evidence Pack is the largest output. Where the platform imposes tight output limits, produce it in structured sections and assemble them separately.

## Prompt 1: Draft the BioConomy Value Proposition

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am a coordinator with [BioHub name, as established in the Identity Statement]. Our BioHub operates within [BioRegion name, as established in the BioRegion Definition]. I am running the BioConomy Value Proposition Template, the third template in the suite. This is the first of three prompts. Its purpose is to produce a first-pass draft value proposition for the cohort to review before deeper evidence work begins.

The BioHub Identity Template and BioRegion Establishment Template have both been completed. I am attaching all six outputs (Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, Founding Compact, BioRegion Definition, BioRegion Atlas, BioRegion Charter) which supply the cohort structure, the BioRegion boundaries and profiles, the governance principles, and the multi-BioHub coordination protocols.

I work within the BioConomy framework, in which a BioHub is the innermost layer of the BioStack (BioHub → BioRegion → BioConomy), organized on retention logic (the opposite of throughput logic) and positioned as needed (the opposite of needy).

I am attaching the following foundational documents:

[List attached documents, including all six from preceding templates]

I am providing the following additional context (not already in the preceding documents):

- Metropolitan counterparty focus: [insert]
- Perceived counterparty challenges: [insert with citations to current public sources]
- Key institutional relationships in tender view: [insert]
- Existing commitment pools and tender-relevant coordination: [insert]

### Task

Draft a BioConomy Value Proposition for [BioHub name], populated to the six-panel BioConomy Value Proposition Canvas.

The value proposition covers the full portfolio of six services this BioHub can tender: water yield, carbon sequestration, biodiversity data, heritage and tourism, food systems, and coordination-as-employment. Coordination-as-employment includes gainful employment pathways for community members who wish to be gainfully employed. Treat this as an intended output of the coordination work, alongside the tenderable services in the other portfolio categories.

The draft is a hypothesis. Treat it as subject to challenge in Prompt 2. It should draw on the six preceding documents, the framework, and the new counterparty context to propose:

1. What the Living Substrate holds specifically for this BioRegion (populated from the BioRegion Atlas).
2. What the Coordination Layer holds that no single participant holds alone (populated from the Founding Compact and BioRegion Charter).
3. What services this BioHub can tender in each of the six portfolio areas, with retention logic named for each.
4. Who the counterparties are for each service (external face).
5. Who the participants are and what each contributes and receives (internal face).
6. How value circulation is retained within the BioRegion (aligned with the BioRegion Charter's governance principles).

### Output specification

Produce three components:

1. **The populated canvas.** Six panels, each with a paragraph or structured list. Total length between 1,500 and 2,500 words. Draw explicitly on the BioRegion Atlas for Panel 1 and the Founding Compact and BioRegion Charter for Panel 2.

2. **An executive statement.** One page, single voice, presenting the BioHub's value proposition as a coherent whole. This is the paragraph a coordinator would read out at a stakeholder meeting or send to a counterparty as first-contact material.

3. **A portfolio summary.** For each of the six service areas, a short paragraph naming: the service, the retention logic, the primary counterparty, the primary participant contribution, and the pricing basis (if any is known at this stage).

### Evidentiary discipline

Tag every substantive claim as one of:

- **IC** (Independently Corroborated) for claims supported by cited sources outside the BioHub's own documents.
- **MS** (Mission-Sourced) for claims drawn from the six preceding template outputs or other cohort documents.
- **TBV** (To Be Verified) for claims that are plausible but need Prompt 2 to substantiate.

Structural inferences (claims of the form "this pattern implies that outcome") should be explicitly labeled as inference. Do not attribute them to source authors.

### Constraints

- The purpose is a strategic hypothesis draft. Prose should be direct and structural, with evidence-tagged claims.
- Do not use the vocabulary of the conventional bioeconomy (sector growth, feedstock volume, biological substitution) except to distinguish the BioConomy from it.
- Do not conflate "needed" with "worthy." Needed means the counterparty market is already structured to purchase the service, or can be structured to do so within known regulatory and financial architecture.
- Do not fabricate specific counterparty names, currency figures, or precedent cases at this stage. That is Prompt 2's work.
- Do not restate content already documented in the six preceding template outputs. Cross-reference and build on, do not duplicate.
- Flag any panel where the preceding documents provide thin context, so the cohort knows what to gather before running Prompt 2.

## Prompt 2: Build the Evidence Pack

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am continuing the BioConomy Value Proposition Template for [BioHub name]. In the previous run I produced a draft BioConomy Value Proposition. The cohort has reviewed the draft and confirmed the following priorities for deeper evidence work:

- Services warranting deep evidence work in this cycle: [list]
- Counterparty types warranting deep research: [list]
- Any revisions to the draft canvas: [state]

I am now asking for the evidence pack that supports, extends, or challenges the draft.

Attached:

- [Prompt 1 output with cohort review notes]
- [Six preceding template outputs]
- [Original foundational documents]
- [Original new counterparty context]

### Task

For each service in the portfolio drafted in Prompt 1, produce:

1. **Counterparty identification.** Named organizations (utilities, corporate ESG buyers, family offices, government agencies, philanthropic institutions, research partners) that either already purchase this service or have publicly stated requirements the service would meet. Cite official strategy documents, ESG disclosures, utility planning documents, and grant frameworks.

2. **Market signal quantification.** Existing spending, published planning budgets, tender values, bond issuances, or grant volumes that establish the market's willingness to pay. Cite specific figures with source. If a market does not yet exist in mature form but is emerging (as with nature-related disclosure buyers under TNFD), identify the leading indicators.

3. **Contractable mechanism mapping.** The specific regulatory, financial, or contractual instrument through which the service can be delivered and paid for. Include Payment for Ecosystem Services instruments, performance-based bonds, ESG procurement frameworks, PES-eligible grant programs, offsetting frameworks, tourism levies, or novel instruments where none of the existing ones fit.

4. **Precedent case citation.** At least one documented case (nationally or internationally) of a comparable service being tendered under a comparable mechanism. Cite the case, the counterparty, and the outcome where known.

5. **Price-discovery evidence.** Where the service has an established price (per hectare, per megalitre, per ton of CO2, per data unit), cite it. Where no price exists, propose a pricing basis grounded in avoided cost, replacement cost, willingness to pay, or comparable transaction values.

6. **Retention economics scaffolding.** How this service is designed to increase substrate function while being tendered, drawing on the BioRegion Charter's governance principles. What monitoring and verification supports the retention claim. What safeguards prevent throughput capture.

Also produce:

- **External-face evidence.** Buyer research for each counterparty type. Patron research for capital partners considering transvestment or Mycelial Patronage arrangements (drawing on the patronage architecture established in the Founding Compact).
- **Internal-face evidence.** Gainful-employment pathway research (which of the six services generates what kind of work, what skills are required, what cooperative or employment forms are appropriate). Participant-benefit research (what each participant class receives from participating). Commitment-pool design considerations (drawing on Ruddick's Grassroots Economics framework and comparable precedents).
- **Multi-BioHub tender context.** Where more than one BioHub operates in the BioRegion (as documented in the BioRegion Definition), how tender coordination is structured. Which services might be tendered jointly, which independently, which through peer recognition arrangements.

Also produce the readiness diagnostic and gap register:

- **Readiness diagnostic.** For each service, categorize into: contractable now; contractable after specified build-out; speculative pending research. Specify what needs to be built for the second category and what research would move a service from the third to the second.
- **Gap register.** Structured list of the gaps identified. Each gap has: the service it constrains, the build-out or research required, an estimated timescale, and a proposed owner (the BioHub itself, a specific partner, or a to-be-identified research collaborator).

### Output specification

Produce, as separate sections in a single evidence pack:

1. **Service-by-service evidence dossier.** One section per service in the portfolio, structured to the six research categories above.
2. **External-face research brief.** Counterparty profiles and patron profiles.
3. **Internal-face research brief.** Employment pathways, participant benefits, commitment pool design notes.
4. **Multi-BioHub tender context.** Coordination protocols for tenders where more than one BioHub is active in the BioRegion.
5. **Readiness diagnostic.** Tabular where possible.
6. **Gap register.** Structured list.
7. **Cross-portfolio synthesis.** Two to three pages identifying which services carry strong evidence, which are supported by emerging evidence, and where the evidence base is currently thin.

Length: thirty to fifty pages depending on evidence available.

### Evidentiary discipline

Same IC / MS / TBV tagging as Prompt 1. Every citation should carry source name, publication date, publisher or institution, and a direct quote or paraphrase locating the claim. Where a source is behind a paywall or otherwise not verifiable in real time, mark it as TBV and note the citation for later verification.

### Constraints

- Do not accept the Prompt 1 draft uncritically. If the evidence contradicts a claim in the draft, say so.
- Do not fabricate citations. If a plausible source cannot be found, mark the claim TBV.
- Do not confuse general bioeconomy literature (throughput-logic sources) with BioConomy-aligned evidence. When citing bioeconomy literature, note whether the source's logic is throughput or retention.
- Do not deprioritize internal-face research to focus on external-face research. The internal face is structurally equal in this template.
- Do not restate content already documented in the six preceding template outputs. Cross-reference and extend.
- Do not omit the multi-BioHub tender context. Where the BioRegion Definition records other BioHubs operating in the same BioRegion, tender coordination is load-bearing.

## Prompt 3: Value Proposition Statement, Evidence Pack, and Tender Compact

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am completing the BioConomy Value Proposition Template for [BioHub name]. I have the Prompt 1 draft value proposition and the Prompt 2 evidence pack. The cohort has resolved the following:

- Priority tenders for this cycle: [list, drawn from readiness diagnostic]
- Retention terms for each priority tender: [state]
- Multi-BioHub tender coordination: [state, referencing Founding Compact and BioRegion Charter protocols]
- Any revisions to the draft canvas or the evidence pack: [state]
- Any other open questions from Prompt 2: [state resolutions]

I am now asking for the three final documents.

Attached:

- [Prompt 1 output]
- [Prompt 2 output]
- [Six preceding template outputs]
- [Original foundational documents]

### Task

Produce three separate documents.

#### Document 1: Value Proposition Statement

A short, formal, foundational reference document stating what the BioHub tenders and why. Structure follows the six-panel Canvas, populated from the evidence pack. Structure:

1. Statement of the BioHub's value proposition (name, anchor, BioRegion).
2. The Living Substrate (short summary drawing from the BioRegion Atlas).
3. The Coordination Layer (short summary drawing from the Founding Compact and BioRegion Charter).
4. The Tenderable Services Portfolio (six services, each with retention logic named, priority for tendering indicated).
5. The Counterparty Portfolio (external face: counterparties and capital patrons summarized).
6. The Participant Portfolio (internal face: participants and community summarized, including gainful employment pathways).
7. The Retention Guarantee (short summary).
8. Priority tenders (drawn from the readiness diagnostic: those categorized as contractable now).
9. Relationship to the BioRegion and the BioHub (short summary referencing the preceding template outputs).
10. Provisions for revision.

Length: five to ten pages. Prose-forward, formal register. This is the document the BioHub uses to introduce its offer to counterparties, capital patrons, community members, and peer BioHubs. It speaks to both the external face and the internal face in single voice.

#### Document 2: Value Proposition Evidence Pack

A long, structured referential document compiling the operational knowledge base for tender preparation and participant engagement. Structure follows the seven sections of the Prompt 2 evidence pack, edited into a coherent reference form:

1. Service-by-service evidence dossier.
2. Counterparty profiles.
3. Participant profiles.
4. Multi-BioHub tender context.
5. Readiness diagnostic.
6. Gap register.
7. Cross-portfolio synthesis.

Length: thirty to fifty pages. Reference register. Structured for section-by-section reference use. This is what the BioHub uses when preparing specific tenders, engaging counterparties, briefing participants, and structuring commitment pools.

#### Document 3: Tender Compact

A governance and commitment document establishing what the BioHub commits to tender, on what retention terms, to which counterparties and participants. Structure:

1. Preamble stating what the BioHub commits to tender and why (drawing on the BioRegion Charter's governance principles and the Founding Compact's cohort commitments).
2. Committed tenders (each priority tender specified with: service, counterparty, mechanism, retention terms, pricing basis, delivery commitment).
3. Counterparty commitments (what will be delivered, on what terms, to which counterparty types, with quality and verification standards named).
4. Participant commitments (what participants receive, on what terms, including gainful employment provisions and pool-participation benefits, aligned with the Founding Compact).
5. Retention guarantees (how value circulates within the BioRegion, how substrate function is monitored and reported, how coordination-form capture is prevented).
6. Multi-BioHub tender coordination protocols (where more than one BioHub operates in the BioRegion; drawing on and refining the Founding Compact and BioRegion Charter protocols).
7. Sequencing of tenders (which come first, which follow, with reasoning drawn from the readiness diagnostic).
8. Provisions for tender revision (how a tender is amended once entered).
9. Provisions for participant dissent (how a participant registers dissent from a tender commitment and what standing that dissent carries).
10. Provisions for tender dissolution (how a tender is concluded if the retention terms cannot be maintained or the counterparty relationship becomes unviable).
11. Co-signatures section for the cohort and, where applicable, for counterparty and participant representatives.

Length: five to ten pages. Commitment register. Co-signed by the cohort. Where a counterparty enters into a specific tender agreement, that agreement operates under this Compact and can reference it by co-signature.

### Output specification

Produce all three documents as separately structured sections within a single deliverable, or as three separate deliverables where the platform's output limits require it. Each document should be self-contained (readable without reference to the others) while cross-referencing where appropriate.

For each document, include:

- Full front matter (title, type, status, date, version).
- Table of contents.
- Full body per specification.
- Reference list.
- Revision history section.

### Evidentiary discipline

Same IC / MS / TBV tagging. Every claim in the Value Proposition Statement and Tender Compact should trace to evidence in the Prompt 2 pack, cohort input, or the six preceding template outputs. Claims in the Value Proposition Evidence Pack should carry their citations inline.

For the Value Proposition Statement and Tender Compact specifically: if a claim cannot be traced to evidence, either revise it or remove it. These documents will be read by counterparties, capital patrons, community members, and peer BioHubs, and must withstand due diligence.

### Constraints

- Do not present the Value Proposition Statement, Value Proposition Evidence Pack, and Tender Compact as interchangeable. Each has a distinct purpose and audience.
- Do not present speculative services (categorized as such in the readiness diagnostic) as current offerings in the Value Proposition Statement or Tender Compact. Speculative services may be flagged as forthcoming. They belong in the Evidence Pack, tagged as such.
- Do not close the Value Proposition Statement with a request for funding. The document establishes what the BioHub tenders. Specific counterparty proposals follow from it as separate documents.
- Do not close the Tender Compact with a summary or a call to action. The Compact ends when its content ends. Its function is to commit.
- Do not omit the Retention Guarantee. It is the structural distinction from the conventional bioeconomy and must be visible across all three documents.
- Do not omit the multi-BioHub tender coordination from the Tender Compact where the BioRegion Definition records other BioHubs operating in the same BioRegion.
- Do not omit the provisions for tender dissolution. Tender commitments should include their own end-conditions.
- Do not exceed the length guidelines significantly. If the Evidence Pack is running past fifty pages, split it into a main pack and a supplementary volume.

## Handoff to Operational Work

This template is the third and last of the establishment templates. Its three outputs (Value Proposition Statement, Value Proposition Evidence Pack, Tender Compact) are prerequisites for the [[templates/place-mandate-template|Place Mandate Template]], which runs next, and they feed the BioHub's operational work:

**Tender preparation and submission:**
- Value Proposition Statement supplies the introductory material.
- Evidence Pack supplies the substantive backing.
- Tender Compact establishes the retention terms and commitments the specific tender must operate under.

**Counterparty engagement and negotiation:**
- Value Proposition Statement is the first-contact document.
- Evidence Pack answers due diligence questions.
- Tender Compact governs how any resulting agreement is structured.

**Contract structuring:**
- Tender Compact provides the standing terms.
- Specific contracts operate under it and can reference it by co-signature.

**Revenue disbursement:**
- Founding Compact (from the Identity Template) established the patronage architecture and how capital is held.
- Tender Compact establishes how tender revenues flow to participants and back into the BioRegion.
- Together they specify the full disbursement architecture.

**Monitoring, verification, and reporting on retention outcomes:**
- BioRegion Atlas (from the BioRegion Template) supplies the substrate baselines.
- Tender Compact's Retention Guarantee specifies what must be monitored.
- Reporting cycles verify that substrate function is being maintained or increased.

**Participant onboarding and pool operation:**
- Founding Compact and Tender Compact together specify what participants receive and on what terms.
- Evidence Pack's Participant Portfolio supplies the operational detail.

**Revision cycles for all three establishment templates:**
- As tenders are executed, evidence accumulates that may prompt revision of the Identity, BioRegion, or BioConomy documents. The revision provisions in each Compact and Charter govern these updates.

## Flags

- The tripartite structure (Value Proposition Statement, Value Proposition Evidence Pack, Tender Compact) is proposed here in v0.2 as parallel to the Identity template's tripartite structure and the BioRegion template's tripartite structure. Test against at least one BioHub value proposition cycle beyond the Overberg case before treating the tripartite structure as settled.

- The BioConomy Value Proposition Canvas is preserved as the working structure that populates the Value Proposition Statement. The Canvas is not itself a final output. Where a coordinator or cohort wishes the Canvas to be a standalone deliverable (for facilitation or communication purposes), it can be produced from the Value Proposition Statement retrospectively.

- The relationship between the BioConomy canvas and the Osterwalder canvas is asserted here as substantively different. If elements of the Osterwalder canvas prove useful in specific BioHub contexts, integrate them inside Panel 4 without disrupting the overall structure.

- The three-prompt sequence assumes the deep research platform can carry context across prompts within a single session. Where a platform cannot, or where the coordinator prefers separate sessions for each prompt, the outputs of Prompts 1 and 2 must be manually attached to the subsequent submissions.

- The gainful-employment framing in Panels 3 and 5 is treated here as structurally equivalent to the counterparty framing in Panel 4. This is a deliberate departure from conventional value proposition templates. Test it against the community-facing coordination work of at least one operational BioHub.

- The template does not include a specific pricing methodology for coordination-as-employment. This is an open question in the field. The template surfaces it as a research task in Prompt 2 and leaves it unresolved here.

- Between-prompt cohort review steps (draft review between Prompts 1 and 2, evidence pack review between Prompts 2 and 3) are essential to the template. They cannot be delegated to the AI.

- The Tender Compact's provisions for tender dissolution may sit uncomfortably at the point of committing to a tender cycle. The provision is nonetheless load-bearing: a tender that cannot specify its own end-conditions cannot honestly claim retention guarantees. Frame the provision as a mark of commitment seriousness at cohort review.

- The Value Proposition Evidence Pack overlaps in some sections with the BioRegion Atlas (for ecological, hydrological, cultural, and infrastructural material relevant to tenders). The Evidence Pack should cross-reference the Atlas and avoid duplicating its content. Where a section of the Evidence Pack draws heavily on Atlas material, cite the Atlas section explicitly.

- The multi-BioHub tender coordination in the Tender Compact refines the working protocols established in the Founding Compact (BioHub-level coordination) and the BioRegion Charter (BioRegion-level coordination). This is a three-layer coordination stack: cohort, region, tenders. The layers should be internally consistent and cross-referenced.

- The Prompt 3 output has three documents. Some deep research platforms will struggle with combined outputs of this length. Where the platform imposes limits, split Prompt 3 into three separate submissions (3a, 3b, 3c) with the same input context.
