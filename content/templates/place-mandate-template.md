The fourth template in the [[templates/index|founding suite]]. A [[biohub|BioHub]] states what its place knows needs doing over twenty years or more, values what that work offers institutions and markets over the same period, and sets the terms on which outside parties may engage. Produces three outputs: the [[place-mandate|Place Mandate]], the [[twenty-year-value-ledger|Twenty-Year Value Ledger]], and the [[terms-of-engagement|Terms of Engagement]].

## Overview

Most development finance sets the shape of the work first. A grant program or a financial instrument defines what it will pay for, and a place rewrites its plans to qualify. Each program brings its own reporting and its own idea of the problem, and a place that depends on many programs ends up with many partial plans and no plan of its own. The wiki names this posture [[needy|needy]].

This template reverses the direction of fit. The place states the work. The Value Ledger shows what the work is worth to the institutions that hold public obligations and to the buyers who pay for services, over at least twenty years. The Terms of Engagement set what no engaging party may change and where each kind of party may come in. Grants and instruments are then tested against the Terms, and the Terms are revised only through the place's own process. A BioHub that has run the template can say: here is what we know needs doing, and these are the terms on which we work. That is the [[needed|needed]] posture.

The template joins two bodies of practice. From [[glossary/f-l/integrated-landscape-management|integrated landscape management]] it takes the generational horizon, the planning of habitat, production and settlement together, and the division of investment into enabling and asset investment. From the BioConomy it takes [[concepts/retention-logic|retention logic]], the [[tenderable-services-portfolio|tenderable services portfolio]] and outcomes finance such as the [[performance-based-bond|performance-based bond]].

## When to run this template

Run it after the three establishment templates, when the nine establishment outputs are in hand and accepted, and before the [[bankable-service-alignment-template|Bankable Service Alignment Template]]. A BioHub may also run it when outside parties begin to shape its work more than its own people do.

Do not run it as a fundraising exercise. The Place Mandate asks no one for money. A cohort that wants a grant application should write one; this template produces the standing position every application and instrument is measured against.

## Prerequisite documents

- The nine outputs of the three establishment templates.
- The [[place-knowledge-brief|Place Knowledge Brief]], produced in Step 0 and accepted by the ratifying authorities.
- A list of the place's ratifying authorities and the decisions each holds.
- Any existing place-based proposals, strand plans or landscape investment portfolios.
- [[biostack|BioStack]] and [[bioconomy|BioConomy]] foundational document.
- Framework of coordination forms ([[frameworks/time-framework|TIME]], or an equivalent).

## Step 0: the Place Knowledge Brief

Every other template in the suite opens with an AI prompt. This one opens with work the cohort does without any AI. The coordinator and cohort hold listening sessions in each settlement and land-use group, with elders, land users, custodial knowledge-holders, workers, youth and the economically excluded, following the protocols of any Indigenous or first-nation authority. The questions ask what the place has always known about its land and water; what has been lost; what needs doing over the next generation; what must never be traded; what has been tried; what the place already does for itself; who decides; and what date gives the place's future its meaning.

Answers are recorded in the speakers' words and languages. [[indigenous-knowledge|Custodial knowledge]] stays with its custodians: the Brief records that it exists and who holds it, never its content or the location of sensitive sites. The ratifying authorities accept the Brief before Prompt 1 runs, and its list of things that need doing becomes fixed input. The AI prompts may organize, evidence, value and cost that list. They may not add to it.

## The three prompts

**Prompt 1: Draft the Place Mandate.** From the Brief, drafts the goal (three candidate sentences built from the Brief's own phrases, tied to a date at least twenty years away), what the place knows, the work grouped into strands, the overlaps where one piece of ground serves several strands, the shared support layer and its handover, what is held, who decides, and what the place does anyway. Anything the AI thinks is missing goes into a list of questions for the cohort.

**Prompt 2: Build the Twenty-Year Value Ledger.** Across four five-year periods, values what each strand offers institutions and markets, states what is held and what is retained, prevents double counting where outcomes overlap, places the three layers of money, projects the [[needed-trajectory|needed trajectory]], sets up the 100-unit trail, plans for proceeding without outside engagement, costs each strand, and records risks and gaps.

**Prompt 3: The three final documents.** Produces the Place Mandate, the Twenty-Year Value Ledger and the Terms of Engagement.

## The three outputs

**Place Mandate.** Five to ten pages. Formal register. The twenty-year concept note: the goal, what the place knows, the work by strand, the shared support layer, what is held, who decides, and what the place does anyway. It quotes the Place Knowledge Brief, asks for no money and ends without a call to action.

**Twenty-Year Value Ledger.** Twenty-five to fifty pages. Reference register. The evidence: value to institutions, value to the market, value held, stacked returns, the money sequence, the needed trajectory, the 100-unit trail, the plan for proceeding without, costing, risks, and the gap register. Projections beyond the first five years are labeled as scenarios.

**Terms of Engagement.** Five to ten pages. Co-signed by the cohort and the ratifying authorities; countersigned by any party that engages. Sets out standing, the fixed ground, the ways in, reporting, handover, the instrument test, proceeding without, and revision. Its register is firm and courteous: it states the terms without arguing for them.

## Cohort work between prompts

Between Prompts 1 and 2, the cohort chooses or rewrites the goal, confirms the strands and overlaps, answers the AI's questions, and marks what is fixed. At minimum the fixed ground covers the list of things that need doing, what is held, and who decides.

Between Prompts 2 and 3, the cohort corrects the costing and the trajectory from local knowledge, decides the fixed ground and the ways in, sets the reporting rule and the handover schedule, and takes the draft to the ratifying authorities. Prompt 3 waits until they have ruled on the decisions they hold.

Both steps operate under the [[founding-compact|Founding Compact]]'s decision-making forms. See [[running-a-template|Running a Template]] for the shared conventions.

## Four features that give the Terms their force

**Local knowledge comes first.** A concept note whose vision an AI drafted cannot claim to come from deep knowledge of the place. Step 0 and the fixed list protect that claim.

**Standing.** A document that says "here is what we know needs doing" has to show who "we" are. Ratification by the place's own authorities is what separates a Place Mandate from a BioHub speaking over the people it serves, the failure the integrated landscape literature calls planning that consults people without acting on what they say.

**Proceeding without.** Declining to fit a funder's process only has force if the work goes on without that funder. The Value Ledger plans for it: what continues with the place's own labor, [[commitment-pool|commitment pools]], existing buyers and any local currency, and what delay costs.

**Measured progress toward needed.** The needed trajectory projects the share of the BioHub's income from grants against the share from contracted or earned payments, period by period, with targets. The 100-unit trail follows each 100 units of money committed to the place and asks how much arrived, how much reached local people and enterprises, and whether the place is now better able to raise the next 100.

## Value to institutions and to the market

The Ledger reads value through the [[frameworks/time-framework|TIME]] forms. Value to the [[i-form-institutional|Institutional form]] is the public obligations the work fulfils or makes cheaper to fulfil (statutory environmental flows, biodiversity and climate targets, skills and employment duties, basic service delivery) and the public costs it avoids. Value to the [[m-form-market|Market form]] is the services a buyer can contract, carried over from the tenderable services portfolio with their readiness categories. Heritage, identity, custodial knowledge and the [[commons|commons]] are recorded as held: named, never priced, and outside every way in. The [[e-form-emergent|Emergent]] coordination the BioHub carries is what the Terms protect.

## The money sequence

The Ledger places three layers of money across the twenty years. Enabling money pays for the shared support layer: coordination, records, monitoring, consultation and contracting. Asset investment builds each strand. Outcomes finance pays against checked results and starts only when a checked result exists to pay for. As each layer arrives, functions any outside body holds for the place pass to local bodies on a published schedule.

## Handoff to the Bankable Service Alignment Template

The Terms of Engagement include an instrument test: the conditions any grant or financial instrument must meet to be compatible with the fixed ground. The [[bankable-service-alignment-template|Bankable Service Alignment Template]] applies that test to a target instrument before it maps the BioHub's services onto the instrument's architecture. An instrument that requires the place to change what the Terms fix fails, however well the services would otherwise align.

## Multi-BioHub regions

Where more than one BioHub operates in a [[bioregion|BioRegion]], the BioHubs either produce one Place Mandate jointly under the BioRegion Charter, or produce one each and reconcile their fixed ground and ways in under the Charter's multi-BioHub protocols. Two Terms of Engagement in one BioRegion must not offer the same outcome to different parties. See [[using-templates-across-biohubs|Using Templates Across BioHubs]].

## Provenance

Written 30 September 2026 from *Place Mandate Template* v0.1 (30 September 2026), which holds the full prompt text, the Step 0 questions and the output specifications. The template generalizes a place-based investment proposal on the Ward 2 Development Forum register, [Ridge to River to Table](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/concepts/ridge-to-river-to-table), which was not yet agreed at the time of writing. The money sequence, the shared support layer and the practice of following each 100 units of money come from that proposal, which in turn draws them from the 1000 Landscapes guide and from an unpublished 2026 concept paper by Ranyaka Community Transformation, *A Third Way of Investing in Places*. Step 0, the fixed ground, the needed trajectory and the instrument test are the wiki author's additions. Ranyaka Community Transformation has not yet replied to this page's use of its method.
