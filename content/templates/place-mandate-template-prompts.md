The runnable text of the [[templates/place-mandate-template|Place Mandate Template]], version 0.1. A [[biohub|BioHub]] cohort uses it to gather the [[place-knowledge-brief|Place Knowledge Brief]] and then to produce the [[place-mandate|Place Mandate]], the [[twenty-year-value-ledger|Twenty-Year Value Ledger]] and the [[terms-of-engagement|Terms of Engagement]]. Read the template page first for what the template is for, and [[templates/running-a-template|Running a Template]] for the conventions every template in the suite shares.

Copy each prompt into a deep-research AI platform with the listed documents attached. Replace the words in square brackets with your own. Do not run a prompt until the cohort work before it is done.

## Prerequisites

- The nine outputs of the three establishment templates.
- The Place Knowledge Brief from Step 0, accepted by the ratifying authorities.
- A list of the place's ratifying authorities and the decisions each holds.
- Any existing place-based proposals, strand plans or landscape investment portfolios the cohort has drafted.
- The BioStack and BioConomy foundational document.
- Framework of coordination forms (TIME, or an equivalent).
- The wiki pages on integrated landscape management, retention logic, needed versus needy, and performance-based bonds, or equivalent references.

## Step 0: The Place Knowledge Brief (cohort only, no AI)

**Who.** The coordinator and cohort convene listening sessions in each settlement, community or land-use group in the region. Include elders, land users, custodial knowledge-holders, farmers, workers, youth and the economically excluded. Where Indigenous or first-nation authority exists, the sessions follow that authority's protocols and its consent is sought before anything it holds is recorded.

**Questions.** Adapt these to the place and its languages:

1. What has this place always known about its water, soil, seasons, plants and animals?
2. What has been lost, and when, and why?
3. What needs doing here over the next twenty years or more? Say it in your own words.
4. What must never be traded, sold or handed to outsiders?
5. What has been tried before, by whom, and what happened?
6. What does the place already do for itself without outside money?
7. Who decides about land, water and heritage here, and how?
8. What date or event gives this place's long-term future its meaning?

**Recording.** Record answers in the speakers' own words and languages, with attribution only where the speaker consents. Custodial and sacred knowledge stays with its custodians: the Brief records that such knowledge exists and who holds it, and never records its content or the location of sensitive sites.

**Output.** The Place Knowledge Brief: a plain document organized by the eight questions, with a closing list of the things that need doing as the place has named them. The ratifying authorities accept the Brief before Prompt 1 runs. The list of things that need doing is the fixed input to everything that follows.

## Prompt 1: Draft the Place Mandate

Attach the Place Knowledge Brief, the list of ratifying authorities, the nine establishment outputs and any existing place-based proposals.

```
You are assisting the cohort of [BIOHUB NAME], a BioHub in [BIOREGION NAME], to draft a
Place Mandate: a twenty-year concept note stating what the place knows needs doing.

The attached Place Knowledge Brief is the authority for what needs doing. Treat its list
of things that need doing as fixed. Do not add, remove, merge or reword items on that list.
If you believe something is missing, write it as a question in the section "Questions for
the cohort". Never write it into the Mandate.

Draft the following sections:

1. The goal. One sentence stating what the place will be like by [MEANINGFUL DATE AT LEAST
   TWENTY YEARS AWAY, FROM BRIEF QUESTION 8]. Offer three candidate sentences, each built
   from phrases in the Brief, for the cohort to choose from or rewrite.

2. What the place knows. A summary of the Brief's answers to questions 1, 2 and 5, quoting
   the Brief directly. No outside sources in this section.

3. The work. Group the Brief's items into strands. For each strand, state what it would do,
   quote the Brief items it carries, and name which of three kinds of area it works in:
   natural habitat; regenerative production and land use; settlements, infrastructure and
   industry. Then identify places where one piece of ground or one activity serves more
   than one strand (for example, a restored hectare that yields water, habitat, a visitor
   trail and a learning site) and describe each overlap.

4. The shared support layer. The functions every strand needs and that should be provided
   once for all of them: coordination, finance, records and data, monitoring, consultation,
   contracting, and a registered body able to sign agreements. For each function, state
   whether the place holds it now, whether an outside body would hold it for a period, and
   which local body it would pass to.

5. What is held. From Brief question 4, the things the place does not offer to any
   outside party. State them plainly.

6. Who decides. From the list of ratifying authorities, which authority decides what,
   including any authority that decides first on particular land or water.

7. What the place does anyway. From Brief question 6, a first-pass list of work that
   proceeds with the place's own labor and resources.

8. Questions for the cohort. Anything unclear, missing or contradictory in the Brief.

Constraints:
- Use the place's own words wherever the Brief supplies them.
- Do not request money anywhere in the Mandate. Do not close with a call to action.
- Tag every factual claim: [IC] independently corroborated, [MS] from the cohort's own
  documents or the Brief, [TBV] to be verified. Never invent a source.
- Plain, formal register. No promotional language.
```

### Cohort work between Prompts 1 and 2

- Choose or rewrite the goal sentence.
- Confirm the strands and the overlaps, and answer the questions for the cohort.
- Mark which elements of the Mandate are fixed. At minimum these are the list of things that need doing, what is held, and who decides.
- Confirm the time horizon and the date that gives it meaning.
- Confirm the ratifying authorities have seen the draft, or schedule that they will.

## Prompt 2: Build the Twenty-Year Value Ledger

Attach the confirmed Place Mandate draft, the Brief, the nine establishment outputs (especially the Value Proposition Evidence Pack, its readiness diagnostic and its gap register) and any existing costings.

```
You are building the Twenty-Year Value Ledger for [BIOHUB NAME]: the evidence behind its
confirmed Place Mandate (attached). The Ledger values what the place offers institutions
and markets over twenty years, what it holds back, and what it asks of any party that
engages.

Use four periods: Period 1 (years 1 to 5), Period 2 (years 6 to 10), Period 3 (years 11
to 15), Period 4 (years 16 to 20). Add a fifth period if the Mandate's horizon is longer.

Build these sections:

A. Value offered to institutions. For each strand and period: the public mandates the
   work fulfils or makes cheaper to fulfil (statutory obligations, national and provincial
   targets, international commitments, service-delivery duties); the institution that
   holds each mandate; the public costs the work avoids (infrastructure, disaster response,
   health, unemployment support); and the evidence. Name the statute or target document.

B. Value offered to the market. For each strand and period: the services a buyer could
   contract; named buyers or buyer classes; market signals; precedent instruments or
   contracts; price evidence; and the readiness category carried over from the Value
   Proposition Evidence Pack (contractable now, contractable after specified build-out,
   speculative pending research).

C. Value held. The heritage, identity, custodial knowledge and commons the Mandate lists
   as held. State them. Do not price them. Then state what the place retains from the
   value it offers: money, skills, water in the ground, soil, and anything else the
   Mandate names, with how each would be measured.

D. Stacked returns. For each overlap identified in the Mandate, the values the same
   ground or activity yields to institutions and markets. State a rule that prevents the
   same unit of outcome being sold or reported twice.

E. The money sequence. Three layers, placed in periods:
   1. Enabling money for the shared support layer (grant).
   2. Asset investment for each strand (grant, concessional or equity).
   3. Outcomes finance paid against checked results, starting only when a checked result
      exists to pay for.
   For each layer, what triggers it, which strands it serves, and which functions of the
   shared support layer pass to local bodies as it arrives.

F. The needed trajectory. For each period, the projected share of the BioHub's income
   from grants against the share from contracted or earned payments, with the assumptions
   behind each figure. Propose a target for the end of Period 2 and the end of Period 4.

G. The 100-unit trail. A method for following each 100 units of money committed to the
   place: how much reaches the place, how much reaches local people and enterprises, and
   what remains afterwards, including whether the place is better able to raise the next
   100. Give a baseline estimate for today where evidence exists.

H. Proceeding without. What the place does if no outside party engages: the work that
   continues with its own labor, commitment pools, existing buyers and any local currency,
   and the cost of delay for each strand. Treat this as a serious plan.

I. Costing. For each strand and period, an indicative cost with its basis. Compare the cost
   of delivering all strands through one shared support layer with a costed estimate of
   buying the same functions separately.

J. Risks. Include the failure modes documented for integrated landscape management:
   capture by private actors or by government, powerful actors who stay outside the
   partnership, planning that consults people without acting on what they say, and
   planning that involves people without including them in implementation. For each, the
   safeguard in the Mandate or the Terms.

K. Open questions and gap register.

Constraints:
- Projections beyond Period 1 are scenarios. Label them as such.
- Every figure carries its source, or is labeled as the cohort's estimate.
- Tag every factual claim [IC], [MS] or [TBV]. Never invent a source, a buyer or a figure.
  If evidence cannot be found, say so and mark the item [TBV].
- Structural inferences are labeled as inferences and not attributed to source authors.
- Do not revise the Mandate. If the evidence suggests a strand is weak, record that in
  section K for the cohort.
```

### Cohort work between Prompts 2 and 3

- Test the costing and the needed trajectory against local knowledge and correct them.
- Decide the fixed ground of the Terms: what no engaging party may change.
- Decide the ways in: which strands and money layers outside parties may join, and what each receives.
- Decide the reporting the place will provide to every party, and the rule for extra reporting requests.
- Decide the handover schedule for any function an outside body would hold.
- Take the draft to the ratifying authorities for the decisions they hold. Prompt 3 waits until they have ruled.

## Prompt 3: The three final documents

Attach the confirmed Mandate, the reviewed Value Ledger and the cohort's decisions on the Terms. On platforms with tight output limits, run this prompt as 3a, 3b and 3c, one document each.

```
Produce three documents for [BIOHUB NAME] from the attached confirmed Mandate, Value Ledger
and cohort decisions.

Document 1: Place Mandate (five to ten pages)
Sections: the goal; what the place knows; the work, by strand, with the overlaps; the
shared support layer and its handover; what is held; who decides; what the place does
anyway. Formal register. Quote the Place Knowledge Brief where it carries the meaning.
The Mandate does not ask for money and does not close with a call to action. It ends
when its content ends.

Document 2: Twenty-Year Value Ledger (twenty-five to fifty pages)
The reviewed Ledger, sections A to K, structured for reference use: numbered sections,
a table per strand per period, a sources list, and all tags preserved.

Document 3: Terms of Engagement (five to ten pages)
Sections:
1. Standing. Who speaks for the place, and the ratifying authorities and their decisions.
2. The fixed ground. The Mandate's list of things that need doing, what is held, who
   decides, the place's own measures of progress, and the place's ownership of data about
   itself. No funding agreement, grant condition or instrument term may override these.
3. The ways in. For each strand and money layer: what an engaging party may fund or
   contract, what it receives (verified outcomes, access to the Ledger, reporting,
   recognition), and the language of that party's own programs so it can see where it
   fits.
4. Reporting. One report on the place's measures, including the 100-unit trail, issued
   to every engaging party on the same cycle. Additional reporting a party requires is
   costed and paid for by that party.
5. Handover. The schedule by which functions held by outside bodies pass to local bodies,
   and how progress is published.
6. The instrument test. The conditions any grant or financial instrument must meet to be
   compatible with these Terms. The Bankable Service Alignment Template applies this test.
7. Proceeding without. A plain statement that the place continues its work whether or
   not a given party engages, with reference to Ledger section H.
8. Revision. The Terms and the Mandate are revised only through the place's own process:
   on a five-year cycle, or when the ratifying authorities call for it. A request from an
   outside party does not by itself open a revision.
9. Signatures. The cohort and the ratifying authorities sign. Engaging parties countersign
   to confirm they engage on these Terms.

Register for all three documents: firm and courteous. State the terms without arguing for
them or pleading. The reader draws the conclusion.

Constraints: preserve all tags; never invent a source, figure, buyer or signatory; no
closing summaries; no promotional language.
```

## Multi-BioHub regions

Where more than one BioHub operates in a BioRegion, either produce one Place Mandate jointly under the BioRegion Charter, or produce one per BioHub and reconcile the fixed ground and the ways in under the Charter's multi-BioHub protocols. Two Terms of Engagement in one BioRegion must not offer the same outcome to different parties.

## Constraints

- The AI does not set the list of things that need doing, the goal, what is held or who decides.
- The AI does not assume consent. Where a decision belongs to a ratifying authority, the document records that the decision is pending until it is made.
- Never record the content of custodial or sacred knowledge, or the location of sensitive heritage sites.
- Evidence tagging and the no-fabrication rule apply throughout, as in every template in the suite.
- Voice: no promotional language, no closing summaries, no call to action.

## Cross-platform notes

As for the rest of the suite (see Running a Template). Step 0 is done by people and needs no platform. Prompt 2's Ledger is the largest output: on platforms with tight output limits, produce sections A to D, E to H and I to K as separate submissions and assemble them.
