The [[templates/index|founding suite]] turns a [[biohub|BioHub]] into a counterparty that family and institutional capital can engage on the place's own stated terms.

## Overview

Advisors who present place-based work to families and institutions often receive it in a form their clients cannot use: a project that asks for money on the funder's terms, with no plan that outlasts the grant. This wiki calls a posture like this [[needy|needy]]. The founding suite moves a BioHub to a [[needed|needed]] posture. It gives what a BioHub offers the outside world a professional form: standing documents, written in one consistent pattern, that an investment committee or a family council can read and countersign.

A BioHub that has completed the entire process develops a position the place's stakeholders has already ratified. The [[place-mandate|Place Mandate]] states what the place knows needs doing over twenty years or more, and asks no one for money. The [[twenty-year-value-ledger|Twenty-Year Value Ledger]] values that work across four five-year periods, for the institutions that hold public obligations and for the buyers who pay for services. The [[terms-of-engagement|Terms of Engagement]], co-signed by the cohort and the place's ratifying authorities, set what no engaging party may change and where each kind of party may come in. A family or institution that engages countersigns against what the place **knows** it can offer AND knows what is **needed**.

## Four questions settled before the first meeting

- **Who decides.** The Place Mandate names the place's ratifying authorities and the decisions each one holds.
- **What the money is for.** The Ledger places every commitment in one of the layers of money: 
	- enabling money for coordination and monitoring, 
	- asset investment that builds each strand of work, and 
	- outcomes finance that pays against checked results.
- **What the client may shape.** The Terms list the fixed ground and the ways in, strand by strand. Any additional reporting a party requires is costed and paid for by that party.
- **What happens if the client withdraws.** The Ledger and the Terms each carry a plan for proceeding without outside engagement.

The measure of progress is written down as well. The [[needed-trajectory|needed trajectory]] projects the share of the BioHub's income from grants against the share from contracted or earned payments, period by period, with targets for years ten and twenty. The 100-unit trail follows each 100 units of money committed to the place and asks how much reached local people and enterprises, and whether the place is now better able to raise the next 100. A client can judge an engagement partly by whether it moves the place along that line.

## Reading the Ledger in five returns

Some advisors report social, environmental, technological, political and economic return on the work they present. Each of the five has a section of the Ledger where it is evidenced.

| Return        | Where the Ledger evidences it                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Economic      | Value offered to the market: the services a buyer can contract, drawn from the BioHub's [[tenderable-services-portfolio\|tenderable services portfolio]], and the costing of each strand.                |
| Environmental | Ecological results that have been checked on the ground. Outcomes finance, such as a [[performance-based-bond\|performance-based bond]], pays for these results. When one piece of land produces results for several strands of work, each result is counted once. |
| Social        | Value held and value retained in the place, in the terms of [[retention-economics\|retention economics]], and the 100-unit trail.                                                                        |
| Political     | Value offered to institutions: the public obligations the work fulfils or makes cheaper. The Terms carry the signatures of the place's ratifying authorities.                                            |
| Technological | The shared support layer (records, monitoring and contracting) and the place's ownership of data about itself, which the Terms fix.                                                                      |

Every figure in the Ledger carries its source or is marked as the cohort's estimate, and projections beyond the first five years are labeled as scenarios. Heritage, identity, custodial knowledge and the [[commons|commons]] are recorded as held and never priced. They appear in the Ledger without a figure.

## Families and custodial authorities

Families who hold capital across generations, and communities that hold custodial or First Nations authority, plan on a long horizon. So does the Place Mandate, which ties its goal to a date at least twenty years away that gives the place's future its meaning.

The Mandate's first input, the [[place-knowledge-brief|Place Knowledge Brief]], is gathered by the cohort in listening sessions held, following the protocols of any Indigenous or first-nation authority in the place. The ratifying authorities accept the Brief before any AI prompt runs. [[indigenous-knowledge|Custodial knowledge]] stays with its custodians: the Brief records that it exists and who holds it, never its content. A family that engages meets a place whose purpose is already written in its own words, and whose authorities have already agreed who decides.

## BioHubs as nodes

Advisors who build impact nodes or centers of excellence will recognize the BioConomy approach. A BioHub is a coordination body. It organizes distributed regenerative contributions within a living landscape into verifiable commitments the outside world can contract with.

Each of the five templates ends in three documents of the same kind: a short formal statement, a long evidence pack and a co-signed compact. A reader who learns the pattern on one BioHub can read any BioHub in the network and compare them. When a client brings a specific financial instrument, the [[bankable-service-alignment-template|Bankable Service Alignment Template]] first tests that instrument against the Terms of Engagement, and maps the BioHub's services onto it only if it passes.
