The runnable text of the [[templates/bankable-service-alignment-template|Bankable Service Alignment Template]], version 0.2. A [[biohub|BioHub]] cohort uses it to test a financial instrument against its [[terms-of-engagement|Terms of Engagement]] and to produce the [[alignment-statement|Alignment Statement]], the Alignment Evidence Pack and the [[alignment-compact|Alignment Compact]]. Read the template page first for what the template is for and where it sits in the five-template [[templates/index|founding suite]], and [[templates/running-a-template|Running a Template]] for the conventions every template shares.

Copy each prompt's context and task into a deep-research AI platform with the listed documents attached. Replace the words in square brackets with your own. Do not run a prompt until the cohort work before it is done.

## When to Run This Template

Run this template after the Place Mandate Template has been completed and the cohort has identified at least one financial instrument (existing or planned) that purchases services the BioHub can deliver. The BioHub should have:

- The nine outputs of the three establishment templates produced and accepted by the cohort.
- The three Place Mandate outputs, with the Terms of Engagement co-signed by the cohort and the place's ratifying authorities.
- At least one target financial instrument identified by name, issuer or instrument class.
- Access to the instrument's public documentation (prospectus, term sheet, implementation plan, verification methodology) or, where the instrument does not yet exist, to comparable instruments in the same class.
- A service from the Tenderable Services Portfolio that the target instrument is designed to purchase.

Do not map services onto an instrument before applying the Terms of Engagement's instrument test.

Do not run this template as a substitute for the preceding templates. A BioHub that has not established its identity, BioRegion, value proposition and terms has nothing to align.

Do not run this template against an instrument that does not purchase services the BioHub offers. If the instrument purchases carbon outcomes and the BioHub's carbon sequestration service is categorized as speculative pending research in the Value Proposition Evidence Pack's readiness diagnostic, the alignment exercise is premature. Resolve the categorization first through field research.

## Prerequisite Documents to Attach

Attach to every submission:

1. **Nine outputs of the establishment templates:** Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, Founding Compact, BioRegion Definition, BioRegion Atlas, BioRegion Charter, Value Proposition Statement, Value Proposition Evidence Pack, Tender Compact.
2. **Three outputs of the Place Mandate Template:** Place Mandate, Twenty-Year Value Ledger, Terms of Engagement.
3. Your BioStack and BioConomy foundational document.
4. Your framework of coordination forms (TIME, or the framework you work within).
5. Your commitment pooling document (the Ruddick and Burgess-Bergstra basis, and your local adaptation).
6. **Target instrument documentation:** all publicly available material on the specific instrument or instrument class the BioHub is targeting.

## User-Supplied Inputs to Gather

Most inputs are inherited from the twelve preceding outputs. The coordinator compiles only the following:

- **Target instrument.** Name, issuer, arranger, instrument class, and the tranche or series extension the BioHub would target where the instrument is designed as a series.
- **Instrument documentation inventory.** What documents are available, what is public, what has been requested and not received.
- **Service or services to align.** Drawn from the Tenderable Services Portfolio, with their readiness categories from the Value Proposition Evidence Pack.
- **Current monitoring capacity.** What the BioHub or its partners measure now, where, how often and by what method.
- **Existing relationships with the instrument's parties.** Any contact with the arranger, implementation agent, verification agent, anchor investors or outcomes funders, and its status.
- **Other alignment runs.** Any previous run of this template against another instrument, whose Evidence Pack may be cross-referenced.
- **Multi-BioHub context for this instrument.** Any other BioHub in the BioRegion targeting the same instrument or series.

## The Three Output Documents

1. **Alignment Statement.** A short, formal document mapping the BioHub's retention services onto the target instrument's architecture. States what the BioHub delivers, what the instrument purchases, how the two align, where the gaps are, and what makes the BioHub a credible counterparty. This is the first-contact document for the instrument's arranger, verification agent or implementation partner, sent with the Terms of Engagement. Length: five to ten pages.

2. **Alignment Evidence Pack.** A long, structured referential document compiling the instrument research, the Terms test record, the verification methodology analysis, precedent instrument cases, the specific alignment map, regulatory and legal requirements, and price-discovery evidence. The operational knowledge base for preparing the instrument-aligned tender. Length: twenty-five to forty-five pages.

3. **Alignment Compact.** A coordination and commitment document establishing what the cohort commits to build toward readiness for this instrument: build-out steps, the monitoring and verification architecture, governance requirements, partnerships, timeline, resources, risks. Co-signed by the cohort. It operates under the Terms of Engagement and may not contradict them. Length: five to ten pages.

## Sequencing

- **Prompt 1** applies the Terms test to the instrument. If the test passes, it produces the alignment hypothesis: a first-pass alignment map with two faces (bottom-up: what the BioHub delivers; top-down: what the instrument demands), an alignment summary and a gap inventory.
- **Between Prompts 1 and 2:** the coordinator convenes the cohort to review the Terms test and the alignment hypothesis. If the test found an incompatible element, the cohort decides whether to stop the run or to take the matter to the place's own revision process. Otherwise the cohort confirms which alignment pathways warrant deeper evidence work, adds what local knowledge can close, and names open questions for Prompt 2.
- **Prompt 2** builds the alignment evidence pack across seven sections, ending with a stress test that revises the alignment map and rechecks the Terms test. It also produces a readiness diagnostic and gap register specific to this instrument.
- **Between Prompts 2 and 3:** the coordinator convenes the cohort to review the evidence pack, resolve tensions, confirm build-out priorities and agree timeline commitments.
- **Prompt 3** produces the three final documents.

Run the prompts in sequence, on the same platform where possible.

## Cross-Platform Notes

The prompts are written for portability across ChatGPT Deep Research, Claude Research, Perplexity Deep Research and Gemini Deep Research.

Prompt 2's verification methodology deep dive receives the most research depth and may need its own submission on platforms with tight output limits. Where the platform cannot produce the whole evidence pack in one response, produce it in structured sections and assemble them.

Prompt 3 produces three documents. Where a platform cannot produce all three in a single response, split Prompt 3 into three submissions (3a Alignment Statement, 3b Alignment Evidence Pack, 3c Alignment Compact), each carrying the Prompt 2 evidence pack as an attached input.

## Prompt 1: Test the Terms and Map the Alignment Hypothesis

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am a coordinator with [BioHub name, as established in the Identity Statement], operating within [BioRegion name, as established in the BioRegion Definition]. I am running the Bankable Service Alignment Template, the fifth template in the suite. This is the first of three prompts. Its purpose is to test a target financial instrument against our Terms of Engagement and, if the test passes, to produce a first-pass alignment hypothesis for the cohort to review.

I work within the BioConomy framework. Our BioHub tenders retention services into markets structured to purchase them. Our Terms of Engagement set what no engaging party may change. The instrument is tested against the Terms; the Terms are not adjusted to fit the instrument.

I am attaching the following documents:

[List attached documents: the twelve preceding template outputs, foundational documents, target instrument documentation]

I am providing the following context:

- Target instrument: [name, issuer, arranger, class, tranche or series extension targeted]
- Instrument documentation inventory: [insert]
- Service or services to align, with readiness categories: [insert]
- Current monitoring capacity: [insert]
- Existing relationships with the instrument's parties: [insert]
- Other alignment runs: [insert or state none]
- Multi-BioHub context for this instrument: [insert or state none]

### Task

**Part A: The Terms test.** Before mapping any service, apply the attached Terms of Engagement to the target instrument.

For each element of the Terms' fixed ground (section 2) and instrument test (section 6), read the instrument's documentation and mark the element:

- **Compatible:** the instrument accepts it as it stands.
- **Negotiable:** the instrument's documents are silent or allow variation, and agreement would need to be sought.
- **Incompatible:** the instrument requires the place to change or give up the element.

Quote the instrument documentation for every mark. Where documentation is unavailable, mark the element TBV and say what would settle it.

If any element is incompatible, stop after Part A. Report the incompatible elements and what the instrument would require. Do not propose changes to the Terms: they are revised only through the place's own process, as the Terms set out.

If no element is incompatible, continue to Part B. Carry the negotiable elements forward as a list of matters to raise with the instrument's parties.

**Part B: The alignment hypothesis.** For each outcome the instrument purchases, build one row of an alignment map with two faces:

- **Bottom-up:** what the BioHub delivers, drawn from the establishment documents, the Place Mandate and the Twenty-Year Value Ledger.
- **Top-down:** what the instrument requires, drawn from its documentation.

Categorize each row:

- **Aligned.** The BioHub's current capacity meets the requirement. Cite the specific evidence from the preceding documents.
- **Alignable.** The BioHub's capacity is oriented toward the requirement and does not yet meet it. Specify the gap and the build-out needed.
- **Misaligned.** The BioHub's current work does not correspond to the requirement. State whether the misalignment is structural (the BioHub's retention logic conflicts with the instrument's design) or developmental (the capacity can be built and is not yet present).
- **Not applicable.** The requirement does not apply to this BioHub's context.

Add a Terms column to every row, recording whether meeting the instrument's requirement would touch the fixed ground of the Terms of Engagement. A row that is aligned on capacity and incompatible with the Terms is categorized as misaligned, and the misalignment is structural.

Where the instrument is designed as a series, identify which tranche or series extension the BioHub would target and why. Where no tranche covers the BioHub's geography, assess whether the instrument's design accommodates expansion to it.

### Output specification

Produce:

1. **Terms test record.** One row per element of the fixed ground and the instrument test, with its mark and the quoted documentation.
2. **Alignment map.** One row per purchased outcome, with both faces, the category, the Terms column and the evidence.
3. **Alignment summary.** One to two pages: what aligns, what is alignable, what is misaligned, and the tranche targeted where applicable.
4. **Gap inventory.** A first list of the gaps the map surfaces, grouped by monitoring, verification, governance, evidence and partnerships.
5. **Negotiable matters.** The elements marked negotiable, for the cohort and later for the instrument's parties.
6. **Open questions for the cohort** to resolve before Prompt 2.

### Evidentiary discipline

Tag every substantive claim as:

- **IC** (Independently Corroborated) for claims supported by cited sources outside the BioHub's own documents.
- **MS** (Mission-Sourced) for claims drawn from the twelve preceding template outputs or other cohort documents.
- **TBV** (To Be Verified) for plausible claims that need Prompt 2 to substantiate.

Structural inferences are labeled as inference and not attributed to source authors.

### Constraints

- Do not map services before completing the Terms test.
- Do not propose revisions to the Terms of Engagement or the Place Mandate.
- Do not treat an instrument's requirements as fixed and the BioHub's as adjustable. Both faces are evidence.
- Do not fabricate instrument terms, verification requirements or party names. Where documentation is missing, mark TBV.
- Do not categorize a row as aligned without citing the evidence of current capacity.
- Do not align speculative services. Where a purchased outcome depends on a service categorized as speculative pending research, mark the row misaligned (developmental) and note the research required.

## Prompt 2: Build the Alignment Evidence Pack

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am continuing the Bankable Service Alignment Template for [BioHub name] against [target instrument]. The cohort has reviewed the Terms test and the alignment hypothesis and confirmed the following:

- Terms test outcome and any matters taken to the place's own revision process: [state]
- Alignment pathways warranting deeper evidence work: [list]
- Gaps the cohort can close from local knowledge: [state]
- Open questions for this prompt: [list]

I am now asking for the alignment evidence pack.

Attached:

- [Prompt 1 output with cohort review notes]
- [Twelve preceding template outputs]
- [Target instrument documentation]
- [Evidence Pack from any previous alignment run, where cross-reference applies]

### Task

Produce an evidence pack in seven sections:

1. **Instrument class research.** A survey of the instrument class globally: named instruments, issuers, sizes, geographies, outcome metrics and performance to date.
2. **Instrument mechanics analysis.** How the target instrument works: capital structure, investor tiers, outcomes funders, payment triggers, performance premium or discount, implementation period, the roles of arranger, issuer, implementation agent and verification agent.
3. **Verification methodology deep dive.** The methodology the verification agent applies: what is measured, by what method, at what frequency, with what baseline, what counts as an outcome, and what data form the agent accepts. Compare it with the BioHub's current monitoring capacity and state precisely what would need to be built. Give this section the most research depth: verification is the point at which an alignment succeeds or fails.
4. **Regulatory and legal mapping.** The regulatory regime governing the instrument and the service (water rights, land tenure, environmental authorizations, securities and disclosure rules, contracting requirements for implementation partners) in the BioHub's jurisdiction.
5. **Precedent alignment cases.** Documented cases of place-based bodies aligning to this instrument or its class: what they built, how long it took, what failed.
6. **Price-discovery evidence.** The price or value the instrument attaches to the outcome (per hectare, per megalitre, per tonne, per verified unit), and comparable prices elsewhere, including avoided-cost comparisons.
7. **Alignment stress test.** Revise the Prompt 1 alignment map in light of the evidence, stating each change and its reason. Recheck the Terms test against the evidence gathered, and revise any element's mark where the evidence changes it.

Also produce:

- **Readiness diagnostic.** For each alignment pathway, categorize as ready now; ready after specified build-out; or not ready. Distinguish not-ready pathways whose gap is closable from those whose gap is structural.
- **Gap register.** For every gap: the specific gap, the build-out or research required to close it, an estimated timescale, a proposed owner (the BioHub, a named partner, or a specialist to be identified), and dependencies on other gaps. Flag any gap whose closure would take longer than the instrument's timeline allows.
- **Cross-section synthesis.** Two to three pages naming where the evidence is strong, where it is thin, and what specialist research would strengthen the alignment.

Where a previous alignment run's Evidence Pack covers shared ground (a common verification methodology or implementation agent structure), cite it by reference and research only what differs.

### Output specification

Produce the seven sections, the readiness diagnostic, the gap register and the synthesis as one evidence pack. Length: twenty-five to forty-five pages. Include named citations with source, date and locator; tables for quantitative evidence; and reference lists by section.

### Evidentiary discipline

Same IC / MS / TBV tagging as Prompt 1. Every citation carries source name, publication date, publisher or institution, and a direct quote or paraphrase locating the claim. For instrument mechanics and verification, prioritize the instrument's own documents and the verification agent's published methodology. Where a source is behind a paywall or otherwise not verifiable in real time, mark it TBV and note the citation for later verification.

### Constraints

- Do not accept the Prompt 1 map uncritically. Where evidence contradicts it, say so and revise it in the stress test.
- Do not present a gap as closed because it is closable. The readiness diagnostic states what is true now.
- Do not fabricate prices, instrument terms, precedent cases or verification requirements.
- Do not confuse monitoring with verification. Monitoring is the ongoing measurement the BioHub or its partners conduct. Verification is the independent third-party assessment of whether outcomes were achieved. Both are needed, and they serve different parties under different standards.
- Do not restate content already documented in the preceding outputs. Cross-reference and extend.
- Do not omit the multi-BioHub context where another BioHub in the BioRegion targets the same instrument or series.

## Prompt 3: Alignment Statement, Alignment Evidence Pack and Alignment Compact

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am completing the Bankable Service Alignment Template for [BioHub name] against [target instrument]. I have the Prompt 1 output and the Prompt 2 evidence pack. The cohort has resolved the following:

- Final alignment map and Terms test record: [confirm or state revisions]
- Build-out priorities: [list]
- Timeline commitments: [state]
- Partnerships to formalize: [list]
- Multi-BioHub coordination for this instrument: [state, referencing the BioRegion Charter's protocols]
- Any other resolutions: [state]

I am now asking for the three final documents.

Attached:

- [Prompt 1 output]
- [Prompt 2 evidence pack]
- [Twelve preceding template outputs]
- [Target instrument documentation]

### Task

Produce three separate documents.

#### Document 1: Alignment Statement

A short, formal document mapping the BioHub's retention services onto the target instrument's architecture. Structure:

1. Statement of the alignment (BioHub name, BioRegion, target instrument and tranche, service or services aligned).
2. The BioHub's delivery capacity.
3. The instrument's architecture.
4. The Terms of Engagement and the result of the Terms test, including the matters to be negotiated.
5. The alignment map (final version).
6. Principal alignment strengths.
7. Principal alignment gaps, stated plainly, with no reassurance that they will be closed.
8. The BioHub's structural position as a counterparty.
9. Relationship to the BioConomy framework.
10. Provisions for revision.

Length: five to ten pages. Prose-forward, formal register. It is sent with the Terms of Engagement. It establishes alignment and does not ask for investment; specific funding proposals follow from it as separate documents.

#### Document 2: Alignment Evidence Pack

A long, structured referential document. Structure follows the Prompt 2 evidence pack, edited into reference form:

1. Terms test record.
2. Instrument class research.
3. Instrument mechanics analysis.
4. Verification methodology analysis.
5. Regulatory and legal mapping.
6. Precedent alignment cases.
7. Price-discovery evidence.
8. The final alignment map.
9. Readiness diagnostic.
10. Gap register.
11. Synthesis.

Length: twenty-five to forty-five pages. Reference register. This is what the cohort uses to prepare the instrument-aligned tender, what the instrument's parties read in due diligence, and what counsel draws on when drafting the resulting contract.

#### Document 3: Alignment Compact

A coordination and commitment document establishing what the cohort commits to build toward readiness for this instrument. Structure:

1. Preamble stating what the cohort commits to build and why.
2. Build-out commitments, drawn from the gap register.
3. Monitoring and verification architecture to be constructed to meet the instrument's verification standard.
4. Governance requirements to be met.
5. Partnership commitments.
6. Timeline, milestone-based, with dependencies.
7. Resource requirements and funding pathway.
8. Risk register.
9. Review and revision cycle.
10. Relationship to the Tender Compact and to the Terms of Engagement. The Alignment Compact operates under the Terms of Engagement and may not contradict them. Where a build-out commitment would touch the fixed ground, the Compact records the matter as pending the place's own revision process.
11. Co-signatures section for the cohort.

Length: five to ten pages. Commitment register. Co-signed by the cohort. It binds the cohort to a shared build-out trajectory without depending on any single institution's mandate or any single funder's cycle. The instrument's market logic sits inside the boundary the Terms of Engagement set: the BioHub tenders a service and does not contract out its governance.

### Output specification

Produce the three documents as separately structured sections within one deliverable, or as three deliverables where output limits require it. Each document is self-contained and cross-references the others where appropriate. Each includes front matter (title, type, status, date, version), a table of contents, the full body, a reference list and a revision history section.

### Evidentiary discipline

Same IC / MS / TBV tagging. Every claim in the Alignment Statement and Alignment Compact traces to evidence in the Prompt 2 pack, cohort input or the preceding template outputs. If a claim cannot be traced, revise or remove it. These documents will be read by the instrument's parties and must withstand due diligence.

### Constraints

- Do not present the three documents as interchangeable. Each has a distinct purpose and audience.
- Do not present alignable or misaligned pathways as current capability in the Alignment Statement.
- Do not include any provision in the Alignment Compact that contradicts the Terms of Engagement.
- Do not include marketing language. None of the three documents is promotional.
- Do not close any document with a summary or a call to action. Each ends when its content ends.
- Do not omit the risk register or the review and revision cycle from the Alignment Compact.
- Do not exceed the length guidelines significantly. If the Evidence Pack runs past forty-five pages, split it into a main pack and a supplementary volume.

## Handoff to Operational Work

The three outputs feed:

**Instrument-party engagement.** The Alignment Statement, sent with the Terms of Engagement, is the first-contact document. The Evidence Pack answers due diligence questions. The Alignment Compact demonstrates that a credible build-out plan exists and is governed by cohort commitment.

**Tender preparation.** When the readiness diagnostic's ready-after-build-out items are complete, the BioHub prepares a specific tender proposal. The proposal operates under the Tender Compact and draws on the Alignment Statement and Evidence Pack for instrument-specific content.

**Partnership formalization.** The Alignment Compact's partnership commitments drive formal engagement with conservation bodies, verification agents, implementation partners and other coordination partners.

**Monitoring and verification build-out.** The Alignment Compact's monitoring and verification architecture section specifies the infrastructure the BioHub commits to build.

**Subsequent instrument alignment.** Run this template again for another instrument or tranche. Where two instruments share verification requirements or governance conditions, cross-reference the earlier Evidence Pack. Where their requirements conflict, the Alignment Compacts state how the BioHub holds both; where the conflict cannot be held, one alignment takes precedence and the other is deferred or withdrawn.

**Multi-BioHub coordination.** Where more than one BioHub in a BioRegion targets the same instrument, the respective Alignment Compacts are coordinated under the BioRegion Charter's multi-BioHub protocols: whether monitoring infrastructure is shared, whether the implementation agent role is held jointly, and whether the Alignment Statements go to the instrument's parties jointly or separately.

## Flags

- This version is a reconstruction. Where the reconstructed wording departs from the lost v0.1 in substance, the cohort should correct it against any surviving copy or working memory of v0.1.

- The Terms test is new in v0.2. It has not yet been run against a live instrument. Test it against the Cape Water bond series (Appendix A) before treating its three marks (compatible, negotiable, incompatible) as settled.

- Instrument documentation is often incomplete in public. Expect TBV marks in the Terms test and the verification deep dive, and plan for direct requests to the instrument's parties.

- The verification methodology deep dive depends on the verification agent publishing its method. Where it does not, Prompt 2 produces a proposal for what the BioHub needs to learn from the agent, and the alignment map stays provisional until that is known.

- Between-prompt cohort review steps are essential to the template. They cannot be delegated to the AI.

## Appendix A: The Cape Water Bond Reference Case

South Africa's Cape Water Performance-Based Bond (JSE ticker FR31PB) is the worked reference case for this template. The bond was issued by FirstRand Bank at ZAR 2.5 billion on 1 April 2026 and listed on the JSE on 17 April 2026, arranged and structured by Rand Merchant Bank, with a five-year implementation period. Investor returns are tied in part to independently verified outcomes from clearing invasive alien plants in the catchments supplying Cape Town. Outcomes-based funders, including the FirstRand Foundation, settle the performance premium against verification. The Nature Conservancy South Africa is the implementation agent for the first tranche, Conservation Alpha is the independent design and verification agent, and the Greater Cape Town Water Fund coordinates the underlying restoration work. RMB describes the transaction as the first of a replicable series indexed to South Africa's Strategic Water Source Areas.

A BioHub operating within a designated Strategic Water Source Area outside the first tranche's geography would run this template against a subsequent tranche. It would apply the Terms test to the bond's documentation, align its Water Retention Landscape restoration work to Conservation Alpha's verification methodology, and commit in the Alignment Compact to the monitoring infrastructure that methodology requires. A BioHub whose streams join a river below the dams the bond pays for would also need to establish whether its water can be counted at all; that question belongs in the Terms test and the verification deep dive.

The reference case is illustrative. Instrument mechanics, regulatory regimes and verification methodologies vary across jurisdictions and asset classes. Check every figure above against the instrument's current documentation before relying on it.
