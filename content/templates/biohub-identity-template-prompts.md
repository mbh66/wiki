The runnable text of the [[templates/biohub-identity-template|BioHub Identity Template]], version 0.3. A founding cohort uses it to produce the [[identity-statement|Identity Statement]], the Field and Lineage Positioning and the [[founding-compact|Founding Compact]] of a [[biohub|BioHub]]. Read the template page first for what the template is for and where it sits in the five-template [[templates/index|founding suite]], and [[templates/running-a-template|Running a Template]] for the conventions every template shares.

Copy each prompt's context and task into a deep-research AI platform with the listed documents attached. Replace the words in square brackets with your own. Do not run a prompt until the cohort work before it is done.

## When to Run This Template

Run this template at the founding of the BioHub, alongside the TIME Diagnostic. The two can proceed in parallel. Where TIME Diagnostic output is available before Prompt 2 runs, attach it. Do not delay Prompt 1 to wait for TIME Diagnostic output.

The BioHub should have:

- A founding cohort assembled (at minimum, a coordinator and two to five founding participants who have committed to the founding work).
- An anchor location settled.
- A working sense of the BioConomy framework.
- Preliminary agreement among the cohort that a BioHub is what is being founded.

Do not run this template with a solo coordinator. The Founding Compact requires a cohort to have standing. Where only a solo coordinator is in place, use the template as a working draft to be brought to the cohort once assembled.

## Prerequisite Documents to Attach

Attach to every submission:

1. Your BioStack and BioConomy foundational document (definitions and dependency direction).
2. Your framework of coordination forms (TIME, or the framework you work within).
3. The Metabolic BioHubs Best Practices Research Brief (May 2026), or the equivalent global field baseline document. Once the Field and Lineage Positioning document is produced in Prompt 3, subsequent templates in the suite inherit the field baseline through that document.
4. Any bioregional field-mapping documents (P4P Archipelago positioning, Design School for Regenerating Earth mappings, or equivalents you work with).
5. Any published essays, briefs, or research the coordinator or founding cohort members have produced.
6. TIME Diagnostic output where available (attach when it becomes available, whether before Prompt 1, before Prompt 2, or before Prompt 3).

## User-Supplied Inputs to Gather

Before running Prompt 1, compile the following:

- **Coordinator profile.** Name, background, prior work in the anchor location and adjacent fields, existing relationships, publication history where applicable.
- **Founding cohort.** Named individuals who have committed to the founding, with their roles, standing in the anchor location, existing bodies of work, and specific commitments to this founding.
- **Anchor location.** The settlement or place from which the BioHub will operate. Include GPS coordinates or named location.
- **Working name candidates.** Any names the cohort has already considered, with reasoning for each.
- **Existing entity or affiliation.** Any pre-existing entity the BioHub might work from (an existing NPC, forum, cooperative, informal association) or affiliate with.
- **Jurisdiction.** Country and sub-national jurisdiction, so entity form and regulatory analysis is grounded in the correct legal context.
- **Existing patronage or funding relationships.** Any grants, memberships, philanthropic relationships, or corporate partnerships already in play, along with their terms.
- **Adjacent BioHubs or coordination bodies.** Any known other BioHubs or bioregional initiatives operating in the anchor location's broader region or in adjacent domains.
- **Published or draft essays and briefs.** Any documents by the coordinator or cohort members that establish intellectual position, along with citation and reception data where available.

## The Three Output Documents

The template's final deliverables are three separate documents produced in Prompt 3, each with a distinct purpose and audience.

1. **BioHub Identity Statement.** A short, formal, foundational reference document. States who this BioHub is: name, founding cohort, anchor location, purpose, position within the field in brief, intellectual lineage in brief, entity form. This is what the BioHub uses to introduce itself. Length: five to ten pages.

2. **Field and Lineage Positioning.** A long, structured referential document. Compiles the global BioHub field mapping, the intellectual lineage with full citations, the research base, the cohort's published work, and the specific contribution this BioHub makes to the field. This is the BioHub's operational reference for engaging with the global field. Length: thirty to fifty pages.

3. **Founding Compact.** A governance and commitment document. Establishes the BioHub as a coordination body worthy of commitment, articulates the founding cohort's commitments to each other, establishes the entity form and patronage architecture, and sets out provisions for cohort expansion, revision, dissent, and dissolution. Co-signed by the founding cohort. Length: five to ten pages.

## Sequencing

- **Prompt 1** produces candidate options across five identity dimensions (name, entity form, patronage architecture, cohort framing, positioning statement) with a recommendation for each.
- **Between Prompts 1 and 2:** the coordinator convenes the founding cohort to review candidates and make selections. This is cohort work and cannot be delegated to the AI. Selections should be documented with reasoning for use in Prompt 2.
- **Prompt 2** builds the field, lineage, and working reference pack. Deep research into the global BioHub field, intellectual lineage, research base, cohort published work, entity form precedents, patronage architecture precedents, and multi-BioHub context.
- **Between Prompts 2 and 3:** the coordinator convenes the cohort to review the reference pack and make final adjustments before document production. This is also cohort work.
- **Prompt 3** produces the three final documents, incorporating cohort selections and reference pack findings.

Run the prompts in sequence, on the same platform where possible.

## Cross-Platform Notes

The prompts are written for portability across ChatGPT Deep Research, Claude Research, Perplexity Deep Research, and Gemini Deep Research.

Prompt 1's naming dimension requires domain-availability checks. Some platforms can perform these in real time; others cannot. Where the platform cannot check domain availability, mark the check TBV and perform it separately using standard registrar tools.

Prompt 2 is the largest research task. Where the platform imposes tight output limits, produce the reference pack in structured sections and assemble them separately.

Prompt 3 produces three separate documents with a combined length that may exceed some platforms' output limits. Where a platform cannot produce all three in a single response, split Prompt 3 into three separate submissions (3a Identity Statement, 3b Field and Lineage Positioning, 3c Founding Compact), each carrying the Prompt 2 reference pack as an attached input.

## Prompt 1: Candidate Identity Proposals

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am a coordinator with a BioHub being founded by [names of founding cohort] in [anchor location]. I am running the BioHub Identity Template. This is the first of three prompts. Its purpose is to produce candidate identity options for the founding cohort to review before deeper reference work begins.

I work within the BioConomy framework, in which a BioHub is the innermost layer of the BioStack (BioHub → BioRegion → BioConomy). More than one BioHub can operate within a single BioRegion. My BioHub's identity should be distinct enough that it is legible as a specific coordination body when peers or counterparties encounter it alongside others.

I am attaching the following foundational documents:

[List attached documents]

I am providing the following context:

- Coordinator profile: [insert]
- Founding cohort: [insert names, roles, standing, existing work, commitments]
- Anchor location: [insert]
- Working name candidates already considered: [insert]
- Existing entity or affiliation: [insert]
- Jurisdiction: [insert country and sub-national jurisdiction]
- Existing patronage or funding relationships: [insert]
- Adjacent BioHubs or coordination bodies: [insert]
- Published or draft essays and briefs: [insert]
- TIME Diagnostic output (if available): [attach or note absence]

### Task

Produce candidate options across five identity dimensions. For each dimension, produce two to four candidates and a recommendation.

1. **Candidate names.** Names the BioHub might adopt. For each candidate, produce:
   - Domain availability check across `.earth`, `.org`, `.co.[country]`, and any other domain conventions the coordinator specifies. Mark TBV where the platform cannot verify in real time.
   - Cultural resonance analysis in the anchor location context, drawing on the languages, historical references, and cultural traditions present in the anchor location.
   - Field legibility analysis: does the name read as a BioHub in the emerging vocabulary; is it distinct from other named initiatives in the global field; does it work in the languages of the anchor location.
   - Trademark or registered-mark risk where identifiable.

2. **Candidate entity forms.** Entity structures the BioHub might adopt, appropriate to the coordinator's jurisdiction. For each candidate:
   - Legal form (non-profit company, cooperative, foundation, informal association, hybrid stack).
   - Governance implications (board composition, member rights, decision-making forms).
   - Patronage-holding implications (what forms of capital the entity can accept and hold, tax status, transvestment compatibility).
   - Cost and complexity of establishment and ongoing maintenance.
   - Precedents in the coordinator's jurisdiction, with named comparable bodies where possible.

3. **Candidate patronage architectures.** How the BioHub holds capital and relates to funders. For each candidate:
   - Mycelial Patronage (transvestment-based, capital held in the commons substrate).
   - Conventional grant model (project-based grants from foundations and government).
   - Member-funded (subscription or membership dues from participants).
   - Corporate partnership (retention-aligned corporate ESG procurement).
   - Hybrid architectures combining the above.
   - For each: what it enables, what it constrains, what it demands of the founding cohort.

4. **Candidate founding cohort framings.** Structural options for how the founding cohort relates. For each candidate:
   - Solo coordinator with advisory circle.
   - Equal founding cohort with rotating coordination.
   - Tiered founding cohort with distinct roles (coordinator, custodians, contributors, advisors).
   - Formal board plus operational team.
   - For each: implications for decision-making, accountability, cohort expansion, and succession.

5. **Candidate positioning statements.** One-paragraph positioning statements the BioHub might use to introduce itself. For each candidate:
   - The framing (finance-and-governance-first; heritage-anchored; catchment-service-provider; cosmo-local research node; other).
   - The audience it is aimed at.
   - The trade-off it makes (what it foregrounds and what it leaves for later documents).

For each dimension, produce a recommendation with reasoning. Recommendations should draw on the cohort's inputs, the anchor location context, the TIME Diagnostic output where available, and precedents from the global BioHub field.

### Output specification

Produce:

1. **Candidate profiles.** One section per dimension, containing the two to four candidates with the analysis specified above.
2. **Comparative tables.** One table per dimension where useful.
3. **Recommendation per dimension.** Naming the recommended candidate, the reasoning, and the specific circumstances under which one of the alternatives would be preferable.
4. **Cohort selection form.** A short document the coordinator can bring to the cohort review, listing each dimension with the recommended candidate, alternatives, and space for cohort decisions and reasoning.
5. **Open questions for the cohort to resolve** before Prompt 2 can proceed.

### Evidentiary discipline

Tag every substantive claim as:

- **IC** (Independently Corroborated) for claims from cited external sources.
- **MS** (Mission-Sourced) for claims drawn from the coordinator's or cohort's attached documents.
- **TBV** (To Be Verified) for plausible claims that need Prompt 2 to substantiate.

Cite entity form precedents, patronage architecture precedents, and field positioning references where available.

### Constraints

- Do not fabricate domain availability. Where the platform cannot verify a domain, mark it TBV.
- Do not fabricate trademark or registered-mark data. Where a real check is not possible, mark TBV and note that a formal search is required before adoption.
- Do not force the cohort into a single option. The cohort needs alternatives to make an informed choice.
- Do not treat entity form as merely regulatory. It shapes what the BioHub can hold, patronage-wise and governance-wise, and these implications should be surfaced.
- Do not default to conventional NGO structures. Consider entity form and patronage architecture together.
- Do not name specific individuals for cohort framing roles without the coordinator's confirmation. Frame roles structurally.

## Prompt 2: Field, Lineage, and Working Reference Pack

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am continuing the BioHub Identity Template. The founding cohort has reviewed the Prompt 1 candidate proposals and made the following selections:

- Name: [insert selection with reasoning]
- Entity form: [insert selection with reasoning]
- Patronage architecture: [insert selection with reasoning]
- Cohort framing: [insert selection with reasoning]
- Positioning statement: [insert selection or note that final positioning will be drafted in Prompt 3 with reference base support]

I am now asking for the full reference pack that will inform the Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, and Founding Compact documents in Prompt 3.

Attached:

- [Prompt 1 output with cohort selections marked]
- [Original foundational documents]
- [Original context inputs]
- [TIME Diagnostic output, now attached if available]

### Task

Produce a comprehensive reference pack for the BioHub, organized into the following ten sections:

1. **Global BioHub field mapping.** Where does the selected BioHub sit in the global field? Include:
   - Named BioHubs and bioregional initiatives with similar positioning.
   - The Metabolic 152-initiative mapping (or its most current version), with attention to any subset most analogous to the founding BioHub.
   - The P4P Archipelago and other network positionings.
   - Regional and continental groupings where applicable.
   - A "closest analogues" section: the three to five BioHubs whose work is most instructive for the founding BioHub, with brief profiles of each.

2. **Intellectual lineage documentation.** Full citations for the lineage the BioHub draws on. Organize by domain:
   - Coordination forms scholarship (Ronfeldt TIMN and its extensions, or equivalent frameworks).
   - Commons scholarship (Ostrom and successors).
   - Commitment pooling, mutual credit, and grassroots economics (Ruddick, Burgess-Bergstra Promise Theory, and adjacent work).
   - Regenerative and bioregional thought (Fullerton, Raworth, Life, Bauwens, Reed, and others in the founding cohort's working library).
   - Pedagogy and formation (Steiner, Brewer, Freire where relevant, along with any pedagogical lineage specific to the anchor location's cultural traditions).
   - Any lineage specific to the anchor location's indigenous or heritage traditions.

3. **Research base documentation.** The specific books, papers, essays, and reference documents that inform the BioHub's work. Organized by theme. Include publication dates and publisher or journal where known.

4. **Cohort published work.** The coordinator's and cohort members' published essays, briefs, research reports, and books. Where possible, include citation counts, republication history, and documented reception (reviews, references by other authors, adoption by other initiatives).

5. **Contribution to the field.** What specific contribution does this BioHub make to the global field? Draw on:
   - The Metabolic assessment of field gaps (finance-and-governance intersection, post-colonial contexts, contested tenure situations, and other gaps identified in the field baseline).
   - The P4P Archipelago's positioning.
   - Any gap or opportunity the cohort has identified through the TIME Diagnostic.
   - The specific position the BioHub occupies (heritage-anchored, catchment-service-provider, cosmo-local research node, or the positioning selected in Prompt 1).

6. **Entity form precedents.** For the selected entity form, provide precedents in the coordinator's jurisdiction. Include:
   - Named comparable bodies with brief profiles.
   - Documented governance experiences (what has worked, what has produced friction).
   - Regulatory considerations specific to the jurisdiction.
   - Cost and time to establish, based on precedent.

7. **Patronage architecture precedents.** For the selected patronage architecture, provide precedents globally and in the jurisdiction. Include:
   - Where the selection is Mycelial Patronage or a transvestment-based model, reference the Michael Cox and Michael Haupt writing on Mycelial Patronage and the case examples they document.
   - Where the selection is a conventional grant model, member-funded, corporate partnership, or hybrid, provide equivalent precedent-writing and case examples.
   - Documented pitfalls of each architecture.
   - Compatibility with the selected entity form.

8. **Multi-BioHub context.** Where more than one BioHub operates in the anticipated BioRegion (or is expected to over the founding horizon), document:
   - Precedents for peer coordination between BioHubs, drawn from the global field.
   - Protocols for shared governance, mutual recognition, and dispute handling.
   - Case examples of successful and unsuccessful multi-BioHub coordination.
   - Implications for the Founding Compact.

9. **Naming and domain research.** For the selected name (and any alternatives still under consideration):
   - Confirm domain availability across the specified TLDs.
   - Perform trademark search where feasible.
   - Verify cultural resonance through primary sources in the anchor location.
   - Note any potential conflicts with named initiatives in the global field.

10. **Cross-pack synthesis.** Which parts of the reference base are strongest? Which are thinnest? Where does the evidence support the cohort's selections, and where does it suggest revision? What further specialized research would strengthen the BioHub's positioning?

### Output specification

Produce all ten sections as a single reference pack document. Length: 40 to 70 pages depending on evidence available. Where possible, include:

- Named citations with source, date, and locator.
- Tables and structured data where the evidence is quantitative.
- Reference lists organized by section.

### Evidentiary discipline

Same IC / MS / TBV tagging as Prompt 1. Every citation should carry source name, date, publisher, and a direct paraphrase or quote locating the claim.

For field mapping and intellectual lineage claims, prioritize primary sources (books, peer-reviewed papers, published essays by the named authors) over secondary summaries.

For entity form and patronage architecture precedents, prioritize official documents from the bodies concerned and jurisdiction-specific legal references.

For cohort published work, use the coordinator's and cohort's own citations and any independent verification available.

### Constraints

- Do not conflate the BioHub's positioning with the field baseline. Distinguish clearly between what the field is and where the BioHub sits within it.
- Do not omit multi-BioHub context. This is load-bearing since more than one BioHub can operate in a single BioRegion, and the Founding Compact must anticipate this.
- Do not present the cohort's published work uncritically. Where reception has been mixed or the work is contested, say so.
- Do not fabricate citation counts, reception data, or trademark search results.
- Do not import the BioRegion Atlas material here. The BioRegion Establishment Template produces that separately. Where a claim depends on BioRegion-specific evidence, mark it as awaiting the BioRegion template output.

## Prompt 3: BioHub Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, Founding Compact

### Context (paste at top of submission)

I am completing the BioHub Identity Template. I have the Prompt 1 candidate proposals with cohort selections and the Prompt 2 reference pack. The cohort has reviewed the reference pack and made the following final adjustments:

- Any revisions to Prompt 1 selections: [state]
- Any additions or revisions to intellectual lineage: [state]
- Any adjustments to positioning statement: [state]
- Any decisions on multi-BioHub protocols: [state]
- Any other resolutions: [state]

I am now asking for the three final documents.

Attached:

- [Prompt 1 output with cohort selections]
- [Prompt 2 reference pack]
- [Original foundational documents]

### Task

Produce three separate documents.

#### Document 1: BioHub Identity Statement

A short, formal, foundational reference document stating who this BioHub is. Structure:

1. Name and founding date.
2. Anchor location.
3. Founding cohort (with roles as agreed).
4. Purpose statement.
5. Position within the field (short version drawing from the Field and Lineage Positioning document).
6. Intellectual lineage in brief (short version drawing from the Field and Lineage Positioning document).
7. Entity form and its implications.
8. Relationship to the BioRegion. Include a placeholder section noting that this will be completed once the BioRegion Establishment Template produces the BioRegion Definition. State the working BioRegion candidate if one has been identified.
9. Relationship to any other BioHubs operating in the anticipated BioRegion (where applicable).
10. Provisions for revision.

Length: five to ten pages. Prose-forward, formal register. This is the document the BioHub uses to introduce itself to counterparties, funders, peer BioHubs, and prospective cohort additions.

#### Document 2: Field and Lineage Positioning

A long, structured referential document. Structure follows the ten sections of the Prompt 2 reference pack, edited into a coherent reference form:

1. Global BioHub field mapping.
2. Closest analogues.
3. Intellectual lineage documentation.
4. Research base documentation.
5. Cohort published work.
6. Contribution to the field.
7. Entity form precedents.
8. Patronage architecture precedents.
9. Multi-BioHub context.
10. Naming and provenance.

Length: thirty to fifty pages. Reference register. Structured for section-by-section reference use. This is what the BioHub uses when engaging with the global field, defending its positioning, and orienting new cohort members and collaborators.

#### Document 3: Founding Compact

A governance and commitment document. Structure:

1. Preamble stating why this BioHub exists as a coordination body (drawing on the anchor location, the ecological and cultural context, the field gap being addressed, and the cohort's shared purpose).
2. Founding cohort commitments to each other (what each contributes, what each receives, what each is accountable for).
3. Entity form and its implications (what the entity can hold, what governance forms bind it, what protections and obligations it creates).
4. Patronage architecture and how capital is held (transvestment provisions where applicable, grant policies, member contributions, corporate partnership terms).
5. Decision-making forms (which decisions require what cohort process; where consensus is required, where majority suffices, where the coordinator holds delegated authority).
6. Multi-BioHub coordination protocols (how this BioHub will relate to other BioHubs operating in the anticipated BioRegion, including mutual recognition, shared governance where relevant, and dispute handling).
7. Custodial and consent principles for cultural, indigenous, and heritage knowledge that the BioHub encounters or coordinates.
8. Provisions for cohort expansion (how new members are added, on what terms, with what process).
9. Provisions for revision (how the Compact itself is amended).
10. Provisions for dissent (how a cohort member registers dissent from a decision, and what standing that dissent carries).
11. Provisions for dissolution (how the BioHub concludes its work if the cohort determines the coordination is no longer viable or needed).
12. Co-signatures section for the founding cohort.

Length: five to ten pages. Commitment register. Co-signed by the founding cohort. This is what the BioHub uses to convene formal coordination relationships and to demonstrate governance standing to counterparties, funders, and peer BioHubs.

### Output specification

Produce all three documents as separately structured sections within a single deliverable, or as three separate deliverables where the platform's output limits require it. Each document should be self-contained (readable without reference to the others) while cross-referencing where appropriate.

For each document, include:

- Full front matter (title, type, status, date, version).
- Table of contents.
- Full body per specification.
- Reference list.
- Revision history section.

### Evidentiary discipline

Same IC / MS / TBV tagging. Every claim in the Identity Statement and Founding Compact should trace to evidence in the Prompt 2 reference pack, cohort input, or attached foundational documents. Claims in the Field and Lineage Positioning should carry their citations inline.

For the Identity Statement and Founding Compact specifically: if a claim cannot be traced to evidence, either revise it or remove it. These documents will be read by counterparties, funders, and peer BioHubs and must withstand due diligence.

### Constraints

- Do not present the Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, and Founding Compact as interchangeable. Each has a distinct purpose and audience.
- Do not import BioRegion-specific claims into the Identity Statement or Founding Compact except as forward references to the BioRegion Establishment Template output. The templates are sequenced and their outputs cross-reference.
- Do not import BioConomy Value Proposition material into any of the three documents. That is the BioConomy Value Proposition Template's work.
- Do not include marketing language in any of the three documents. The Identity Statement is formal. The Field and Lineage Positioning is referential. The Founding Compact is committing. None of them is promotional.
- Do not close any document with a summary or a call to action. Each ends when its content ends.
- Do not omit the multi-BioHub protocols from the Founding Compact. More than one BioHub can operate within a single BioRegion, and the Compact must anticipate this even where no adjacent BioHubs are currently identified.
- Do not omit the provisions for dissolution. Governance forms should include their own end-conditions.
- Do not exceed the length guidelines significantly. If the Field and Lineage Positioning is running past fifty pages, split it into a main volume and a supplementary volume.

## Handoff to the BioRegion Establishment Template

Once the three Identity documents are produced, the cohort proceeds to the BioRegion Establishment Template. The BioRegion template inherits from this template as follows:

**Identity Statement inherits as:**
- The anchor location (BioRegion template no longer needs to ask for this).
- The coordinator and cohort profile (BioRegion template proceeds on the cohort's authority).
- The purpose statement (BioRegion template's scoping proposals will be evaluated against this purpose).
- The working BioRegion candidate where one has been identified (BioRegion template can validate or revise this candidate).

**Field and Lineage Positioning inherits as:**
- The global BioHub field baseline (BioRegion template no longer needs the Metabolic Research Brief attached separately; it is inherited through this document).
- The intellectual lineage (BioRegion template's ecological and cultural boundary reasoning draws on this lineage).
- The known adjacent BioHubs (BioRegion template's multi-BioHub context research extends what is documented here).

**Founding Compact inherits as:**
- The cohort structure and decision-making forms (BioRegion template's cohort review steps operate under this structure).
- The multi-BioHub coordination protocols (BioRegion template's BioRegion Charter draws on these protocols).
- The custodial and consent principles (BioRegion template applies these principles when engaging cultural and indigenous knowledge in the ecological-cultural boundary work).

Attach all three Identity documents to the first prompt of the BioRegion Establishment Template. Preserve the vocabulary and conventions established here across the suite.

## Flags

- The three-document structure (Identity Statement, Field and Lineage Positioning, Founding Compact) is proposed here in parallel to the BioRegion template's tripartite structure. Test against at least one BioHub founding beyond the Overberg case before treating the structure as settled.

- The five-dimension candidate structure in Prompt 1 (name, entity form, patronage architecture, cohort framing, positioning statement) assumes the cohort has open choices on all five. Some founding cohorts arrive with the name or entity form already settled. Where a dimension is settled, the Prompt 1 output should treat it as an input and proceed to the remaining dimensions. Note this to the deep research AI at submission time.

- The cohort review steps between Prompts 1 and 2, and between Prompts 2 and 3, are essential to the template. They cannot be delegated to the AI. This introduces facilitation work for the coordinator and may benefit from a separate cohort-facilitation guide.

- The template runs alongside the TIME Diagnostic. Where TIME Diagnostic output becomes available mid-sequence, attach it at the earliest opportunity so subsequent prompts can draw on it. TIME output particularly informs the intellectual lineage documentation, the field positioning, and the multi-BioHub context.

- The Identity Statement's Section 8 (Relationship to the BioRegion) is a forward reference to the BioRegion Establishment Template. This creates a documented cross-reference across templates. Update the Identity Statement once the BioRegion Definition is produced.

- The Founding Compact's provisions for dissolution may sit uncomfortably at the founding of a cohort committing to durable work. The provision is nonetheless load-bearing: a governance form that cannot specify its own end-conditions is not fully governed. Frame the provision as a mark of governance seriousness at cohort review.

- Naming candidates depend on the deep research platform's ability to check domain availability in real time. Some platforms cannot. Where the platform cannot, mark checks TBV and perform domain and trademark verification through standard registrar and IP-search tools before adoption.

- The multi-BioHub protocols in the Founding Compact anticipate a BioRegion within which other BioHubs may operate. The specific protocols cannot be finalized until the BioRegion Establishment Template has run and any other BioHubs in the BioRegion are identified. The Founding Compact should include working protocols that can be refined once BioRegion evidence is in hand.

- The Metabolic BioHubs Best Practices Research Brief (May 2026) is treated as the field baseline. Where a more recent field-mapping document has been published, substitute it and note the substitution.
