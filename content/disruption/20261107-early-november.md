In the first week of November 2026, US voters handed both houses of Congress to the Democrats and approved local limits on data centers in almost every place that held a vote, and two days later the Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.5 percent in a single session, its worst day since April 2025, as investors repriced the AI build-out. [IC] This page records what happened, what the first evidence says about AI's economic return, and what the week suggests for people building place-based economies. It is one of the wiki's [[disruption/index|disruption snapshots]], which track the decade that began with the closure of the Strait of Hormuz in February 2026.

## Black October

The week came at the end of a month that security researchers and commentators had already named "Black October," for an unprecedented run of cyberattacks, many of them carried out or sped up with AI agents. [IC] The threat intelligence firm ScruteX, which appears to have coined the name, tracked 28 major incidents by 23 October, most of them attacks on software suppliers whose updates then carried malicious code to their customers. [TBV] The month's attacks reached money, medicine and food.

Crypto. On 29 October about $6.3 billion left Binance wallets across ten blockchains, most of it within 20 minutes, the largest crypto theft on record. [IC] Binance confirmed a security incident and suspended some withdrawals. The analytics firm TRM Labs attributed the theft with high confidence to a North Korean state unit; no government had made an attribution by early November. [TBV]

Healthcare. Ransomware groups claimed 85 attacks on healthcare providers in October, the highest monthly count of the year, though only 10 had been confirmed by the providers. [TBV] At the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center, an attack forced the closure of clinics and the diversion of patients. [TBV]

Food. Attacks on supermarket refrigeration began in France in October, and on 2 November fridges and freezers ran hot at more than 100 shops in Belgium, Luxembourg, France, Sweden and Ireland, some reaching 50°C, with food destroyed and units damaged. [IC] Delhaize, Carrefour, Coop and Musgrave's SuperValu stores were among those hit, and Belgian prosecutors opened a criminal investigation. [IC] The EU's agriculture commissioner called the attacks a "deliberate attempt to disrupt" the EU's food supply, while saying food security was not at risk. [IC] No group had claimed them. [IC] Security researchers pointed to remotely managed refrigeration controllers, often run by third-party contractors, with missing updates and weak passwords. [TBV]

For a bioregion, the refrigeration attacks are the instructive case. A town's cold chain now depends on controllers that someone far away can switch off, and local food storage that can run without a network connection is a form of resilience. [structural inference]

## The political shift

CBS News projected on 5 November that Democrats will hold at least 54 Senate seats, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncounted, and estimated a House majority of between 251 and 268 seats once all votes are in. [TBV] Democrats flipped more than 40 House seats and Senate seats in Texas, South Carolina, Kansas and Maine. [IC] In Texas, Gina Hinojosa defeated Governor Greg Abbott, and CBS reported data centers as a major issue in that race. [TBV] Both chambers change hands when the new Congress sits in January 2027. The president keeps his veto, and analysts quoted by CBS expect the practical effect on data centers to come through state and local government more than through federal law. [TBV]

On 6 November the president said he fully backs the AI build-out. [TBV]

## The data center votes

Data centers were on dozens of local ballots, and the measures that limited them passed almost everywhere. Redbud Consulting's live tally on 6 November showed restriction winning 44 of 45 fully counted measures, with a typical vote of about 77 percent for restriction and 73 percent pooled across all votes counted. [IC] It also recorded that restriction had won all six data center measures decided earlier in 2026. [TBV] A law firm review counted 41 measures in 10 states and found results "almost unanimously skewed against data center development." [IC] The counts differ because the two sources drew their lists differently; both show the same direction.

The measures took several forms. [IC]

- Moratoria of six months or 180 days, in several Maine towns.
- Caps of 25 megawatts per facility, in towns in Kansas and Ohio.
- Outright or large-scale bans in a group of Ohio, Kansas and California municipalities.
- A requirement that voters approve large projects, including an 800-megawatt campus proposed for Janesville, Wisconsin.
- Financial guarantees as a condition of hyperscale tax incentives, with noise limits, in Perry, Ohio.

Gary Marcus, an AI researcher and critic of the industry, described the opposition as bipartisan, passing "in red states and blue (and purple, too)." [IC] The reasons voters gave for each measure were not reported in the sources checked for this page. [TBV]

## The market repricing

On Wednesday 4 November the market divided. Shares of the largest technology companies rose while chipmakers and data center operators such as CoreWeave fell. [TBV] One reading offered at the time was that investors wanted the hyperscalers to spend less on data centers and expected the election to force that outcome. [IC]

Thursday 5 November was a broad sell-off. The Nasdaq Composite fell more than 5.5 percent and entered correction, the Nasdaq-100 fell about 6.3 percent and the S&P 500 about 4.4 percent, each the worst day since the tariff shock of April 2025. [IC] CNN put the value erased at $2.5 trillion. [IC] Chip stocks took the heaviest losses: Micron and AMD fell about 17 percent, Marvell about 16 percent, Arm about 13 percent, Broadcom and Intel about 10 percent, and Nvidia and Qualcomm about 9 percent. [TBV] A CNBC broadcast that evening showed the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.84 percent and December gold at about $4,098 an ounce, with commentators discussing whether government support for AI companies could be justified on national security grounds. [TBV]

Friday 6 November brought a partial rebound. The Nasdaq closed at 24,579 (up 1.09 percent), the S&P 500 at 7,377 (up 0.31 percent) and the Dow at 51,286 (down 0.46 percent). [IC] The eight chip stocks named above won back about 36 percent of their Thursday losses on average. [TBV] A Jefferies strategist called the sell-off "reactionary, not a fundamental reset." [TBV]

The AI listings on the calendar are a second pressure point. Anthropic filed for an IPO in October and is expected to list on the Nasdaq in November. [IC] As recently as October, reports put its target valuation at about $2 trillion. [IC] On 5 November the Wall Street Journal reported that three major investors planning to take part said the valuation would likely have to come down, one saying it could be closer to $1 trillion. [IC] SpaceX, which debuted on 12 June at $135 and reached $225.64 four days later, closed at $131.87 on 6 November. [TBV]

## The productivity evidence

The Bank of England's November 2026 Monetary Policy Report estimates that AI has raised UK productivity by about 0.5 percent over three years, adding 0.1 to 0.2 percentage points a year since early 2024. [IC] AI-related activity accounts for just under a fifth of UK GDP growth in 2026, and the Bank attributes most of that to building and deploying AI. Gains from firms using it to work more productively make up a smaller share. [IC] Firms in its Decision Maker Panel report little effect on overall employment so far, though some are slower to hire or to replace staff who leave. [IC] The same firms expect AI to add about 0.9 percentage points a year to productivity over the next three years. [IC]

The finding matters for the market story. Growth that comes from the build-out itself lasts only while the spending continues, so a slowdown in data center construction would remove part of the growth it was meant to justify. [structural inference]

## Rates and inflation

The Federal Reserve, chaired by Kevin Warsh since May 2026, has turned toward raising rates this year, with analysts in October expecting a couple more increases. [IC] Economists have tied part of the inflation pressure to AI investment: data center construction drives demand for memory chips, processors and electricity, and estimates in mid-2026 put the effect at about half a percentage point on core inflation. [TBV] October payrolls rose by 165,000 against forecasts of 59,000, unemployment held at 4.1 percent, and futures markets on 6 November priced better than a 65 percent chance of a December hike. [IC]

This produces an awkward pairing for the AI sector: the spending that props up growth also props up inflation, and higher rates raise the cost of the capital the spending depends on. [structural inference]

## One critic's reading

Gary Marcus wrote the two posts that prompted this snapshot. His argument is that the market rested on an assumption that AI companies could act without consequence, and that the election removed it: "The (new!) thought that companies might actually be held accountable for what they have done has completely spooked the market." [IC] He makes five predictions: AI is not over; the era of very high generative AI valuations and exponentially rising data center investment may be; people will keep using generative AI, now mostly through models combined with tools and loops; more reliable architectures will eventually displace today's systems; and a later generation of AI will be worth a lot of money. [IC] He opposes any public bailout of AI companies. [IC] The opposing reading, from investors quoted on 5 and 6 November, is that the sell-off was a reaction to the election and that spending will continue, with Democrats expected to make peace with data centers. [TBV] Which reading holds will show in capex guidance and the Anthropic pricing over the coming weeks.

## What it means for bioregional practice

These are readings for practitioners, offered as inference from the record above.

Places can say no to extraction, and they did so at the ballot box. The data center measures were local decisions about how much power and water a single facility may draw from a place, and how much ground it may cover. That is [[concepts/retention-logic|retention logic]] expressed through municipal law, and it passed across party lines. [structural inference] A [[glossary/a-e/bioregional-development-organization-bdo|Bioregional Development Organization]] that can show its community what a proposed facility would take out of the local [[glossary/a-e/commons|commons]] gives residents the same tool earlier and with better evidence.

The capital that funded the build-out is repricing, and the cost of money is rising. Long-dated, patient instruments such as [[concepts/performance-based-water-bonds|performance-based water bonds]] and [[concepts/outcome-payments-for-community-restoration|outcome payments]] compete for investors on terms different from growth equity, since they pay against measured outcomes in a place. Higher rates make every long-dated project harder to finance, so the case for them has to rest more heavily on the measured outcome. [structural inference]

The week fits the [[glossary/m-s/s-curve|S-curve]] reading. When an investment wave keeps growing while the productivity it promised stays modest, it shows one of the signs the wiki tracks under [[concepts/deceleration-indicators|deceleration indicators]]. [structural inference] [[research/growth-economics|The S-Curve Research Brief]] sets out the longer argument.

Practitioners in emerging economies should watch their currencies and borrowing costs. A US rate-hike cycle combined with a sell-off in global risk assets has, in past episodes, raised borrowing costs and weakened currencies in emerging markets. [TBV] No South African market data for this week was checked for this page. For the country's longer position, see [[research/south-africa-on-the-curve|South Africa on the Curve]].
