This folder holds brief, dated snapshots of a decade of dramatic disruption to the global economy and its politics, a decade this wiki dates from the closure of the Strait of Hormuz on 28 February 2026.

## Overview

The working hypothesis behind this folder is that the closure of the Strait of Hormuz opened a decade in which shocks arrive faster than the institutions built for the growth era can absorb them, first in energy and shipping and then in markets and politics. The hypothesis is the wiki's own claim. It is under test, and the snapshots are the test. Each one records what happened over a short period, grades the evidence, and asks what the period means for a [[glossary/a-e/biohub|BioHub]] and the people around it.

The folder sits beside the wiki's longer argument about growth. [[research/growth-economics|The S-Curve Research Brief]] holds that the industrial [[glossary/m-s/s-curve|S-curve]] is entering its [[glossary/a-e/deceleration-phase|deceleration phase]], and [[concepts/deceleration-indicators|Deceleration Indicators]] collects the long-run evidence for that claim. The snapshots supply the short-run record: the specific events through which a deceleration is lived. If the hypothesis is wrong, the snapshots should show it, as a run of periods in which the shocks fade and the old arrangements hold.

## Why February 2026

On 28 February 2026, after coordinated US and Israeli strikes on Iran, Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed to shipping, and tankers and container lines stopped transiting. [IC] Before the war the strait carried about a third of the world's seaborne oil exports and about a fifth to 30 percent of its liquefied natural gas exports. [IC] QatarEnergy declared force majeure on LNG cargoes in early March after attacks on its Ras Laffan facilities. [TBV] Most major carriers moved Asia to Europe routing around the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10 to 14 days to the voyage. [TBV]

The closure has not ended. A ceasefire in April and a reopening agreement in mid-June gave about three weeks of reduced traffic before the strait closed again in early July. [TBV] A crisis tracker recorded it as effectively closed on 2 November 2026, day 248, with transits near a fifth of the pre-crisis level, reopening talks stalled, and Brent crude at about $110 a barrel, some 50 percent above its pre-crisis price. [TBV]

The date is chosen as the start because the closure touched every economy at once, through the prices of energy and freight and, behind them, food, and because it has lasted. Earlier shocks of the 2020s (the pandemic, the 2022 energy crisis) were followed by recovery. This one has not been, so far. [structural inference]

## How a snapshot works

- Each snapshot covers a short period, usually a week or a month, and is written soon after it.
- Its file name starts with the snapshot date, written as YYYYMMDD, so the folder sorts in order: `20261107-early-november.md`.
- It opens with a dated table of events, then a section for each strand, then a section on what the period means for bioregional practice, then a list of what to watch.
- Every factual claim carries a tag: `[IC]` for two or more independent reports or an official record, `[TBV]` for a single report or anything unconfirmed. The page's own readings are marked `[structural inference]`.
- A snapshot written after its date says so at the top and uses only what had been reported by that date.
- A snapshot is not updated after its date. Corrections of fact are recorded in its Provenance section; new developments go in the next snapshot.
- Material about one specific place belongs on the BioHubs directory or the W2DF register. A snapshot names a place only as a brief example.

## Provenance

Written on 7 November 2026 at Michael's request as the landing page for a new folder of disruption snapshots. The dating of the decade from the Hormuz closure is Michael's, stated here as the folder's working hypothesis, which sets the page's `epistemic_status`. The account of the closure was checked against news reports and a crisis tracker on 7 November 2026. Iran had also closed parts of the strait for a few hours during military drills on 17 February 2026; the page dates the decade from the closure of 28 February, which has lasted. The seaborne trade shares come from UNCTAD's 2025 figures as reported on 28 February. The first snapshot was moved here from `research/` on the day it was written, before publication.
