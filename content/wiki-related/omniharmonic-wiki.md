The research wiki of [[life-benjamin|Benjamin Life]], author of *An Introduction to Bioregional Economics* (2026). Omniharmonic sits closest to the BioConomy corpus of any external wiki currently listed. Both projects are built on Quartz. Both organize a body of concept pages, essays, and source material around bioregional coordination, commons governance, and the meta-crisis. Life's *Introduction to Bioregional Economics* is one of the BioConomy corpus's most-cited source works, and the material on Omniharmonic extends and updates that treatment.

The wiki's framing borrows a Miles Davis line: *It's not the note you play that's the wrong note. It's the note you play afterwards that makes it right or wrong.* The name gestures at the same question: how fragmented modern systems reach coherence through emergent harmony instead of imposed consensus.

## Editorial approach

Omniharmonic is a research-and-writing project maintained by one author rather than a distributed contributor network. That gives it consistency of voice and framing across the whole corpus, at the cost of the broader case coverage a network wiki like the P2P Foundation's can accumulate. The corpus is organized in three sections: Writing (essays), Concepts (a library of 144 interconnected concept pages), and Structure (the growing knowledge base itself).

## Where its coverage is strongest

Bioregional economics as a discipline. Life's *Introduction to Bioregional Economics* is a founding document for the discipline; the wiki carries the extended treatment, including the Two Machines diagnosis, the four pillars, and the missing-middle question. Where the BioConomy corpus refers to these as external constructs, Omniharmonic treats them as its own primary material.

Post-capitalist transition frameworks. Essays on "Protocols of Place," "Collapse, Parallel Societies, and the Bioregional Imperative," and related themes give a systematic treatment of the terrain the [[concepts/bioconomy-developmental-arc|BioConomy Developmental Arc]] traverses at higher altitude.

Cosmolocalism, polycentric governance, metamodernism, commons stewardship, and the meta-crisis. The 144-page concept library covers this territory in interconnected form, with the same Quartz-style bidirectional linking this wiki uses.

Regenerative accelerationism. Omniharmonic treats this framing as a live category and works out its implications for coordination design.

## Traffic between the wikis

BioConomy pages that already draw on Life's work include the [[life-benjamin|Benjamin Life]] person page, the [[content/glossary/a-e/bioregional-economics|Bioregional Economics]] glossary entry, the [[concepts/bioregional-economics|Bioregional Economics]] concept page, the [[two-machines|Two Machines]] glossary entry, and any citation of the *Introduction to Bioregional Economics* source.

Because the two wikis share a platform, a shared vocabulary, and overlapping source material, cross-linking between specific pages is worth setting up deliberately.
