A campaign that asks rural communities to restore a shared regional good, such as rainfall, holds together when it pays every community for verified stocks that community controls (trees, soil and groundwater) and keeps the regional outcome as its research question; a single long-dated prize for the regional outcome asks communities to work toward something none of them can cause or be credited with.

## Overview

Restoration campaigns at regional scale meet a design problem that local projects avoid. The benefit the campaign is after (earlier rains and fuller rivers) is produced by thousands of separate landholdings acting together, and no one of them can deliver it or prove its share. If the campaign rewards that regional outcome directly, it rewards luck and invites free riding. If it rewards nothing, people respond to the message and do no more work, because the work costs them time and the return is shared with everyone.

This page sets out a structure that answers the problem with tools the wiki already describes. Communities are paid for local stocks they can change and an independent party can count. Customary territories register as light [[glossary/a-e/coordination-surface|coordination surfaces]], so a chief's council can take part without running the full [[glossary/a-e/biohub|BioHub]] founding suite. A citizen-science platform carries the evidence that keeps people engaged and feeds the research. A standing body with a fiduciary host holds the money and the alignment, and the payment record it builds is what a later [[glossary/m-s/performance-based-bond|performance-based bond]] would need.

The worked example is [Bruce Danckwerts](https://substack.com/@pasaradio)' campaign to [Bring Back the Rains to Southern Africa](https://www.radio4pasa.com/bring-back-the-rains), run from Choma in Zambia's Southern Province since 2024. His reports are candid about what has and has not worked, which makes them unusually useful evidence for the design question. The analysis on this page is the wiki's own and has not been tested against the campaign. Material specific to the campaign's territories belongs on the [BioHubs directory](https://biohubs.bioconomy.earth) once a BioRegion takes it up.

## The worked example: Bring back the Rains

### The campaign

Danckwerts, a farmer in Zambia's Southern Province who broadcasts weekly radio programs to small-scale farmers through Radio4PASA, began the campaign in 2024 after a friend suggested using Farmer Managed Natural Regeneration (FMNR) to restore tree cover. He reasoned that trees in one province would make no difference to rainfall, and that restoration would need a chain of trees from the Mozambique coast to the Congo rainforest. He then asked "Why not?" and launched the campaign anyway (Danckwerts, October 2024).

The argument draws on the [[glossary/a-e/biotic-pump|biotic pump]] theory of Anastassia Makarieva and Victor Gorshkov, and on [Judith Schwartz](https://www.judithdschwartz.com/water-in-plain-sight)'s *[Water in Plain Sight](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1250069912)*. Danckwerts attributes the decline in the Zambezi's flow since its 1960s and 1970s peak to falling tree cover, degraded soils and a falling water table, and argues the decline cannot be explained by El Niño cycles [TBV]. He soon widened the aim from trees to three resources: restore the trees, repair the soils so they can receive and store rain, and raise the water table so mature "rain trees" can reach water at the end of the dry season and help start the next rains. His working distinction is between trees in full leaf before the rains arrive and trees that leaf only after (Danckwerts, October 2024; April 2025).

His practical program pairs FMNR (which he calls Konzani Mitengo in Nyanja) with the Moto waMvula ("Fire for Rain"), a stove that cooks on small sticks from FMNR thinning or bush clearing, and with a proposed regional ban on charcoal (Danckwerts, April 2025).

### What the reports record

Three six-month reports and a competition paper record the same pattern.

- **Awareness without uptake.** A headman who had banned tree cutting received a stove and building instructions; two months later his village had built none, though his wife praised the stove. Of the schools in Choma District given stove designs for the national school-lunch program, Danckwerts knew of one that had built one. Five chiefs he met on a 2024 road trip lost interest (Danckwerts, October 2025; September 2026).
- **No income for the work.** A donation drive raised about US$600 of a US$20,000 annual radio budget; bumper stickers sold so slowly that they were eventually given away. Small-stick firewood from FMNR found no buyers (Danckwerts, April 2025; October 2025).
- **One person carrying it.** Danckwerts wrote that he did not want the responsibility of handling money, and that he might not be alive or alert when a 20-year prize fell due (Danckwerts, October 2024; September 2026).
- **No political route.** After attempts to reach ministers and the President, he concluded the campaign would have to proceed without political endorsement (Danckwerts, October 2025).
- **An outcome that will not settle.** He could find no climate model run with different tree cover for the region, and he asked readers to help reconcile a record Limpopo flood with the story of a weakened biotic pump (Danckwerts, April 2025; October 2025).

### The competition and the charter

In September 2026 Danckwerts circulated a proposal, suggested by a fellow traveler, for an inter-community competition [MS]. Communities would register through a phone app, a large prize would go to the community that most improved its trees, soils and groundwater after 20 years, and a smaller annual prize would go to the best community Facebook page. The draft put the main prize at US$50 million and the annual prize at US$200,000, proposed AI judging, and named elite capture of the prize and the legality of offering money not yet raised as unresolved problems [MS].

The Founding Charter published on 23 October 2026 develops the draft (Danckwerts, October 2026). The competing unit is generally a chiefdom. The main prize is "perhaps as big as $200m", and the annual Facebook prize at least US$50,000. Seven principles open it, starting with "evidence before ideology" and including "technology serves people", under which AI and satellite data support human judgement and do not replace it. Governance passes to a board with seats for chiefs, climate science, soil science, hydrology, IT, law and fundraising, members limited to two five-year terms, staggered elections, and a hired project manager. An interim volunteer board could form from January 2027, and the competition would launch only once several years of funding are secured.

The charter answers the succession problem the reports kept raising. The design questions below concern what the competition pays for and to whom.

## Challenge: A prize for a regional outcome will see slow uptake

**Attribution.** Rainfall and river flow integrate the whole region. A chiefdom of 40,000 to 200,000 hectares can change its own tree cover, soil and water table, and cannot change regional rainfall on its own or prove that it did. The biotic pump itself remains disputed in atmospheric science (see [[glossary/a-e/biotic-pump|Biotic Pump]]), so any payment that depends on it carries the dispute into the payout.

**Tournament structure.** A single main prize produces one winner and hundreds of losers. Most communities can see early that they will not win, and the incentive for them falls toward zero for most of the 20 years. The annual prize keeps attention, but a prize judged on a Facebook page rewards whoever tells the story best, which the charter's own first principle warns against.

**Money that does not yet exist.** A prize promised 20 years ahead, from funds not yet raised, asks communities to trust a promise the campaign cannot make from what it controls. [[glossary/m-s/promise-theory|Promise Theory]] names the problem: an agent can promise only what it controls.

**Distribution.** A large sum arriving at once in one territory concentrates exactly the capture risk Danckwerts names. Deciding who receives it after the award leaves the hardest question to the moment of greatest temptation.

## Solution: Pay for stocks a community controls

The [[performance-based-water-bonds|Cape Water Bond]] in South Africa already shows a possible approach. It settles its performance premium on hectares of invasive alien plants cleared, the only payment metric it names publicly, and makes no attempt to settle on megalitres delivered to the city's dams [IC]. Its funders care about water supply and pay for the counted work that produces it, with an independent agent doing the counting.

The same move applied to a rain campaign gives three paid metrics and one research metric.

| Resource        | Paid metric                                                                                                           | How it is counted                                                           | Timing                                               |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trees**       | Hectares under FMNR with indigenous stems retained above an agreed density through one dry season and one fire season | Sample plots by the verification agent; canopy trend from satellite imagery | Paid from year one                                   |
| **Soils**       | Fields with in-field water harvesting and no visible runoff; infiltration rate on sample plots                        | Geotagged photographs, simple ring-infiltration tests                       | Paid from year one                                   |
| **Groundwater** | Dry-season minimum depth in an agreed set of wells in each territory                                                  | Monthly dip readings by trained community members, spot-checked             | Reported from year one, paid on trend from year five |
| **Rain trees**  | Leaf-flush date of each species relative to the first rains                                                           | Geotagged phenology observations                                            | Research only, never paid                            |

Each paid metric is worth paying for whether or not the biotic pump proves strong. Restored woodland, absorbent soil and a higher water table raise yields and firewood supply and lengthen stream flow locally, the case the wiki makes on [[concepts/water-retention-landscapes|Water Retention Landscapes]]. Taking the rainfall hypothesis out of the payment chain removes the scientific dispute from the money while leaving the campaign's founding question open for its data to answer.

Paying every community that delivers also changes the arithmetic of the prize. The charter's US$200 million spread over 20 years is US$10 million a year. Paid per verified hectare, it reaches every performing village every year, and the campaign can still mark the best performers with recognition prizes.

## The Chiefdom Coordination Surface

The full BioHub founding suite (identity, BioRegion and value proposition templates, then the [[templates/place-mandate-template|Place Mandate Template]], then the [[templates/bankable-service-alignment-template|Bankable Service Alignment Template]]) asks more than a chief's council can supply. A [[glossary/a-e/chiefdom-coordination-surface|Chiefdom Coordination Surface]] is the light entry point: a customary territory registers to coordinate with a campaign and its funders by producing three things.

1. **A boundary.** The territory's outline, entered once, with the headman areas inside it marked as the units where work is done. FMNR is household work, so the payment unit has to be small enough to reach households.
2. **Chiefdom Terms.** One page, a short form of the [[glossary/t-z/terms-of-engagement|Terms of Engagement]]: consent for outside projects, who holds the data, how payments are divided, and the graduated sanctions the customary authority will enforce.
3. **Offers, seeks and a baseline commitment.** What the territory can supply (hectares available for FMNR, small-stick firewood, seed), what it needs (a mentor, stove builders, a firewood buyer), and its agreement to the baseline survey.

Registration has to state that it is an act of coordination **and makes no claim on land**. Customary tenure is contested in much of the region, and a published map of registered territories will otherwise be read as a land document. The [[research/landscape-restoration-sub-saharan-africa|survey of community-led restoration in sub-Saharan Africa]] found that restoration spread where people held rights they could defend, and that services sold from land under disputed governance can be suspended by a court; tenure questions come before any sale of outcomes.

Payments go to a village [[glossary/a-e/commitment-pool|commitment pool]] governed by a committee with seats for women and youth. The customary authority receives a fixed stewardship fee for enforcing the Terms, published on the territory's surface. The distribution question that the competition left until the award is settled at registration.

The structure maps onto the design principles [[ostrom-elinor|Elinor Ostrom]] found in long-lived [[glossary/a-e/commons|commons]] (Ostrom, 1990). Danckwerts raised those principles himself in discussion with Makarieva (Danckwerts, October 2025).

| Ostrom principle | Where it sits in the structure |
|---|---|
| Clearly defined boundaries | The registered territory and its headman areas |
| Benefits proportional to contribution | Payment per verified hectare to the village pool |
| Collective-choice arrangements | The village committee and the Chiefdom Terms |
| Monitoring | Community well readings and observations, checked by the verification agent |
| Graduated sanctions | Customary enforcement, paid through the stewardship fee |
| Conflict resolution | Customary courts first, the campaign board on appeal |
| Recognized right to organize | Registration accepted by the campaign and its funders |
| Nested enterprises | Headman area, chiefdom, campaign, financing instrument |

Where more than one territory works toward the same instrument, the Bankable Service Alignment Template's multi-BioHub provisions apply: the campaign body runs the alignment once on behalf of every registered territory, and no chief's council has to run it alone.

## The evidence layer

The Guardians of Earth platform offers a ready form for the territory and its evidence. Each [[glossary/m-s/nature-realm|Nature Realm]] is a geofenced layer for a real place, carries a [[glossary/a-e/bioscore|BioScore]] built from sub-scores that include community engagement and restoration evidence, and takes part in BioQuest rounds each April and October (see the glossary entries). In southern Africa those months fall at the end of the rains and at the pre-rain leaf flush, the moment the rain-tree question needs observers in the field. Expert validation on the platform's Oracle of Life gives the phenology record a check that a Facebook page cannot.

Two limits apply. The verification agent for any payment needs its own method (sample plots, satellite canopy series, well records), and platform data supports that method without standing in for it. The platform's [[glossary/a-e/biocultural-unit-bcu|BioCultural Unit]], a capped and tradeable token, should stay outside the payment chain; a scarcity-priced token beside outcome payments would confuse both funders and verifiers. Whether Realms can nest (territory over headman areas), whether the app captures data offline, and whether validators exist for the region's flora are open questions for the platform [TBV].

## A fiduciary host

The charter's board answers succession. It still needs a host that can hold money in several countries, audit grants and reach villages. A service organization with clubs across the region can play that part. [Rotary](https://d9350.co.za/africa) is a workable option: environment became one of its areas of focus in 2021 [TBV]; its clubs run grants under foundation stewardship rules; its Rotary Community Corps form lets a club sponsor a team of non-members in a village; and the Environmental Sustainability Rotary Action Group links members working on the subject. Its Programs of Scale award gives US$2 million over three to five years to one program a year, and asks for proof of concept, baseline data and a monitoring plan [IC].

District leadership in such organizations rotates each year, which does not match a 20-year campaign. The structure that fits is for the organization to host the campaign body and for the campaign body to govern itself: the charter's staggered board holds decisions, the host holds accounts and escrow, and a sponsoring club for each registered territory supplies the mentor the competition paper asked for. Rotary districts 9350, 9370, and 9400 cover the campaign's countries (all countries south of the Democratic Republic of Congo and Tanzania [IC].

## A route to performance finance

The Cape Water bond rested on years of philanthropic and public funding before issue (see [[concepts/performance-based-water-bonds|Performance-Based Water Bonds]]). A rain campaign needs its own record before any bond can follow.

| Phase                      | Work                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Who pays                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Proof**                  | A small group of territories register; baselines; a verification protocol drafted with an independent university                                                                                                                   | Small grants, service-club grants                                                                                                                                           |
| **Results-based payments** | A fixed price per verified hectare and per qualifying field, paid each year to every performing village pool                                                                                                                       | Outcome funders: foundations, development finance institutions, companies with water exposure downstream, and restoration carbon where it meets current integrity standards |
| **Performance instrument** | A bond or sustainability-linked note: base return from an issuing bank, premium settled on the metrics proven in the previous phase, the campaign body as coordinating vehicle, an implementation partner, an independent verifier | Noteholders and the outcome-funder pool                                                                                                                                     |

The roles carry over from the Cape case. The campaign body plays the part of the Greater Cape Town Water Fund as the coordinating vehicle. An implementation partner with FMNR experience coordinates field delivery. A university or specialist firm verifies. The second phase is where the [[glossary/m-s/readiness-diagnostic|readiness diagnostic]] of the alignment template can be run with real data.

If the campaign raises its main prize fund, the fund itself can serve as the outcome-funder pool.

## What the structure does not solve

- **The firewood market.** FMNR thinning yields fuel only if someone buys it. School feeding programs that cook on biomass are the likeliest early buyer.
- **Stove finance.** Cookstove carbon credits face integrity scrutiny; any claim needs an independent efficiency test first.
- **Contracting and securities law** in each country, which the template's regulatory mapping step has to cover before the third phase.
- **Device access.** The evidence layer assumes smartphones in each headman area and someone trained to use them.
- **The rainfall question itself.** The structure keeps it open, and it will take the full 20 years of observations to inform it.
