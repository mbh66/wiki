Glocalization is the economic form that fits the deceleration phase of the industrial [[glossary/m-s/s-curve|S-curve]]: it keeps what [[bioconomy/concepts/globalization|globalization]] built that every place can share (open knowledge, common standards, planetary communications and observation, cross-border finance instruments) and places it under the governance of place-based economies.

## Overview

[[concepts/globalization|Globalization]] was the economic form of the acceleration phase. It joined national economies into one system of trade, capital flow and production, and for several decades the joining paid off. Cheap fossil energy, unfilled ecological sinks, low-wage labor in newly opened economies and compounding returns to scale made long supply chains and mobile capital the lowest-cost way to organize production. The [[glossary/f-l/global-economy|global economy]] it built remains the outermost layer of the [[glossary/a-e/economy|Economy]].

Glocalization is a separate form with a separate job. Its home is the deceleration phase, when the conditions that rewarded globalization weaken one after another (see [[concepts/deceleration-indicators|Deceleration Indicators]]). In a glocal economy the place governs. The [[glossary/a-e/bioregion|bioregion]] decides what it produces and keeps and what it trades, and it draws on a planetary layer of shared knowledge and infrastructure that globalization left in place. The direction of service is reversed. Under globalization, places serve the global system. Under glocalization, the global layer serves places.

The wiki treats the two as distinct forms suited to different phases of the curve, with glocalization inheriting from globalization selectively. Glocalization also differs from autarky. A glocal economy trades with the world, and it decides what to trade from the standpoint of the place that has to live with the consequences.

## Two phases, two economic forms

### Globalization on the steep part of the curve

The [[frameworks/s-curve-thesis|S-Curve Thesis]] describes the acceleration phase as the period in which [[glossary/t-z/throughput|throughput]] compounds. Its coordination substrate, debt-based money with positive interest, requires growth for its own repayment, and so makes throughput the [[concepts/cheapest-available-behavior|cheapest available behavior]] at every scale from household to state. Globalization extended that throughput across the largest surface available: the whole planet.

Under acceleration conditions this was a fit. Energy return on investment was high, so moving heavy goods across oceans cost little. The atmosphere and the rivers absorbed waste without a price. Returns to scale rewarded concentration of production in whichever place offered the lowest cost. Globalization also produced lasting gains: a planetary communications network, a shared scientific enterprise, common technical standards and a habit of cooperation across borders. These are the parts glocalization keeps.

### Glocalization on the flattening part

Each indicator of deceleration weakens one of globalization's supports. Falling final-stage [[glossary/a-e/eroi|EROI]] raises the real cost of moving heavy things. Transgressed [[glossary/m-s/planetary-boundaries|planetary boundaries]] mean the sinks are full, and their costs return to the places that bear them. Rising debt-to-output ratios show growth being drawn from future claims. The divergence of wages from productivity shows that the gains of growth no longer spread through the population that produces them.

Information is unaffected by these limits. A design, a protocol or a research finding costs almost nothing to move, whatever happens to the price of diesel. The trade record bears this out: services reached 27.5 percent of world trade in 2025, their highest share since 2005 [IC] (WTO). So the arithmetic of the deceleration phase separates the light from the heavy: what is light stays cheap to share globally, and what is heavy grows dearer to ship. Resilience also gains weight against efficiency once a system is near its limits, because redundancy and local reserves are what carry a place through a shock. Glocalization is the economic form built on both observations.

## Where the word comes from

The word has a longer history than its use here. *[[sources/tulloch-oxford-dictionary-of-new-words|The Oxford Dictionary of New Words]]* (Tulloch 1991, p. 134), quoted by Robertson (1995, p. 28), records that glocalization was "formed by telescoping global and local to make a blend," modeled on the Japanese *dochakuka* (from *dochaku*, "living on one's own land"), originally the agricultural principle of adapting farming technique to local conditions and later adopted in Japanese business for global products adjusted to local markets [IC]. [[sources/robertson-glocalization|Roland Robertson]] brought it into sociology in 1995, arguing that the global and the local produce each other and that globalization does not simply make the world uniform. Erik Swyngedouw used the word in [[sources/swyngedouw-neither-global-nor-local|1997]] and again in [[sources/swyngedouw-globalisation-or-glocalisation|2004]] for the simultaneous rescaling of state power and economic organization upward to supranational bodies and downward to cities and regions.

In the business usage, design and capital sit at the center and the local market receives an adapted product. The menu changes and the ownership stays where it was. That arrangement is globalization with a local surface. In the terms of [[concepts/participation-and-conformity|Participation and Conformity]], it governs places by conformity to a central design, with permitted local variation.

This wiki reframes the word for the reverse arrangement. Ownership and governance sit in the place, and the global layer supplies what places can share. Robertson's point that global and local shape each other survives the reframing. The corporate meaning does not. Readers from business backgrounds will meet the older sense first, and pages that use the word should say which sense they mean.

## What glocalization keeps from globalization

Globalization produced some things every bioregion now needs. Glocalization keeps them and changes who governs them.

### Shared knowledge and open science

Research findings and designs circulate globally and improve as they travel. A restoration protocol tested in one catchment can be adapted in another within weeks. The [[glossary/a-e/commons|commons]] of knowledge is the clearest inheritance, and the one [[concepts/cosmo-local-production|cosmo-local production]] is built on.

### Communications infrastructure

Undersea cables and mobile networks let a rural place publish to the world and learn from it. A BioHub in a small valley can use the same tools as a city institution. This infrastructure was built for global markets, and it now carries place-based coordination as well.

### Common standards and protocols

Units of measurement, internet protocols, data formats and accounting conventions let places that have never met work together without a central authority. Interoperability is what makes a federation of bioregions possible. Each place keeps its own rules and still reads the others' records.

### Planetary observation

Earth observation satellites and planetary-boundary science give each place a view of its own condition inside the whole. Remote sensing can verify catchment restoration in places no auditor visits. The same instruments can serve enclosure, which the caveats below address.

### Cross-border finance, directed to place

Globalization built capital markets that move money across borders at scale. A [[glossary/m-s/performance-based-bond|performance-based bond]] uses that machinery to bring outside capital to a defined outcome in a defined place, paid on measured results (see [[concepts/performance-based-water-bonds|Performance-Based Water Bonds]]). The [[concepts/transvestment|transvestment]] pathways describe how value crosses from the Economy into a BioConomy without carrying the Economy's logic with it.

### Cooperation across distance

Globalization accustomed people to working with partners they would never meet. Bioregions learning from one another, sharing failures and adapting each other's methods, use the same habit for a different purpose.

## What it leaves with the acceleration phase

Glocalization sets aside the parts of globalization that only made sense while the curve was steep.

Capital mobility as a first principle goes. In a glocal economy capital is judged by what it builds and keeps in a place, following [[concepts/retention-logic|retention logic]]. Long supply chains for heavy goods stop being the default; they become a deliberate choice for what a place cannot produce. Arbitrage between jurisdictions with weaker labor or environmental protection loses its function once each place governs its own production. The single aggregate gauge of output gives way to measures of what a place holds and regenerates, for the reasons set out in [[research/what-gdp-leaves-out|What GDP Leaves Out]]. Uniform products designed at a center give way to local designs that draw on shared knowledge.

## Globalization and glocalization compared

| | Globalization | Glocalization |
|---|---|---|
| Phase of the S-curve | Acceleration | Deceleration and maturation |
| Governing unit | Firms and nation-states, coordinated through global markets | The bioregion, federated with other bioregions |
| What travels | Goods, capital, labor and information | Information, designs, standards and finance directed to place |
| What stays in place | As little as the market allows | Heavy production, ownership, ecological yield and surplus |
| Capital | Mobile, seeking the highest return anywhere | Anchored, judged by what it retains and regenerates |
| Measure of success | Throughput: output, trade volume, foreign investment | Retention: what the place holds and renews |
| Priority under stress | Efficiency | Resilience |
| Relation to the local | Local adaptation of a central design | Local design drawing on a shared commons |

## Relation to neighboring ideas

[[concepts/cosmo-local-production|Cosmo-local production]] is the production rule inside glocalization: what is light is global, what is heavy is local. Glocalization is the wider economic form, covering governance, finance and measurement as well as making things. Kostakis, Niaros and Giotitsas reach a similar position from the production side in their [[sources/kostakis-niaros-giotitsas-2023|2023 argument]] that the choice between global and local is a false one.

[[concepts/retention-logic|Retention logic]] supplies the measure: value is counted by what stays. [[glossary/m-s/subsidiarity|Subsidiarity]] supplies the rule for where decisions sit. [[concepts/bioregional-economics|Bioregional economics]] supplies the discipline that makes a glocal economy legible.

[[concepts/structural-prematurity|Structural prematurity]] explains why earlier arguments for place-based economies struggled. E.F. Schumacher's *[[sources/schumacher-small-is-beautiful|Small Is Beautiful]]* (1973) and the relocalization movements after it made their case while globalization's cost advantage was still real. The flattening of the curve changes that arithmetic. The arguments were early; the conditions are arriving.

## What it means for a BioHub

For a [[glossary/a-e/biohub|BioHub]], glocalization becomes a set of questions to put to every flow of goods, money, knowledge and people in and out of the bioregion:

1. Is this flow light or heavy? Light flows can travel freely. Heavy flows should be local by default and imported by decision.
2. Who governs it? A flow the place cannot govern is a dependency, however useful it is.
3. Does the global link serve the place? A global platform, buyer or investor earns its place when the bioregion is better able to hold and renew its own wealth because of it.
4. What does the place give back to the global layer? A BioHub that publishes its methods and data, failures included, adds to the commons it draws on.

## Caveats and open questions

The mapping of globalization and glocalization onto phases of the S-curve is a structural inference. It inherits the status of the [[frameworks/s-curve-thesis|S-Curve Thesis]], which the wiki holds as a working hypothesis. The individual deceleration indicators are documented; their reading as a single civilizational curve is interpretive.

Glocalization does not settle who owns the global layer. Much of the communications and observation infrastructure it keeps is privately held, and some of it is built for surveillance and enclosure (see [[concepts/surveillance-capitalism|Surveillance Capitalism]] and [[research/dark-fiber-and-the-ai-overbuild|Dark Fiber and the AI Overbuild]]). A glocal economy that depends on infrastructure it cannot govern carries globalization's dependency forward through a different door. The global layer needs commons governance of its own, and how bioregions secure that is an open question.

The word itself carries risk. Its corporate meaning is older and more widely known, and a firm can describe local adaptation of a central product as glocalization while leaving ownership untouched. The test is where governance sits.

The evidence to November 2026 meets the location half of this page's reading and not yet the governance half. The [[research/glocalization-and-the-evolution-of-production|production-location brief]] finds that the labor saving behind offshoring has narrowed and that risk and carbon costs have returned to the delivered price of heavy goods, so production is moving toward markets. It also finds that the move runs mostly toward political blocs, under the same owners as before, and that production governed in place (cooperatives, citizen energy) exists at real scale without a measured trend toward it. The brief amends the statement "glocalization is the evolution of production" to "glocalization is the latest stage in the evolution of production, in which production moves toward its markets and materials and comes to be governed in the place where it happens," and grades it a working hypothesis.
