Mycelial Consciousness names the fourth Cognitive Major Evolutionary Transition: the shift from Material to Mycelial modes of perception, cognition, and coordination that the current civilizational transition is calling for and, in some places, already producing.

## Overview

Major Evolutionary Transitions in biology (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry, 1995) describe the small number of moments in the history of life where the level of biological organization itself changed: they count eight, among them the steps from replicating molecules to bounded cells, from prokaryotes to eukaryotes, from single cells to multicellular organisms, from solitary organisms to colonies, and from primate societies to human societies with language. Each transition produced a level of organization that had not existed before, capable of feats the prior level could not achieve alone.

Cognitive METs (Godfrey-Smith, Ginsburg and Jablonka) apply the same framework to cognition. They identify a sequence of transitions in how organisms perceive, learn, remember, and coordinate. The Mycelial Consciousness thesis applies the idea to human consciousness: five structures joined by four transitions (see [[concepts/major-evolutionary-transitions|Major Evolutionary Transitions]]). It proposes that the fourth is now underway, from Material Consciousness (the operating mode of the last several centuries) to Mycelial Consciousness (the operating mode that what is emerging requires).

The framework is a working hypothesis. It draws on Sahtouris and Margulis in biology, on Graves and Steiner in psychology and social philosophy, on Ronfeldt in coordination theory, and on the practitioner traditions (Bauwens on cosmo-local, Ruddick on commitment pooling, Life on [[glossary/a-e/bioregional-economics|bioregional economics]]) that are producing the observable expressions of the transition.

## The five stages

The Mycelial Consciousness framework proposes a five-stage sequence of consciousness evolution, each associated with an information revolution and a dominant coordination form:

- **Mimetic**: early imitation-based social learning; the substrate on which all later forms rest.
- **Magical**: animistic perception, the world as agentic and personal; the substrate of the Tribal (+T) form.
- **Mythic**: narrative-based coordination through shared story; the substrate of the Institutional (+I) form.
- **Material**: objectifying perception; the substrate of the Market (+M) form and of the peak of the civilizational S-curve.
- **Mycelial**: network-relational perception; the substrate of the Emergent (+E) form.

Each stage does not replace the prior stages. It integrates them. A person operating in the Mycelial mode has not lost the capacity for material analysis or mythic narration; they hold each mode as a tool available to the task at hand, and none of them is the single lens through which all reality must be understood.

## The endosymbiosis analogy

The biological precedent that best illustrates Mycelial Consciousness is [[glossary/a-e/endosymbiosis|endosymbiosis]]. Roughly two billion years ago, one single-celled organism engulfed another. The host cell kept it alive and incorporated it. The engulfed organism became the mitochondrion, and every complex cell on Earth now runs on the energy that partnership produces. Lynn Margulis argued that symbiosis drove key steps in the evolution of life, beginning with the complex cell: formerly separate organisms became structurally incorporated into new, higher-order wholes whose capabilities exceeded anything either partner could achieve in isolation.

Mycelial Consciousness is the human expression of this pattern. It is the capacity to hold multiple perspectives simultaneously without collapsing into any single one, to see what is composting and what is sprouting at the same time, and to collaborate with people whose worldview one does not necessarily share, because the challenges facing the species cannot be addressed by any single perspective operating alone.

[[stretch-collaboration|Stretch Collaboration]] is the practical form the capacity takes. It captures the endosymbiotic quality of what is required: the integration of formerly competing entities into a functional whole that is more capable than any of its parts.

## Relationship to Mycelial Coordination

Mycelial Consciousness is the psychological and perceptual dimension; [[concepts/mycelial-coordination|Mycelial Coordination]] is the social and institutional dimension. The frameworks are complementary, and neither comes first. Mycelial Consciousness is what makes Mycelial Coordination possible in practice, and Mycelial Coordination is the substrate in which Mycelial Consciousness matures.

The relationship is analogous to the relationship between literacy and the printing press. Literacy is a cognitive capacity. The printing press is a coordination technology. Each enabled the other; neither would have taken hold without the other.

## Cross-reference to the Three Futures

Mycelial Consciousness is the psychological substrate the [[frameworks/three-futures|Transcendence]] trajectory requires. Clare Graves called it [[second-tier-thinking|Second Tier thinking]]: the leap from deficiency motivation to growth motivation, from getting to giving, from destroying to constructing. Graves was writing in 1974 and identified the leap as the most significant threshold in human psychological development. The [[frameworks/emancipation-architecture|Emancipation Architecture]] is one attempt at building the institutional infrastructure that would make the leap possible at civilizational scale.
