The Impact Measurement Guidelines are seven practices for measuring the social and environmental effect of an investment, set out in *Measuring Impact*, the September 2014 subject paper of the Impact Measurement Working Group of the Social Impact Investment Taskforce, established under the UK's presidency of the G8.

## Overview

The working group of 24 practitioners wrote for impact investors, profit-seeking or not, and for the organizations they invest in. Its founding principle is that measurement should help an organization manage its performance, learn, improve outcomes, and answer to the people it means to serve.

The paper groups its seven guidelines under four phases (Plan, Do, Assess, Review) adapted from the UK program Inspiring Impact. It stresses that the steps interact, that their order and frequency vary, and that every organization will apply them differently.

For the [[glossary/a-e/biohub|BioHub]] corpus the guidelines matter because funders of place-based work ask for them, often in this vocabulary. A BioHub that can show its measurement in these terms can talk to impact capital without adopting impact capital's assumptions.

## The seven guidelines

| Phase | Guideline | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Set goals | State the difference the investment seeks to make, and a clear thesis (the paper calls it a Theory of Value Creation) to guide planning and judge performance. |
| Plan | Develop framework and select metrics | Choose the metrics, and build a framework that says how each piece of data is captured and used. Use metrics that align with existing standards. |
| Do | Collect and store data | Capture data on time and in order, with tools, people and methods that work. |
| Assess | Validate data | Check that data is complete and transparent by cross-checking calculations and assumptions against known sources. |
| Assess | Analyze data | Draw out what the data shows about progress against the goals. |
| Review | Report data | Share progress with stakeholders in a form they can rely on and use for decisions. |
| Review | Make data-driven management decisions | Strengthen the process, take stakeholder feedback on the reports, and change the thesis where the evidence says so. |

Before starting, the paper asks each organization to judge its readiness: what it wants measurement for (reporting, better decisions, or payment for success), what resources it has, and what outside parties require.

## Three trends and a long-term aim

The paper named three outside trends it expected to shape the field: the convergence of impact investing with mainstream markets, the attempt to put financial values on social outcomes, and growing attention to unforeseen external impacts such as carbon emissions and water use.

Its long-term aim was impact data that is material, reliable, comparable, "additional" and universal, supported by a shared measurement convention. It called on everyone in the field to treat "impact accountability" as a common value, apply the guidelines, build a common impact language and data infrastructure, and keep learning.

## What it offers a BioHub

- **A structure funders recognize.** A BioHub's program can state its goals, metrics, data, validation and reporting in this order.
- **Validation that already exists.** The fact-status tags used across this wiki ([IC], [MS], [TBV]) function as a validation layer: every claim says how it was checked.
- **A link to verification.** [[concepts/performance-based-water-bonds|Performance-based water bonds]] pay on verified outcomes, which is the "payment for success" case the paper names. Its Validate step is where an independent verifier sits.
- **A loop back to the work.** The Review phase matches the "thinking and working politically" practice in [[frameworks/political-economy-analysis|political economy analysis]]: evidence changes the plan.

## Where the wiki differs

The paper treats impact as something investors measure alongside a financial return. The wiki's entry on [[glossary/f-l/impact-investing|impact investing]] reads that arrangement as one in which capital keeps its exit and the impact can be dropped without breaking the instrument. [[glossary/t-z/transvestment|Transvestment]] is the wiki's alternative: capital that crosses into the regenerative regime and is bound to the outcome.

The guidelines themselves are neutral between the two. Goals, metrics, data, and review serve a commitment pool as well as a fund. What changes is who sets the goals. In a BioHub, the place sets them, and the investor's money aligns to them, which is the foundation principle that [[concepts/direction-of-fit|direction of fit]] describes.

## How it joins the other frameworks

- The [[frameworks/sustainable-livelihoods-framework|Sustainable Livelihoods Framework]] supplies outcome measures: the five capitals and the five livelihood outcomes.
- [[frameworks/political-economy-analysis|Political economy analysis]] says which outcomes depend on a bargain that has not yet been struck, so that measurement does not blame a program for a stall it cannot control.
