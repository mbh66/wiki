A [[biohub|BioHub]] that carries capital functions has to meet thirty-nine requirements drawn from Benjamin Life's two essays on [[glossary/a-e/bioregional-economics|bioregional economics]], running from organizing work that precedes any money to the mutualist institutions of a mature bioregional economy.

## Overview

[[life-benjamin|Benjamin Life]] published *An Introduction to Bioregional Economics* in two parts during 2026. Part I sets out the design principles of a bioregional economy: the [[two-machines|Two Machines]] diagnosis, four pillars and two commitments. It closes on the question of the [[missing-middle|missing middle]]: who convenes the cooperatives, who capitalizes their shared infrastructure, and what institution can hold a bioregional economy in view. Part II answers with two meso-scale institutions. The [[bioregional-development-organization-bdo|Bioregional Development Organization]] (BDO) plans and capitalizes the economy as one system. The [[bioregional-learning-centre|bioregional learning center]] grows the capacity to steward it.

This wiki treats a BioHub that carries capital functions as a BDO in Life's sense. The table below gathers every requirement the two essays place on that institution and its economy, so that a BioHub cohort can see what Life's framework asks of it in one place. Each row names the source essay and the stage at which Life places the requirement.

Life does not discuss BioHubs. Reading his BDO requirements as BioHub requirements is this wiki's construction. Life's BDO serves a whole [[bioregion|bioregion]], while a BioRegion in this wiki may hold several BioHubs that either federate as BDOs or seat themselves inside one capital-holding BioHub. The BioRegion Charter records which arrangement applies, and that choice changes how rows 4, 19 and 23 apply.

## How to read the table

- **Src.** "I" is Part I (3 August 2026). "II" is Part II (25 October 2026).
- **Stage.** *Before money* covers work Life places ahead of significant capital. *Founding* covers the plan and the institution's constitution. *Pilot* covers the first closed loop, usually food. *Maturity* covers what follows once a vertical has closed. *Throughout* and *onward* mark requirements that persist.

The capital and facilities rows draw on the [[frameworks/bioregional-financing-facility|Bioregional Financing Facility]] of Samantha Power and Leon Seefeld, which Life places beneath the BDO. Rows 8 and 9 draw on carrying-capacity analysis and the [[metabolic-study|metabolic study]]. Row 15 restates [[subsidiarity|subsidiarity]] as one of Life's two commitments. Rows 24 and 34 concern assets and governance held in [[commons|commons]]. Rows 27 to 29 describe the [[federated-cooperative|federated cooperative]] structure Life sets out in Part I.

## The requirements

| # | Area | Requirement | What it involves | Src | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Precondition | Organizing comes before money | "The organizing precedes the money, always." Organizing teams, community trusts and grassroots networks have to exist before capital is sought. | II | Before money |
| 2 | Precondition | Education and capacity | Learning is a precondition for forming a bioregional economy. That capacity is grown at a bioregional learning center (row 25). | I, II | Before money |
| 3 | Precondition | Enough aligned enterprises | There must be enough values-aligned producers and cooperatives for loops to close. | II | Before money |
| 4 | Scale | Bioregional boundary | Boundaries follow the watershed or bioregion. County and municipal lines do not set them. The bioregion is the "middle band" where loops can close and people still know each other. | I, II | Founding |
| 5 | Scale | Case for the meso-scale | An institution between village and nation, with enough "requisite variety" to handle landscape-level complexity. | II | Founding |
| 6 | Mandate | Transmute the Regional Development Organization (RDO) | Keep the RDO's convening role, strategy instrument and public-private architecture. Add the power to deploy capital. Invert ownership so that value "composts downward into community." | II | Founding |
| 7 | Plan | Bioregional Development Plan | Three maps braided together, held as a "living map" and redrawn as the economy develops. It operates inside a Regenerative Development District. | II | Founding |
| 8 | Plan | Carrying-capacity analysis | Water budget, soil carbon and foodshed potential, using thresholds-and-allocations methods (r3.0). Life calls setting the needs, capacities and carrying budget "the founding act." | I, II | Founding |
| 9 | Plan | Metabolic study | Traces material and money flows, imports and leakage, and finds where circular loops could close. It sets the circulation goals inside the carrying-capacity limit. | II | Founding |
| 10 | Plan | Social map | Drawn with frontline communities and Indigenous nations as co-authors. It measures the gap between current conditions and an adequate social foundation. | II | Founding |
| 11 | Plan | Governance map | Seats and decision rights are fixed before significant capital arrives: "governance negotiated in the presence of capital is governance negotiated at gunpoint." | II | Before money |
| 12 | Plan | Investment thesis | Organized around food, health, housing and ecology, resting on energy, water and infrastructure. | II | Founding |
| 13 | Governance | Four membership classes | Sensing layer (organizers, trusts, networks), productive layer (cooperatives and regenerative enterprises), capital layer (family offices, foundations, aligned funds, community investors), and public layer (commons and the more-than-human). | II | Founding |
| 14 | Governance | Sensing-layer majority | The sensing layer holds structural authority over the plan with real decision rights. | II | Founding |
| 15 | Governance | Subsidiarity and consent | Decisions sit at the most local scale able to hold them, and no state, market or movement coordinates by coercion. | I | Founding |
| 16 | Anti-capture | Steward-ownership | Provisions that make the institution unsellable. | II | Founding |
| 17 | Anti-capture | Exit clauses | Member facilities can fork and take their assets with them. | II | Founding |
| 18 | Anti-capture | Open books | Accounts are transparent across the whole consortium and every cooperative node. | I, II | Founding onward |
| 19 | Capital | Evergreen fund of funds | It capitalizes the facilities that choose projects and rarely picks projects itself. It turns many small projects into one investable thesis at institutional ticket size. | II | Maturity |
| 20 | Capital | Direct underwriting of shared infrastructure | Only for assets no single facility can carry: processing plants, land acquisition, an energy commons. | I, II | Pilot onward |
| 21 | Capital | Blended capital stack | Catalytic philanthropy takes first loss, patient equity sits in the middle, and community investment notes come later. Returns are tied to the health of the whole portfolio and the whole economy. | II | Phased |
| 22 | Capital | Ways past the three ceilings | Designs that end dependence on philanthropy, close the "open loop" so capital stops leaking into extraction, and bridge the aggregation gap. | II | Throughout |
| 23 | Facilities | Four BioFi facilities, phased | Bioregional trust, venture studio, investment company and bank. The trust comes first and tests governance while stakes are low. Each facility is designed from the start as one of four organs, and the plan sets the conditions for activating the later ones. | I, II | Phased |
| 24 | Facilities | Commonified assets | Land trusts, housing stock and processing facilities held in common, beyond the reach of an acquirer or a downturn. | I | Pilot onward |
| 25 | Capacity | Bioregional learning center | A site where financial, social, intellectual and living capital convert into one another: reskilling, apprenticeships, ritual and gift exchange. Its ancestor is the Grange hall. "A BDO without a BLC deploys capital into a culture that cannot yet receive it." | II | Before money onward |
| 26 | Capacity | Learning center physical kit | Demonstration farm, tool library and teaching kitchen. | II | Pilot |
| 27 | Enterprise | Cooperative ownership | Worker or multi-stakeholder ownership in each unit, so surplus goes to members and costs cannot be pushed onto distant parties. | I | Pilot onward |
| 28 | Enterprise | Federation ("cooperative of cooperatives") | Shared processing, storage and aggregation held by the federation, with capital pooled through special purpose vehicles and cooperative banks. | I, II | Pilot |
| 29 | Enterprise | Intercooperation mechanisms | Solidarity funds (Mondragón), multi-class membership, mutual vesting, intercooperation equity pools, and workers able to move between co-ops without losing rights. | I, II | Pilot |
| 30 | Sequence | Pilot vertical | One loop closed end to end, food first. Grant-funded convenings gather producers into a co-op, and chokepoints are funded in order of severity. | II | Pilot |
| 31 | Sequence | Consumers as a member class | Opt-in thresholds: member, patron, currency-holder, investor, worker-owner. Household spending moves over through patronage. | II | Pilot |
| 32 | Sequence | Fund of funds capitalized after closure | Comes only once a vertical has closed and there is a governance track record, audited flows and a pipeline. | II | Maturity |
| 33 | Sequence | Flywheel across verticals | Foodshed, then housing, then energy commons and care co-ops. The flywheel compounds through obligation clearing (WIR), rent payable in local currency, and rising density. Risk is managed by concentrating interdependence. | II | Maturity |
| 34 | Public partners | Public partnership on defined terms | Municipalities contribute land, financing capacity or regulatory alignment, and the commons keeps governance. They usually join once capacity has been shown. | II | Maturity |
| 35 | Money | Bounded and asset-backed currencies | Several community currencies with exit penalties acting as a firewall, issued against verified regeneration. None is issued as debt. Mutual credit and import substitution sit alongside them. | I, II | Maturity |
| 36 | Trade | Rules for trade across the boundary | Essential provisioning (food, shelter, energy, care) is met locally. Only genuine surplus is traded, and trade is judged by what each place is ecologically suited to produce, which Life puts ahead of lowest price. | I | Maturity |
| 37 | Coordination | Shared digital commons | Open books, shared provenance and patronage protocols, cooperative treasuries, and ecological ledgers (for example Regen Network). | I, II | Pilot onward |
| 38 | Measure | Multi-capital accounting | Wellbeing is both the purpose and the measure. It covers internal circulation share and leakage, verified soil, water and biodiversity state, carrying-capacity metrics residents can read, and wellbeing baselines from the social map. | I, II | Throughout |
| 39 | Later phase | Mutualist insurance pools | Life lists these among the mature-phase institutions. | II | Maturity |

## The sequence in brief

Life gives the requirements an order. Organizing, the learning center and the governance map come first. The plan follows, built from carrying capacity, the metabolic study and the social map. The bioregional trust opens as the first facility and funds the convenings that gather producers into a cooperative. One vertical, usually food, closes end to end. Only then does the fund of funds formally capitalize, public partners join, and the flywheel carry the economy into housing, energy and care.

## What the essays leave open

- **Measurement.** Life names a handful of metrics and says outright that the field has no complete measurement framework. The [[templates/metabolic-study-template|Metabolic Study Template]] now carries rows 8, 9 and 38, and [[templates/bioregional-development-plan|Assembling a Bioregional Development Plan]] sets out the process for rows 1, 7 and 10 to 12.
- **Legal form and registration.** Neither essay specifies entity types, jurisdiction or registration. In the founding suite these come from the [[bioregion-establishment-template|BioRegion Establishment Template]] and the [[biohub-identity-template|BioHub Identity Template]].
- **More than one BioHub per BioRegion.** Rows 4, 19 and 23 assume one BDO per bioregion. Where a BioRegion federates several BioHubs, the Charter assigns each capital function to a BioHub or to the federation.

Compiled 28 October 2026 from the published texts of Benjamin Life's two essays: *An Introduction to Bioregional Economics (Part I)* (omniharmonic, 3 August 2026) and *The Missing Middle of Bioregional Development* (Part II, omniharmonic, 25 October 2026).
