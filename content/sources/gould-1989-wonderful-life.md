Stephen Jay Gould (1989). *Wonderful Life: The Burgess Shale and the Nature of History*. W. W. Norton, New York.

Gould reads the Burgess Shale fauna as evidence that the history of life is contingent [TBV]. He proposes a thought experiment of "replaying life's tape" (p. 48, as quoted in Blount, Lenski and Losos 2018) [TBV]. He expects that a replay would send evolution down another path, with little chance that anything like humans would evolve again [TBV].

Zachary Blount, Richard Lenski and Jonathan Losos review the experimental tests of the idea in *Science* 362(6415) (2018), DOI 10.1126/science.aam5979 [IC]. They find evidence of both contingency and repeatable outcomes, and they note that Gould used the word contingency in two senses [IC]. The book was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a palaeontologist's argument that the history of life has no built-in direction [TBV].
