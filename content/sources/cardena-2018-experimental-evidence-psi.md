Etzel Cardeña (2018). *The Experimental Evidence for Parapsychological Phenomena: A Review*. American Psychologist 73(5): 663-677. DOI: [10.1037/amp0000236](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000236).

Etzel Cardeña, director of the Centre for Research on Consciousness and Anomalous Psychology at Lund University, sets out the domain of what the field calls psi, summarises theories from physics and psychology that he presents as making psi at least plausible, and then reviews recent and updated meta-analyses [IC]. The categories include forced-choice and ganzfeld studies of anomalous cognition and studies of anticipation before random events [IC]. He finds the evidence "comparable to that for established phenomena" in psychology and other disciplines, and grants that there is no agreed understanding of how psi works [IC]. He closes with recommendations, among them project and data repositories and multidisciplinary studies with enough statistical power [IC].

The review appeared in the flagship journal of the American Psychological Association and drew wide attention [IC]. Arthur Reber and James Alcock replied, first in *Skeptical Inquirer* (2019) and then in *American Psychologist* (2020), arguing that psi claims cannot be true because they conflict with established science; Cardeña and others answered that the reply sets the data aside [IC].

The wiki records the review as the main current statement of the case for step T2, and reads it beside its critics. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.
