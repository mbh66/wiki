Paul Edwards (1996). *Reincarnation: A Critical Examination*. Prometheus Books, Amherst, NY. 313 pages.

Edwards, the editor of Macmillan's *Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, sets out the main arguments for reincarnation and karma and argues that each one fails [IC]. His chapters cover the moral argument, the law of karma, hypnotic regressions, spontaneous memories, the astral body, the population problem and what happens between lives, according to the Internet Archive catalogue record [IC]. Two chapters address Ian Stevenson. Drawing on an unpublished report by Champe Ransom, a former assistant of Stevenson's, Edwards argues that leading questions and late interviews may explain the details in the cases [IC].

The Society for Psychical Research's *Psi Encyclopedia* replies that Edwards summarised Ransom without Stevenson's answers to him [IC]. A review by Peg Tittle praises the book's rigour and faults its ad hominem remarks [IC].

The wiki takes from it the main critical reply to Stevenson's cases, on both the survival claim and the information claim [TBV].
