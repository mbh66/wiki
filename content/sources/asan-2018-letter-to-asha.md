Autistic Self Advocacy Network (2018). "ASAN Letter to ASHA On The Right To Communicate". 2 July 2018 [IC]. [Autistic Self Advocacy Network](https://autisticadvocacy.org/2018/07/asan-letter-to-asha-on-the-right-to-communicate/).

The letter, signed by Samantha Crane as ASAN's Director of Public Policy, urges an ad hoc committee of the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association against adopting its proposed statements on facilitated communication and the Rapid Prompting Method [IC]. It objects that the committee did not engage with self-advocates, and that ASAN was refused contact with its members [IC]. It states: "We do not take positions on individual communication support methods" [IC]. It argues that each non-speaking person has a right to the method that works best for them, judged case by case [IC]. It holds that none of the cited studies supports a blanket finding that facilitator-dependent messages are inauthentic, and notes that other methods of augmentative and alternative communication have not been tested by message-passing either [IC].

ASAN opposed ASHA's final statements later in 2018 [TBV]. Those statements are filed as [[sources/asha-2018-facilitated-communication|ASHA (2018)]] [IC]. The letter was read in full on ASAN's site [IC].

The wiki takes from it the self-advocates' published case on access and authorship [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes no position for it: the letter makes no claim about telepathy or unexplained information, so it is silent on T2.
