Morgan, T. (2020). "Solving the Wickedest Problem: Reconciling Differing Worldviews." *Journal of Futures Studies* 24(4): 83-94. DOI: [10.6531/JFS.202006_24(4).0008](https://doi.org/10.6531/JFS.202006_24(4).0008).

Tim Morgan, of the North Texas Foresight Institute, joins David Ronfeldt's [[frameworks/timn-framework|TIMN framework]] to Andy Hines's sequence of worldviews, which Hines drew from models such as Maslow's, Kohlberg's and Graves's [IC]. He gives each TIMN form a worldview and its own test of truth, from shared narrative in the tribe to meaning in the network [IC]. He argues that the networked worldview has emerged strongly over the past century, against Ronfeldt's view that it is still in its infancy, and that the older worldviews now block it [IC]. He calls that deadlock our wickedest problem, and holds that any solution must give space to every worldview, since none is superior to another [IC].

The essay is the one independent account of [[sources/ronfeldt-timn|Ronfeldt's 1996 paper]] found so far, and the research uses it to confirm two of that paper's squares. Ronfeldt thanks Morgan in the acknowledgements of his 2026 paper on the noosphere [IC].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a context work in futures studies, with evidence type E4 and claim codes B2, B3, C1, C2, C3, C4. Morgan writes of worldviews, which he calls our perceptual and cognitive maps of the world, so his squares are read against the claim chain's structures of awareness directly; TIMN itself, a framework of forms of organisation, qualifies every step it holds.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it qualifies T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
