Shannon Des Roches Rosa (2026). "The Telepathy Tapes Is Such A Hot Mess". *Thinking Person's Guide to Autism*, 9 February 2026 [IC]. [Thinking Person's Guide to Autism](https://thinkingautismguide.com/2026/02/the-telepathy-tapes-is-such-a-hot-mess.html).

Rosa accepts that nonspeaking autistic people are underestimated, and calls the claim that they are telepathic "questionable at best" [IC]. She argues that casting people who use augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) as spiritual messengers is patronising, and that many who gained communication later in life carry trauma and isolation [IC]. She warns that telepathy claims could suggest AAC users do not need language-based communication, which she treats as a human right [IC]. She quotes [[sources/whitty-2025-on-telepathy-tapes|Danny Whitty's]] objection [IC]. She cites an autistic commenter who reads nonverbal cues well and reports no telepathy, and treats such sensitivity as the likeliest account [TBV]. She holds that the podcast should not be used to doubt that nonspeakers communicate [IC].

No published reply was found [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a disability-rights critique that keeps the telepathy claim apart from the right to communicate [TBV].
