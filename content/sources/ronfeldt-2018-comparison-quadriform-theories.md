Ronfeldt, D. (2018). "Comparison of TIMN to Three Parallel Theories: Striking Similarities, Key Disparities." Draft chapter, posted on the P2P Foundation wiki as "Comparison of Four Quadriform Theories of Social Change" [IC]. The text matches Ronfeldt's blog series "Notes for a quadriformist manifesto, #3" (September to October 2018); the date of the wiki posting is unconfirmed [TBV]. [Read online](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Comparison_of_Four_Quadriform_Theories_of_Social_Change).

David Ronfeldt sets his TIMN framework beside three other theories that divide social life into four parts: Kate Raworth's doughnut economics, Michel Bauwens's peer-to-peer theory of state, market, commons and household, and Kojin Karatani's modes of exchange [IC]. He finds strong likenesses and states where they part [IC]. He holds that all four TIMN forms are always present, that each rising form modifies the others, and that advanced societies are on the cusp of a fourth form, which will build a bounded commons sector for health, education, welfare and the environment [IC]. Bauwens's work underpins the network's account of cosmo-local production, so the chapter sits close to the network. It was read from the wiki text, and no independent account of it has been found [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in political science, with evidence type E4 and claim codes B3, C1, C2. It speaks of forms of organisation where the claim chain speaks of structures of awareness, so every grid square it holds qualifies.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T4; it is silent on T6; it qualifies T7; it qualifies T9.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|claim grid]] codes its positions on the claim chain: it qualifies B3, C1 and C2.
