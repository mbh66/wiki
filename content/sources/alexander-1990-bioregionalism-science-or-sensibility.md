Donald Alexander (1990). *Bioregionalism: Science or Sensibility?*. Environmental Ethics 12(2): 161-173. DOI: [10.5840/enviroethics199012217](https://doi.org/10.5840/enviroethics199012217).

Donald Alexander examines the bioregional writing that followed Kirkpatrick Sale's *Dwellers in the Land* (1985) and asks whether bioregions are found in nature or made by people [IC]. He argues that bioregional writers tend to mystify the region and to lean toward a view that nature knows best [IC]. In practice, he holds, the boundaries of a bioregion depend on culture and on human choice as much as on watersheds or ecosystems, so the region cannot be read off the land as a fact of science [IC].

The paper is a standard critique in debates on bioregionalism [IC]. Alexander returned to the subject in 1996 and called for a firmer foundation for the idea, which suggests he meant to repair the idea (inference).

The wiki records the paper as a dissent on step T9. It questions the claim that bioregionalism shows how a joining of knowledges looks on the ground, because it doubts that the bioregion itself is a natural given (inference).
