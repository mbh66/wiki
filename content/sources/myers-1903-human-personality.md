F. W. H. Myers (1903). *Human Personality and Its Survival of Bodily Death*. 2 vols. Longmans, Green, London [IC]. Read in the abridged edition, edited by L. H. Myers (Longmans, Green, 1906; 1918 impression), on [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/38492), no. 38492 [IC].

Myers presents the book as an exposition of evidence gathered by the Society for Psychical Research, and says it does not yet amount to proof [IC]. He holds that converging evidence supports telepathy, and that apparitions after death point to the continued action of the dead [IC]. He also criticises the Spiritist view, holding that most such phenomena come from the living person's own embodied spirit [IC]. On the mind, he describes a larger self "from which the consciousness and the faculty of earth-life are mere selections" (ch. I, p. 13) [IC]. The organism shows that larger self only in part, and he compares the limit to the ends of the visible spectrum, which are set by the power of the eye (p. 18) [IC]. His own words are selection and fragment; filter and transmission are later terms, from James and others [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a founding statement of the view that the organism restricts a larger consciousness [TBV].
