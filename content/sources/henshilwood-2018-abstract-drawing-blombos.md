Christopher S. Henshilwood, Francesco d'Errico, Karen L. van Niekerk, Laure Dayet, Alain Queffelec, Luca Pollarolo (2018). *An abstract drawing from the 73,000-year-old levels at Blombos Cave, South Africa*. Nature 562: 115-118. DOI: [10.1038/s41586-018-0514-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0514-3).

The team, from institutions including the University of Bergen, the University of the Witwatersrand and the University of Bordeaux, report nine red lines in a cross-hatched pattern on a small silcrete flake from the Still Bay levels of Blombos Cave on the southern Cape coast [IC]. They date it to about 73,000 years and argue that it was drawn deliberately with an ochre crayon [IC]. The French CNRS reported it as at least 30,000 years older than previously known drawings [IC]. The wiki takes it as Southern African dated evidence for symbolic behaviour (step A2) and as a key point in the dating table.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as an evidence work in palaeoanthropology, with evidence type E2 and claim codes A2.
