Cumming, G. S., & Peterson, G. D. (2017). "Unifying Research on Social-Ecological Resilience and Collapse." *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* 32(9): 695 to 713.

The authors argue that collapse can only be studied against a clearly defined system identity and quantitative thresholds, that collapse processes should be linked to system structure, and that rival explanations should be compared explicitly. Across 17 representative cases they identify 14 mechanisms of social-ecological collapse in five classes, and find that a system's structure shapes the kind of collapse it can undergo. The wiki uses the paper to frame the last phase of the Cycle of Globalization: whether a reorganization counts as collapse or as transformation depends on what is taken to be the system's identity.
