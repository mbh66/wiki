Phyllis Trible (1978). *God and the Rhetoric of Sexuality*. Overtures to Biblical Theology. Fortress Press, Philadelphia [IC]. [Fortress Press](https://www.fortresspress.com/store/product/9780800604646/God-and-the-Rhetoric-of-Sexuality).

Trible reads Genesis 2-3, the Song of Songs and Ruth through rhetorical criticism, against readings that find male dominance in the texts [IC]. Chapter 4, "A Love Story Gone Awry", has two parts, Eros Created and Eros Contaminated [IC]. She reads the first human, made from the soil, as an earth creature not yet marked as male or female, so that woman and man are equal in origin [TBV]. Her reading ends in the loss of that mutuality after the act in the garden [TBV].

*The Christian Century* reports that she set out to counter the traditional reading of male supremacy, and it and a BAS Library article treat the chapter as her best-known work [IC]. The contents come from a lawful Biblia preview; the book was not read [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a Hebrew Bible scholar's reading of Eden, in her own terms [TBV]. On step T7 it is silent. The thesis's reading of the Garden of Eden is its own image, outside the claim [MS], and the book says nothing about farming or a change in thought outside Genesis. The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/t7-the-bifurcation|T7 page]] reports what it says about Eden.
