Arun Agrawal (1995). "Dismantling the Divide Between Indigenous and Scientific Knowledge". *Development and Change* 26(3): 413-439. DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-7660.1995.tb00560.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7660.1995.tb00560.x).

Agrawal, then a political scientist at the University of Florida, questions the idea of indigenous knowledge as development agencies use it [IC]. He examines the substantive, methodological and contextual grounds offered for separating indigenous from scientific knowledge, and argues that none of them holds [IC]. He argues that efforts to archive indigenous knowledge place power with institutions at the centre [IC]. He calls for moving beyond the dichotomy, and for greater autonomy for Indigenous peoples [IC].

The article is widely cited in conservation and development studies, often beside Paul Nadasdy's critique of integration [IC]. It questions the premise that two bounded systems exist to be joined (inference).

The wiki takes from it a qualifier on the two-stream framing that T9 assumes, coded as Michael Haupt decided on 20 November 2026 [MS].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T9.
