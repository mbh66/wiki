Ronfeldt, D. (2009). "Explaining social evolution: standard cause-and-effect vs. TIMN's system dynamics." *Materials for Two Theories: TIMN and STA:C* (blog), 18 September 2009. [Read online](http://twotheories.blogspot.com/2009/09/explaining-social-evolution-standard.html).

David Ronfeldt sets the dynamics of his TIMN framework against cause-and-effect accounts of social change [IC]. A rising form, he argues, first weakens the existing order, partly because those who break the rules exploit it before legitimate actors do; only later does it add a stable new order [IC]. Its growing realm takes over some actors and activities of the older forms, which narrow, change over generations and, if the combination works, grow stronger within their narrower scope [IC]. The post was read from the page and a search extract, and no independent account of it has been found [TBV]. It restates, in short form, the propositions of the 1996 [[sources/ronfeldt-timn|TIMN paper]].
