Marina Weiler and Marjorie Woollacott (2025). "Rethinking communication and consciousness: Lessons from the telepathy tapes podcast". *Explore* 21(6): 103271. DOI 10.1016/j.explore.2025.103271. PMID 41213232 [IC].

The authors argue that nonspeaking autistic people have long been wrongly classed as profoundly intellectually disabled, and that recent findings support their authorship of what they type [IC]. They hold that anecdotal reports of possibly telepathic communication warrant consideration in light of earlier parapsychology research [IC]. They call [[sources/dickens-2024-telepathy-tapes|the podcast]] a cultural turning point that should prompt controlled experiments [IC]. They ask science to treat "carefully documented anecdotes as valuable starting points" for inquiry [IC]. The abstract stops short of claiming that telepathy has been shown (inference) [TBV].

[[sources/delgado-ron-2026-response-to-weiler-and-woollacott|Jorge Andrés Delgado-Ron]] replied in *Explore* in 2026, and the authors answered in "Response to Delgado-Ron (2026)", *Explore* 22(3) [IC]. The University of Virginia's Division of Perceptual Studies lists the paper; its PDF was not opened [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the newest paper that keeps the telepathy question open [TBV].
