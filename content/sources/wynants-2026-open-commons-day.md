Wynants, K. (2026). *A milestone in the evolution of the modern commons: The First Open Commons Day in Flanders*. Guest article in Michel Bauwens, Fourth Generation Civilization (Substack), 20 November 2026. [Read online](https://4thgenerationcivilization.substack.com/p/a-milestone-in-the-evolution-of-the).

Koen Wynants describes the first Open Commons Day, when 52 commons initiatives in Flanders and Brussels opened to the public on the same day [TBV]. He reads it as autonomous local commons acting together as one network, and asks whether the format could travel as an international network of local days [TBV]. He sets out its limits too. Visitors were counted at only 27 of the 52 sites, participants rated the day 6.4 out of 10 on average, and visitors found the word commons unclear [TBV]. His closing note says the long-term impact has not been shown [TBV].

The article appears on Michel Bauwens's Substack with his introduction, so that site is no independent account of it [TBV]. The introduction spells the author's name Wijnants; the byline gives Wynants [TBV]. The wiki takes from it a worked example of networked local commons, with its own evaluation of what one day can do (inference).
