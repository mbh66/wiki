Alan Dundes (2005). "Folkloristics in the Twenty-First Century (AFS Invited Presidential Plenary Address, 2004)". *Journal of American Folklore* 118(470): 385-408. DOI 10.2307/4137664. [Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/188764/summary).

Dundes gave this address as an invited plenary to the American Folklore Society in 2004, and the journal printed it the next year [IC]. Among other points on the state of folklore study, he rejects Joseph Campbell's claim, in *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949), of a single hero pattern found in every culture [TBV]. He argues that many of Campbell's examples are folktales and legends, which folklorists keep apart from myths, and he says Campbell "plays fast and loose with folklore data" (p. 396) [TBV]. He also argues that the pattern holds only because Campbell chose the tales that fit it [TBV].

A 2024 critique of the hero's journey, "One myth to rule them all", draws on the address and gives the page numbers used here [TBV]. The address sits behind a paywall and was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the main folklorist objection to a hero story every person and culture is said to share [TBV].
