Stuart J. Ritchie, Richard Wiseman, Christopher C. French (2012). *Failing the Future: Three Unsuccessful Attempts to Replicate Bem's 'Retroactive Facilitation of Recall' Effect*. PLoS ONE 7(3): e33423. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0033423](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0033423).

Stuart Ritchie at the University of Edinburgh, Richard Wiseman at the University of Hertfordshire and Christopher French at Goldsmiths, University of London, each ran a preregistered, close replication of Bem's Experiment 9, on retroactive facilitation of recall [IC]. Each laboratory tested 50 participants [IC]. None found the effect, the combined result gave p = .83, and the authors conclude that the results "do not support the existence of psychic ability" [IC]. They favour an artefact explanation of Bem's original results [IC].

The authors first sent the paper to the *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, which had published Bem and which rejected the paper without review because it did not publish replications [IC]; French reports that two other journals did the same [TBV]. The episode became part of a wider debate on the place of replication in psychology [IC]. Bem replied alongside the paper that it was premature to judge replicability, and suggested that sceptical experimenters might affect their participants [IC].
