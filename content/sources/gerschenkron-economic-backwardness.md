Gerschenkron, A. (1962). *Economic Backwardness in Historical Perspective: A Book of Essays*. Cambridge, MA: Belknap Press of Harvard University Press.

Gerschenkron argues that the more backward an economy when it began to industrialize, the faster its spurt, the larger its plants, and the greater the role of banks and the state in supplying capital and organization. Late developers skipped and recombined the stages Britain had passed through, which undercuts stage theories such as W. W. Rostow's. The wiki uses the book against teleology in its own sequences: a bioregion can add a later form of production without passing through the earlier forms' dominance.
