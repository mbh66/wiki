Cox, M. (2014). "Applying a Social-Ecological System Framework to the Study of the Taos Valley Irrigation System." *Human Ecology* 42: 311-324. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10745-014-9651-y

Cox uses Ostrom's social-ecological system framework, interviews and court testimony to diagnose how the Taos valley acequias have sustained cooperation for several hundred years. He maps their governance onto the branching ditch system: private parcels and headgates at the farm level, subject to strong community pressure and in some cases rules on whether they can be sold out of the community; common property over each acequia's main canal, its water and the surrounding land; and inter-acequia agreements (*repartimientos*) over shared river headgates. He contrasts the acequias' emphasis on equality among users with the state's adjudication of each individual water right by priority date, and names economic development, changing demographics, that dissonance with state water management, and the penetration of water markets as the threats they face. The wiki uses the paper on the acequias case page as a working example of claims kept bound to a place by community rules.
