Michael Levin (2022). *Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere: An Experimentally-Grounded Framework for Understanding Diverse Bodies and Minds*. Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience 16: 768201. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201

Michael Levin introduces TAME (Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere), a non-binary, experimentally grounded approach to agency that treats morphogenesis as basal cognition and proposes ways cognitive capacities increase during evolution [IC]. He argues that all known minds are collective intelligences made of parts that are themselves agents, joined into larger wholes through developmental bioelectricity [IC]. The article was published as a peer-reviewed Hypothesis and Theory paper in *Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience* on 24 March 2022, edited by Eva Jablonka [IC]. Basal cognition, the research programme TAME belongs to, has met a fair amount of scepticism from philosophers and scientists who hold cognition to be unique to animals with neurons; Fábregas-Tejeda and Sims (2025) argue that sweeping scepticism is unwarranted [IC]. Tom Morgan's *Anomaly Grid* cites Levin's work on bioelectricity. The wiki takes from it the strongest current case that mind comes in graded forms well below the level of nervous systems.
