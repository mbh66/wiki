Quilligan, J. (2026). *Negotiating the Planetary Commons: A Biosocial Compact for the Governance of Earth Systems*. Global Perspectives 7(1): 163283. University of California Press. DOI: [10.1525/gp.2026.163283](https://doi.org/10.1525/gp.2026.163283). Open access.

James Quilligan proposes a biosocial compact for governing Earth systems [TBV]. Nation-states would delegate power downward to communities, municipalities and bioregions, which govern their own commons, and upward to new planetary institutions that set biophysical limits [TBV]. States and corporations would keep the work of allocating credit, resources and goods [TBV]. Bioregional assemblies of residents, indigenous stewards, cooperatives, planners and scientists would track the energy stocks and flows of their regions [TBV]. He grounds value in biophysical energy accounting, and closes by saying the odds of such a compact by 2050 are long [TBV].

The article appeared in June 2026, and no review or citing work has been found yet [TBV]. The wiki reads it as place-based and planetary governance joined by subsidiarity, built on general biophysical metrics (inference).
