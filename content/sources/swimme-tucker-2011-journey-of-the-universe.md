Brian Thomas Swimme and Mary Evelyn Tucker (2011). *Journey of the Universe*. Yale University Press, New Haven [IC]. [Yale University Press](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300209433/journey-of-the-universe/).

The book tells the story of the universe from its origin to human consciousness [IC]. It draws on science and on traditions of the West, China, India and Indigenous peoples, and asks what role humans play in the history of the Earth [IC]. It presents cosmic evolution as a creative, interdependent process in which humans emerge and have a part to play [TBV].

Julianne Lutz Warren's review praises its craft [TBV]. Josh Rothman, quoted by the publisher, writes that "you may or may not be convinced" by its readings [TBV]. Yale's Forum on Religion and Ecology reports that Yale offers courses on the book through Coursera, and that the film made with it won an Emmy in 2012 [TBV]. The book was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a cosmic story that places humans inside the process, and a record of that story being taught [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it affirms T5; it is silent on T7.
