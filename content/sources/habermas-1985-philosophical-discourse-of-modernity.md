Habermas, J. (1987). *The Philosophical Discourse of Modernity: Twelve Lectures*. Trans. F. G. Lawrence. MIT Press. German original 1985.

In twelve lectures Jürgen Habermas reads Nietzsche, Heidegger, Derrida and Foucault as offering total critiques of reason, and argues that such critiques fall into a performative contradiction, since they must use reason to indict it [IC]. He grants that subject-centred reason has produced pathologies, and holds that the remedy is a shift to communicative, intersubjective reason, which completes the project of modernity [IC]. The book sets the terms of much of the later debate [IC]. Habermas does not discuss Gebser or McGilchrist; the wiki reads his argument as a dissent from the premise of C4, that rational modernity is a deficient mode holding back a higher one (inference).
