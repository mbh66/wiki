Krugman, P. (1991). "Increasing Returns and Economic Geography." *Journal of Political Economy*, 99(3), 483–499.

The founding paper of the new economic geography. Krugman showed how increasing returns, transport costs and the size of markets together decide whether manufacturing concentrates in a core or spreads across regions, and that small changes in transport costs can tip the outcome. The wiki takes from it the prediction that rising trade costs favor regional dispersion toward markets, and the warning that existing cores hold their advantage once established.
