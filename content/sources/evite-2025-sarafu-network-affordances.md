Evite, P. M. (2025). Affordances of digital and blockchain-based community currencies: the case of Sarafu Network in Kenya. arXiv:2512.04030. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04030

Using the idea of affordances from human-computer interaction and fieldwork in 2024, the study asks what digital tools and blockchain made possible for Sarafu Network and the community currencies before it. It finds that they widened what communities could do, supporting both everyday trade and reciprocal labour exchange. The blockchain-specific gains were automatic fees on holdings and links to mainstream money through stablecoins, via the smart contracts the network calls pools. The cost was a more complex interface. The wiki cites it for its description of how [[concepts/commitment-pooling|commitment pools]] work in practice, and as a record of local exchange that runs with no AI component.
