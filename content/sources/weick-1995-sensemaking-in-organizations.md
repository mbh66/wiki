Weick, K. E. (1995). *Sensemaking in Organizations*. Thousand Oaks: Sage.

Weick sets out sensemaking as a process with seven properties: it is grounded in identity, retrospective, enactive of its environment, social, ongoing, focused on cues people extract, and driven by plausibility more than accuracy. People act, then work out what their action meant, and the story that lets them keep acting is the one that holds. The wiki borrows the term for place-based sensemaking: a BioHub or community body building a shared account of its place that it can act on. Weick's point about plausibility is the caution that comes with it.
