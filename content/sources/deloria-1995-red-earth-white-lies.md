Vine Deloria Jr. (1995). *Red Earth, White Lies: Native Americans and the Myth of Scientific Fact*. Scribner, New York. 286 pages. ISBN 0-684-80700-9 [IC]. Reissued by Fulcrum, Golden, Colorado, 1997 [IC].

Deloria argues that the scientific account of prehistoric North America is largely fictional, and that Indian oral history describes the history of the earth more truly [IC]. He challenges the Bering Strait migration theory, the claim that Indians killed off the Pleistocene megafauna, and parts of evolutionary theory, and he proposes catastrophe to explain some landforms [IC].

Reviewers divide [IC]. *Kirkus Reviews* found the case unconvincing, and judged that he places traditional beliefs beyond scientific challenge while using science against science [IC]. *Publishers Weekly* found the tone intemperate and the charges worth weighing [IC]. Gregory Gagnon, in *Tribal College Journal* (1996), found some correlations faulty and the reasoning plausible, and urged scientists to read the book [IC]. His son, the historian Philip J. Deloria, later reread it as a book about power over knowledge of the deep past [TBV]. The text was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it an Indigenous scholar's claim for the standing of his people's oral traditions, in his own terms [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it qualifies T7.
