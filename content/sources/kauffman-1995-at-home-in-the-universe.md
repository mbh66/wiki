Kauffman, S. A. (1995). *At Home in the Universe: The Search for the Laws of Self-Organization and Complexity*. Oxford University Press, New York. A UK edition appeared from Viking in the same year [TBV].

Stuart Kauffman argues that complex systems order themselves [IC]. In his account, life and much of the order in biology are expected outcomes of natural law, which he calls order for free, working beside natural selection [IC]. The opening chapters set out the case, including "We the Expected" and "Order for Free" [TBV].

Reviews by Gert Korthof and others confirm this reading [IC]. On the Anabranch layer, run 7a codes the book as qualifying T4 and T6: it finds lawful tendencies to order in complex systems, and claims no striving and no trend to wholeness.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it qualifies T4; it is silent on T5; it qualifies T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
