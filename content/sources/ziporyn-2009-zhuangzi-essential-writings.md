Zhuangzi, *Zhuangzi: The Essential Writings, with Selections from Traditional Commentaries*, translated by Brook Ziporyn. Hackett Publishing Company, Indianapolis, 2009 [IC]. [Hackett](https://hackettpublishing.com/zhuangzi-the-essential-writings).

In an essay Hackett posts with the book, Ziporyn names transformation and the limits of what any perspective can know as core themes of the text [TBV]. He reads the opening images in that light: the fish Kun turns into the bird Peng, and the wind sounds through every hollow with no tone of its own [TBV]. He writes: "The way is no specific way." [TBV]. He argues that no perspective can witness its own origin, and he reads the text as doubting that any perspective can predict what follows [TBV].

Reviewers in *Philosophy East and West* and *China Review International* praise the translation for its clarity and accuracy [IC]. The book itself was not read for this record; the coding rests on the publisher's essay [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a Daoist scholar's reading of the *Zhuangzi* as transformation with no known end [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T5; it qualifies T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
