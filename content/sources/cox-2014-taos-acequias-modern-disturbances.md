Cox, M. (2014). "Modern Disturbances to a Long-Lasting Community-Based Resource Management System: The Taos Valley Acequias." *Global Environmental Change* 24: 213-222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2013.12.006

Cox finds that the acequias produce less than they did and have mostly lost their common-property livestock pasturing. Some of the change follows falling water availability, but much of it comes from social drivers: demographic change, regional and global markets, and public policy. Members ranked drought as the worst disturbance, then newcomers who do not recognize acequia traditions and falling participation; pressure to sell water rights ranked seventh of eleven, lower than Cox expected. Hispano members were far more concerned than newcomers about the Abeyta adjudication, which quantifies each member's individual water right, and user boundaries have become more porous. The paper corrects the wiki's earlier statement that the acequias weakened mainly through water-right sales.
