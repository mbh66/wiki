Lawhon, M. and Murphy, J.T. (2012). Socio-technical regimes and sustainability transitions: insights from political ecology. *Progress in Human Geography* 36(3), 354-378.

A main critique of the field. Lawhon and Murphy argue that transitions research treats the regime as too singular and too detached from place, and neglects how problems are defined and who wins and loses. They draw on political ecology to address this, with a case on genetically modified crops. The wiki gives the critique full weight beside the framework's authors, and uses it on what a forum should ask before it decides what to protect.
