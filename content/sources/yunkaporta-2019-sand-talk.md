Tyson Yunkaporta (2019). *Sand Talk*. Text Publishing.

Tyson Yunkaporta, a member of the Apalech Clan in far north Queensland and an academic at Deakin University, writes through yarning and sand talk, drawing images in the ground to think with [IC]. He argues that knowledge lives in relations and patterns, and that civilisations built on endless growth break those patterns [IC]. The book has been widely read in Australia and abroad [TBV]. The wiki takes it as Indigenous knowledge in its own terms: the grid codes his rejection of progress narratives (step B3) and his account of relational knowledge (C3), and translates neither into the network's structure names.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a context work in Indigenous knowledge, with evidence type E5 and claim codes B3, C3.


The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T9.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|claim grid]] codes its positions on the claim chain: it qualifies C3; it dissents from B3.
