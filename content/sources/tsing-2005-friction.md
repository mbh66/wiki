Tsing, A. L. (2005). *Friction: An Ethnography of Global Connection*. Princeton University Press. DOI: [10.1515/9781400830596](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400830596).

Anna Lowenhaupt Tsing studies how global connections are made in the rainforests of Indonesia [TBV]. She argues that universal claims about nature, science or rights travel only through friction: the awkward, unequal and creative encounters in which local people, activists and scientists take them up [TBV]. She calls these engaged universals, made and remade in each encounter [TBV]. Collaboration across difference, in her account, makes new links and also instability, with no promise of a shared whole [TBV].

The book was reviewed in the American Journal of Sociology and is widely discussed in the anthropology of globalisation [TBV]. Some sources date it 2004; the publisher gives 2005 [IC]. A reissue with a new preface carries the DOI 10.1515/9780691263526 [TBV]. The wiki reads it as global and local knowledge joined inside the encounter, with the costs that brings (inference).
