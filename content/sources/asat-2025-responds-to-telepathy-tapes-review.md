Association for Science in Autism Treatment (2025). "ASAT Responds to The Skeptical Inquirer: 'The Telepathy Tapes: A Dangerous Cornucopia of Pseudoscience'". Media Watch, 27 January 2025 [IC]. [ASAT](https://asatonline.org/media_watch/asat-responds-to-the-skeptical-inquirer-the-telepathy-tapes-a-dangerous-cornucopia-of-pseudoscience/).

The statement highlights [[sources/vyse-2025-telepathy-tapes|Stuart Vyse's review]] of *The Telepathy Tapes* in *Skeptical Inquirer* [IC]. The association says it is deeply concerned that the podcast spreads misinformation about facilitated communication and about "the presence of paranormal abilities in nonspeaking autistic individuals" [IC]. It notes that several episodes promote methods it calls pseudoscientific [IC]. It supports families' right to current information on interventions backed by science, and it urges listeners to keep a healthy scepticism [IC].

*Discover Magazine* and the Society for Psychical Research's Psi Encyclopedia both report the statement as backing Vyse [IC]. One critics' site names two authors for it; that attribution is unconfirmed and is kept out of the author field [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the position of an autism treatment organisation on the T2 claim [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a critic work in autism treatment science, with evidence type E4 and claim codes H2.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it dissents on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
