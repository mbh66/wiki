Jacobs, J. (1969). *The Economy of Cities*. New York: Random House.

Jacobs argues that cities grow by import replacement: they begin making for themselves goods they once imported, which frees earnings to import new things and adds new kinds of work. Diversity and local improvisation, more than specialization, drive the process. The wiki reads import replacement as a place-based theory of production that sits alongside cosmo-local production, and notes that no aggregate measure of it was found for the present period.
