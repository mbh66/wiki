Tengö, M., Brondizio, E. S., Elmqvist, T., Malmer, P. and Spierenburg, M. (2014). *Connecting Diverse Knowledge Systems for Enhanced Ecosystem Governance: The Multiple Evidence Base Approach*. Ambio 43(5): 579-591. DOI: [10.1007/s13280-014-0501-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13280-014-0501-3). [Open copy](https://research.vu.nl/files/1069711/Ambio%202014%20Tengo%20et%20al.pdf).

The authors propose a multiple evidence base for ecosystem governance [TBV]. Indigenous, local and scientific knowledge systems stand side by side as parallel sources of equally valid evidence, each judged by its own criteria, and are brought together across scales, including in global assessments such as IPBES and the Convention on Biological Diversity [TBV].

On step T9, run 7b codes the paper as one of two works independent of the network that affirm part (a), and as naming a further form beside the two the step names. The reading rests on the text, with no independent account checked, so it is graded [TBV].
