Aldous Huxley (1954). *The Doors of Perception*. Two rare-book dealer listings give the first editions as Chatto and Windus, London, and Harper and Brothers, New York, published at the same time [TBV].

Huxley describes a session under mescaline [IC]. Drawing on a 1949 paper by C. D. Broad that summarises Bergson, he proposes that each person is potentially Mind at Large [IC]. He holds that, for the sake of survival, Mind at Large is narrowed by the "reducing valve" of the brain and nervous system, and that mescaline loosens it [IC]. [[sources/webb-2023-nested-hermeneutics|Webb (2023)]] shows that Huxley's quotation of Broad drops Broad's psychical-research setting and adds a biological mechanism, glucose in the brain [IC]. A Springer chapter by Peter Sjöstedt-Hughes reads the same passages as Bergsonian metaphysics and argues for correcting and strengthening them [IC]. This record codes Huxley's own claim; Webb's reading stays on Webb's page.

The book is in copyright and no lawful preview was read, so no page is named [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the best-known modern statement of the filter image [TBV].
