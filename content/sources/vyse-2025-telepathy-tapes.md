Stuart Vyse (2025). *The Telepathy Tapes: A Dangerous Cornucopia of Pseudoscience*. Skeptical Inquirer, 6 January 2025. [Link](https://skepticalinquirer.org/exclusive/the-telepathy-tapes-a-dangerous-cornucopia-of-pseudoscience/).

Stuart Vyse, a psychologist who writes on superstition and pseudoscience, reviews *The Telepathy Tapes* [IC]. He argues that its evidence rests on facilitated communication and its offshoots, in which a partner holds or presents the letter board, and that controlled tests of who writes the messages have shown the facilitator authoring them [IC]. He holds that the podcast does not acknowledge this research [IC], and warns that methods of this kind can harm the people they are meant to help [IC].

The Association for Science in Autism Treatment shared his concern on 27 January 2025 [IC]. Vyse and other writers took the critique further in an issue of *Skeptical Inquirer* in September and October 2026 [IC]. Supporters of the podcast, and some researchers of motor-supported typing, dispute that the older authorship studies settle the question for the methods used today [TBV]. This run did not read the article itself; its content here comes from accounts of it [TBV].

The wiki records the article as a dissent on step T2. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.
