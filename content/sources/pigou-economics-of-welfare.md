Pigou, A. C. (1920). *The Economics of Welfare*. London: Macmillan.

Pigou argued that where the private cost of an activity falls below its social cost, the market produces too much of it, and that a tax equal to the uncharged damage (a bounty in the reverse case) brings private and social cost together. The Pigouvian tax descends from this book. The wiki takes from Pigou the standard definition of negative externalities, and reads carbon border charges such as the EU's as Pigouvian instruments applied to trade.
