Ronfeldt, D. (2007). *In Search of How Societies Work: Tribes, the First and Forever Form*. RAND Pardee Center working paper WR-433-RPC. RAND Corporation, Santa Monica. Title page dated December 2006; RAND lists it as published on 10 January 2007. [Read online](https://www.rand.org/pubs/working_papers/WR433.html).

David Ronfeldt returns to the first form in his TIMN framework, the tribe, which he grounds in kinship, identity and belonging [IC]. He holds that the tribal form persists in every later society beside institutions, markets and networks, and that societies advance by adding the forms in a preferred order, though success is never assured [IC]. He reports Alan Fiske's finding that people acquire three of Fiske's four relational models in the order of the tribal, institutional and market forms [IC]. He also reports anthropologists who hold that most tribes formed in response to states, and concedes that this would weaken the framework if they are right [IC]. Here, and not in the 1996 paper, he says that a fifth form would need yet another information revolution, centuries away (pp. 25 to 26) [IC]. The paper assigns named countries and regions to its types of society; the wiki records that it does so and repeats none of the placements. It develops the 1996 [[sources/ronfeldt-timn|TIMN paper]], and appeared outside formal peer review [IC].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in political science, with evidence type E4 and claim codes B2, B3, C1, C2, C3, C4. It speaks of forms of organisation where the claim chain speaks of structures of awareness, so every grid square it holds qualifies.
