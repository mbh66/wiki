Andersen, L. R. and Björkman, T. (2017). *The Nordic Secret: A European Story of Beauty and Freedom*. Stockholm: Fri Tanke.

The book argues that the Nordic countries became prosperous, high-trust democracies through "popular enlightenment" and without revolution, and that the secret ingredient was Bildung, spread from the 1840s through the folk high schools. It traces Bildung to German idealism, British pragmatism and Nordic heritage, and links nineteenth-century Bildung to Robert Kegan's third and fourth orders of consciousness, arguing that the twenty-first century needs a Bildung that supports the fifth. The wiki takes three things from it: the working definition of Bildung as growing responsibility joined to growing freedom, the historical case that formation can be organized for whole populations, and the bridge between Bildung and the developmental frameworks behind [[glossary/m-s/mycelial-consciousness|Mycelial Consciousness]]. The causal claim about Nordic success is the authors' thesis and the wiki treats it as such.
