Robert N. Bellah (2011). *Religion in Human Evolution: From the Paleolithic to the Axial Age*. Belknap Press of Harvard University Press. DOI: [10.4159/harvard.9780674063099](https://doi.org/10.4159/harvard.9780674063099).

Robert N. Bellah, a sociologist of religion, builds on Donald's three stages to trace religion from play and ritual through tribal and archaic religion to the Axial Age in ancient Israel, Greece, China and India [IC]. His refrain is that nothing is ever lost: earlier capacities persist alongside later ones [IC]. The book led to an edited volume, *The Axial Age and Its Consequences* (2012), with Hans Joas [IC]. The wiki takes it as a scholarly bridge between cognitive archaeology and cultural history (steps A2 and A3), and as a qualified support for co-presence (C1): Bellah applies the refrain to capacities and concedes that much of the past itself is lost [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in sociology of religion, with evidence type E3 and claim codes A2, A3, C1.

Glossary: [[glossary/a-e/axial-age|Axial Age]].
