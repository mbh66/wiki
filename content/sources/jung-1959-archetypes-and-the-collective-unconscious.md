C. G. Jung (1959). *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious*. Princeton University Press, Bollingen Series XX, Collected Works volume 9, part 1; translated by R. F. C. Hull; second edition 1969.

This volume collects Carl Jung's main essays on the collective unconscious and the archetypes, from 1934 onward, with a final section relating them to the process of individuation [IC]. Jung holds that beneath each person's personal unconscious lies an inherited layer shared by all people, whose archetypes appear in myth, dream and symbol [IC]. The volume was first published in English in 1959, with a second edition in 1969 [IC]. Within analytical psychology the biological account of archetypes is contested: Christian Roesler asked in the *Journal of Analytical Psychology* (2012) whether archetypes are carried more by culture than by biology, while Roesler and Milena Sotirova-Kohli (2013) reviewed studies they read as evidence for archetypes and a collective memory [IC]. Tom Morgan's *Anomaly Grid* uses Jung for individuation and synchronicity. Synchronicity has no step in this wiki's chain; the wiki takes from the volume Jung's claim that an archaic layer of the psyche stays active in everyone, and his account of development across a life.
