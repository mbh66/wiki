Baldwin, R. (2016). *The Great Convergence: Information Technology and the New Globalization*. Cambridge, MA: Belknap Press of Harvard University Press.

Baldwin describes globalization as a sequence of unbundlings. Steam power and cheap transport separated production from consumption; information technology from about 1990 separated the stages of production, so that a factory could be run across borders from a rich-country headquarters. The wiki takes from Baldwin the coordination cost that Weber's model lacks, and the distinction between moving goods, moving ideas and moving people, whose costs fell at different times.
