Cox, M. (2012). "Diagnosing Institutional Fit: A Formal Perspective." *Ecology and Society* 17(4): 54. https://doi.org/10.5751/ES-05173-170454

Cox argues that there is no single overall fit: a system can fit with respect to one outcome and not another, and each theory of fit relates a set of variables to an explicitly desired outcome. He expresses three such theories formally: extent fit, where a governance system covers the same area as the resource it governs; spatiotemporal fit, where highly variable resources call for mobile users and flexible, negotiable boundaries; and spatial nesting fit, where layers of governance mirror nested resource units, with the Taos valley acequias as the example. He also notes that environmental monitoring matters across many kinds of system regardless of these variables, so it may belong in its own theory. The wiki uses the paper for the institutional fit codes in the currency case register.
