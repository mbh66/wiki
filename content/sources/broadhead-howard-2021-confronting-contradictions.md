Lee-Anne Broadhead, Sean Howard (2021). *Confronting the Contradictions between Western and Indigenous Science: A Critical Perspective on Two-Eyed Seeing*. AlterNative: An International Journal of Indigenous Peoples 17(1): 111-119. DOI: [10.1177/1177180121996326](https://doi.org/10.1177/1177180121996326).

Lee-Anne Broadhead and Sean Howard, at Cape Breton University, write a critical perspective on Two-Eyed Seeing [IC]. They contrast Indigenous worldviews that are, in their terms, mainly wholistic with Eurocentric science that is mainly reductionist [IC]. They argue that the hope for dialogue built into the principle can lead people to pass over real contradictions between the two, and that some approaches cannot be combined [IC]. They call for a more critical use of the principle, one that names those contradictions openly [IC].

This run did not check how the paper has been received [TBV]. The authors write from the university where Two-Eyed Seeing first entered a degree programme, which gives their critique an inside view (inference). They keep a place for dialogue between knowledge systems; their target is a use of the principle that smooths over conflict (inference).

The wiki records the paper as a dissent on step T9: it questions whether the two ways of knowing can be joined where they contradict each other.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it qualifies T9.
