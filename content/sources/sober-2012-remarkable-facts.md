Elliott Sober (2012). "Remarkable Facts: Ending Science as We Know It". *Boston Review*, November/December 2012, pp. 50-55. [Boston Review](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/remarkable-facts/).

Sober, a philosopher of biology at the University of Wisconsin, reviews Thomas Nagel's *Mind and Cosmos* (2012) [IC]. He reports that Nagel wants science to invoke goals as well as mechanisms [IC]. In the section headed "Brute Facts" he argues that Nagel's assumptions about explanation are too strong: that remarkable facts must be explained, must be shown to be fairly probable, and cannot be byproducts [IC]. On Sober's account, current biology may be incomplete, and Nagel's requirements do not show that it is [IC]. The page numbers come from Sober's own list of papers [IC].

Later commentators treat the review as one of the most useful on the substance of the disagreement [TBV]. It sits beside John Dupré's review in *Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews*, which is also critical [IC].

The wiki takes from it the main reasoned rejection of a striving built into nature [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it dissents on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
