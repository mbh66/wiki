Sophie I. G. Roher, Ziwa Yu, Debbie H. Martin, Anita C. Benoit (2021). *How is Etuaptmumk/Two-Eyed Seeing Characterized in Indigenous Health Research? A Scoping Review*. PLOS ONE 16(7): e0254612. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0254612](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0254612).

Sophie Roher and colleagues review 80 articles in Indigenous health research that use Etuaptmumk, Two-Eyed Seeing [IC]. They find the principle described in inconsistent ways. Many studies leave out the spiritual and ecological dimensions the Mi'kmaw Elders stress, and some describe it as blending or integrating two knowledge systems [IC]. Against that reading they cite Iwama and colleagues, for whom Two-Eyed Seeing "neither merges two knowledge systems into one" [IC]. They also report Elder Albert Marshall's warning that work done in its name can slip into tokenism [IC].

The authors state that they do not intend to criticise individual researchers [IC]. A correction to the paper was published in 2024 [IC]. With Wright and colleagues (2019), the review is the main evidence that the principle is often used loosely [IC].

The wiki records the review on step T9 as a qualifier. Its finding that the two knowledge systems stay distinct bears on how the Confluence is worded, and the hand-back of 19 November 2026 sets out that question for Michael.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T9.
