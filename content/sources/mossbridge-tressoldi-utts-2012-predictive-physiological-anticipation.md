Julia Mossbridge, Patrizio Tressoldi, Jessica Utts (2012). *Predictive Physiological Anticipation Preceding Seemingly Unpredictable Stimuli: A Meta-Analysis*. Frontiers in Psychology 3: 390. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00390](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00390).

Julia Mossbridge, Patrizio Tressoldi and Jessica Utts meta-analyse 26 reports, published from 1978 to 2010, on what they call predictive anticipatory activity: shifts in the body that come before a stimulus a person cannot predict [IC]. The studies measured skin conductance, heart activity, pupil size, EEG and BOLD signals in brain imaging [IC]. The pooled effect is small, with a random-effects effect size of 0.21, and runs in the direction of the body's later response to the stimulus [IC]. The authors call the effect unexplained [IC].

Duggan and Tressoldi (2018) added 19 newer studies and reported similar results [IC]. This run found no direct published reply to the meta-analysis [TBV]. Tom Morgan's *Anomaly Grid* cites Mossbridge on two of its steps, one of which the wiki maps to T3; the paper does not discuss whether the brain filters consciousness, so T3 is not coded here.

The wiki records the meta-analysis as a supporting study on step T2. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.
