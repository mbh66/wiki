Born, B. and Purcell, M. (2006). *Avoiding the Local Trap: Scale and Food Systems in Planning Research*. Journal of Planning Education and Research 26(2): 195-207. DOI: [10.1177/0739456X06291389](https://doi.org/10.1177/0739456X06291389). [Author copy](https://faculty.washington.edu/mpurcell/jper.pdf).

Branden Born and Mark Purcell argue that food planners often assume local food systems are more sustainable or more just than food systems at other scales [IC]. They call this assumption the local trap [IC]. Scale, they hold, has no qualities of its own: it is a strategy, and what a local system does depends on the agenda of those it empowers [IC]. A local system can be just or unjust, sustainable or unsustainable, as a global one can [IC].

The paper carries the local trap critique of [[sources/purcell-brown-2005-against-the-local-trap|Purcell and Brown (2005)]] into food systems, where it is widely cited [IC]. The wiki records it as a qualification on T9, part (b): it holds that a local food system can be just or unjust, so it accepts that place-based arrangements can do good while denying them any built-in good (inference). Michael Haupt moved it from a dissent on 25 November 2026, for consistency with the 2005 paper after Anabranch run 9 read that paper in full [MS].
