Hoover, E. M. (1948). *The Location of Economic Activity*. New York: McGraw-Hill.

Hoover extended Weber by adding terminal costs, which are paid whatever the distance, and freight rates that fall in steps as distance grows, which favors sites at the ends of a haul and at transshipment points such as ports. The wiki cites Hoover as one of the standard extensions of Weber, and because terminal and handling costs explain why ports and hubs keep their pull when transport risk rises.
