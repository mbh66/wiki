Alexander, D. (1996). *Bioregionalism: The Need for a Firmer Theoretical Foundation*. The Trumpeter 13(3): 1-7. [Author copy](https://www.viurrspace.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/e2bf38c1-20cd-4aa7-bdfc-7422a2b4d32a/content).

Don Alexander, then a doctoral student in urban and regional planning at the University of Waterloo, returns to the critique he made in [[sources/alexander-1990-bioregionalism-science-or-sensibility|Bioregionalism: Science or Sensibility? (1990)]] [TBV]. He sets out four ways of drawing a bioregion, from environmental determinism to the view that a region is wherever people think they are, and holds that regions are mainly products of culture [TBV]. Peoples have always migrated and borrowed practices, he argues, which sits badly with readings that give each bioregion one essence [TBV]. He concludes that bioregions may not exist on the ground, or may shift as culture and nature interact, and that the concept still stands: a sustainable society may need a frame larger than the ecosystem and smaller than the biogeographical province [TBV].
