Michael Ruse (1996). *Monad to Man: The Concept of Progress in Evolutionary Biology*. Harvard University Press, Cambridge, Mass. 628 pages [IC]. [Harvard University Press](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674032484).

Ruse traces the idea of progress through evolutionary biology from its beginnings to the 1990s [IC]. Reviewers report that he ties the doubtful standing of evolutionary biology as a science to its links with the cultural idea of Progress [TBV]. He separates that cultural Progress from biological progress, and he draws on interviews with working evolutionists, Stephen Jay Gould among them [TBV].

A review in *The Thomist* calls progress the concept that has given philosophy of biology the most trouble [TBV]. The publisher quotes the historian Robert Richards, who judges that Ruse has shown how closely the ideas of evolution and progress have been linked [TBV]. The book was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a historian's case that belief in a direction in evolution carries cultural baggage [TBV].
