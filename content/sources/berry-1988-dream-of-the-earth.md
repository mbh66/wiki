Thomas Berry (1988). *The Dream of the Earth*. Sierra Club Books, San Francisco [IC]. Reissued by Counterpoint, Berkeley, 2015 [IC]. Its chapter The New Story first appeared in *Teilhard Studies* no. 1 (1978) [IC].

Berry argues that humans are in trouble because they lack a good story, and that a new story of the universe can guide them [IC]. In his chapter on the college he holds that the aim of education is for students to understand the universe story and "the role of the student in creating the next phase of the story" (p. 98, as quoted by the Thomas Berry Foundation) [TBV]. He holds that traditional origin stories, though still needed, cannot give education the wide setting the new story offers [TBV].

Frederic and Mary Ann Brussat review the book favourably at Spirituality and Practice [IC]. Lou Rodenberger, in *Western American Literature*, describes his integrated programme of study as a way to reshape how people see themselves on the earth, and notes that the book covers familiar philosophical ground [IC]. No lawful preview was read [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a direct statement that each person has a part in the universe story, with a call for education to teach it [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it affirms T5.
