Fischer, K. W. and Bidell, T. R. (2006). Dynamic development of action and thought. In R. M. Lerner (ed.), *Theoretical Models of Human Development*, volume 1 of *Handbook of Child Psychology*, 6th edition (W. Damon and R. M. Lerner, eds-in-chief), chapter 7, pp. 313-399. John Wiley & Sons, Hoboken, NJ.

Kurt Fischer and Thomas Bidell set out dynamic skill theory: skills are built from simpler ones into higher levels, but a person acts across a range of levels that shifts with the task, the setting and the support on hand, and moves backward as well as forward [IC]. Development is pictured as a web of partly separate strands, where stage theories draw a ladder [IC]. The chapter is among the most cited statements of the neo-Piagetian view [IC]. The handbook's table of contents and the chapter's first page give the same title, as chapter 7, pages 313 to 399 [IC]. The wiki takes it as a qualifier of C1: lower levels stay available within a context-dependent range, a weaker claim than whole structures staying active in everyone (inference).

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a critic in developmental psychology, with evidence type E3 and claim codes B1, C1.
