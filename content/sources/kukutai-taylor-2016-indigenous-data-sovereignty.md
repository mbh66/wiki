Kukutai, T., and Taylor, J. (eds.) (2016, November). *Indigenous Data Sovereignty: Toward an Agenda*. CAEPR Research Monograph 38. Canberra: ANU Press. ISBN 9781760460303 (print), 9781760460310 (online). https://doi.org/10.22459/CAEPR38.11.2016

The volume opens with the question "As the global 'data revolution' accelerates, how can the data rights and interests of indigenous peoples be secured?" Its contributors start from the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and argue that Indigenous peoples hold inherent and inalienable rights over data about themselves, their ways of life and their territories, and they ask what data sovereignty means for Indigenous self-determination. The book is open access through ANU Press. The wiki takes from it the founding statement of [[data-sovereignty|data sovereignty]] in its Indigenous sense, and in particular its extension of the claim from data about people to data about territories, which is the step that lets a community ask who governs information about its land.
