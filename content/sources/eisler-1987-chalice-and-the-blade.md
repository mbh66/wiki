Riane Eisler (1987). *The Chalice and the Blade: Our History, Our Future*. Harper & Row, San Francisco.

Eisler sets a partnership model of society against a dominator model [IC]. Drawing on the archaeology of Marija Gimbutas and James Mellaart, she argues that partnership societies of Neolithic Europe were overrun by dominator invaders between about 5000 and 3000 BCE [IC]. She holds that humanity can make a cultural transformation back to partnership [IC]. Christine Hoff Kraemer's review reports that Eisler uses the Adam and Eve story as a proof text for the shift [TBV]. Roger Briggs cites the book for the dominator era in *Emerging World* (2021), ch. 5, p. 161 [TBV].

Kraemer's review (2004) finds that the archaeology Eisler relies on has been discredited, and that she misdates the sources of Genesis [TBV]. Grindell's review in *Arizona Anthropologist* finds the evidence for a mother goddess wanting [TBV]. The chapters were not read for this record, and the imprint is unchecked [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the main published source for a split from partnership to domination [TBV]. On step T7 it qualifies on part (a), for its date of the shift; the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/t7-the-bifurcation|T7 page]] reports its use of the Eden story separately, since the thesis's Eden reading sits outside the claim [MS].
