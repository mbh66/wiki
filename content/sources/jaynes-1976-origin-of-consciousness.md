Julian Jaynes (1976). *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind*. Houghton Mifflin.

Julian Jaynes, a psychologist at Princeton, defines consciousness narrowly as an introspective inner space built from language and metaphor [IC]. He argues that before about 3,000 years ago people lived with a [[glossary/a-e/bicameral-mind|bicameral mind]], hearing commands from one hemisphere as the voices of gods, and reads the *Iliad* and other early texts as records of it [IC]. Ned Block's 1981 review argued that Jaynes confuses consciousness with the concept of consciousness; the Julian Jaynes Society has published replies, and most specialists reject the dating [IC]. The wiki takes the book as the boldest claim that the structure of awareness changed in historical time (step A3), and reports it as contested.

The work is on Michael Haupt's reading for the evolution of consciousness [MS].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in psychology, with evidence type E4 and claim codes A2, A3, C1, H.
