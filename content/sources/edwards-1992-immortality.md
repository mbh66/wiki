Paul Edwards (ed.) (1992). *Immortality*. Macmillan, New York [IC]. Reissued by Prometheus Books, 1997 [TBV].

The anthology runs from Plato and Lucretius to Derek Parfit, with a long editorial introduction [IC]. Chapter 32 is William James's "Consciousness, the Brain and Immortality", the source of the transmission theory, and chapter 33 is Edwards's "The Dependence of Consciousness on the Brain" (pp. 292-307) [IC]. Edwards argues that the evidence that certain brain states bring about certain conscious states is as strong as for any accepted physical cause (p. 294, as quoted in Keith Augustine) [TBV].

The philosopher Adolf Grünbaum points readers to the essay for the fuller case that personal immortality fails against this dependence [IC]. Augustine relies on it in his own case against survival [IC]. Whether the essay answers James directly was not checked, and the essay was not read [TBV]. The volume is a separate work from Edwards's [[sources/edwards-1996-reincarnation-critical-examination|Reincarnation]] (1996).

The wiki takes from it a standard modern statement of the production view, set beside James's filter essay [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it dissents on T1; it dissents on T3.
