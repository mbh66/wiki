Robert A. Segal (1991). "Joseph Campbell's Mythology: A Review Essay". *Southern Humanities Review* 25(3): 267-275 [TBV].

Segal, a scholar of religion, reviews Joseph Campbell's theory of myth [TBV]. He notes that Campbell urged people to live by myth, and that Campbell became famous after his 1988 television interviews with Bill Moyers [TBV]. He writes that "Campbell goes beyond other theorists in his disregard for differences" among myths [TBV]. He adds that Campbell ignores the people who believe in the myths [TBV]. In earlier work (1978) Segal judged that Campbell's view of myth had stayed much the same since 1943 [TBV].

Later critics of the monomyth cite Segal beside [[sources/dundes-2005-folkloristics-twenty-first-century|Alan Dundes (2005)]], among them a 2024 critical study of the hero's journey and a theological thesis [IC]. The essay was read in a reprint on eNotes, and its year and journal come from the reprint's header [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a second scholar of religion who disputes the universal pattern that T5 draws on through Campbell [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it dissents on T5; it is silent on T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
