Andrey V. Korotayev (2020). "The Twenty-First-Century Singularity in the Big History Perspective: A Re-analysis". In Andrey Korotayev and David LePoire (eds), *The 21st Century Singularity and Global Futures*, Springer, Cham, pp. 19-75. DOI: [10.1007/978-3-030-33730-8_2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-33730-8_2).

Korotayev, of the Higher School of Economics in Moscow, takes the dated lists of milestones compiled by Theodore Modis and Alexander Panov and fits hyperbolic equations to the acceleration of global complexity growth over four billion years [IC]. He places the singularity of these curves in the twenty-first century [IC]. In related work he holds that the hyperbolic pace now appears to be levelling off, much as world population growth has [IC].

The same volume carries Modis's update of his logistic reading of similar series (pp. 101-104) [IC], and a chapter by Graeme Snooks asking whether the singularity is a scientific concept at all [IC]. The two readings differ on the shape of the curve: Modis sees the first half of a logistic, and Korotayev sees hyperbolic growth that is now bending (inference).

The wiki takes from it the main published reading of the long series as accelerating, set beside the logistic readings of Modis and Smil [TBV].
