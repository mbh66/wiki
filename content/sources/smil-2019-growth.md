Vaclav Smil (2019). *Growth: From Microorganisms to Megacities*. The MIT Press, Cambridge, MA. xxv + 634 pages. ISBN: 9780262042833.

Smil, a distinguished professor emeritus at the University of Manitoba, surveys growth in four domains: living matter, energy, human artefacts, and populations, societies and economies [IC]. He finds S-shaped, confined growth common across them [IC]. In chapter 5 he fits a logistic curve to the territory of the Roman republic and empire, using data from Rein Taagepera (Figure 5.11) [TBV], and treats the course of civilisations in a section of its own [TBV]. His publisher's summary says that course encompasses both decline and renewal, and that the trajectory of modern civilisation remains uncertain [IC].

Reviewers draw out his caution about models. Physics World reports his example of four different growth curves that all fit Mozart's output well, yet project very different totals at age 50 [IC]. A catalogue review notes his argument that growth in a finite universe entails decline [IC].

The wiki takes from it a careful user of logistic curves who doubts that a fit can show where a system now stands [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it qualifies T7; it qualifies T8.
