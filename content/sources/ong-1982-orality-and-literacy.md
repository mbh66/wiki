Walter J. Ong (1982). *Orality and Literacy*. Methuen.

Walter J. Ong, a Jesuit scholar of language and literature, draws on Milman Parry, Albert Lord and Eric Havelock to describe how people think in cultures with no writing: through formulas, stories and memory held in common [IC]. He argues that writing, and later print, made thought more analytic and inward, and that radio, television and the telephone brought a secondary orality [IC]. The book is a standard text in media and communication studies [TBV]. The wiki takes it as the clearest account of external memory changing the mind (steps A2 and A3), and of older modes surviving inside literate cultures (C1).

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in history of ideas, with evidence type E3 and claim codes A2, A3, C1, C2.
