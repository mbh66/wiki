Jeffrey N. Rouder, Richard D. Morey and Jordan M. Province (2013). "A Bayes Factor Meta-Analysis of Recent Extrasensory Perception Experiments: Comment on Storm, Tressoldi, and Di Risio (2010)". *Psychological Bulletin* 139(1): 241-247. DOI 10.1037/a0029008.

The authors reanalyse the free-response studies gathered in [[sources/storm-tressoldi-di-risio-2010-meta-analysis-free-response|Storm, Tressoldi and Di Risio (2010)]] with Bayes factors, which weigh the evidence for an effect against the evidence for none [IC]. They grant that the data set, taken as presented, favours an effect by a very large margin, and argue that problems in the data set weaken it [TBV]. The run that found the paper reports the abstract's figures as about 6 billion to 1 before their corrections and at most 330 to 1 after [TBV]. The record is confirmed by Crossref; the full text was not read for this record [IC].

Lance Storm, Patrizio Tressoldi and Jessica Utts replied in the same journal in 2013, defending the original analysis [IC]. Ray Hyman's comment of 2010, filed as [[sources/hyman-2010-meta-analysis-conceals|Hyman (2010)]], raises related objections from outside the Bayesian frame [IC].

The wiki takes from it a statistical reading that grants a large effect in the data and questions how much that effect shows [TBV].
