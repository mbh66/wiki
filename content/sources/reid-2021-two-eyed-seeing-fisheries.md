Andrea J. Reid, Lauren E. Eckert, John-Francis Lane, Nathan Young, Scott G. Hinch, Chris T. Darimont, Steven J. Cooke, Natalie C. Ban, Albert Marshall (2021). *"Two-Eyed Seeing": An Indigenous Framework to Transform Fisheries Research and Management*. Fish and Fisheries 22(2): 243-261. DOI: [10.1111/faf.12516](https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12516).

Andrea Reid and eight co-authors, Elder Albert Marshall among them, argue that Two-Eyed Seeing can change how fisheries are studied and managed [IC]. They describe a way of weaving Indigenous and Western knowledge in fisheries work in which neither is absorbed into the other [IC]. They hold that much talk of integrating other knowledge systems has been a euphemism for assimilation, and they set Two-Eyed Seeing against that pattern [IC]. The paper appeared in *Fish and Fisheries*, a review journal in fisheries science [TBV].

This run did not check how the paper has been received [TBV]. Fisheries tie the principle to particular waters and the people who know them, which is why the wiki reads it as place-based (inference). The paper shares an author with the founding statement of the principle, Bartlett, Marshall and Marshall (2012) [IC].

The wiki records the paper in its own terms on step T9, as an argument for joining two ways of knowing in the management of particular waters, each kept whole.
