Lewis, W. A. (1954). "Economic Development with Unlimited Supplies of Labour." *The Manchester School*, 22(2), 139–191.

Lewis's dual-economy model: a modern sector can draw labor from a traditional rural sector at a near-constant wage until the surplus is absorbed, after which wages begin to rise. The point at which that happens is now called the Lewis turning point. The wiki uses the model to ask whether the labor saving that drove offshoring still pays in a given country, and cites the evidence that China passed its turning point in the 2000s.
