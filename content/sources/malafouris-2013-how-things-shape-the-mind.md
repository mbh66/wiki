Malafouris, L. (2013). *How Things Shape the Mind: A Theory of Material Engagement*. Foreword by Colin Renfrew. MIT Press, Cambridge, MA. ISBN 978-0-262-01919-4.

Lambros Malafouris sets out material engagement theory: the mind extends into the things people make and use, and cognition and material culture cannot be separated [TBV]. He builds it on three hypotheses, the extended mind, the enactive sign and material agency, and tests them on Stone Age knapping, early mark-making, Mycenaean Linear B tablets and pottery [TBV].

In chapter 5 he follows the Near Eastern accounting system, drawing on Denise Schmandt-Besserat and Peter Damerow. Clay tokens from the Neolithic, then envelopes and impressed tablets, then the pictographic tablets of the first city-states, about 3100 BC, mark the stage at which number was abstracted from the things counted [TBV]. He holds that this abstraction was brought forth by handling clay, and stays part of a system of mind and material [TBV]. How the work is received in its own field is still to be checked [TBV].

On step T7 of the Anabranch layer it is the work read so far that most directly dates a turn towards abstract knowledge to the Neolithic. It qualifies part (a), because it ties the abstraction to material engagement in one regional case.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as an evidence work in cognitive archaeology, with evidence type E3 and claim codes A3.
