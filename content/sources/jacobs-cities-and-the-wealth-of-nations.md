Jacobs, J. (1984). *Cities and the Wealth of Nations: Principles of Economic Life*. New York: Random House.

Jacobs argues that the city region is the real unit of economic life, and that national economies are aggregates of city regions with different needs, so that a single national currency and policy misreads them. She extends import replacement to explain why regions that only supply distant cities stagnate. The wiki takes from the book the case for reading a region as an economic unit in its own right, which bioregional economics shares.
