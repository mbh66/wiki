Bernardo Kastrup (2019). *The Idea of the World: A Multi-Disciplinary Argument for the Mental Nature of Reality*. Iff Books. ISBN 978-1-78535-739-8. [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/KASTIO-6).

The book gathers ten of Kastrup's journal articles into a case for idealism, the view that reality is essentially mental [IC]. He holds that individual minds are dissociated segments, or alters, of one universal consciousness, and that brain activity is what such a segment looks like from outside [TBV]. Part IV includes the chapter "Self-transcendence correlates with brain function impairment" (ch. 11, from p. 178), which bears on how brain and experience relate [IC]. The chapters were not read; the coding rests on the abstract and contents [TBV].

His idealism is debated within philosophy of mind, and one reviewer questions how a metabolic process could ground the dissociation the account needs [TBV]. Version 1.4 of the research's standing instructions named Kastrup among the library's absences [MS].

The wiki takes from it a current idealist account that recasts filter language as dissociation [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in philosophy of mind, with evidence type E4 and claim codes H.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it qualifies T3; it is silent on T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
