Weber, A. (1929). *Theory of the Location of Industries*. Trans. C. J. Friedrich. Chicago: University of Chicago Press. (Original work published 1909 as *Über den Standort der Industrien*.)

Weber's founding work of industrial location theory treats the siting of a plant as a problem of least cost. The firm finds the point that minimizes the cost of moving materials in and product out, using the locational triangle, the material index and isodapanes, and then moves away from it only when cheaper labor or the savings of a cluster exceed the added transport cost: the labor and agglomeration deviations. The wiki reads Weber's labor deviation as the mechanism of offshoring on the steep part of the S-curve, and asks what the same model predicts once the labor saving shrinks and transport risk rises. Its answer is regional production with no change of owner.
