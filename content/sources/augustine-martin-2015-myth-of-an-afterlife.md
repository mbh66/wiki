Keith Augustine and Michael Martin (eds) (2015). *The Myth of an Afterlife: The Case against Life After Death*. Rowman & Littlefield, Lanham, MD. xxxi + 675 pages. ISBN: 9780810886773.

The book gathers thirty chapters in four parts: empirical arguments for annihilation, conceptual and empirical difficulties for survival, problematic models of the afterlife, and dubious evidence for survival, according to the WorldCat record and the Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews account [IC]. In Part I, contributors argue from brain damage, the dying brain and the neural basis of emotion that mental life depends on the brain [IC]. In Part IV, Susan Blackmore disputes that out-of-body experiences show survival, and Augustine argues that near-death experiences are hallucinations [IC]. The editors present the collection as a casebook against survival [IC]. Writers who defend survival have answered it; that exchange was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the main published case against T1, and a direct challenge to filter and transmission readings of the brain on T3 [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a critic work in philosophy of mind, with evidence type E4 and claim codes H, H2.
