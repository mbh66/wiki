Scott, J. C. (1998). *Seeing Like a State: How Certain Schemes to Improve the Human Condition Have Failed*. Yale University Press, New Haven.

James C. Scott argues that states simplify what they govern, so that it can be counted and administered [IC]. He holds that high-modernist schemes built on those simplifications fail where they override metis, the practical knowledge held by people in particular places [IC]. He dates the simplifying state to the early modern period and its high-modernist form to the twentieth century [TBV].

Reviews by Cass Sunstein and others confirm this reading [IC]. On the Anabranch layer it speaks to the turn towards general, measurable knowledge on step T7, which it dates late, and to the persistence of metis beside it.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it qualifies T7; it is silent on T8.
