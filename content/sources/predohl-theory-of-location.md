Predöhl, A. (1928). "The Theory of Location in Its Relation to General Economics." *Journal of Political Economy*, 36(3), 371–390.

Predöhl's English statement of an argument he first made in German in 1925: that the choice of location is a case of the general economic principle of substitution, in which a producer trades one input against another at the margin. Transport costs become one input among others. The wiki takes from Predöhl the correction that the substitution principle came into location theory from general economics, and did not grow out of Weber; Isard and Moses later used it to absorb Weber's deviations as special cases.
