Perez, C. (2002). *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital: The Dynamics of Bubbles and Golden Ages*. Cheltenham: Edward Elgar.

Perez reads economic history since the 1770s as five great surges, each set off by a technological revolution and lasting about half a century. In the installation period financial capital funds the new infrastructure, and the boom ends in a frenzy and a crash. At the turning point that follows, institutions are rebuilt; in the deployment period production capital leads and the technology spreads, sometimes into a golden age. The wiki takes from the book its vocabulary for what a crash leaves behind and who decides its use. The [[research/dark-ai-and-the-place-based-inheritance|Dark AI brief]] tests one reading of it, finding that the postwar golden age ran largely through national institutions.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it qualifies T7.
