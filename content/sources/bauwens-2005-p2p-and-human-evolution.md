Bauwens, M. (2005). *P2P and Human Evolution: Peer to peer as the premise of a new mode of civilization*. Foundation for P2P Alternatives, Draft 1.1, 1 March 2005; a later version is dated July 2005. [Open copy](https://library.uniteddiversity.coop/Money_and_Economics/P2P_essay.pdf). [Wiki version](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/P2P_and_Human_Evolution), reconstructed in 2017.

Michel Bauwens describes peer to peer as a form of network organisation: free participation by equipotent partners in producing common resources, with no monetary reward as the main motive and no command hierarchy [TBV]. He traces it in technology, the economy, politics, culture and spirituality, and argues that it is the premise of a new civilisation [TBV]. He sets out three scenarios for its life beside cognitive capitalism, one of them a dark scenario of information feudalism [TBV].

Michel Bauwens supplied the essay's full text for this research, and he is close to the BioConomy network. The essay frames history through a premodern, modern and postmodern triad; the wiki repeats none of its placements.
