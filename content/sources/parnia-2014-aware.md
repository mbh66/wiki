Sam Parnia, Ken Spearpoint, Gabriele de Vos, Peter Fenwick and colleagues (2014). *AWARE—AWAreness during REsuscitation—A prospective study*. Resuscitation 85(12): 1799-1805. DOI: [10.1016/j.resuscitation.2014.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2014.09.004).

Sam Parnia and colleagues ran a four-year study across hospitals in the United Kingdom, the United States and Austria to test whether people are aware during cardiac arrest, and whether claims of seeing the room from above can be checked [IC]. Shelves holding images that could be seen only from near the ceiling were placed in areas where resuscitation was likely [IC]. Among 2,060 cardiac arrest events, 140 survivors completed first-stage interviews and 101 the second stage [IC]. Of these, 46 per cent had memories of the arrest, 9 per cent had near-death experiences, and one had what the authors call "a verifiable period of conscious awareness" at a time when brain function was not expected [IC].

No interviewed survivor identified a hidden image [TBV]. Supporters of the survival hypothesis cite the verified case; critics cite the absence of target hits (inference). The design led to AWARE II (2023) [IC].

The wiki records the study on steps T1 and T2, where its findings fall on both sides. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T1; it qualifies T2.
