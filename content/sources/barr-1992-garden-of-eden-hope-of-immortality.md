James Barr (1992). *The Garden of Eden and the Hope of Immortality*. The Read-Tuckwell Lectures for 1990. SCM Press, London, 1992; Fortress Press, Minneapolis, 1993. xiii + 146 pages. [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4096422W/The_Garden_of_Eden_and_the_hope_of_immortality).

Barr reads Genesis 2 and 3 as a story of how human immortality was almost gained and then lost [IC]. As Terence Fretheim quotes him, Barr writes in the preface that Old Testament scholars have long known the Fall of Man reading does not survive a close reading of the text (p. ix) [IC]. Fretheim also reports Barr's view that the first humans were imperfect from the start (p. 92) [TBV].

Richard Hess's review in *Themelios* summarises the book's case that death and sexuality were present before Genesis 3 and that Genesis does not explicitly teach original sin; he adds that Barr's solutions need careful exegesis before they can be accepted [IC]. A review in the *Scottish Journal of Theology* confirms the record [IC]. Fretheim's article "Is Genesis 3 a Fall Story?" (1994) is the main reply, and keeps a modified fall reading [IC]. The book itself was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the strongest scholarly case that the story narrates no fall [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it dissents on T7.
