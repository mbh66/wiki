Arthur S. Reber, James E. Alcock (2020). *Searching for the Impossible: Parapsychology's Elusive Quest*. American Psychologist 75(3): 391-399. DOI: [10.1037/amp0000486](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000486).

Arthur Reber, a psychologist, and James Alcock, a social psychologist, write in reply to Cardeña's 2018 review in the same journal [IC]. They argue that the claims of parapsychology conflict with well-established principles of science, so that any reported effect must come from weak methods, flawed analysis or false positives, whatever the meta-analyses show [IC]. Their abstract states that "Claims made by parapsychologists cannot be true" [IC]. They point to nearly 150 years of research that, on their reading, has produced no stable and replicable effect [IC].

An earlier version of the argument appeared in *Skeptical Inquirer* in 2019 [IC]. Replies to that version, among them Westcombe (2019) and Williams (2019), and Cardeña's own answer, hold that the paper rules psi out on prior grounds and does not engage with the data [IC]. The exchange is the clearest recent statement of the two sides on whether evidence or prior principle should decide the question (inference).

The wiki records the paper as the reply of record to Cardeña on step T2. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.
