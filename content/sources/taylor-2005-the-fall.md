Steve Taylor (2005). *The Fall: The Insanity of the Ego in Human History and the Dawning of a New Era*. O Books, Ropley. ISBN 978-1-905047-20-8. New edition with an afterword, 2018 (ISBN 978-1-78535-804-3). [Author's page](https://www.stevenmtaylor.com/books/the-fall/).

Taylor, a lecturer in psychology who writes on spirituality, argues that at a point in history some human groups developed a strongly individuated sense of self [IC]. He holds that this over-developed ego is the root of war, patriarchy and social inequality, and that it marks about 6,000 years of collective insanity [IC]. He presents the present as the dawning of a new era [IC]. Part One runs from the pre-Fall era through the Fall to the Ego Explosion, and includes a chapter titled "Unfallen Peoples" [IC]. The run that found the book reports that it places some living peoples outside the Fall; the wiki records that it does and repeats none of it [TBV].

Elías Capriles reviewed it in the *International Journal of Transpersonal Studies* 26(1): 110-114 (2007), reading it as a degenerative view of human history [IC]. The run reports that Capriles rejects as untenable the Saharasia thesis of James DeMeo, on which Taylor draws for his dating [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the fullest published case for a split in the human psyche some 6,000 years ago [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T7.
