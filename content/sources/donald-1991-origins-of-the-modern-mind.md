Merlin Donald (1991). *Origins of the Modern Mind*. Harvard University Press.

Merlin Donald, a cognitive neuroscientist, argues that the modern mind is a hybrid built in three transitions from an episodic primate base: [[glossary/m-s/mimetic-culture|mimetic culture]] with *Homo erectus*, mythic culture with spoken language, and theoretic culture with external memory such as writing [IC]. Each new system of representation is added to the older ones, which stay active [IC]. A précis of the book drew a multi-author commentary in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* in 1993 [IC]. Roger Briggs and Robert Bellah build on its stages. The wiki takes from it the clearest dated account of cognitive change (step A2) and strong scholarly support for co-presence (step C1); it is also where the network's first structure takes its name.

The work is on Michael Haupt's reading for the evolution of consciousness [MS].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in cognitive science, with evidence type E3 and claim codes A1, A2, A3, C1, C2.


The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T6; it qualifies T7.
