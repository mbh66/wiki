Sam Parnia, Tara Keshavarz Shirazi, Jignesh Patel and colleagues (2023). *AWAreness during REsuscitation - II: A Multi-Center Study of Consciousness and Awareness in Cardiac Arrest*. Resuscitation 191: 109903. DOI: [10.1016/j.resuscitation.2023.109903](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2023.109903).

AWARE II followed 567 in-hospital cardiac arrests at 25 sites in the United States and the United Kingdom, from May 2017 to March 2020 [IC]. During CPR it tested awareness with a computer and headphones, giving a visual image and an auditory stimulus that a patient could later report, and it monitored brain activity in some patients [IC]. Of the 567 patients, 53 survived and 28 completed interviews; 11 of the 28 reported memories or perceptions suggestive of consciousness, and 6 reported what the authors call recalled experiences of death [IC]. Nobody identified the visual image, and 1 of 28 identified the auditory stimulus [IC]. A second arm, with 126 survivors of cardiac arrest in the community, added a further category of experience, delusions [IC].

Parnia described recalled experiences of death as a real but little understood part of human consciousness [IC]. Critics point to confounds from CPR and EEG [TBV]. The SPR *Psi Encyclopedia* says no survivor identified either stimulus; the abstract reports one auditory identification, and the wiki follows the abstract.

The wiki records the study on steps T1 and T2. On T2 it is coded as a dissent for the visual result; the one auditory identification could support a qualifying reading. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.
