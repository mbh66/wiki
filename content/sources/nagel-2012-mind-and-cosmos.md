Thomas Nagel (2012). *Mind and Cosmos: Why the Materialist Neo-Darwinian Conception of Nature Is Almost Certainly False*. Oxford University Press, New York. x + 130 pages. ISBN: 9780199919758.

Nagel, a philosopher at New York University, argues that materialist reductionism cannot account for consciousness, cognition and value [IC]. The book's chapters, per the Wellcome Collection catalogue, are an introduction, "Antireductionism and the Natural Order", "Consciousness", "Cognition", "Value" and a conclusion [IC]. The publisher's description says he suggests that principles of the growth of order, teleological in their logical form, may be at work in the history of nature [IC]. In Elliott Sober's account, Nagel admits he has no teleological theory of his own [IC]. The publisher also states that his scepticism rests on no religious belief [IC].

Sober argues that the book rests on assumptions about explanation that are too strong, and John Dupré's review in *Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews* is also critical [IC]. Other reviewers welcomed the book as a challenge to a stale debate [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the strongest recent philosophical case for a direction built into nature [TBV].
