Arnold, D. (2019). Where in the brain does Buddhism come from? Critical thoughts regarding Iain McGilchrist's reflections on religion. *Religion, Brain and Behavior* 9(4): 345-362.

Dan Arnold, of the Divinity School at the University of Chicago, reads McGilchrist as making two claims: a scientific one about the differing attention of the two hemispheres, and a normative one, that modern culture is in the grip of instrumental reason [IC]. He grants that the first is cogent and argues that no empirical finding about the brain or about history can deliver the second; categories such as theism or Eastern culture also resist being mapped onto a hemisphere [TBV]. The article appeared in a special issue engaging McGilchrist's work [TBV]. The wiki takes it as a qualifier of C4: it accepts the neuroscience and isolates the cultural diagnosis as the part that fails. The reading rests on summaries; the article has not yet been read in full here [TBV].
