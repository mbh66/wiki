Stephen Jay Gould (1996). *Full House*. Harmony Books.

Stephen Jay Gould, a palaeontologist and historian of science, uses the disappearance of the .400 hitter in baseball and the history of life to argue that many apparent trends are changes in the spread of variation [IC]. Life began near a wall of minimal complexity and could only spread away from it; the most complex organisms sit in a thin tail, and the typical form of life remains bacterial [IC]. The book is a classic statement of the case against progress in evolution, and some biologists, among them Simon Conway Morris, argue for directional trends against it [TBV]. The wiki takes it as the main dissent on step B3.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a critic in palaeontology, with evidence type E3 and claim codes B3.
