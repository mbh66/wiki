Daniel Quinn (1996). *The Story of B*. Bantam Books, New York. 325 pages [IC].

The novel follows a priest sent to investigate a travelling teacher, B, whose lectures make up much of the book [IC]. B carries on the argument of Quinn's *Ishmael* (1992) [IC]. He divides human cultures into two kinds, which Quinn names Takers and Leavers, calls modern agriculture totalitarian, and warns that population growth driven by food production threatens humanity [IC]. One summary reports that B reads the Fall in Genesis as a change in minds, a new way of seeing the world, with no new technology involved [TBV]. Quinn sorts cultures, past and living, into his two kinds [IC]; this record notes that he does so and repeats none of his placements.

*Kirkus Reviews* judged the plot artificial and the ideas important [IC]. A 2025 review at Resilience.org treats the book as a guide to the argument [TBV]. The publisher is confirmed in the Colorado Mountain College library catalogue [IC]. The text was not read for this record [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a popular statement of a cultural split tied to agriculture and to the Genesis story [TBV]. On step T7 it qualifies on part (a), for dating the change to the start of farming; the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/t7-the-bifurcation|T7 page]] reports its reading of the Fall separately.
