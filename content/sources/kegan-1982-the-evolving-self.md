Robert Kegan (1982). *The Evolving Self*. Harvard University Press.

Robert Kegan, a developmental psychologist at Harvard, builds on Piaget to describe meaning-making as a lifelong process in which what a person was embedded in (subject) becomes something they can see and reflect on (object) [IC]. Each such shift marks a new order of consciousness; his later *In Over Our Heads* (1994) argues that modern life demands more than most adults' current order can meet [IC]. The Subject-Object Interview that measures the orders rests mostly on its own manual [TBV]. The wiki takes the book as a core source for step B1 and, through Kegan's claim that each order holds the earlier ones, for C1.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in developmental psychology, with evidence type E3 and claim codes B1, C1.


The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it qualifies T5; it is silent on T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
