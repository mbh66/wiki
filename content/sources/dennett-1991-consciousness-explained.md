Daniel C. Dennett (1991). *Consciousness Explained*. Little Brown.

Daniel Dennett, a philosopher at Tufts University until his death in 2024, attacks the picture of a Cartesian theatre in which experience is presented to an inner observer [IC]. He proposes in its place a multiple drafts model, in which many parallel processes in the brain edit and revise content with no single finish line, and treats the human self as a virtual machine installed by language and culture [IC]. Critics argue that the book explains consciousness away [TBV]. The wiki takes it as the strongest case against the [[glossary/f-l/hard-problem|hard problem]] (step H), and gives it the same weight as Chalmers.

The work is on Michael Haupt's reading for the evolution of consciousness [MS].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a critic in philosophy of mind, with evidence type E3 and claim codes A1, A3, H.



The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it dissents on T1; it dissents on T3; it is silent on T7.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|claim grid]] codes its positions on the claim chain: it qualifies A1 and A3; it dissents from H.
