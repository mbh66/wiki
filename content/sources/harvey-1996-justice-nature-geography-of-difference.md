Harvey, D. (1996). *Justice, Nature and the Geography of Difference*. Cambridge, MA and Oxford: Blackwell.

David Harvey argues that political action usually begins in particular places, among people bound by shared loyalties, which he calls militant particularism [IC]. He holds it a needed start [IC]. Left there, he argues, it stays limited: it has to be translated into a global ambition that links struggles across places [IC]. That translation needs abstraction, which helps places defend themselves together and also loses some of what makes each one particular (inference).

The book drew responses in Antipode soon after it appeared [TBV], and the chapter is still discussed in debates on place and politics, including by Erik Swyngedouw in 2025 [IC]. One recent review dates the book 1993; library catalogues give 1996 [IC]. The wiki reads it as a critic who keeps the local as a starting point and warns against stopping there (inference).
