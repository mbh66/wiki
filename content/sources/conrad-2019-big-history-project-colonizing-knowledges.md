Jenni Conrad (2019). "The Big History Project and colonizing knowledges in world history curriculum". *Journal of Curriculum Studies* 51(1): 1-20. Published online 28 August 2018. DOI 10.1080/00220272.2018.1493143 [IC].

Conrad uses content and frequency analysis to study how the online curriculum of the [[sources/christian-2011-big-history-project|Big History Project]] represents racial and cultural groups, across units that join science and history [IC]. Drawing on Indigenous, postcolonial and critical social studies scholarship, she sets out five features of a Eurocentric world history curriculum and applies them to the course [IC]. She notes the irony that a world history course can be culturally irrelevant or inaccurate for non-Europeans [IC]. A 2026 study of imperialism in open educational resources, in *Social Studies Research and Practice*, reports her finding that the materials often use what it calls settler colonial grammars [IC].

The project presents itself as a modern, science-based origin story [IC]. The full text is paywalled and was not read; the coding rests on the abstract and the citing study [TBV].

The wiki takes from it the first scholarly critique on the wiki of a cosmic story taught as a curriculum [TBV].
