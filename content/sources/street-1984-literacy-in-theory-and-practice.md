Street, B. V. (1984). *Literacy in Theory and Practice*. Cambridge University Press.

The anthropologist Brian Street, drawing on fieldwork in Iran, distinguishes an autonomous model of literacy, which he finds in Jack Goody and Walter Ong and which treats writing as a neutral technology with fixed effects on thought, from an ideological model, in which literacy is a social practice embedded in relations of power [IC]. He rejects the divide between oral and literate minds [IC]. The book founded the New Literacy Studies and remains standard in the field [IC]. The wiki takes it as a direct dissent from Ong on step A3, and as a qualifier of C1: Street does not deny that older forms persist, and he removes the oral and literate categories on which talk of residual orality rests (inference).
