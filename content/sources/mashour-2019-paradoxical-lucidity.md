George A. Mashour, Lori Frank, Alexander Batthyany and colleagues (2019). "Paradoxical Lucidity: A Potential Paradigm Shift for the Neurobiology and Treatment of Severe Dementias". *Alzheimer's & Dementia* 15(8): 1107-1114. DOI: [10.1016/j.jalz.2019.04.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalz.2019.04.002).

The paper reports a workshop held in June 2018 by the US National Institute on Aging, which gathered experts in dementia, neurology, consciousness and ethics [IC]. It defines paradoxical lucidity as an unexpected return of clear speech or memory in people with severe dementia, often near death, and argues that such episodes appear to occur [IC]. If they are confirmed, the authors write, they would challenge the view that severe dementia is wholly irreversible, and could point to new routes for treatment [IC]. They set out an agenda for studying the episodes with clinical and neurobiological methods [IC].

A National Institute on Aging perspective in the same issue notes that the evidence so far rests on anecdotal reports [IC]. Bruce Greyson and Michael Nahm, co-authors here, also write on terminal lucidity in near-death research [TBV].

The wiki takes from it a documented clinical puzzle beside T1, framed by its authors as a question for neurobiology [TBV].
