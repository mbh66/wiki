Terence E. Fretheim (1994). "Is Genesis 3 a Fall Story?". *Word & World* 14(2): 144-153. [Luther Seminary](https://wordandworld.luthersem.edu/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/14-2_Genesis/Is%20Genesis%203%20a%20Fall%20Story.pdf).

Fretheim writes in answer to James Barr's book on Eden [IC]. He notes that the word "fall" does not appear in Genesis 3, and that the traditional reading took shape in later Jewish and Christian interpretation [IC]. He holds that "fall language of some sort is an accurate reading of the text" (p. 145), while traditional views of death and the serpent need revision [IC]. He rejects Barr's view that disobedience came naturally to the first humans [IC]. He also rejects the upward-fall reading, which goes back to Irenaeus and treats the story as a step to maturity and higher self-consciousness (pp. 147-148) [IC]. Citing Phyllis Trible, he reads patriarchy as an effect of sin (p. 148, n. 15) [IC].

Later Genesis scholarship cites the article for its account of downward, upward and falling-out readings [TBV]. The pages to p. 150 were read in the publisher's PDF [IC].

The wiki takes from it a reading of Eden that keeps a break in the relationship with God and describes no split in consciousness (inference) [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T7.
