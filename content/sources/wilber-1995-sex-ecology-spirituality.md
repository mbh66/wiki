Ken Wilber (1995). *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality: The Spirit of Evolution*. Shambhala.

In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* Ken Wilber maps development in four quadrants (interior and exterior, individual and collective) and argues that each level of development transcends and includes the one before it [IC]. The book is the founding statement of his integral theory and draws on Gebser for its stages of culture [IC]. Jeff Meyerhoff's *Bald Ambition* (2010) criticises its use of sources and the reach of its claims [TBV]. The wiki takes it as the fullest synthesis behind the claimed parallel between individual and cultural development (step B2) and a spiritual direction in evolution (B3); the grid codes Wilber through *Integral Psychology* (2000).

The work is on Michael Haupt's reading for the evolution of consciousness [MS].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in integral theory, with evidence type E4 and claim codes B2, B3, C1, C2.
