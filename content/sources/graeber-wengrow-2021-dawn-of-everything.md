David Graeber, David Wengrow (2021). *The Dawn of Everything*. Allen Lane.

David Graeber, an anthropologist who died in 2020 before the book appeared, and David Wengrow, an archaeologist, draw on recent archaeology of early towns, seasonal societies and large settlements without clear hierarchy to argue that people have long chosen and changed their social arrangements [IC]. They reject the evolutionary story of bands, tribes, chiefdoms and states [IC]. The book was widely reviewed, and some archaeologists and anthropologists questioned its choice of cases [TBV]. The wiki takes it as the strongest critique of a single linear sequence of social forms (steps B2 and B3); the book accepts long-standing human cognitive capacities, which leaves sequences of capacity open.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a critic in archaeology, with evidence type E3 and claim codes A2, B2, B3.



The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it dissents on T7; it is silent on T8.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|claim grid]] codes its positions on the claim chain: it qualifies A2; it dissents from B2 and B3.
