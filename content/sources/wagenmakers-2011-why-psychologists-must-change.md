Eric-Jan Wagenmakers, Ruud Wetzels, Denny Borsboom, Han L. J. van der Maas (2011). *Why Psychologists Must Change the Way They Analyze Their Data: The Case of Psi: Comment on Bem (2011)*. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 100(3): 426-432. DOI: [10.1037/a0022790](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022790).

Published in the same issue as Bem's paper, this comment treats psi as a test case for how psychologists analyse their data [IC]. Eric-Jan Wagenmakers and colleagues argue that parts of Bem's analysis were exploratory, that one-sided p-values overstate the evidence, and that a default Bayesian t test applied to his data shows the evidence for psi is "weak to nonexistent" [IC]. Their wider point is about method: the same habits that produced Bem's results run through ordinary psychology, so the field should change its standards of analysis (inference from the title and abstract).

Bem, Jessica Utts and Wesley Johnson answered in the same journal that a more reasonable prior gives strong evidence for psi [IC]. The exchange became a standard reference in later debates over Bayesian and conventional statistics in psychology (inference), and it fed into the move towards preregistered replications, such as Ritchie, Wiseman and French (2012) [TBV].

The wiki records the comment as a dissent on step T2, alongside the reply it drew. Anomalous experience research is recorded on the Anabranch layer, never adopted.
