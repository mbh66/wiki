David J. Chalmers (1996). *The Conscious Mind*. Oxford University Press.

David Chalmers, now at New York University, develops the distinction he drew in 1995 between the easy problems of consciousness and the [[glossary/f-l/hard-problem|hard problem]] [IC]. He argues that consciousness does not logically supervene on the physical, using arguments such as the conceivability of zombies, and proposes a naturalistic dualism in which experience is a fundamental feature tied to information [IC]. The book is among the most cited in philosophy of mind, and physicalists, Daniel Dennett among them, reject its central arguments [IC]. The wiki takes it as the main statement of one side of the hinge (step H).

The work is on Michael Haupt's reading for the evolution of consciousness [MS].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a context work in philosophy of mind, with evidence type E4 and claim codes A1, H.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it dissents on T3.
