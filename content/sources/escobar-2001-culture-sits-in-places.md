Escobar, A. (2001). *Culture sits in places: Reflections on globalism and subaltern strategies of localization*. Political Geography 20(2): 139-174. DOI: [10.1016/S0962-6298(00)00064-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0962-6298(00)00064-0).

Arturo Escobar argues that much social theory has erased place in favour of the global [TBV]. He treats local knowledge as specific to a place without being bound to it [TBV]. Drawing on the struggles of Afro-Colombian and Indigenous movements, he reads their defence of place as a strategy that works across scales and links places through networks, which he calls meshworks [TBV]. He presents this as a response to globalism, grounded in the movements' own terms [TBV].

The article is widely cited in political geography and the anthropology of place [TBV]. The wiki records the movements only through Escobar's account. It reads the article as place-based knowledge joined to wider networks, on the movements' terms (inference).
