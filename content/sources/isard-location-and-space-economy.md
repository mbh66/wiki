Isard, W. (1956). *Location and Space-Economy: A General Theory Relating to Industrial Location, Market Areas, Land Use, Trade, and Urban Structure*. Cambridge, MA: Technology Press of MIT; New York: John Wiley & Sons.

The book that founded regional science as a field. Isard combined Weber's least-cost framework with Predöhl's substitution principle, so that choosing a location became a choice of how to substitute transport inputs for other inputs, and tied location theory to the general theory of production and trade. The wiki uses Isard to trace the lineage of the substitution principle and to show how Weber's deviations became special cases inside a general model.
