William James (1898). *Human Immortality: Two Supposed Objections to the Doctrine*. The Ingersoll Lecture. Houghton, Mifflin and Company. Public domain. [Religion Online](https://www.religion-online.org/book/human-immortality/).

James grants the physiologists' premise that thought is a function of the brain [IC]. He argues that "we are entitled also to consider permissive or transmissive function", in which the brain lets through and sifts a wider consciousness, as a prism shapes light [IC]. On this reading the brain's dependence fits a consciousness that exists before and beyond any one brain [IC]. In the Preface he limits his aim to showing that immortality is compatible with that dependence, and he claims no proof of survival [IC].

Edward F. Kelly and colleagues revive the Myers and James filter model in *Irreducible Mind* (2007), ch. 9 [TBV]. Paul Marshall names the lecture, with Bergson's *Matter and Memory* (1896), as a founding filter text, and notes that its founders gave analogies and no mechanism [TBV]. Augustine and Martin (2015) give the main published case against transmission readings [IC].

The wiki takes from it the primary text for the filter claim on T3, and a qualified case on T1 [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a canon work in philosophy of mind, with evidence type E4 and claim codes H, H2.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it qualifies T1; it affirms T2; it affirms T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
