Henri Bergson (1896). *Matter and Memory*. Translated by Nancy Margaret Paul and W. Scott Palmer. George Allen and Unwin, London, 1911. Public domain [IC]. [Mead Project, Brock University](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Bergson/Bergson_1911b/Bergson_1911_01.html).

In chapter 1 Bergson treats the body as a centre of action [IC]. He argues that the brain, an image among other images, cannot create the image of the universe [IC]. He calls it "an instrument of selection in regard to the movement executed" [IC]. He holds that its work is limited to transmitting and dividing movement [IC]. On his view, brain damage breaks the path by which memories become actual, while the memories themselves survive [TBV].

Early expositors, Gunn and Slosson among them, read the book this way [IC]. Bergson does not use James's word filter (inference). The translation was read in extracts on the Mead Project site [IC].

The wiki takes from it a second primary text, beside [[sources/james-1898-human-immortality|James (1898)]], for the claim that the brain selects and does not produce [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it affirms T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8; it is silent on T9.
