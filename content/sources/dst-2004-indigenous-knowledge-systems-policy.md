Department of Science and Technology, Republic of South Africa (2004). *Indigenous Knowledge Systems Policy*. Department of Science and Technology, Pretoria. [Link](https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/23085/).

South Africa's Indigenous Knowledge Systems Policy, adopted by Cabinet in November 2004, sets out to recognise, affirm, develop, promote and protect indigenous knowledge [IC]. It names four drivers: affirming African cultural values, developing the services that knowledge holders provide, the contribution of indigenous knowledge to the economy, and interfacing with other knowledge systems [IC]. Knowledge holders took part in drafting it [IC]. Government has described the policy as a response to the marginalisation and misappropriation of indigenous knowledge [IC].

The policy led to the Protection, Promotion, Development and Management of Indigenous Knowledge Act 6 of 2019, which established the National Indigenous Knowledge Systems Office (NIKSO) to put the Act into effect [IC]. The policy text was read in the copy South Africa sent to the World Intellectual Property Organization, circulated in March 2006 as document WIPO/GRTKF/IC/9/11 [IC].

The wiki records the policy in its own terms on steps T7 and T9. On T7 it speaks only to the continuity of indigenous knowledge, as living systems held by practitioners. On T9 it calls for interfacing between knowledge systems at national scale, chiefly for innovation, new products and national competitiveness, under protection that secures benefits to knowledge holders. It says little about place.
