Luigi Fantappiè (1942). *Principi di una teoria unitaria del mondo fisico e biologico*. Rome: Società Editrice Humanitas Nova, 1944 (134 pp.); reprinted by Di Renzo Editore, Rome, 1993. Theory presented to the Accademia d'Italia, 30 October 1942.

The mathematician Luigi Fantappiè presented his theory to the Accademia d'Italia on 30 October 1942 [IC]. The book itself appeared in Rome in 1944 from Humanitas Nova, and Di Renzo Editore reprinted it in 1993 [IC]. He argued that the physical world is governed by forward-in-time solutions and the law of entropy, while life is governed by backward-in-time solutions and a symmetrical law he named syntropy, a tendency toward convergence [IC]. The idea sits outside mainstream physics; it has been carried forward mainly by Ulisse Di Corpo and Antonella Vannini [IC]. Tom Morgan's *Anomaly Grid* cites syntropy for its claim that complexity has a direction. The wiki takes it as an early statement of that claim, drawn from mathematics.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|library]] codes it as a context work in mathematical physics, with evidence type E4 and claim codes B3.
