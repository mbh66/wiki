Cheryl Bartlett, Murdena Marshall, Albert Marshall (2012). *Two-Eyed Seeing and Other Lessons Learned within a Co-Learning Journey of Bringing Together Indigenous and Mainstream Knowledges and Ways of Knowing*. Journal of Environmental Studies and Sciences 2(4): 331-340. DOI: [10.1007/s13412-012-0086-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13412-012-0086-8).

Cheryl Bartlett, a professor at Cape Breton University, and the Mi'kmaw Elders Murdena Marshall and Albert Marshall of Eskasoni First Nation describe the Integrative Science programme they built at the university to bring Indigenous and mainstream sciences together [IC]. At its centre is Etuaptmumk, Two-Eyed Seeing, the guiding principle Elder Albert Marshall brought to the programme in 2004 [IC]. In the words Cape Breton University uses, it means learning to see from one eye with the strengths of Indigenous knowledges and from the other with the strengths of Western knowledges, and using both eyes together for the benefit of all [IC]. Elder Albert describes it as a guiding principle for co-learning [IC]. The paper draws eight lessons from the programme [IC].

Two-Eyed Seeing has since been taken up in health research, education and resource management in Canada and beyond [IC]. Reviews find its later use uneven: Wright and colleagues (2019) and Roher and colleagues (2021) [IC].

The wiki records the paper in its own terms on step T9, as an argument for joining two ways of knowing while keeping each whole.
