Cox, M. and Ross, J. M. (2011). "Robustness and Vulnerability of Community Irrigation Systems: The Case of the Taos Valley Acequias." *Journal of Environmental Economics and Management* 61(3): 254-266. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeem.2010.10.004

Using crop production estimated from satellite imagery as a measure of collective action, the authors find that acequias with water-sharing agreements and access to groundwater perform better, and that those whose land has been more subdivided since 1970, as new residents bought and split parcels, perform worse, as do those more affected by urbanization. The fragmentation they measure is of land parcels, which divides each acequia's water rights among more users and raises transaction costs. The wiki uses the paper on the acequias case page in the catchment register.
