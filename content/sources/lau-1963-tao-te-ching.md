Laozi, *Tao Te Ching*, translated by D. C. Lau. Penguin Classics, 1963 [TBV].

The *Daodejing* is a short classical Chinese text of 81 chapters, traditionally attributed to Laozi [IC]. As two secondary pages quote Lau's rendering, chapter 40 opens: "Turning back is how the way moves" (p. 47) [TBV]. Other translators give the chapter's opening character as returning (Arthur Waley, 1934), reversion (Lin Yutang, 1955) or a movement by contraries (James Legge, 1891) [TBV]. Run 3d2 looked for a lawful preview of Lau's chapter and found none, so his wording and his notes on the chapter are unchecked [TBV].

Lau's version is cited as a standard English translation in later commentary [TBV]. This record keeps the translator's own words for the chapter and reads nothing into them from outside the text.

The wiki takes from it a Daoist text that describes the movement of the way as return, with no end-point named [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T5; it qualifies T6; it is silent on T7; it is silent on T8.
