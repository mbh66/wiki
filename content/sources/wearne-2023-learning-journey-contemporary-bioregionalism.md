Wearne, S., Hubbard, E., Jónás, K. and Wilke, M. (2023). *A learning journey into contemporary bioregionalism*. People and Nature 5(6): 2124-2140. DOI: [10.1002/pan3.10548](https://doi.org/10.1002/pan3.10548). Open access (CC BY).

The authors interviewed leading bioregional thinkers and practitioners across seven countries and analysed the interviews reflexively [TBV]. They describe a new wave they call bioregioning [TBV]. In their account it holds knowledge tied to a place in dialogue with general knowledge drawn from exchange between places, and so avoids both isolated localism and imported global best practice [TBV]. They find the movement engaging the critiques bioregionalism has drawn, including its record with Indigenous worldviews, and they name its risks, among them sidelining decolonial alternatives and attracting ethno-nationalists [TBV].

On step T9 the article is the closest statement of part (a) in print from outside the network. It affirms the step, at Michael Haupt's ruling of 25 November 2026; it says bioregioning might open just pathways, and names the risks. The interviewees include Joe Brewer, whose design pathway the BioConomy network has adopted, and Daniel Christian Wahl. The [[glossary/a-e/bioregionalism|bioregionalism]] entry gives the wider tradition.
