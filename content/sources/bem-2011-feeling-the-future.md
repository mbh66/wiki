Daryl J. Bem (2011). *Feeling the Future: Experimental Evidence for Anomalous Retroactive Influences on Cognition and Affect*. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 100(3): 407-425. DOI: [10.1037/a0021524](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021524).

Daryl Bem, a social psychologist and emeritus professor at Cornell University, reports nine experiments with more than 1,000 participants that test for what he calls anomalous retroactive influence [IC]. Each "time-reverses" a well-established effect in psychology, so that a person's responses are recorded before the stimulus thought to cause them [IC]. The mean effect size across the nine was 0.22, and all but one gave a statistically significant result [IC]. Experiments 8 and 9 tested whether practising words after a surprise memory test improved recall of those same words on the test [IC]. Bem chose procedures that others could repeat and invited independent replication [IC].

The paper set off a wide debate on research methods in psychology [IC]. Wagenmakers and colleagues reanalysed the data in the same issue, and Ritchie, Wiseman and French reported three failed replications of the recall effect [IC]. Bem replied that it was premature to judge whether the effect replicates [IC].
