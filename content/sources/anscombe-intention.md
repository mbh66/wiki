Anscombe, G. E. M. (1957). *Intention*. Oxford: Basil Blackwell.

A short monograph on what makes an action intentional. Section 32 contrasts a shopper's list with a detective's record of the same purchases: when they disagree, the record is at fault, while the list stays as it is and the purchases fall short of it. Philosophers since, John Searle among them, use the example to illustrate what they call "direction of fit." The wiki takes the example as the plainest statement of the question the founding suite asks of every funding relationship: whose statement stays fixed, and whose is corrected to match.
