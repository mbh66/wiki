Sanderson, S. K. (1990). *Social Evolutionism: A Critical History*. Studies in Social Discontinuity. Basil Blackwell, Cambridge, MA, and Oxford. xviii + 251 pp. ISBN 1-55786-073-4; paperback 1992, ISBN 1-55786-337-7.

Stephen K. Sanderson traces evolutionary theories of society from the nineteenth century onwards [IC]. By the publisher's account he criticises some of their modes while defending an evolutionary reading of world history as yielding real insight [TBV]. His later *Evolutionism and Its Critics* defends the approach against its critics, so the book sits closer to a reformer of the sequence than to an opponent (inference). [[sources/ronfeldt-timn|Ronfeldt (1996)]] cites it for three great transformations and for pitfalls he hopes to avoid, among them teleology and a presumption that progress is good; the wiki has not checked that list in Sanderson's text [TBV]. This entry rests on the publisher's description and review records; the book was not read [TBV].
