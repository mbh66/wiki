Mark Purcell, J. Christopher Brown (2005). *Against the Local Trap: Scale and the Study of Environment and Development*. Progress in Development Studies 5(4): 279-297. DOI: [10.1191/1464993405ps122oa](https://doi.org/10.1191/1464993405ps122oa).

Mark Purcell and J. Christopher Brown name and argue against what they call the local trap: the assumption that decisions made at the local scale are inherently more just or more sustainable than decisions made at other scales [IC]. They hold that scales are produced through social and political struggle and have no fixed qualities of their own [IC]. On this view, whether a local arrangement serves justice or sustainability depends on who holds power at that scale and what they do with it, and has to be shown case by case [IC]. They address the argument to research on environment and development [IC].

The paper founded a line of critique that geographers and planners have taken up widely [IC]. Born and Purcell (2006) extended it to food systems and the call to eat local [IC].

The wiki records the paper as a qualification on step T9, part (b). Read in full, it allows that a local arrangement can lead to good outcomes, as a honey project on undisturbed forest likely would, and denies only that any scale is better in itself [IC]. The authors write that their critique leaves room for localisation, judged case by case [IC]. Michael Haupt moved the paper from a dissent on 25 November 2026, after Anabranch run 9 [MS].
