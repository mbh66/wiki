Mark Purcell, J. Christopher Brown (2005). *Against the Local Trap: Scale and the Study of Environment and Development*. Progress in Development Studies 5(4): 279-297. DOI: [10.1191/1464993405ps122oa](https://doi.org/10.1191/1464993405ps122oa).

Mark Purcell and J. Christopher Brown name and argue against what they call the local trap: the assumption that decisions made at the local scale are inherently more just or more sustainable than decisions made at other scales [IC]. They hold that scales are produced through social and political struggle and have no fixed qualities of their own [IC]. On this view, whether a local arrangement serves justice or sustainability depends on who holds power at that scale and what they do with it, and has to be shown case by case (inference from the abstract). They address the argument to research on environment and development [IC].

The paper founded a line of critique that geographers and planners have taken up widely [IC]. Born and Purcell (2006) extended it to food systems and the call to eat local [IC].

The wiki records the paper as a dissent on step T9. It leaves Indigenous knowledge aside and questions the premise that place-based arrangements carry a built-in good, which bioregional writing often assumes (inference).

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it dissents from T9.
