Turnbull, D. (2000). *Masons, Tricksters and Cartographers: Comparative Studies in the Sociology of Scientific and Indigenous Knowledge*. Harwood Academic, Amsterdam.

David Turnbull compares scientific and Indigenous knowledge traditions, Pacific navigation among them [IC]. He argues that all knowledge is local, that local knowledge has always been mobile, and that what passes as universal knowledge is local knowledge assembled so that it can travel [IC].

A review by Ron Eglash in Science as Culture and another in Technology and Culture confirm this reading [IC]. On step T7 the book is the strongest dissent: it disputes that place-held and portable knowledge ever split.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T1; it is silent on T2; it is silent on T3; it is silent on T4; it is silent on T5; it is silent on T6; it dissents on T7; it is silent on T8.
