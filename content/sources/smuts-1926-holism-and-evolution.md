Smuts, J. C. (1926). *Holism and Evolution*. Macmillan, London. A second edition (1927) adds a preface on Whitehead; a third is dated 1936 [TBV].

Jan Christiaan Smuts coined the word holism for a tendency he held to run through the universe: a whole-making factor that builds ever more organised wholes, from the inorganic to the spiritual [IC]. He treats personality as the highest whole the process has yet produced [TBV].

Shula Marks (2001) records that the book was well regarded in its day and has few admirers now [IC]. Smuts was a statesman whose segregationist politics are well documented. Peder Anker (2001), Marks and Saul Dubow (2008) argue that he used holism to justify racial segregation; Garson (2007) and William Sweet dispute that link, noting that the book's talk of race almost always means the human race [IC]. The book is coded here on what it argues.

On the Anabranch layer, run 7a codes it as the closest statement in print of T6 (b), the trend towards wholeness.

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it affirms T4; it qualifies T5; it affirms T6.
