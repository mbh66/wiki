Catherine A. Odora Hoppers (ed.) (2002). *Indigenous Knowledge and the Integration of Knowledge Systems: Towards a Philosophy of Articulation*. New Africa Books, Claremont, South Africa. xiv + 285 pages. ISBN 9781919876580 [IC].

The volume gathers essays on indigenous knowledge systems, with a focus on Africa [IC]. Its catalogue summary says it explores how the social and natural sciences can support these systems, and how the systems can help change scientific and higher education institutions and the policy domain [IC]. The AfricaBib abstract adds that it sets the systems against the history of colonialism, the growth of science and the African Renaissance [IC]. It also treats their part in healing the harms of colonial oppression, their protection as intellectual property, and their place in a shift it calls post-postmodern [IC].

Its reception was not checked, and no chapter was read; the coding rests on the catalogue summary, the abstract and the contents in WorldCat, Stanford SearchWorks and Google Books [TBV].

The wiki takes from it an African scholarly voice that speaks of its own knowledge systems in its own terms, and argues for joining them with the sciences through what it calls articulation [TBV].

The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] codes its positions: it is silent on T2; it qualifies T7; it affirms T9.
