Corliss Lamont (1935). *The Illusion of Immortality*. Introduction by John Dewey. G. P. Putnam's Sons, New York. 292 pages [IC]. Google Books lists the 1935 printing as a second edition [TBV].

Lamont surveys ideas of an afterlife from ancient times onward and weighs them against science and philosophy [IC]. His central argument is that personality and body form a unity, so that the evidence linking mind to brain counts against survival [IC]. He also replies to William James's transmission analogy; Run 3d found that reply only as quoted by other writers, and Run 3d2 did not read it [TBV].

James H. Leuba reviewed the book in *The Journal of Religion* 15(3) (1935): 323-325, DOI 10.1086/481628, though the review itself was not read [IC]. Later editions include Philosophical Library (1950) and a fourth edition (1985) [IC]. The copyright status of the 1935 text was not checked, so no digital copy was opened [TBV].

The wiki takes from it an early and often reprinted critic of survival who answers [[sources/james-1898-human-immortality|James (1898)]] [TBV].
