Costs that an economic activity imposes on people or ecosystems outside the transaction, which the producer and buyer do not pay and the price does not show.

## Extended definition

Arthur Cecil Pigou set out the idea in *The Economics of Welfare* (1920): where private cost falls below social cost, markets produce too much of the harmful activity, and a tax equal to the uncharged cost (now called a Pigouvian tax) brings the two together. Ronald Coase replied in 1960 that where property rights are clear and bargaining is cheap, the parties can settle the cost between them, and that the real obstacle is the cost of transacting. Pollution of air and rivers, depletion of soil and aquifers, and the loading of the atmosphere with carbon are the standard cases.

The term carries weight across this wiki. The [[frameworks/time-framework|TIME framework]] holds that each coordination form pushed costs outside itself, and the Market form's are recorded as labor, communities and the biosphere. TIME uses the word in a broader sense than Pigou and Coase: it counts costs to communities, to labor and to the biosphere whether or not a price could be attached, and it includes unpaid inputs such as care and the [[glossary/a-e/commons|commons]]. The research brief [[research/evolution-of-production|The Evolution of Production]] gives an operational test (an identifiable harm or uncompensated input, borne by a party outside the producing form's decision and accounting boundary, and charged by none of the form's own prices or rules) and finds that every production form met it, the Emergent form included. It finds that the costs were taken up by whichever form chose to organize them, most often the state pricing them, as Britain did with the Factory Act 1833 and the Alkali Act 1863; [[frameworks/sequence-and-the-debt|The Sequence and the Debt]] sets out the record. Transgressed [[glossary/m-s/planetary-boundaries|planetary boundaries]] are negative externalities accumulated at planetary scale. The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, in force from January 2026, is a Pigouvian charge on carbon embedded in imports, and the wiki's [[research/glocalization-and-the-evolution-of-production|production-location brief]] reads it as part of the cost globalization left unpriced returning to the delivered price of heavy goods.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Positive externalities**: benefits that fall outside the transaction, such as the industrial commons of skills a cluster of firms creates.
- **[[glossary/t-z/unrecognized-contribution|Unrecognized contribution]]**: the wiki's term for work that maintains a place and earns no price, a benefit the market leaves uncounted. Unpaid labor is also a cost in TIME's sense when a producing form relies on it without paying for it.
