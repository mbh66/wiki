The administrative unit that coordinates local economic activity in the conventional [[economy|Economy]], defined by political boundary.

## Extended definition

The municipality is to the conventional economy what the [[biohub|BioHub]] is to the [[bioconomy|BioConomy]]: the body that holds a local economy in view and coordinates its activity. The difference is what sets the boundary and what the coordination optimizes for.

A municipal boundary is administrative. It is drawn by legislative act, historical accident, or political negotiation. A watershed does not respect it. A food system does not respect it. The ecological processes that sustain a community cross municipal lines without registering in the governance structure. A municipality coordinates what happens inside an arbitrary perimeter; a BioHub coordinates what happens inside an ecological one.

In the [[time|TIME]] framework, the municipality is an [[i-form-institutional|I-form]] body operating inside an [[m-form-market|M-form]] economy. Its tools are zoning, planning permission, rates collection, infrastructure provision, and the attraction of investment. Each of these is organized around [[throughput-economics|throughput]]: the municipality succeeds, on its own metrics, when money flows through its territory. Industrial development zones, tax incentives for inward investment, rate-funded infrastructure serving commercial property: these are the instruments of a coordination body optimizing for flow.

The transition to [[e-form-emergent|+E]] coordination replaces the municipality's role with something structurally different. The BioHub does not attract investment into an administrative area. It convenes cooperatives, coordinates landscape-scale commitments, and connects community-level [[commitment-pool|commitment pooling]] to bioregional-scale financing. Its authority is voluntary and commitment-based, not statutory. Its boundary is set by the living system it manages, not by the jurisdiction that drew the line on a map.

The [[coordination-node|coordination node]] analysis makes the shift visible architecturally. The M-form coordination node is the skyscraper, the stock exchange, the trading floor: built to concentrate and accelerate flow. The +E coordination node is the BioHub: built to hold a bioregional economy in view and coordinate retention at landscape scale.

## Contrast with BioHub

- A municipality governs by statutory authority within an administrative boundary. A BioHub coordinates by voluntary commitment within an ecological one. 
- A municipality measures success by rates revenue, employment figures, and development applications processed. A BioHub measures success by ecological yield, cooperative density, and community capacity retained. 
- The municipality is accountable upward to provincial or national government. The BioHub is accountable laterally to the community whose commitments it coordinates.
