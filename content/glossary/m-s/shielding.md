Any arrangement that holds back the selection pressure of the dominant system from a young initiative, such as a subsidy, a pilot permission, a sympathetic buyer or a remote place.

Shielding is the first of the three properties of [[glossary/m-s/protective-space|protective space]] named by Smith and Raven (2012). It keeps the prices, rules, measures and expectations of the [[glossary/m-s/regime|regime]] from bearing fully on something new before it's ready. Smith and Raven separate two kinds. *Passive* shielding uses spaces that already exist: an area the grid doesn't reach, general research funding, a group of committed users. *Active* shielding is built on purpose by advocates: a feed-in tariff, an exemption, a protected share of public buying.

The forms can be financial, regulatory, market, spatial, temporal, social, cognitive or informational. The shielding documented in the research was almost all public. A low profile appears in early niches as a condition of being small, and no study found treats it as a designed tool.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Ontological shielding**, in [[people/theorists/eisenstein-charles|Charles Eisenstein]]'s essay of February 2026, is a proposal to protect phenomena he holds are changed by sceptical exposure. It makes a claim about reality that transitions research doesn't make or test; the research claim is social and economic.
- **[[glossary/m-s/nurturing|Nurturing]]** builds an initiative up inside the shield; shielding only holds pressure back.
- **Secrecy** hides an initiative from view. Shielding in the research is usually open and recorded.
