Merlin Donald's name for the stage of human culture built on mimesis: the deliberate use of the whole body to imitate, rehearse and communicate, before spoken language.

Merlin Donald describes four stages of human culture in *Origins of the Modern Mind* (1991): episodic, mimetic, mythic and theoretic [IC]. Mimetic culture is the second. Its medium is mimesis, which Donald defines as intentional, representational action: gesture, mime, rehearsed skill and shared ritual, all of which can carry meaning without words. He ties its arrival to *Homo erectus*, near the appearance of that species; his 1993 précis gives about 1.5 million years ago [IC]. Each stage, Donald holds, is kept inside the next, so mimetic skill remains part of the modern mind [IC].

Robert Bellah builds his history of religion on Donald's stages [IC]. The wiki's Mimetic structure takes Donald's word for its first structure; the network's pages date it differently, and the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/dates/index|dated thresholds]] set those dates beside Donald's and the archaeological record [MS].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Imitation in other animals** copies a behaviour. Donald's mimesis is deliberate and representational: the actor knows the action stands for something.
- **The wiki's Mimetic structure** borrows Donald's word and places it in Michael Haupt's five-structure sequence [MS]. Donald's own stage is defined by its medium of communication.
- **Memetics,** the study of ideas spreading like genes, shares a Greek root and nothing else.
