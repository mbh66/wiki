The return of production to the home country of the firm that owns it, after a period of making it abroad.

## Extended definition

Reshoring changes where production happens and leaves its owner unchanged. Its scale is disputed. The Reshoring Initiative, an advocacy group, counted 244,000 US manufacturing jobs announced in 2024 through reshoring and foreign investment combined. Kearney's index of realized imports against US output stayed negative in 2024 and 2025, so announcements outrun relocation. Much of what is announced is new capacity in strategic sectors such as semiconductors, placed at home with public subsidy, more than existing plants moved back.

In the wiki's terms reshoring is a shift of location without a shift of governance, and does not by itself meet the definition of [[glossary/f-l/glocalization|glocalization]].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[glossary/m-s/nearshoring|Nearshoring]]**: moving production to a neighboring country.
- **[[glossary/f-l/friendshoring|Friendshoring]]**: moving it to a politically aligned country.
- **Relocalization**: returning production to the place of consumption. Only relocalization under place-based governance matches the wiki's glocalization.
