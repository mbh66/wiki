Production in which many people contribute freely to a shared resource and coordinate through the resource itself, with no firm or market price directing their work. Yochai Benkler named it commons-based peer production in 2002.

## Extended definition

Yochai Benkler introduced the term in "Coase's Penguin, or, Linux and The Nature of the Firm" (2002) and developed it in *The Wealth of Networks* (2006). His examples were free and open-source software and Wikipedia: large projects built by volunteers who choose their own tasks, with the shared work itself showing what needs doing next.

[[people/theorists/bauwens-michel|Michel Bauwens]] and the P2P Foundation extended the idea to physical production and to the governance and property forms a peer-produced commons needs. The wiki's [[glossary/a-e/cosmo-local-production|cosmo-local production]] comes from that line of work.

Benkler argues that peer production "has an advantage over firms and markets" in matching people to creative tasks, because "the declining price of computation, communication, and storage have... placed the material means of information and cultural production in the hands of a significant fraction of the world's population." The practice is older than the term. Richard Stallman announced the GNU project in 1983 and set out its reasons in [[sources/stallman-gnu-manifesto|The GNU Manifesto]] (1985); he cited the restrictions of proprietary software licenses.

In the wiki's production sequence, peer production is one of the two production forms of the [[e-form-emergent|Emergent form]], beside cosmo-local production, and is tied to networked information as its communications revolution. See [[research/evolution-of-production|The Evolution of Production]] and [[frameworks/sequence-and-the-debt|The Sequence and the Debt]].

Its scale and its costs. A Harvard Business School working paper (Hoffmann, Nagle and Zhou, 2024) puts the demand-side value of widely used open source software at US\$8.8 trillion, and Chad Whitacre disputes the method [TBV]. The work rests heavily on unpaid labor: in Tidelift's 2024 survey of more than 400 maintainers, 60 percent were unpaid and 60 percent had quit or considered quitting [MS, Tidelift sells maintainer funding]. Peer production also runs on computing, storage and networks it does not govern, and so draws energy and water through data centers. These are costs it pushes outside itself, in the broader sense the wiki gives [[glossary/m-s/negative-externalities|negative externalities]].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[p4p-peer-for-peer|P4P (peer for peer)]]** names a later step: from horizontal digital exchange between peers to place-based coordination between communities for mutual benefit.
- **[[glossary/m-s/stigmergy|Stigmergy]]** names the coordination mechanism, in which traces left in a shared medium guide the next contribution. Peer production names the mode of production that often runs on it.
- **[[glossary/t-z/unrecognized-contribution|Unrecognized contribution]]** names unpaid work that maintains a place. The unpaid maintenance peer production depends on is a case of it.
