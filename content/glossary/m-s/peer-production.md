Production in which many people contribute freely to a shared resource and coordinate through the resource itself, with no firm or market price directing their work. Yochai Benkler named it commons-based peer production in 2002.

## Extended definition

Yochai Benkler introduced the term in "Coase's Penguin, or, Linux and The Nature of the Firm" (2002) and developed it in *The Wealth of Networks* (2006). His examples were free and open-source software and Wikipedia: large projects built by volunteers who choose their own tasks, with the shared work itself showing what needs doing next.

[[people/theorists/bauwens-michel|Michel Bauwens]] and the P2P Foundation extended the idea to physical production and to the governance and property forms a peer-produced commons needs. The wiki's [[glossary/a-e/cosmo-local-production|cosmo-local production]] comes from that line of work.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[p4p-peer-for-peer|P4P (peer for peer)]]** names a later step: from horizontal digital exchange between peers to place-based coordination between communities for mutual benefit.
- **[[glossary/m-s/stigmergy|Stigmergy]]** names the coordination mechanism, in which traces left in a shared medium guide the next contribution. Peer production names the mode of production that often runs on it.
