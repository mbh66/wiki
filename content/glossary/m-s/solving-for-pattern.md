Wendell Berry's test for a good solution: it solves more than one problem and creates no new ones, because it fits the larger pattern it belongs to.

## Extended definition

[[people/ancestors/berry-wendell|Wendell Berry]] set out the idea in his essay "Solving for Pattern", collected in *The Gift of Good Land* (1981). His examples come from farming. A solution that answers one problem in isolation tends to create problems elsewhere in the larger pattern of the farm and its community. A good solution works with that pattern, so that one change answers several problems at once.

Berry goes on to list the qualities good solutions tend to share. Among them, a good solution accepts given limits and makes use, as far as it can, of what is already at hand.
