An agreement in which downstream water users compensate upstream landholders, usually in kind, for protecting the forest and land that feed their water.

## Extended definition

Fundación Natura Bolivia brokered the first agreement in 2003 at Los Negros, where downstream irrigators gave upstream landholders beehives, fruit seedlings, irrigation pipe and wire in return for protecting forest. Compensation flows through a local fund under municipal and cooperative rules, and its design draws on Andean norms of reciprocity (*ayni* and *minka*). Natura reports agreements in around 80 Bolivian municipalities.

The model has been tested once at scale. A randomized trial across 129 communities found local water-quality gains where cattle were kept out of streams and no detectable effect at the scale of community water supplies ([[sources/pynegar-2018-watershared-water-quality|Pynegar et al. 2018]]).

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- [[glossary/m-s/payment-for-ecosystem-services-pes|Payment for ecosystem services (PES)]]. The wider family. A reciprocal agreement is a PES scheme paid in kind, set locally, and framed as exchange between neighbors.
- Water fund. A trust in which a city's utility and other contributors pay for upstream work. Water funds are usually governed by their contributors, with upstream communities as project recipients.
