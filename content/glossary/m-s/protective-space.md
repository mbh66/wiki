The arrangements that shield a young innovation from the full pressure of the dominant system, nurture its development and empower it to survive outside.

Smith and Raven (2012) asked what protection for a [[glossary/m-s/niche|niche]] actually does, and named three properties. [[glossary/m-s/shielding|Shielding]] holds back the selection pressure of the [[glossary/m-s/regime|regime]]. [[glossary/m-s/nurturing|Nurturing]] helps the innovation learn, build networks and form shared expectations. [[glossary/a-e/empowerment|Empowerment]] prepares it to stand outside. They argued that protection is political, since advocates must win it and keep it with persuasive stories, and they named its risks: capture by those who gain from it, protection that governments find hard to withdraw, and protected space that comes to shelter weak work.

The evidence that protective space leads to lasting survival comes mostly from single cases of initiatives that lasted. The largest comparisons, of Transition initiatives and of business incubators, find that material resources, context and the moment of exit matter as much as the shelter itself (see the brief below).

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[glossary/f-l/infant-industry-protection|Infant industry protection]]** is List's case for temporary tariffs on a nation's new industries. Protective space covers any provider and any form: money, rules, a buyer, a place, a period of time, a network or a shared story.
- **[[glossary/a-e/entity-gestation|Entity gestation]]** is the wiki's fixed period before a BioHub cohort registers an entity: one temporal form of protective space.
- **Business incubation** is one programme type that provides protective space for firms.
