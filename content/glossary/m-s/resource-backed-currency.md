A unit of payment that a holder can redeem for a defined quantity of a natural resource, or for a defined right to it.

The wiki uses the term narrowly. A currency is resource-backed only when redemption is possible: Virginia's tobacco notes, redeemable for inspected tobacco at a named warehouse, and modern [[glossary/t-z/warehouse-receipt|warehouse receipts]] are examples. The term does not cover a currency held one for one by national money, even when it is issued for ecological work, or a unit whose story or name refers to a resource. No documented currency has been backed by water in this sense; the units honestly tied to water were rights, turns and fees held by the people using it.

**Contrast.** The wiki's case registers separate three relationships between a unit and a resource. A *backed* unit can be redeemed for the resource or a claim on it. An *anchored* unit has its issuance tied by rule to a measured or modeled quantity of the resource, without redemption, as with a token minted only against carbon credits held in reserve. A *themed* unit names the resource or is issued for work on it, while something else stands behind it. A [[glossary/a-e/bioregional-currency|bioregional currency]] as the wiki's frameworks describe it would be themed or anchored unless it offered redemption.
