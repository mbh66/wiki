In transitions research, a protected space where a new technology, practice or organisation can be tried and develop under less pressure than the dominant system applies.

Kemp, Schot and Hoogma (1998) proposed that governments and firms could create niches on purpose, so that users, producers and regulators learn together before an innovation meets full market selection. They called this strategic niche management. Geels (2002) placed the niche at the lowest of three levels in the multi-level perspective: niches sit beneath the [[glossary/m-s/regime|regime]], the settled way a sector works, and both sit within the landscape, the wider context neither controls. A niche innovation breaks through when pressure from the landscape loosens the regime.

Most of the founding work studies technologies and firms. Seyfang and Smith (2007) carried the idea to community groups and co-operatives, which they called grassroots innovations, and argued that such niches run on voluntary work, grants and shared purpose, so findings from firms can't be carried over to them unchanged.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **An ecological niche** is the role and conditions of a species within an ecosystem. The transitions term borrows the image of a sheltered place and drops the biology.
- **A market niche** is a segment of customers a business serves. A transitions niche is defined by the protection it gives, and may have no market at all.
- **A regime** is what a niche is protected from, and what a successful niche may change.
