The process by which people turn an ambiguous, unfolding situation into an account they can put into words and act on.

The term comes from organisation studies, where Karl Weick developed it (1995). Weick, Sutcliffe and Obstfeld (2005) describe sensemaking as turning circumstances into a situation that people understand explicitly in words and that serves as a springboard into action. In their account it looks back over what people have already done and said. It is social, because people make sense together. It runs on plausibility: a group acts on a story good enough to go on with, and that is also how a group can act together on a story that's wrong.

Collective-intelligence tools use the word in a narrower sense. Polis and Talk to the City gather many people's views and show where they agree and differ, so a group can see its own picture of an issue.

On this wiki, place-based sensemaking means a BioHub or community body gathering what its members know about a place (its water, soil, land use and economy) into a shared account it can act on. That use joins the collective-intelligence sense to Weick's stress on acting while things are still unclear.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Decision-making** chooses among options. Sensemaking comes first and decides what the options are.
- **Data analysis** works on measured data. Sensemaking includes stories, local knowledge and argument.
- **Sensegiving** is the effort to shape how others make sense of a situation, for example by a leader or a funder.
