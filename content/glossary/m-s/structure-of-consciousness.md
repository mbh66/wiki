Jean Gebser's term for a basic, shared way in which awareness organises space, time and self, of which he named five: archaic, magic, mythical, mental and integral.

Jean Gebser set out the idea in *The Ever-Present Origin* (German in two parts, 1949 and 1953). Each structure differs in how people experience space and time: in the magic structure space is point-like and time barely exists; in the mythical structure time turns in cycles; in the mental structure space becomes measurable and time a line [IC]. Gebser called the changes from one structure to the next mutations, and declined to read them as progress. He held that every earlier structure stays active in every person, which is the point of his title [IC].

The wiki's five structures (Mimetic, Magical, Mythic, Material and Mycelial) take Gebser's term and Roger Briggs's synthesis; the five names and the sequence are Michael Haupt's [MS]. The [[concepts/structures-of-consciousness|Structures of Consciousness]] concept page carries the full treatment, and the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|grid]] tests the evidence for the idea step by step.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **A worldview or belief system** is a matter of content: what people hold to be true. A structure of consciousness, in Gebser's sense, is the form of awareness in which any content is held.
- **A developmental stage,** as Robert Kegan or Jane Loevinger measure it, belongs to one person's life and is tested with an instrument. Gebser read his structures from art, language and myth across whole cultures.
- **A value system** in Spiral Dynamics names a set of motives and coping strategies. It overlaps with Gebser's structures in later stages and draws on different evidence.
