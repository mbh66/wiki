A for-profit company whose charter names a public benefit that its directors must balance against the financial interests of its shareholders, and on which it must report.

## Extended definition

The wiki uses the term as Delaware company law defines it, because most large public benefit corporations are incorporated there. Delaware added the form to its General Corporation Law in 2013. Section 362 defines a public benefit corporation as "a for-profit corporation ... that is intended to produce a public benefit or public benefits and to operate in a responsible and sustainable manner," and defines a public benefit as "a positive effect (or reduction of negative effects) on 1 or more categories of persons, entities, communities or interests (other than stockholders in their capacities as stockholders)" [IC]. The charter must name the specific benefit the company pursues.

Two duties follow. Section 365(a) requires the board to manage the company "in a manner that balances the pecuniary interests of the stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by the corporation's conduct, and the specific public benefit or public benefits identified in its certificate of incorporation" [IC]. Section 366(b) requires a statement to stockholders on the public benefit "no less than biennially" [IC]. Only stockholders can sue to enforce the balancing duty, and section 367 limits them to holders of at least 2 percent of the shares or, for a listed company, the lesser of 2 percent or shares worth US\$2 million [IC].

The form binds directors to weigh the stated benefit. It does not set how much weight the benefit must get, and the shareholders who could enforce it are the same people who elect the board. Where founders hold a majority of the votes, as at [[research/planet-labs-ownership-and-mission|Planet Labs PBC]], they decide in practice how the benefit is weighed [IC].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- Benefit corporation. A similar legal form in other US states, based on a model statute that Maryland enacted first in 2010. Most benefit corporation statutes require reporting against a third-party standard; Delaware's public benefit corporation statute does not [TBV].
- Certified B Corporation. A private certification granted by the nonprofit B Lab to companies that meet its assessment. It is a label, and a company of any legal form can hold it.
- [[company|Company]]. The general term. A public benefit corporation is one legal form a company can take.
