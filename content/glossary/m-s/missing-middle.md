Benjamin Life's term for the institutional gap between the design principles of bioregional economics (which the discipline can now articulate) and the operating economies specific bioregions are attempting to build.

## Extended definition

Life named the gap at the close of Part I of *An Introduction to Bioregional Economics* (August 2026), as a set of questions: who convenes the cooperatives and capitalizes their shared infrastructure, and what kind of institution can hold a bioregional economy in view without becoming the extraction it was built to end.

Part II, *The Missing Middle of Bioregional Development* (October 2026), fills the gap with two meso-scale institutions. The [[bioregional-development-organization-bdo|Bioregional Development Organization]] plans and capitalizes the bioregional economy as one system. The [[bioregional-learning-centre|bioregional learning center]] is where the bioregion's people grow the capacity to steward it. Life traces the gap to three ceilings in the field: dependence on philanthropy, capital composted into open loops, and the distance between institutional ticket sizes and the scale of projects on the ground.

In this wiki, the [[biohub|BioHub]] is the institution of the missing middle. A BioHub that carries capital functions is a Bioregional Development Organization in Life's sense; see [[concepts/bioregional-economics|Bioregional Economics]] for the full treatment.
