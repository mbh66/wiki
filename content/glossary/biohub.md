A bioregional coordination body that convenes the people, institutions, and economic actors whose activity shapes a [[bioconomy|BioConomy]] in a [[bioregion|BioRegion]].

## Extended definition

The BioHub is the second layer of the [[biostack|BioStack]]: the scale at which place-based coordination makes a BioRegion function as a coherent economic unit. It holds a bioregional economy in view, convenes its [[federated-cooperative|cooperatives]], coordinates landscape-scale commitments, and connects community-level [[commitment-pool|commitment pooling]] to bioregional-scale financing. It does this without becoming a command hierarchy that captures the commons it was built to steward.

The BioHub is also the [[coordination-node|coordination node]] of the [[e-form-emergent|+E form]]: the characteristic physical and institutional structure the Emergent coordination form produces as it matures, the way sacred sites belong to T, cathedrals and capitols to I, and skyscrapers and trading floors to M.

## Contrast with the Municipality

The BioHub occupies the same structural position as the [[municipality|Municipality]]: the body that holds a local economy in view and coordinates its activity. The systems differ on what sets the boundary, what the coordination optimizes for, and where authority comes from.

A municipal boundary is administrative, drawn by legislative act or historical accident. A watershed does not respect it. A food system does not respect it. The municipality coordinates what happens inside an arbitrary perimeter; the BioHub coordinates what happens inside an ecological one.

A municipality succeeds, on its own metrics, when money flows through its territory: rates revenue, employment figures, development applications processed. Its tools are zoning, planning permission, rates collection, infrastructure provision, and the attraction of investment. Each of these is organized around [[throughput-economics|throughput]]. The BioHub succeeds when ecological yield, cooperative density, and community capacity increase. Its tools are commitment pooling, landscape-scale coordination, and the connection of community-level reciprocity to bioregional-scale financing.

A municipality governs by statutory authority, accountable upward to provincial or national government. A BioHub coordinates by voluntary commitment, accountable laterally to the community whose commitments it holds. Its authority is not delegated from the state. It is constituted by the participants through the [[founding-compact|Founding Compact]] and sustained by their continued participation.

## The +E transition

In the [[time|TIME]] framework, the municipality is an [[i-form-institutional|I-form]] body operating inside an [[m-form-market|M-form]] economy. Its coordination instruments (zoning, rates, development incentives) are I-form tools applied to M-form goals. The BioHub is the +E coordination node. Its coordination instruments (compacts, commitment pools, cooperative federation) derive their authority from voluntary participation, not from statutory grant. The [[coordination-node|coordination node]] analysis makes the architectural shift visible: the M-form node (the skyscraper, the trading floor) is built to concentrate and accelerate flow; the +E node (the BioHub) is built to hold a bioregional economy in view and coordinate retention at landscape scale.
