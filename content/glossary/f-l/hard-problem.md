David Chalmers's name for the question of why physical processes in the brain are accompanied by felt experience at all.

David Chalmers drew the line in a 1995 paper and in *The Conscious Mind* (1996). The easy problems ask how the brain discriminates, attends, reports and controls behaviour; they are hard in practice and open to the usual methods of science. The hard problem asks why any of that processing is felt from the inside [IC]. Chalmers argues that no account of function closes the gap, and that consciousness may be a fundamental feature of the world [IC].

Responses divide. Daniel Dennett argues that the problem rests on intuitions that a full account of the brain dissolves; illusionists such as Keith Frankish and Susan Blackmore hold that the felt mystery comes from how brains model themselves [IC]. Panpsychists such as Philip Goff, and idealists such as Bernardo Kastrup and Donald Hoffman, take consciousness as basic [IC]. Anil Seth sets the question aside for what he calls the real problem: explaining the features of experience one by one [IC]. The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|grid]] maps this split as step H, the hinge, and leaves it open.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **The mind-body problem** is the older and wider question of how mind and matter relate. The hard problem is the part of it about felt experience.
- **The easy problems** concern what the brain does. The hard problem concerns why doing it feels like something.
- **The real problem,** in Seth's phrase, sets the hard problem aside and works on the properties of experience.
