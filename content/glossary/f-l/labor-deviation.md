In Weber's location theory, the move of a plant away from its transport-optimal site to a place of cheaper labor, which pays when the labor saving exceeds the added cost of transport.

## Extended definition

The test is geometric. Draw the critical [[glossary/f-l/isodapane|isodapane]], the line on which added transport cost equals the labor saving. A cheap-labor site inside it is worth moving to; a site outside it is not. As the labor saving grows the line widens, and as transport gets dearer it tightens.

The wiki reads offshoring from about 1980 to 2008 as the labor deviation at planetary scale, once the cost of coordinating work across borders is added to Weber's model. Since then the saving from moving to China has narrowed as Chinese wages rose past the [[glossary/f-l/lewis-turning-point|Lewis turning point]], while war-risk premiums and carbon border charges have raised the cost of distance. The evidence shows the deviation moving to the next labor reserve (Vietnam, India, Mexico) more than ending.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Agglomeration deviation**: Weber's parallel move toward a cluster of other firms, for the savings of sharing suppliers, skills and infrastructure.
