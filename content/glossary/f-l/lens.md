The collective interpretive frame a person inherits by belonging to a group, which decides what they notice in the available data and what that data means to them.

## Extended definition

On the [[ladder-of-inference|ladder of inference]], the lens works on the rungs where data is selected and given meaning. A person acquires a lens by belonging: to a coordination form, a community, a profession, a language or a shared history. People who share a lens rarely see it, and they tend to finish each other's sentences.

Everyone carries several lenses and switches between them by setting. A farmer may read a water allocation through a Market lens at the farmers' association and through a Tribal lens at church. The four [[time|TIME]] forms are one family of lenses; faith, class and discipline are others.

## Contrast with self-talk

A lens belongs to a group, so naming one in public costs nobody face, and naming it tends to legitimize it. [[self-talk|Self-talk]] belongs to the individual, and exposing it carries more risk. Lens disagreements concern meaning: the same facts, read coherently in different ways. Self-talk disagreements concern other people's motives.

## Use in governance

Each older TIME form settles lens conflict with a built-in tie-breaker: the elder in the [[t-form-tribal|T form]], the office-holder in the [[i-form-institutional|I form]], price in the [[m-form-market|M form]]. The [[e-form-emergent|E form]] has none, so E-form governance has to make lenses visible. Practical tools include a lens round before debate, in which each party says what it notices in an item and what it means from where they stand, and unscored Q-sorts (see [[research/time-profile|A TIME Profile for Multi-Perspective Governance]]).

The closest empirical research is the cultural cognition work of Kahan and colleagues, which shows worldview shaping which facts people accept. Grid-group cultural theory also names a fatalist position, held by people bound by rules they had no part in making. TIME has no form for it, and a governance process that ignores it will misread those participants.

Lens and self-talk entered the model in a Zoom dialogue between Michael Haupt and Michael Keller (Ecology of Design in Human Systems) on 25 September 2026. This use of the term is Michael Haupt's development of it (working hypothesis).
