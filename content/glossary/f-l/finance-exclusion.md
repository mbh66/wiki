The absence of a community from the governance, payments or eligibility rules of a finance facility that pays for outcomes on its land or in its catchment.

## Extended definition

The wiki gives the term a narrower meaning than the standard "financial exclusion," which refers to people's lack of access to accounts, credit and insurance. Finance exclusion here concerns a particular facility, such as a water fund, a [[performance-based-bond|performance-based bond]] or a [[payment-for-ecosystem-services-pes|payment for ecosystem services]] program, and the people whose land or catchment produces the outcome it pays for. A community is excluded when it holds no seat where the facility's rules are set, cannot meet its eligibility rules, or receives none of its payments.

The mechanisms documented on [[concepts/exclusion-from-water-finance|Exclusion from Water Finance]] include eligibility tied to registered title, payment only for chosen sub-catchments, metrics suited to large or state holdings, boards with no seat for rural land stewards, and bidding or verification costs small holders cannot meet. Whether finance exclusion amounts to [[conservation-violence|conservation violence]] is Michael Haupt's working hypothesis, and the term itself makes no claim about harm.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- Financial exclusion. The standard term: lack of access to financial services. Finance exclusion concerns a community's place in a specific facility.
- [[adverse-inclusion|Adverse inclusion]]. Taking part on harmful terms. A community can be excluded from a facility's governance and adversely included in its labor at the same time.
- [[green-grabbing|Green grabbing]]. Appropriation for environmental ends. Finance exclusion can occur with no change in who holds the land.
