The practice of managing a landscape as one social-ecological system through a long-term, cross-sector partnership of the people and organizations that use and govern it.

## Extended definition

Integrated landscape management (ILM) replaces separate sectoral plans for farming, conservation, water and settlement with one negotiated plan for the whole landscape. The work is carried by a [[landscape-partnership|landscape partnership]] and, in the 1000 Landscapes for 1 Billion People formulation, follows five elements: building the partnership, building shared understanding, visioning and planning, taking action, and learning from impact. The horizon is a generation or more. The wiki uses the term as the field uses it. The full treatment, including how ILM relates to the BioConomy, is on the concept page [[concepts/integrated-landscape-management|Integrated Landscape Management]].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Landscape approach**: the conceptual framework for reconciling competing land uses in one area. ILM is its operational form.
- **Jurisdictional approach**: ILM bounded by an administrative unit, such as a district or state, with government coordinating.
- **Territorial development**: integrated multi-sector development of a territory, entering through the region's control over its own development.
- **Bioregional development**: development organized around the natural and cultural features of a [[bioregion|bioregion]]. Scherr et al. (2026) treat it as closely related to ILM.
- **[[biohub|BioHub]]**: this wiki's bioregional coordination body. It shares ILM's convening function and adds the finance and contracting layer (see the concept page).
