A long-term, cross-sector coalition of the organizations and communities in a landscape, working to a shared vision for its regeneration; the body that carries out [[glossary/f-l/integrated-landscape-management|integrated landscape management]].

## Extended definition

Landscape partnerships (LPs) take many forms. Many begin as informal dialogue platforms where stakeholders share concerns and learn about the landscape; mature partnerships adopt written agreements, defined roles and, in some cases, legal status. The convener may be a local NGO, a university, a land management authority, a local government, a farmer organization or a company, and members can include distant parties with a legitimate interest, such as buyers who source from the landscape. The 1000 Landscapes guide asks each partnership to produce an agreement covering its values, governance, member roles, decision-making, conflict resolution and information-sharing, and to review its own capacity and performance over time.

The field reports two recurring weaknesses. Most partnerships lack long-term funding for coordination and facilitation, and most lack formal legal standing, which limits their access to finance and to public programs (Scherr et al. 2026).

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[biohub|BioHub]]**: both convene the actors whose activity shapes a place. A landscape partnership's defining products are a shared vision, an action plan and an investment portfolio. The BioHub, as this wiki specifies it, also publishes a [[glossary/a-e/coordination-surface|coordination surface]] and tenders verified services to buyers. An existing landscape partnership can serve as the convening core of a BioHub, and a BioHub is a landscape partnership in the field's terms (the wiki's reading).
- **Multi-stakeholder platform**: a general term for any forum of diverse stakeholders. A landscape partnership is one bound to a landscape and a long-term regeneration vision.
