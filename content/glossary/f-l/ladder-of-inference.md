A model of how a person gets from what happened to what they do about it: selecting data, adding meaning, making assumptions, drawing conclusions, adopting beliefs and acting, with beliefs feeding back into which data gets selected next time.

## Extended definition

[[argyris-chris|Chris Argyris]] developed the ladder in his work on organizational learning, and *The Fifth Discipline Fieldbook* (Senge et al. 1994) made it widely known. The rungs run from observable data (what a camera would record) through selected data, added meaning, assumptions, conclusions and beliefs to action. The climb happens fast enough to feel like a single act of perception.

The reflexive loop matters most. A belief that selects its own evidence seals itself against correction. Argyris's remedies work on reasoning: make each rung visible, walk others down their ladders, and test assumptions against directly observable data.

## Use in the BioConomy

The corpus places three layers against the ladder. The substrate decides which data reaches the bottom rung at all; price, for instance, makes priced things visible to everyone who uses the currency. The [[lens]] works on the rungs where data is selected and given meaning. [[self-talk|Self-talk]] works on the upper rungs, where cognitive biases operate. Locating a stalled disagreement in one of these layers indicates its remedy: shared data for the substrate, named and legitimized readings for the lens, time and trust for self-talk.

The [[substrate-hypothesis|Substrate Hypothesis]] predicts that Argyris's remedies fade when the substrate stays unchanged, because the substrate re-selects the data between meetings. Bottom-rung infrastructure changes what enters the ladder for everyone: [[performance-based-bond|performance-based bonds]] that pay on measured outcomes, or a BioHub [[coordination-surface|coordination surface]] that publishes offers and seeks in a common format.

The ladder is Argyris's. Lens and self-talk entered the model in a Zoom dialogue between Michael Haupt and [[keller-michael|Michael Keller]] (Ecology of Design in Human Systems) on 25 September 2026. The three-layer overlay as developed here is Haupt's (working hypothesis).
