The point in a developing economy at which surplus rural labor has been absorbed into modern industry and wages begin to rise, named for W. Arthur Lewis's 1954 model.

## Extended definition

Lewis described a dual economy in which a modern sector draws workers from a traditional rural sector at a near-constant wage, because the countryside holds more labor than it needs. Once that surplus is gone, employers must bid for workers and wages climb.

The concept tells a practitioner whether the [[glossary/f-l/labor-deviation|labor deviation]] still pays in a given country. Survey evidence shows Chinese real wages rising since 2003, even in slack seasons, and Chinese economists conclude that the country passed its turning point in the 2000s. The labor saving that drew assembly to China therefore began to narrow fifteen years before the tariffs of 2018, which the wiki reads as the clearest structural driver behind the shift of production since.
