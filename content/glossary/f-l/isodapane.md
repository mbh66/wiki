A line on a map joining points at which the added transport cost of locating a plant, above the minimum at the transport-optimal site, is equal.

## Extended definition

Weber drew isodapanes as rings around the transport optimum, each marking a higher level of added cost. The critical isodapane is the ring on which the added transport cost equals the saving available at another site, from cheaper labor or from a cluster. A site inside it repays the move.

The idea gives a practitioner a way to picture the present. When war-risk insurance, delay and carbon charges raise the cost of distance, the critical isodapane around each market shrinks, and fewer distant sites fall inside it. That is the mechanism by which rising transport risk pulls heavy production toward markets in the wiki's [[research/glocalization-and-the-evolution-of-production|production-location brief]].
