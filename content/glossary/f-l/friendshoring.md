The move of production and sourcing to countries a government regards as politically aligned, whatever their distance.

## Extended definition

The term entered policy use around 2022, when US officials described a preference for supply chains running through allied countries. It is the mechanism of fragmentation into blocs. McKinsey Global Institute measures it as a fall in the geopolitical distance of trade, about 7 percent between 2017 and 2024, while the geographic distance of trade kept rising. Kearney's 2026 survey found most US firms moving sourcing from China to other Asian low-cost countries.

The IMF estimates the long-run cost of trade fragmentation at 0.2 to 7 percent of world GDP. The wiki's [[research/glocalization-and-the-evolution-of-production|production-location brief]] reads friendshoring as the political driver of present regionalization, distinct from the structural pressures the S-curve reading predicts.
