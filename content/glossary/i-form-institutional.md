Coordination through hierarchy, codified rules, and formalized authority, where legitimacy determines who governs and how resources are allocated.

## Extended definition

The I form is the second coordination layer in the [[time|TIME]] framework. It emerges when a society grows beyond the scale at which reciprocal obligation between known individuals can hold. Its operating logic is codified authority: written law, administrative hierarchy, delegated jurisdiction, and the monopoly on legitimate force. Coordination extends to strangers because the rules apply regardless of kinship.

The I form produced the institutions that still structure most of the world's governance: the state, the church, the bureaucracy, the military, the university, the judiciary. Each coordinates through formal authority delegated downward through a hierarchy and legitimated upward through constitutional, religious, or customary mandate. The I form's contribution is scale and durability: it can coordinate millions of people who will never meet, across centuries, through rules that outlast any individual.

Its limitation is rigidity. I-form institutions optimise for their own continuation. They resist adaptation because adaptation threatens the authority structure. They centralise because hierarchy centralises. They become capture-prone because the authority they concentrate becomes a prize worth capturing.

## Coordination node

The I-form [[coordination-node|coordination node]] is the cathedral, the capitol building, the court: monumental architecture that makes authority visible in stone. The node declares permanence. Its scale and material assert that the institution will outlast any individual. The shift from sacred site (T) to monumental building (I) is a shift from enacted identity to encoded authority.

## Position in the BioStack

The I form maps structurally to the [[municipality|Municipality]] and the [[nation-state|Nation-state]] in the conventional [[economy|Economy]]. Both are I-form bodies operating inside an [[m-form-market|M-form]] economy. The municipality governs by statutory authority within an administrative boundary. The nation-state administers the monetary and legal architecture within a sovereign one. Both coordinate through the I-form instruments of legislation, regulation, zoning, fiscal policy, and the delegation of authority through administrative hierarchy.

In the [[bioconomy|BioConomy]], the I form does not disappear. The [[e-form-emergent|+E]] transition narrows the I form to the work it does well (rule of law, defence, treaty, property registration) while relocating economic coordination to the +E forms ([[biohub|BioHub]], [[bioregion|BioRegion]], federated cooperative). The I form provides the legal ground on which +E coordination stands; the +E form provides the economic coordination the I form cannot do without defaulting to throughput logic.

## Contrast with adjacent forms

The [[t-form-tribal|T form]] coordinates through identity; the I form coordinates through codified authority. The transition from T to T+I extends coordination beyond the boundary of personal relationship.

The I form coordinates through hierarchy; the [[m-form-market|M form]] coordinates through price. The transition from T+I to T+I+M adds a coordination mechanism that does not require either kinship or formal authority: two strangers can exchange through price signals without belonging to the same group or answering to the same hierarchy. The M form's contribution is the decentralisation of allocation decisions. Its limitation is that price signals register only what the market has priced, leaving ecological function, care, and commons outside the frame.
