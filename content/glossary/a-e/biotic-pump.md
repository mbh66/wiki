A theory that forests draw moist air inland from the ocean, because water vapor condensing above the canopy lowers air pressure there. Proposed by Anastassia Makarieva and Victor Gorshkov in 2007.

## Extended definition

Forests transpire large volumes of water vapor. On the biotic pump theory, the condensation of that vapor above the canopy reduces air pressure over the forest, and the pressure difference pulls moist air in from the ocean. The theory offers an explanation for why rainfall can stay high thousands of kilometers inland over continuous forest, and why it falls away where forest cover is broken.

Makarieva and Gorshkov set out the theory in 2007. Other atmospheric scientists dispute how large the effect is, and the theory remains under debate (Sheil and Murdiyarso 2009).

On this wiki the theory connects to the landscape water-cycle work of [[people/practitioners/kravcik-michal|Michal Kravcik]], and to rainfall enhancement through [[research/bioprecipitation|Bioprecipitation]].
