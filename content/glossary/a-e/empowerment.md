In transitions research, the work that prepares a niche innovation to survive outside protection, by fitting the existing rules or by changing them.

Empowerment is the third property of [[glossary/m-s/protective-space|protective space]] in Smith and Raven (2012), and the one they call least developed. They name two routes. *Fit-and-conform* makes the innovation competitive in a [[glossary/m-s/regime|regime]] that stays as it is. *Stretch-and-transform* changes the regime's rules in the innovation's favour. They warn that fit-and-conform can cost an innovation its capacity to break the existing path. Raven and others (2016) read empowerment as socio-political work by advocates, which in six low-carbon cases depended on tying the innovation to the agendas of powerful actors.

For grassroots movements, empowerment on the regime's terms can mean losing their purpose (Smith and others 2017). No study found measures which route spreads faster or changes more. The firm-level evidence on leaving protection is sobering: graduates of German incubators showed a fall in survival lasting up to three years (Schwartz 2009).

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Empowerment in everyday and development use** means people gaining power over their own lives. The transitions term refers to an innovation's readiness to face the regime; the two can pull apart, as when an initiative fits the market by giving up control.
- **Exit** is the end of protection. Empowerment is the preparation for it, and can be missing when exit comes.
