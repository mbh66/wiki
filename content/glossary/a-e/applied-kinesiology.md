A muscle-testing method, begun in 1964 by the chiropractor George J. Goodheart, that reads changes in muscle strength as signs of a person's health or of whether a statement is true [IC].

A practitioner presses down on a person's outstretched arm while the person holds a substance, or has a statement in mind. An arm that holds firm is read as a yes. An arm that gives way is read as a no. The method is used in some chiropractic and complementary health practice.

David R. Hawkins built his Map of Consciousness on it. In *Power vs. Force* (1995) he used muscle testing to calibrate levels of consciousness on a scale from 1 to 1000, and placed nations and groups on that scale. The wiki names the method so readers can see what the scale rests on. It doesn't use the scale, and the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/library|evolution of consciousness library]] repeats none of its placements.

Two double-blind tests found the method no more accurate than chance. Kenney and colleagues (1988) tested it as a way to assess nutrient status. Schwartz and colleagues (2014) tested it as a diagnostic tool [IC].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Kinesiology** is the academic study of human movement, taught in university departments of exercise and sport science. It has no link to truth-testing.
- **Manual muscle testing** in physiotherapy grades a muscle's strength to assess an injury or a nerve. It measures the muscle, and makes no claim about the person's beliefs or surroundings.
