Michael Haupt's seven-phase model of the first globalized civilization, from 1648 to a transition now under way, in which the last phase becomes a fork because a global civilization has no outside successor.

## Extended definition

The model applies the [[glossary/a-e/cycle-of-civilizations|Cycle of Civilizations]] to the global system: Foundation (1648 to 1776), Gestation and early growth (1776 to 1815), Expansion (1815 to 1914), Conflict and warnings of overshoot (1914 to 1971), Universal empire and hubris (1971 to 2008), Decay and crisis (2008 onward) and a Transition that overlaps the sixth phase. The Dutch, British and American hegemonic cycles run inside it. Its inference is that a new hegemon could inherit the system's institutions without inheriting a fresh frontier, so the last phase forks between succession on a shrinking base, global simplification and a change of substrate toward [[glossary/f-l/glocalization|glocalization]].

The full model, with its evidence and caveats, is at [[frameworks/cycle-of-globalization|Cycle of Globalization]].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[glossary/a-e/cycle-of-civilizations|Cycle of Civilizations]]**: the general pattern and its lineage. The Cycle of Globalization is its application to one civilization that covers the planet.
- **Hegemonic cycles** (Arrighi, Modelski, Wallerstein): successive leading centers within the world-system. In this model they are the inner rhythm of one longer cycle.
- **[[glossary/f-l/globalization|Globalization]]**: the growth phases of the cycle, read on this wiki as the juvenile phase of the human species.
