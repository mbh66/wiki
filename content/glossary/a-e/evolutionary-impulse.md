The Evolutionary Impulse is a name, used in evolutionary spirituality, for a creative drive that some hold moves the universe toward greater complexity and awareness. Step T4 of the [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/anabranch/index|Anabranch thesis]] gives it as one of three names for the force behind a striving it attributes to the universe. The other two are building complexity and God [MS].

## Extended definition

The idea of a drive within evolution is older than the term. Henri Bergson's *Creative Evolution* (1907) proposed that evolution is moved by an *élan vital*, a vital impetus, and set it against Darwin's mechanism of natural selection [IC]. Pierre Teilhard de Chardin argued that evolution raises complexity and consciousness together, toward an Omega Point [TBV].

The term itself is most closely tied to the spiritual teacher Andrew Cohen. In *Evolutionary Enlightenment* (2011), Cohen names the evolutionary impulse Eros and holds that it is the same as the spiritual impulse; he presents individual awakening as taking part in it [IC]. Barbara Marx Hubbard's conscious evolution and Steve McIntosh's *Evolution's Purpose* (2012) belong to the same broad current [TBV].

The thesis uses the term as these writers do, as one name among several. It doesn't define the impulse further.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Rising complexity.** That complexity has risen over cosmic and biological history is measured. Eric Chaisson tracks energy rate density rising from galaxies and stars to plants, animals and societies [TBV]. The Evolutionary Impulse is a reading of why it rises, and the measurement doesn't settle that.
- **Teleology in philosophy.** Thomas Nagel proposes that natural teleological principles, with no designer, may make the rise of life and mind likely [IC]. Elliott Sober rejects the argument [IC]. Stephen Jay Gould argues that apparent progress is variation spreading away from a wall of minimal complexity, with no drive behind it [TBV].
- **Critics of evolutionary spirituality.** Frank Visser's review of *Evolutionary Enlightenment*, "Evolutionary Endarkenment", disputes Cohen's reading of evolution [IC].

## Provenance

Written on 20 November 2026, after Anabranch Run 3b named the Evolutionary Impulse as a term the thesis uses without a glossary entry. The research run found no source in the library that uses the term itself. The lineage was checked for this entry: Bergson through standard reference accounts of *Creative Evolution*, and Cohen through his own published teaching and Visser's review. The contrasts follow the wiki's source pages; their grades are carried over. Roger Briggs's "supra-evolution", one pervasive tendency of the universe (*Emerging World*, ch. 5, p. 159), is close in meaning, but he doesn't use the term, so this entry doesn't cite him for it.
