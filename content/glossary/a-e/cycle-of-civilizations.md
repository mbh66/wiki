The recurring pattern of formation, growth, maturity and decline that cyclical theories of history attribute to complex societies, used on this wiki in Michael Haupt's seven-phase synthesis as a diagnostic lens.

## Extended definition

Cyclical theories run from Polybius and Ibn Khaldun through Spengler, Toynbee, Quigley and Glubb to Turchin's quantitative work. They disagree on the number of phases, from four to seven. Michael's synthesis uses seven: Stability, Early growth, Prosperity, Overshoot, Hubris, Totalitarianism, and the Collapse of the old together with the Emergence of the new. Quantitative studies support one part of the cyclical view, that states grow more vulnerable over their first two centuries, and find no fixed lifespan. The wiki reads the phases as conditions and draws no dates from them.

The full treatment is at [[frameworks/cycle-of-civilizations|Cycle of Civilizations]].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[glossary/m-s/s-curve|S-curve]]**: the shape of growth in a bounded system. The cycle describes what a society goes through along that shape.
- **[[glossary/a-e/cycle-of-globalization|Cycle of Globalization]]**: the application of the cycle to the first civilization that covers the planet.
- **Collapse** (Tainter): a theory of why complex societies simplify, with no fixed sequence of phases.
