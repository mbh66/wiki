A carrying budget states how much of each ecological ceiling a bioregion's economy may draw on, and what social floor it must meet, as thresholds for the whole bioregion and allocations within it.

## Extended definition

[[life-benjamin|Benjamin Life]] calls determining a bioregion's needs, capacities and carrying budget "the founding act" of a bioregional economy (Part I, 2026). He sets the limits as particular quantities: aquifer recharge, watershed capacity, soil budgets. In Part II he draws the method from the thresholds-and-allocations work of [[baue-bill|Bill Baue]] and colleagues at r3.0, which makes [[raworth-kate|Kate Raworth]]'s doughnut computable for a territory. The ceiling comes from the ecological side of the doughnut, drawn down from the [[planetary-boundaries|planetary boundaries]] to the bioregion. The floor comes from its social side.

The carrying budget measures stocks against thresholds. It is the first output of the [[templates/metabolic-study-template|Metabolic Study Template]], which builds it from the BioRegion Atlas and then measures flows inside it. Because regeneration raises what a landscape can carry, the budget is redrawn on the plan's revision cycle.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

**Metabolic study.** A [[metabolic-study|metabolic study]] measures flows: how value and materials move through the bioregion. The carrying budget sets the boundary the flows must stay within.

**BioRegion Atlas.** The Atlas compiles baselines and profiles. The carrying budget turns them into limits and allocations.
