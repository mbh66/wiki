The principle that data are subject to the governance of the nation, people or community they come from or describe, including who may collect, hold, use and publish them.

## Extended definition

The term has two traditions. In law and technology policy it usually means that data are subject to the laws of the country where they are collected or stored, a question that grew with cloud computing and cross-border data flows. In Indigenous scholarship it means the right of Indigenous peoples to govern the collection, ownership and use of data about their peoples, lands and resources. The edited volume *Indigenous Data Sovereignty: Toward an Agenda* (Kukutai and Taylor 2016) set out that second sense [IC].

The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, drafted in Gaborone, Botswana in November 2018 and published by the Global Indigenous Data Alliance, give the Indigenous sense a working form: Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility and Ethics [IC]. The principles were written to complement the FAIR principles for open data (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable), which say how data should be shared and leave aside who decides [IC].

The wiki uses the term in the field's sense and applies it to information about a place: satellite-derived reports, ecological surveys and maps of a BioRegion and the people who live in it. The question it asks of a tool or a data supplier is who holds that information, who may use it, and whether the community it describes may publish it. The terms of the [[tools/bioregion/planet-ai-app|Planet AI App]] are a worked case: a user keeps rights in their own questions, and Planet owns the reports the app writes about a place [IC].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- Data protection and privacy. Rules on personal data about identifiable individuals. Data sovereignty concerns collective governance by a nation, people or community, and covers data about land and resources as well as about persons.
- Open data. Data published for anyone to reuse. Open data and data sovereignty can conflict when a community has not consented to release.
- [[commons|Commons]]. A resource governed by a community under rules it makes. Data sovereignty is one claim a community can make to govern information as a commons.
