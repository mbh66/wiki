In Carlota Perez's model of technological revolutions, the second half of a great surge, after the turning point, when production capital and new institutions spread the technology across the economy.

Perez counts five great surges since the 1770s, each set off by a technological revolution and lasting about half a century. Each has an installation period, in which financial capital funds the new infrastructure and the boom ends in a speculative frenzy. A turning point follows, usually a crash and recession. In the deployment period that comes after, production capital leads, institutions are rebuilt around the new technology, and its benefits spread. Perez calls the best of these deployment periods golden ages; the postwar boom was the deployment of mass production. She separates her surges from Kondratiev's long waves, which describe swings in aggregate growth (Perez 2009).

The framework is a reading of history. It says that a turning point opens the chance of a golden age, and that the outcome depends on what institutions do. The [[research/dark-ai-and-the-place-based-inheritance|Dark AI brief]] found that the postwar deployment ran largely through national welfare states and large firms, and that local exceptions such as rural electric co-operatives were created by statute.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Installation period** is the first half of a surge, led by finance. The deployment period follows the crash that ends it.
- **[[glossary/a-e/deceleration-phase|Deceleration phase]]** is the wiki's term for a phase of the industrial civilisation curve. A deployment period belongs to one technological surge; the two operate at different scales.
- **Golden age** names a deployment period that delivered broad prosperity. Perez treats it as possible, and conditional on institutions.
