Jean Gebser's word for the awareness of his integral structure, which holds many viewpoints together and is fixed to none of them.

Gebser named three relations to perspective. The early structures were unperspectival. The mental structure became perspectival: it fixed the world from a single standpoint, as linear perspective in Renaissance painting fixes a scene from one eye [IC]. The integral structure he saw emerging in the twentieth century is aperspectival: it can hold the standpoints together and move among them, and it treats time as a lived whole [IC]. He found signs of it in modern art, physics and literature, and argued that it integrates the earlier structures [IC].

The wiki reads Gebser's integral structure as [[glossary/m-s/mycelial-consciousness|Mycelial Consciousness]] [MS]. The [[research/evolution-of-consciousness/grid|grid]] codes Gebser's claim that the integral structure is emerging under step C2, where it rests on theory and testimony.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **Relativism** holds that no viewpoint is better than another. Gebser's aperspectival awareness integrates viewpoints and makes no claim about their equal worth.
- **Pluralism,** or tolerance of many views, is an attitude a person can adopt. Gebser described a change in the structure of awareness itself.
- **Perspectival** awareness, the mental structure's mode, is in Gebser's account still present in everyone; the aperspectival does not replace it.
