The property of an item, technology or dataset that can serve both civilian and military purposes, so that its effect depends on who uses it and for what.

## Extended definition

The term comes from export control law. The European Union's dual-use regulation defines dual-use items as "items, including software and technology, which can be used for both civil and military purposes" [IC]. Governments that control such items keep lists of them and require licenses to export them; the Wassenaar Arrangement, in force since 1996, coordinates those lists among its participating states [IC].

Satellite imagery is a standard case. The same image of a coastline or a forest serves a fisheries monitor, a deforestation enforcement team and a navy. Commercial imagery companies operate under national licenses, and governments can restrict what is collected or sold. The record on [[research/planet-labs-ownership-and-mission|Planet Labs]] shows both sides of the property in one company: its charter names a "sustainable, secure, and prosperous world" as its purpose, and in 2026 it withheld Middle East imagery from most users at a US government request [IC].

The wiki uses the term to ask a practical question of any data supplier a practitioner relies on: who else buys the same data, and who decides access when civilian and military demands conflict. Arguments differ on where responsibility sits. One position holds that use is the customer's choice; another holds that a supplier which sells dedicated capacity to armed forces, or decides who keeps access in a conflict, shares responsibility for the use. The wiki reports both and takes neither as settled.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- Dual-use research of concern. A narrower term in the life sciences for research whose results could be misused to cause harm. It concerns knowledge produced in a laboratory. Dual use in export control concerns goods, software and data in trade.
- [[data-sovereignty|Data sovereignty]]. Asks who governs data about a people or place. Dual use asks what the data can be used for, by anyone who holds it.
