Karl Jaspers's name for the centuries from about 800 to 200 BCE, when he held that reflective thought about the human condition arose in several regions at once.

Jaspers set out the idea in *The Origin and Goal of History* (1949). He pointed to the same few centuries in China, India, Persia, Israel and Greece, the time of Confucius and Laozi, the Upanishads and the Buddha, Zarathustra, the Hebrew prophets and the Greek philosophers. In each, he argued, people began to question their own traditions and to think about thinking. He saw no single cause and no contact strong enough to explain it.

Robert Bellah's *Religion in Human Evolution* (2011) carried the idea into the study of cultural evolution. Following Merlin Donald, Bellah reads the period as the arrival of theoretic culture, which joined the mimetic and mythic cultures before it without replacing them.

Critics ask whether the period was one event or several, whether its dates hold for each region (the dating of Zarathustra is much disputed), and whether the idea favours traditions that left written texts [TBV].

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **The Neolithic Revolution,** about 12,000 years ago, is the shift to farming and settlement. It is far older than the Axial Age, and concerns how people lived more than how they reasoned.
- **A new axial moment** is a phrase some present-day writers use for a hoped-for shift now. Jonathan Rowson raises it in the film *Living in the Metacrisis* (2023). It borrows Jaspers's term for a claim Jaspers didn't make.

Sources date the beginning of the structure the network calls [[material-consciousness|Material Consciousness]] to the Neolithic, the Axial Age, the Renaissance or the scientific revolution. The second research run on the evolution of consciousness will set these dates side by side.
