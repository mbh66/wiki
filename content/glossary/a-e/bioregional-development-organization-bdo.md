A Bioregional Development Organization (BDO) is the institution [[life-benjamin|Benjamin Life]] proposes to see, plan and capitalize a bioregional economy as a whole system, federating the financing facilities and cooperative enterprises beneath it.

> [!note] Right of reply
> This entry describes Benjamin Life's framework through his published essay and relates it to this wiki's BioHub. Life and the BioFi Project have not yet been invited to comment. Responses will be recorded here.

## Extended definition

Life introduces the BDO in *The Missing Middle of Bioregional Development* (25 October 2026), the second part of his introduction to [[glossary/a-e/bioregional-economics|bioregional economics]]. He derives it from the Regional Development Organizations of the United States: multi-jurisdictional bodies, nearly four hundred of them federally designated as Economic Development Districts, whose Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy brings public and private actors around one regional plan. Life keeps the convening function, the strategy instrument and the public-private architecture. He changes the boundaries from counties to bioregions, adds the capacity to deploy capital, and inverts the ownership logic so that value returns to the community.

The BDO sits one level above the four entities of the [[bioregional-financing-facility-bff|Bioregional Financing Facility]]. In Life's image, the facilities are organs and the BDO is the body. It holds a [[bioregional-development-plan|bioregional development plan]] built from carrying-capacity analysis and a [[metabolic-study|metabolic study]], together with a social map drawn with frontline communities and Indigenous nations, and a governance map seated before significant money arrives. Life's rule is that the organizing precedes the money.

The BDO operates as an evergreen fund of funds. It capitalizes the facilities that select projects, and underwrites directly only the shared infrastructure no single facility can carry, such as a processing plant, a land acquisition or an energy commons. Its capital stack places catalytic philanthropy in first-loss position, patient equity above it, and, as the portfolio matures, community investment notes through which residents hold a stake in their own bioregion. Aggregation is what makes it legible to institutional capital: it turns many small projects, each too small for an allocator to underwrite, into one investable thesis at institutional ticket size.

Governance runs through four membership classes. The sensing layer (organizing teams, community trusts, grassroots networks) holds structural authority over the plan. The productive layer holds the cooperative and regenerative enterprises. The capital layer holds family offices, foundations, aligned funds and community investors. The public layer represents the [[commons|commons]] and the more-than-human. Life treats the protection against capture as the institution's essence: the sensing layer's structural majority, steward-ownership provisions that make the BDO unsellable, exit clauses that let member facilities leave with their assets, and open books across the consortium.

## The BDO and the BioHub

The [[biohub|BioHub]] in this wiki is a similar concept, developed separately. Both are meso-scale bodies that hold a bioregional economy in view, both take their authority from participants, and both invite capital in on the place's own stated terms. A BioHub that takes on Life's capital functions is a BDO in his sense. The BioHub keeps its name and its place in the [[biostack|BioStack]].

Life's BDO serves a whole bioregion, while a [[bioregion|BioRegion]] in this wiki may hold several BioHubs. Each BioRegion decides which arrangement suits it:

- **A federation of BDOs.** Each BioHub carries the BDO functions for its own part of the BioRegion, such as one reach of a river, and the BioHubs federate across the BioRegion through shared infrastructure and reconciled Terms of Engagement.
- **One capital-holding BioHub.** A single BioHub carries the capital functions for the whole BioRegion, and the other BioHubs take seats in its sensing and productive layers.

The BioRegion Charter records the choice. The identification of the BioHub with the BDO, and the two arrangements, are this wiki's construction on Life's framework. Life's essay does not discuss BioHubs.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

**Bioregional Financing Facility.** A BFF is one of four financing entities (trust, venture studio, investment company, bank). Life places the BDO one order of scale above the four. It coordinates them as one system and phases them in sequence, trust first.

**Bioregional Learning Centre.** Life pairs the BDO with the [[bioregional-learning-centre|bioregional learning center]]. The BDO is the bioregion's circulatory organ, where capital is directed; the learning center is its metabolic and cultural organ, where capacity is grown. "A BLC without a BDO remains beautifully undercapitalized. A BDO without a BLC deploys capital into a culture that cannot yet receive it."

**Regional Development Organization.** The legacy form Life transmutes. He describes it as having "the right shape and the wrong soul": it can plan, rarely deploys, draws its regions from counties, and works inside the extractive frame.
