Michel Bauwens's formulation: share knowledge globally, adapt it locally, produce regeneratively in place, share the learning globally.

## Extended definition

Vasilis Kostakis, Vasilis Niaros, George Dafermos and Michel Bauwens named the model "design global, manufacture local" (DGML) in 2015 and described it as "processes through which design is developed, shared and improved as a global digital commons, whereas the actual manufacturing takes place locally through shared infrastructures." Fab labs are one of its shared infrastructures: the Fab Foundation counts more than 1,750 in more than 100 countries [MS, the network's own count]. The wiki's full treatment is the concept page [[concepts/cosmo-local-production|Cosmo-Local Production]].

In the wiki's production sequence, cosmo-local production is one of the two production forms of the [[e-form-emergent|Emergent form]], beside [[glossary/m-s/peer-production|peer production]]. The research brief [[research/evolution-of-production|The Evolution of Production]] finds that its proponents have published life cycle studies favoring local manufacture, which await independent replication [TBV], and that Kostakis and colleagues describe DGML as a proto-model that cannot yet sustain itself independently of capitalism. Whether the Emergent form comes to govern place-based production on this model is graded a speculative projection.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **[[glossary/m-s/peer-production|Peer production]]** names the mode in which contributors build a shared resource, chiefly information. Cosmo-local production carries that commons into physical making, with the design shared globally and the fabrication done in place.
