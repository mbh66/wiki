Coordination through kinship, identity, belonging, and reciprocal obligation, where access to resources follows from membership in a group.

## Extended definition

The T form is the oldest and deepest layer of human coordination. It predates writing, codified law, and monetary exchange. Its operating logic is reciprocal obligation within a bounded community: I share because you are kin, because we belong to the same group, because the group's survival depends on mutual provision. The boundary of coordination is the boundary of identity. Those inside the boundary receive; those outside do not.

The T form never disappears. Every society that adds subsequent coordination forms (I, M, E) retains the tribal layer. Nationalism is T-form coordination at state scale: identity and belonging underwriting the legitimacy of institutional authority. Brand loyalty is T-form coordination inside a market: identity and belonging channelling consumer behaviour. The persistence of the T form is one of the structural features the [[time|TIME]] framework tracks: each new form adds to rather than replaces what came before.

## Coordination node

The T-form [[coordination-node|coordination node]] is the sacred site: the place where identity is enacted, belonging is renewed, and the group's relationship to its landscape is ritually maintained. Stone circles, groves, hilltop shrines, ancestral burial grounds. The node holds the group together by holding its story in a specific place.

## Position in the BioStack

The T form has no direct structural equivalent in the [[biostack|BioStack]] the way [[m-form-market|M]] maps to the [[economy|Economy]] and [[e-form-emergent|E]] maps to the [[bioconomy|BioConomy]]. It sits beneath and within all four BioStack layers. The reciprocal obligation, identity, and belonging that characterise T-form coordination are the social substrate on which [[commitment-pool|commitment pooling]], cooperative membership, and compact-based governance depend. A [[bioplace|BioPlace]] without community is a parcel of land. A [[biohub|BioHub]] without shared identity is a coordination mechanism with nothing to coordinate. The T form provides the relational ground the +E forms build on.

## Contrast with adjacent forms

The T form coordinates through identity; the [[i-form-institutional|I form]] coordinates through codified authority. The transition from T to T+I is the transition from "we do this because we are kin" to "we do this because the law requires it." The I form does not replace kinship. It layers formal authority over it, extending coordination beyond the boundary of personal relationship. The T form's limitation is scale: reciprocal obligation works where everyone knows everyone. The I form's contribution is extending coordination to strangers through codified rules.
