A bounded, practice-based trial of a new way of meeting a need, run to feed learning into a wider change of system.

In transition management, experiments are one step in a cycle that Loorbach (2010) sets out: a small arena of front-runners agrees a long-term agenda, runs experiments that try parts of it in practice, and evaluates them reflexively so that what's learned changes the agenda. An experiment is judged by what it teaches about the system it's meant to change.

Sengers, Wieczorek and Raven (2019) reviewed the field's uses of the word and separated five kinds: niche experiments, bounded socio-technical experiments, transition experiments, sustainability experiments and grassroots experiments. Each comes with its own theory. Their review maps concepts and pools no outcome data, so how often experiments lead to wider change isn't yet known from comparative studies.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **A pilot programme** tests a policy or service before deciding whether to adopt it. A transition experiment aims at learning about the system, and may be designed to show that the system needs changing.
- **A living lab** is a user-centred testing arrangement in a real setting, often run by a university or firm.
- **[[glossary/t-z/transformative-space|A transformative space]]** is a convened setting for trying new social-ecological arrangements. Experiments can run inside one.
- **Business incubation** houses and advises young firms for a period, aiming at their survival and growth.
