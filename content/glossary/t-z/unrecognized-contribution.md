Work that maintains or restores the health of a place but generates no price signal in the +[[m-form-market|M form]] and therefore has no economic standing within +M [[coordination-grammar|grammar]]. The +[[e-form-emergent|E form]]'s term for what the +M form calls [[unemployment|unemployment]].

## Extended definition

The +M form's coordination grammar recognizes economic contribution through a single pathway: wage labor producing a price signal. Activity that does not enter the monetary circuit does not register as contribution. Subsistence farming, watershed stewardship, invasive-species management by community volunteers, care for children and elderly, cultural maintenance, ecological monitoring, firebreak maintenance, soil restoration, seed saving, and local food production all fall outside the frame. The people doing this work are, within +M grammar, unemployed. Their activity is invisible.

The term "unrecognized contribution" reframes the same activity from within +E grammar. The work is real. The ecological and social outcomes are measurable. What is absent is a coordination substrate that makes the work legible to the wider economy and compensates it in a unit the contributor can use. The work is not unproductive. It is unrecognized.

The reframing carries a design implication. If the problem is unemployment, the solution is job creation: move the person into the +M circuit. If the problem is unrecognized contribution, the solution is substrate construction: build a [[concepts/coordination-surface|coordination surface]] that makes the existing work visible and compensates it through a bioregional unit of account. The two solutions are complementary (see [[concepts/obligation-and-invitation|Obligation and Invitation]]), but they begin from different premises and produce different institutional outcomes.

## What counts as unrecognized contribution

The category is defined by two criteria. 
1. The activity must produce a measurable outcome that sustains or restores the health of the bioregion. 
2. The activity must currently generate no price signal, no wage, and no formal economic standing for the person performing it.

Examples from the Valley of Grace BioHub context:

- Clearing invasive alien vegetation from the upper catchment of the Sonderend River. The work restores water yield (approximately 38 million cubic meters per year lost to invasive plants in the Western Cape Water Supply System). It is performed by community members who receive no wage for it outside of periodic Working for Water program contracts. Between contracts, the work is an unrecognized contribution.
- Monitoring water quality at community water points. The data is valuable to municipal water management and to the verification agents of [[concepts/performance-based-water-bonds|performance-based water bonds]]. The monitoring is performed by residents who live near the water points and check them as part of their daily routine. No wage attaches to the activity.
- Growing food in community gardens and distributing it through soup kitchens and informal networks. The food sustains households that cannot afford market-priced groceries. The labor is substantial, skilled, and continuous. It registers nowhere in the local economy's formal accounts.
- Maintaining firebreaks on community and private land. The fire-risk reduction benefits every property in the valley. The maintenance is performed by residents who receive no compensation for it.

Each of these activities produces an outcome the +M form would pay for if it could see it. The [[concepts/performance-based-water-bonds|Cape Water Performance-Based Bond]] demonstrates the unit economics: nature-based solutions in the Cape's mountain catchments deliver water at roughly one-tenth the unit cost of desalination. The work is already being done. The coordination substrate that converts it into standing and compensation is what the +E form builds.

## The shift from needy to needed

The reframing connects directly to the [[needed-vs-needy|needed versus needy]] distinction. A person whose contribution is unrecognized is positioned as [[needy|needy]] within +M grammar: they need a job, they need welfare, they need training to become employable. The same person, once their contribution is recognized through a bioregional coordination surface, is positioned as [[needed|needed]]: the watershed needs their stewardship, the food system needs their labor, the fire-risk management regime needs their maintenance. The shift is not motivational or psychological. It is structural. What changes is the substrate's capacity to see the contribution and compensate it.

## Unpaid care work and the Emergent form's own costs

The largest body of unrecognized contribution is unpaid care work: domestic and care services provided within and between households without pay. The ILO, from time-use data in 64 countries covering two-thirds of the world's working-age population, counts 16.4 billion hours of it every day, the equivalent of 2.0 billion people working eight hours a day without pay, 76.2 percent of it done by women. Valued at an hourly minimum wage it comes to 9 percent of global GDP (ILO, *Care Work and Care Jobs for the Future of Decent Work*, 2018) [IC]. In the wiki's production sequence this is the cost the Tribal form's household production has always carried, and every later form has drawn on it.

The term applies inside the +E form too. [[glossary/m-s/peer-production|Peer production]] runs on unpaid maintenance: in Tidelift's 2024 survey of more than 400 open source maintainers, 60 percent were unpaid and 60 percent had quit or considered quitting [MS, Tidelift sells maintainer funding]. A BioHub that relies on volunteer stewardship without recognizing it pushes the same cost outward that it names in the +M form. [[research/evolution-of-production|The Evolution of Production]] records this, and [[frameworks/sequence-and-the-debt|The Sequence and the Debt]] lists unpaid contribution among the costs the BioHub must name and organize. When a producing form relies on unrecognized contribution without paying for it, the wiki treats the unpaid work as one of that form's [[glossary/m-s/negative-externalities|negative externalities]] in TIME's broader sense.
