An area governed by an Indigenous people or local community that has a close relationship with it, in which that governance sustains nature.

## Extended definition

The term comes from the ICCA Consortium, which uses it for Indigenous peoples' and community conserved territories and areas; UNEP-WCMC keeps a registry of them. Three features define one: a close and deep connection between the people and the place, a functioning governance institution that makes and enforces decisions there, and conservation outcomes that follow from that governance.

On the wiki the term covers cases where a people's claim to govern a catchment is the core of the case, such as the Ogiek claim to the Mau Forest and Te Urewera. The records found describe governance and tenure; none was designed to measure water ([[research/mountain-water-commons|Mountain Water Commons]]).

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- [[glossary/f-l/fortress-conservation|Fortress conservation]]. A protected area governed by the state that removes people. A territory of life is conserved by the people who live in or use it.
- Biocultural heritage territory. A model developed by ANDES and IIED that joins customary law, landscape and genetic resources, with the Potato Park in Cusco as its reference case. It is one form a territory of life can take.
