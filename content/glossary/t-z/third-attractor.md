Daniel Schmachtenberger's term for a path for civilisation that avoids both catastrophe from unmanaged risk and dystopia from the controls imposed to prevent it.

In dynamical systems, an attractor is a state that a system tends to settle into. Schmachtenberger uses the word for the outcomes he sees societies drifting toward. The first is a chain of crises, as powerful technologies are used in competition faster than anyone can govern them. The second is the obvious response: concentrated control strong enough to stop the first, at the cost of freedom. The third attractor names a way of coordinating that can govern powerful technology without becoming oppressive. He set it out in a two-part talk on Rebel Wisdom on 18 and 23 August 2022 [IC]. The talk is not peer-reviewed, and is widely shared as an introduction in metacrisis circles [TBV].

The wiki reports the term as Schmachtenberger uses it. It doesn't adopt his forecasts of catastrophe; the network describes growth within limits without forecasting collapse.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **A strange attractor,** in chaos theory, is the pattern a chaotic system traces over time. Schmachtenberger borrows the word, and none of the mathematics.
- **The attractor** in Tom Morgan's [[sources/morgan-thinking-the-unthinkable|Thinking the Unthinkable]] is a force that draws each person toward the work only they can do. It describes a person's path, where Schmachtenberger's term describes a civilisation's.
- **The Third Way** is a centrist political programme of the 1990s, unrelated.
