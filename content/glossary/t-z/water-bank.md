An intermediary that buys, holds, leases or reallocates water rights or allocations so that water can move among users, often in a drought.

California's 1991 Drought Water Bank is a well-documented example: the state bought over 820,000 acre-feet, mainly from farmers in the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta, and sold almost 390,000 to 12 agencies, with about 150,000 acres taken out of production in the selling areas. The term is also used for storing surplus water underground for later recovery, which this wiki calls aquifer recharge.

**Contrast.** A water bank trades in claims on water; it does not issue a currency backed by water. The claims it moves can leave the place they came from, which is the pattern the wiki records for water markets in Chile and the Murray-Darling Basin.
