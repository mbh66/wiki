A convened setting, often led by researchers, in which people with different stakes try new social-ecological arrangements away from everyday pressures.

Pereira, Karpouzoglou, Frantzeskaki and Olsson (2018) set out the idea for social-ecological systems: places where farmers, officials, scientists and others can meet on equal terms, question how a landscape or resource is managed, and try something different without the usual costs of failure. The space is designed, held for a time and then opened outward. Transformation laboratories, or T-labs, are one method of convening it.

Pereira and fourteen co-authors (2020) drew five lessons from nine such spaces, mainly in the Global South and including Southern African cases, on how they are designed, convened and opened. Members of the author team convened or facilitated the spaces themselves, so the evidence is an account by the people who ran the work.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

- **A [[glossary/m-s/niche|niche]]** protects an innovation from a sector's selection pressure. A transformative space protects a conversation and a set of relationships, and may produce no single innovation.
- **A transition arena**, in Loorbach's transition management, gathers front-runners to set a long-term agenda for a sector.
- **A safe space**, in everyday use, is a setting free of harm or judgement. A transformative space is built to unsettle existing arrangements, and safety is one condition for doing that.
