# Deep Research Brief: MMT as Growth-Phase Story

## Assignment

Produce a research brief suitable for the BioConomy wiki at `research/mmt-as-growth-phase-story.md`. The brief tests the thesis that Modern Monetary Theory's core insight (sovereign governments spend before they tax; taxes do not fund government spending) inadvertently reveals that the orthodox "taxes fund the government" story was a *collective coordination story* that served the steep acceleration phase of the industrial S-curve, and that the same mechanism of collective story can be repurposed for the deceleration phase.

## The thesis to develop

The argument runs in four movements.

**First movement: the orthodox story as coordination infrastructure.** Since economists and philosophers like Adam Smith, David Ricardo, John Stuart Mill, through Karl Marx and Alfred Marshall, the dominant economic story has been that taxes keep the government going. MMT demonstrates that this is operationally false for sovereign currency issuers: the government creates the currency, spends it into circulation, and taxes destroy it. The "taxes fund spending" narrative is not a description of fiscal mechanics. It is a collective story that disciplines behavior: citizens pay taxes because they believe the state depends on it, and this belief underwrites the legitimacy of the entire fiscal contract. The story worked. It was necessary. It was the coordination architecture of the growth phase.

**Second movement: why the story served the steep part of the S-curve.** The steep part of the S-curve (roughly 1750 to 1970, the full arc of industrial acceleration) required massive mobilization of labor, capital, and natural resources. The "taxes fund government" story accomplished several things the growth phase needed: it made citizens feel they were stakeholders in the state's success (legitimacy); it created a compulsory demand for the currency (the tax obligation as currency driver, which MMT itself identifies); it framed the economy as a mechanical system of inputs and outputs where scarcity is the binding constraint (the factory model); and it made deficit spending appear dangerous, which constrained state action in ways that protected capital accumulation from political redistribution. The research should trace how this story was constructed, which thinkers reinforced it, and what coordination behaviors it produced. The Chartalist lineage (Knapp, Innes, Lerner, Minsky, Wray, Kelton, Mosler) should be mapped as the intellectual tradition that eventually decoded the story's operational falsity.

**Third movement: the story breaks down in the deceleration phase.** As the S-curve enters deceleration, the factory model of the economy loses explanatory power. The "taxes fund government" story produces increasingly dysfunctional coordination: austerity politics that prevents states from responding to ecological and social crises; debt ceilings and fiscal rules that treat accounting identities as physical constraints; and a public discourse in which every social expenditure must be "paid for" by a revenue source, which structurally advantages programs that generate taxable throughput over programs that generate ecological retention. The research should document specific cases where the "taxes fund government" story has blocked coordination that the deceleration phase requires.

**Fourth movement: from mechanical factory to ecological factory.** If the steep part of the S-curve was built on a collective story about mechanical production (the factory, the household budget, the ledger that must balance), the deceleration phase requires a collective story about ecological production (the watershed, the mycelial network, the commons that must regenerate). The research should investigate: what does a collective coordination story for the deceleration phase look like? The BioConomy wiki already has the Substrate Hypothesis (coordination is a property of substrate, not participant disposition) and the Cheapest Available Behavior Thesis (participants tend toward whatever the substrate makes cheapest). The new brief should explore whether the shift from "taxes fund government" to a deceleration-phase coordination story is itself a substrate-level shift, or only a narrative overlay that leaves the substrate intact. This is the critical diagnostic, because the wiki's existing position (in "Consensus Is Not the Bottleneck") is that correcting the intellectual model of the substrate does not change the substrate's incentive structure. The brief needs to engage that position honestly and develop it further.

## Research questions for Deep Research

1. **The genealogy of "taxes fund government."** When and how did the idea that taxes fund government spending become the dominant public understanding? Trace from Smith, Ricardo, Say, and the classical economists through the marginalist revolution and into the twentieth-century mainstream. What were the competing stories? Did the Chartalist counter-tradition (Knapp's *State Theory of Money*, 1905; Innes's two papers, 1913-1914) ever reach popular discourse, or was it always an academic minority position?

2. **MMT's own intellectual history and S-curve position.** Map the MMT lineage (Knapp, Innes, Lerner's functional finance, Minsky, Mosler's *Soft Currency Economics* 1993, Wray's *Understanding Modern Money* 1998, Kelton's *The Deficit Myth* 2020) against the S-curve timeline. Are the MMT theorists themselves structurally positioned at the inflection point of the curve, the way the retention economists were positioned on the steep part? Is MMT a deceleration-phase insight that could not have gained traction during the acceleration phase, because the acceleration phase selected for the scarcity story?

3. **Collective stories as coordination mechanisms.** What does the anthropological and sociological literature say about the role of collective economic stories (sometimes called "economic imaginaries," "fiscal myths," or "legitimating narratives") in coordinating large-scale behavior? Key authors to investigate: Benedict Anderson (*Imagined Communities*), Charles Taylor (*Modern Social Imaginaries*), Yuval Noah Harari (*Sapiens*, on "intersubjective realities"), David Graeber (*Debt: The First 5000 Years*), Mariana Mazzucato (*The Value of Everything*), Karl Polanyi (*The Great Transformation*, on the "self-regulating market" as a constructed belief). The question is not whether these stories are true or false. The question is what coordination behaviors they produce and whether those behaviors are fit for the phase.

4. **The "government as household" metaphor.** Deep Research should investigate the specific genealogy of the household-budget metaphor for government spending. When did it enter political discourse? Which political leaders and movements promoted it? What is the evidence that it shapes voter behavior and policy outcomes? Thatcher's "housewife" framing, the Balanced Budget Amendment movement in the US, the EU Stability and Growth Pact, the German Schuldenbremse (debt brake). The research should document how this single metaphor constrains coordination in the deceleration phase.

5. **Functional stories for the deceleration phase.** What coordination stories are emerging that could serve the deceleration phase the way "taxes fund government" served the acceleration phase? Candidates to investigate: the "Doughnut" (Kate Raworth), "Planetary Boundaries" (Rockström/Steffen), "the commons" (Ostrom, Bollier, Helfrich), "the living economy" (various), bioregionalism (Kirkpatrick Sale, Peter Berg), indigenous economic frameworks that never adopted the growth story. The question for each: does it function as a *coordination story* (one that disciplines behavior at scale), or only as an analytical framework (one that describes reality without producing compliance)?

6. **The tax obligation as currency driver: MMT's inadvertent confirmation of the Substrate Hypothesis.** MMT's own account of why fiat currency has value (the state imposes a tax obligation denominated in that currency, which creates demand for it) is itself a substrate-level claim. The tax obligation is the mechanism by which the state forces participation in the monetary substrate. Research whether MMT theorists have recognized this as a substrate argument, or whether they treat it purely as a descriptive claim about currency mechanics. If the BioConomy proposes an alternative substrate (bioregional demurrage currency, commitment pools, Regenerative Participation Income), what plays the role that the tax obligation plays in the fiat system? What creates the demand for the alternative unit of account?

7. **Historical precedents for coordination-story transitions.** Are there documented cases where a large-scale collective economic story was deliberately replaced by another? The shift from mercantilism to free trade (a story transition that Smith and Ricardo engineered); the shift from the gold standard to fiat currency (which required replacing the story that money must be backed by metal); the Keynesian revolution (which replaced the story that governments must balance their budgets in recessions); the neoliberal counter-revolution (which reinstated budget-balance orthodoxy). For each case, trace the mechanism of story replacement: did the new story win by intellectual persuasion, by crisis, by institutional capture, or by demonstrating superior coordination capacity?

## What the brief should NOT do

- It should not argue that MMT is wrong. MMT's operational descriptions are largely sound. The brief's contribution is to ask a different question: why did the pre-MMT story persist, and what coordination work was it doing?
- It should not become a general introduction to MMT. The wiki already engages MMT in "Consensus Is Not the Bottleneck." The new brief picks up where that page stops.
- It should not propose a specific alternative coordination story. It should identify the structural requirements such a story would need to meet, and assess the candidates against those requirements.
- It should not use conspiracy framing. The persistence of the "taxes fund government" story is explained by its coordination utility, not by deliberate deception. Pattern over conspiracy, as the wiki's standard requires.
- It should not end on a hopeful note. The brief ends with the structural requirements and the falsification tests.
